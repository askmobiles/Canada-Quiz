How Personal Income Tax Works in Canada

Written for somebody filing in Canada for the first time. Every number here is from the Canada Revenue Agency, and every number says which tax year it belongs to.

The one idea that stops people worrying

Canada's tax brackets are marginal. That means a higher rate only ever applies to the dollars above the line, never to your whole income. The Canada Revenue Agency puts it this way: only the additional amount of taxable income you made in the next bracket gets taxed at the higher rate.

So a raise cannot leave you with less money. The fear that earning more pushes you into a higher bracket and costs you overall is one of the most common misunderstandings about the Canadian system, and it is simply not how the arithmetic works.

The second thing to know is that there are two taxes, not one. Federal tax and provincial or territorial tax are calculated separately and added together. This page gives the federal numbers only. Provincial rates differ everywhere and are not published here, because we would rather show you none than show you the wrong ones.

Federal rates for the 2026 tax year

These apply to income you are earning now, on the return you will file in 2027.

Up to $58,52314%

$117,045 to $181,44026%

The number nearly every other website still gets wrong

You will read almost everywhere that Canada's lowest federal rate is 15 percent. It is not, and it has not been for over a year. The lowest rate was cut effective the first of July 2025.

For the 2026 tax year it is 14 percent. For the 2025 tax year it is 14.5 percent, because the cut landed halfway through the year and the CRA blended it. Those are the CRA's own figures. If a website or a calculator is still using 15 percent, it has not been updated since the middle of 2025.

The amount you can earn before paying federal tax

This is the basic personal amount. For the 2026 tax year it is 16,452 dollars, and for 2025 it was 16,129 dollars. It shrinks for high earners: above roughly 181,000 dollars of net income it tapers down to 14,829 dollars for 2026.

One subtlety worth understanding. It is a non-refundable credit rather than a deduction, so it reduces your tax at the lowest rate, not at your top rate. And your province has its own separate basic personal amount, which you claim as well.

Deadlines, and the trap that catches self-employed people

For the 2025 tax year, most people had to file and pay by the thirtieth of April 2026. Self-employed people and their spouses had until the fifteenth of June 2026 to file.

Here is the trap. That June extension is for filing only. If you owe money, payment was still due on the thirtieth of April. People miss this every year and are surprised by interest on a return they filed perfectly on time.

When a due date falls on a weekend or a public holiday the CRA recognises, your return counts as on time if it arrives on the next business day. We have not printed dates for the 2026 tax year here, because the CRA has not published that page yet.

If this is your first year in Canada

The CRA considers you a newcomer for the first year you are a resident of Canada for tax purposes. Note that carefully: residency for tax is not the same thing as your immigration status. It is decided by your ties to Canada — a home here, a spouse or dependants here, and secondary things like a bank account, a driver's licence and provincial health coverage.

In your first year you report your world income only for the part of the year you were a resident. You put your date of entry on the return, and income earned before that date is not reported as world income. That reassures a great many newcomers who assume Canada wants to tax what they earned abroad before they ever arrived.

You need a Social Insurance Number to work or to receive benefits, and it is illegal for anyone else to use yours.

Filing is what keeps the benefit money coming

This is the single most practical reason to file even with little or no income. The Canada Child Benefit is the clearest case. The CRA's own words are that you must file every year to continue receiving it, that your spouse or partner must file on time too, and that your payments will stop if you do not do your taxes.

A newcomer can apply for benefits before ever filing a first return, but the payments only keep coming if the returns keep coming.

One name change to know about, because no other website seems to have caught up. The GST/HST credit was renamed the Canada Groceries and Essentials Benefit in July 2026. The last payment under the old name went out on the second of April 2026. The eligibility and the calculation did not change, only the name — and filing your return is still what makes you considered for it automatically.

RRSP and TFSA, in one paragraph each

An RRSP lets you deduct contributions from your income. Your room is the lesser of eighteen percent of last year's earned income and the annual dollar limit, which was 32,490 dollars for 2025 and is 33,810 dollars for 2026. The deadline for a contribution to count on the 2025 return was the second of March 2026.

A TFSA is the opposite shape: no deduction going in, but nothing taxed coming out. The limit for 2026 is 7,000 dollars, the same as 2024 and 2025. Adding up every annual limit the CRA has published since 2009 gives 109,000 dollars, but that is our arithmetic from the CRA's table rather than a figure the CRA publishes, and it only applies to somebody who has been eighteen and resident in Canada for all of those years. A newcomer does not get it. Your own room is in your CRA My Account.

You do not have to pay anybody to file

The CRA runs a free service called SimpleFile, for people with a lower income and a simple tax situation. There is a digital version open to eligible people with or without an invitation, and phone and paper versions by invitation only. The CRA's own listing gives the cost as free.

There are also free tax clinics run by volunteers through the Community Volunteer Income Tax Program, for people with a modest income and a simple situation. And commercial software certified for NETFILE includes free options. The CRA does not charge you to file your return.

How to know a call is not really the CRA

Newcomers are targeted heavily by people pretending to be the CRA. Rather than paraphrase, here is what the CRA itself says it will never do:

  • Send refunds or payments by e-transfer or text message.
  • Provide or accept payment by cryptocurrency.
  • Demand immediate payment by e-transfer, cryptocurrency, prepaid credit card or gift card.
  • Threaten to deport or arrest you, or put you in prison.
  • Use aggressive or threatening language.
  • Set up a meeting in a public place to collect a payment.
  • Ask for personal or financial information in a voicemail or an email.

If a call frightens you, hang up. Then look up the CRA's number yourself and call back. A real officer will not mind, and a scammer will not be there.

Where these facts come from

Every figure here comes from the Canada Revenue Agency: the current and previous year rate tables, the payroll deductions formulas that publish the basic personal amount, the filing date pages, the newcomers pages, the RRSP and TFSA limit tables, the SimpleFile pages and the CRA's own scams and fraud section.

Provincial and territorial rates are not on this page. Each province has its own table and its own basic personal amount, and publishing a wrong one would be worse than publishing none. Quebec residents also file a separate provincial return with Revenu Québec.

Tax figures change every single year. Check the current numbers on canada.ca, and talk to an accountant before you make a decision that costs money.

About this page

Canadian federal tax brackets are marginal, so a higher rate applies only to income above each threshold. For the 2026 tax year the lowest federal rate is 14 percent and the basic personal amount is $16,452. Provincial tax is separate and added on top.

The page covers what a newcomer must report in their first year, why filing keeps benefit payments coming, RRSP and TFSA limits, the CRA's free filing options, and the CRA's own list of things it will never do — useful against scam calls.

Common questions

What is the lowest federal tax rate in Canada?

14 percent for the 2026 tax year, and 14.5 percent for 2025. It was cut from 15 percent effective 1 July 2025, so 2025 is a blended year. Sites still saying 15 percent are out of date.

Does a raise into a higher bracket cost me money?

No. Only the dollars above the threshold are taxed at the higher rate. Earning more never reduces your take-home pay.

When is the tax deadline for self-employed people?

For the 2025 tax year, self-employed people and their spouses had until 15 June 2026 to file — but any balance owing was still due 30 April 2026. The extension is for filing only.

Do I have to file taxes in my first year in Canada?

You must if you owe tax, want a refund, or want to start or continue benefit payments — which covers almost everyone. You report world income only for the part of the year you were a resident.

Is the GST/HST credit still called that?

No. It was renamed the Canada Groceries and Essentials Benefit in July 2026. The eligibility and calculation are unchanged, and filing your return still gets you considered automatically.

Does it cost money to file a tax return?

Not to the CRA. It runs a free service called SimpleFile for simple situations, there are free volunteer tax clinics, and certified software includes free options.
