Beginner Driver Education in Ontario

The course people take for the insurance discount — what it actually gets you, what it does not, and how to tell a real school from a fake one.

The short answer

Ontario has an official driver-training course called Beginner Driver Education, or BDE. It is approved by the Ministry of Transportation, and it is optional — nobody has to take it. It buys you two things, and it is worth being clear that only one of them is guaranteed.

The guaranteed one is time. Finish an approved course and you can take your first road test after eight months with a G1 instead of twelve. The Ministry's own handbook says it reduces your time at Level 1 by four months. The other one is the insurance discount, and that one is real but it is not a rule. Ontario's own page says an insurer may give a discount, and that completing a course does not guarantee one.

What the course actually is

An approved course is at least 40 hours, split three ways:

  • 20 hours of classroom instruction, which may be in person or online.
  • 10 hours of in-vehicle instruction, with an instructor beside you.
  • 10 flexible hours, which the school may spend on more classroom time, more online time, a simulator, more driving, or homework.

That last line is worth reading twice, because a great many websites still describe those ten hours as fixed homework. Ontario changed the standard in 2022 and made them flexible. If a school tells you the rules are still the old ones, it has not read the current standard.

The insurance discount, honestly

Ontario's regulator says most insurance companies offer a discount or a lower premium to a new driver who has finished a recognised driver-training course, and that each company applies it differently. That is as far as any official source goes.

You will see figures like ten or twenty percent quoted all over the internet. Every one of those we could find came from a driving school or a broker, not from the government or the regulator. So this page does not give you a number. Ask your own insurer before you pay for a course, and ask them in writing.

One comparison makes the point. In Ontario the only discount every insurance company must offer is for winter tires. Driver training is not on that list. It is something companies choose to reward, not something they are required to.

How to tell a real school from a fake one

Only a school approved by the Ministry of Transportation can give you the course that counts. A school that is not approved has no authority to train new drivers, and its hours will not shorten anything.

Ontario publishes the list of approved schools on its own website, and it also publishes the whole list as an open data file that anyone can download and search. If a school is not on that list, walk away.

The province also publishes the warning signs of a fraudulent school. These are its own words, not ours:

  • An offer of a guaranteed licence, or of passing without a road test.
  • Training cars that do not have a second brake pedal on the instructor's side.
  • Insisting on cash only, and not giving you a receipt.
  • Signing off hours you did not actually do.

The rest of Canada

This is not an Ontario-only idea. Almost every province has a recognised course, and in two of them it is not optional at all.

Where the discount does not exist

British Columbia, Saskatchewan and Manitoba are different, because the insurer there is public. In all three, what you pay is set by your driving record and your experience.

None of the three publishes a discount for finishing a driver-training course. In those provinces the course is still worth taking, but for the licensing time it saves rather than for the premium. Alberta is the opposite end: its government page says outright that the completion form may be used to get a discount on your insurance, though the amount is still up to the company.

Where these facts come from

Everything on this page comes from what the governments themselves publish: Ontario's driving-school and licensing pages, the Ministry's own curriculum standards and driver's handbook, Ontario's insurance regulator, and each other province's licensing pages. Nothing here was taken from a driving school's advertising, which is exactly why there is no price and no discount percentage on this page.

About this page

Ontario's Beginner Driver Education course is approved by the Ministry of Transportation and is optional. Finishing one lets you take your first road test after eight months with a G1 licence instead of twelve. An insurance discount is common but is not required by law and is not guaranteed.

This page gives no discount percentage and no course price, because no government or regulator publishes either. It also lists what every other province offers, including the two where the course is not optional.

Common questions

Does driving school lower your insurance in Ontario?

Often, but not always, and no law requires it. Ontario's own page says an insurer may give a discount and that finishing a course does not guarantee one. Ask your insurer before you pay for the course.

How much is the discount?

No government or regulator publishes a figure, so we will not print one. The numbers you see online come from driving schools and brokers. Your own insurer is the only reliable answer.

How much sooner can I take the road test?

Four months sooner. Eight months after getting your G1 instead of twelve, if you finish a government-approved course.

How long is the course?

At least 40 hours: 20 in the classroom or online, 10 in the car, and 10 flexible hours the school can use as it chooses. The old rule that fixed those last ten hours as homework was replaced in 2022.

Is the course mandatory in Ontario?

No. Ontario encourages it but does not require it. It is required in Quebec, and in Saskatchewan you cannot move up from a Class 7 licence without it.

How do I know a school is really approved?

Ontario publishes the list of government-approved schools on its own website and as a downloadable open data file. A school that is not on the list has no authority to train new drivers.
