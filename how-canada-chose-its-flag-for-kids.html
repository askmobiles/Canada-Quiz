How Canada Argued for Six Months About a Leaf

Canada is nearly 160 years old, but the red and white flag is younger than many grandparents.

For a long time Canada used flags that had the British flag in the corner. Many people wanted one that belonged only to Canada. Many other people liked the old one and did not want to change.

In 1964 the government asked Canadians to send in their ideas. Several thousand drawings arrived. People sent beavers, hockey sticks, fish, stars, and a great many maple leaves.

A history teacher named George Stanley had a simple idea: two red bands with a white square in the middle, and one red maple leaf in the square. He got the idea from the flag of the college where he taught.

Other Canadians finished it. An artist named Jacques St-Cyr drew the leaf we know, with its clean sharp points. A man named George Bist worked out exactly how wide and tall the flag should be.

Then the arguing started. Members of Parliament debated the flag from June to December 1964. They made hundreds of speeches. The final vote happened at quarter past two in the morning.

On the 15th of February 1965, the new flag was raised on Parliament Hill in Ottawa for the first time. Canada now marks that day every year as Flag Day.

Did you know? A real maple leaf can have any number of points. The one on the flag has eleven, because eleven looked best when the wind blew.
