About this game

This crossword is built entirely around Canada. The clues point to the animals, cities, coins, weather and symbols you meet across the country, so filling in the grid quietly doubles as a vocabulary lesson for anyone new here.

It behaves the way a paper crossword does, minus the eraser marks. Tap a square, type the answer, and tap that same square again to swing between Across and Down. The clue for your current position stays in a bar above the grid.

Nothing is timed and nothing is scored, which makes it comfortable to share. One person can read the clue while another spells the word, and either of them can press Reveal when a stubborn corner refuses to give way.

How it works

  • A clue bar above the grid always displays the clue for the word your cursor is sitting inside.
  • Tapping a square selects it; tapping it once more flips between its Across word and its Down word.
  • An on-screen keyboard with a delete key sits under the grid, and a physical keyboard works too, arrow keys included.
  • Full Across and Down clue lists appear below the puzzle, and tapping a clue jumps to its opening square.
  • Check turns wrong letters red without deleting them, Reveal fills in the answers, and New Puzzle deals a different grid.

Tips for playing

  • The answers lean firmly Canadian: moose, beaver, loonie, canoe, igloo, Ottawa, Quebec and maple all turn up across the grids.
  • Crossing letters are the real gift of a crossword. One answer you are sure of hands you a free letter in every word that crosses it.
  • When a clue defeats you, fill the words around it first. A half-completed slot is far easier to finish than an empty one.
  • The small numbers in the corners mark where a word begins, and one square can start both an Across and a Down word.
  • Check is kinder than Reveal: it shows what is wrong while leaving your correct letters and the rest of the challenge intact.

Common questions

Do I need a pen and paper to do this crossword?

Not at all. Everything happens on the screen, letters go straight into the squares, and mistakes are removed with the delete key rather than rubbed out.

Will my progress still be there tomorrow?

No — the letters you type live only in the open page, so closing or refreshing it starts you fresh. Treat it as a puzzle you finish in one comfortable sitting.

How hard are the clues for a child?

They are written plainly, with definitions like a night bird or a paddled boat rather than wordplay. A child who reads well can solve much of a grid with help on place names.

Can I use my computer keyboard instead of tapping?

Yes. Typing letters, using backspace and moving with the arrow keys all work on a laptop or desktop. The on-screen keys exist mainly to keep the puzzle comfortable on a phone.
