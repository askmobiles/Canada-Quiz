Yukon Rules of the Road

The rules of the road are half of the single test Yukon asks you to pass, and this page holds the rules half of our question bank. Open a question to see the answer and a short explanation, then practise the whole chapter. No timer.

Headlights in daylight, and the school bus rule

One Yukon rule surprises almost every driver arriving from elsewhere. On Yukon roadways you must drive with your low beam headlights on during the day if your vehicle does not have automatic daytime running lights. It is not a suggestion in the handbook, it is how the territory says you must drive, and older vehicles are exactly the ones it applies to.

The school bus rule has an exception that catches out drivers who learned where no such exception exists. When you are driving along a street, an undivided highway or a roadway in any direction, you must stop as you approach a stopped school bus with its upper red lights flashing. Where the highway or roadway is physically divided by a median into two separate roadways, only the drivers approaching from the rear must stop.

Getting going again has two conditions and you need both. Wait until the red lights have stopped flashing and the bus begins to move. In a school zone, reduce your speed to 30 kilometres an hour.

Permafrost, and the highways that heave

This is the chapter that has no equivalent anywhere else in Canada. Yukon publishes its own page on driving permafrost-affected roads, and it names them: the Alaska Highway between Haines Junction and Beaver Creek, the Haines Road, the Dempster, the North and South Klondike, and the Robert Campbell. Where the ground beneath the pavement thaws and refreezes, the road develops waves and dips that the territory describes as making it feel like a roller coaster, and along the Shakwak corridor you also meet cracks, potholes and frequent gravel sections.

The advice on that page is official, and it is the part worth learning by heart before a long northern drive.

  • Slow down and be cautious, because a heave you can see is usually followed by one you cannot.
  • Scan the road well ahead instead of watching the pavement just in front of the hood.
  • Avoid passing on these stretches.
  • Do not stop or pull over except in an emergency, because the shoulders are unstable.
  • Expect longer stopping distances on gravel and increase your following distance to match.
  • Remember that the posted speed limits are set for optimal road conditions, not for these ones.

Winter, and what Yukon does not publish about it

Winter driving here is published as advice with one legal hook in it. Winter tires or chains must be used where the sign requiring them is displayed, which makes that stretch of road a rule rather than a recommendation. Beyond that the territory encourages winter tires without imposing them, and we will say plainly what many sites will not: Yukon publishes no territory-wide winter tire law and no studded tire dates at all. If you read a start date and an end date for studs on another site, it did not come from the government.

What Yukon does publish is a winter driving emergency kit, and it expects every vehicle to carry one. The listed contents run from a shovel, an ice scraper, a spare tire and a flashlight to winter clothing, emergency food, booster cables, a fire extinguisher, a first aid kit and a heat source. On a highway where the next building may be a hundred kilometres away, that list is the difference between an inconvenience and an emergency.

The last two habits are official too and cost nothing. Before you leave, tell someone your route, your destination and when you plan to return. And check the road conditions on 511Yukon before you travel, because a report that a highway is snow covered or closed is far better read at home than met at speed.

Where to get the official information

Everything on this page comes from what Yukon publishes for free. The territory writes its own book, the Yukon Driver's Basic Handbook, and it is free to download from yukon.ca, so any site telling you that Yukon borrows another jurisdiction's handbook is simply wrong. There is an official French edition as well, the Guide de la route du Yukon. Whether the test itself can be sat in French is not published anywhere, even though the handbook and the licensing pages are bilingual, so we do not claim that it can. Read the handbook alongside this practice, not instead of it.

This is an unofficial free practice site. It is not affiliated with Yukon Motor Vehicles, with Highways and Public Works or with the Government of Yukon, and it cannot issue a licence. Always check the official pages for the current rules and fees before you book.

All 30 rules of the road questions, with every answer explained

This is the whole question bank from the test above, written out so you can read straight through it. The right answer is marked, and every question has a short explanation of why it is the right one.

Show all 30 questions

  1. What is the youngest age at which you can apply for a Yukon learner licence?

    • 15Correct answer
    • 14
    • 16
    • 17

    Yukon sets 15 as the minimum age for a Class 7 learner licence. You must be at least 16 before you can move up to the Class 5 Novice licence.

  2. Before a Yukon learner can move on to the next stage, what does the programme ask for?

    • At least six months holding the learner licence and 50 hours of drivingCorrect answer
    • Three months and 20 hours of driving
    • One full year and 100 hours of driving
    • No minimum period, only a passing road test

    The learner licence must be held continuously for six months, and 50 hours of driving must be completed with a co-driver before you apply for the road test.

  3. Where are those practice driving hours written down?

    • On the GDL Driver Record and Declaration, signed by you and your co-driverCorrect answer
    • In any notebook you keep for yourself
    • On the back of the learner licence
    • On a form that only a driving school may complete

    Yukon publishes the GDL Driver Record and Declaration for exactly this purpose. A parent, guardian or other valid co-driver signs it, so fill it in as you go rather than the night before you apply.

  4. How long must the novice stage be held before you can apply for a full Class 5 licence in Yukon?

    • Eighteen monthsCorrect answer
    • Six months
    • Twelve months
    • Three years

    The Class 5 Novice licence must be held continuously for eighteen months before the application for a full licence. Counting from the day you started your learner licence, the whole programme takes a minimum of two years.

  5. Who is allowed to sit beside you as your co-driver while you hold a Yukon learner licence?

    • Someone who has held at least a Class 5 licence for two years or moreCorrect answer
    • Any adult with a valid licence of any class
    • Any driver over the age of 25
    • Anyone who has passed a road test at some point

    The co-driver must hold a valid licence of at least Class 5, and must have held it for at least two years. They have to be with you every single time you drive.

  6. Your co-driver has had one beer with dinner and feels completely normal. May they supervise you?

    • No, because your co-driver must have no alcohol in their bloodCorrect answer
    • Yes, as long as they stay under the criminal limit
    • Yes, if the drive is short
    • Yes, if you keep to quiet streets

    Yukon's zero tolerance binds the supervisor as well as the learner. The co-driver must have no alcohol in their blood and must not be impaired by drugs or any other substance.

  7. During which hours may a Yukon learner drive?

    • Between 5 a.m. and midnightCorrect answer
    • Any hour, provided the co-driver is present
    • In daylight only
    • Between 6 a.m. and 10 p.m.

    The learner stage carries a curfew: driving is allowed between 5 a.m. and midnight. A co-driver beside you does not lift it.

  8. Apart from your co-driver, how many passengers may a Yukon learner carry?

    • One, unless the additional passengers are a parent, grandparent, brother, sister or childCorrect answer
    • None at all
    • Two, with no exceptions
    • One for every working seat belt

    The limit is one passenger in addition to the co-driver. Close family members named in the rule do not count against that limit, so a full car of siblings is allowed while a full car of friends is not.

  9. What sort of vehicle may a Yukon learner drive?

    • A car or light truck up to 4,000 kilograms registered gross vehicle weight, seating no more than seven including the driverCorrect answer
    • Any vehicle the co-driver is licensed for
    • Any vehicle under 10,000 kilograms
    • A passenger van of any size

    The weight cap stays at 4,000 kilograms at the novice stage, but the seating limit rises to nine including the driver. Learners and novice drivers may not tow a trailer.

  10. What has to be shown on the vehicle every time a Yukon learner drives?

    • An L sign on the back, where drivers behind you can see itCorrect answer
    • A green sticker on the windshield
    • A sign on each front door
    • Nothing extra, the licence in your pocket is enough

    The learner L sign goes on the rear of the vehicle every time you drive, visible to drivers approaching from behind. It tells the traffic behind you to expect a driver who is still learning.

  11. What score does Yukon require to pass the knowledge test for a driver's licence?

    • 80 per centCorrect answer
    • 70 per cent
    • 85 per cent
    • 90 per cent

    The Motor Vehicles Regulations set a passing score of 80 per cent, and the same figure applies to every class of licence. The regulations describe a single combined written and sign-recognition test rather than two separate exams.

  12. You do not pass the Yukon knowledge test. When can you try again, and what does it cost?

    • As early as the following day, for another 20 dollarsCorrect answer
    • After seven days, at no charge
    • After thirty days, for 20 dollars
    • Only once more, and only on the same day

    The test fee is 20 dollars, and a failed attempt can be repeated as early as the next day for another 20 dollars. Coming back the following morning with the handbook read properly is cheaper than guessing twice.

  13. What else is checked when you apply for a Yukon driver's licence?

    • Your eyesightCorrect answer
    • Your hearing
    • A short drive around the parking lot
    • A first-aid certificate

    A vision test is part of the process. If you do not pass it you are sent for further testing by an optometrist or ophthalmologist, and if you need glasses or contact lenses to meet the standard, that condition is recorded on your licence.

  14. How many demerit points bring a suspension for a driver in the graduated licensing programme in Yukon?

    • SevenCorrect answer
    • Five
    • Ten
    • Fifteen

    Graduated licensing drivers are suspended at seven demerit points. Every other licence holder is suspended at fifteen, so a new driver has less than half the margin.

  15. May a Yukon learner or novice driver use a phone mounted in a hands-free holder?

    • No, learners and novice drivers may not use any electronic device, hands-free includedCorrect answer
    • Yes, hands-free use is always allowed
    • Yes, but only for navigation
    • Yes, once the vehicle is moving at a steady speed

    Only full licence holders may use a device that is set up and used in a hands-free way. For learners and novice drivers the ban covers hand-held and hands-free alike, so the phone goes away before you start the engine.

  16. What can a distracted driving conviction cost a driver in Yukon?

    • A fine of up to 600 dollars and three demerit pointsCorrect answer
    • A written warning only
    • A fine but never any points
    • One demerit point and no fine

    Distracted driving carries a fine of up to 600 dollars and three demerit points, for full licence holders and graduated licensing drivers alike. For a graduated licensing driver those three points are almost half the way to a suspension.

  17. What happens to your graduated licensing time if you are convicted of a graduated licensing violation?

    • The time already served stops counting towards the next stage, so the clock starts againCorrect answer
    • Nothing changes, the months keep counting
    • You move up one month later than planned
    • Only your insurance is affected

    Yukon's regulations state that the time the licence was held before the violation does not count towards the total required for the next stage. One conviction can therefore cost you the whole six or eighteen months you had already built up.

  18. You are on an undivided Yukon highway and a school bus ahead stops with its upper red lights flashing. What must you do?

    • Stop, whichever direction you are travelling inCorrect answer
    • Pass slowly on the left
    • Stop only if you can see children
    • Sound your horn and keep going

    On a street, an undivided highway or a roadway, traffic in every direction must stop for a stopped school bus showing flashing upper red lights. Children may be crossing in front of the bus where you cannot see them.

  19. The same bus is stopped on a highway that is physically divided by a median into two separate roadways. Who has to stop?

    • Only the drivers approaching the bus from behindCorrect answer
    • Everybody, from both directions
    • Nobody, because the median keeps children away
    • Only the drivers in the left lane

    Where a median physically divides the highway into two roadways, only drivers coming up behind the bus must stop. On any street or undivided road, both directions stop.

  20. You have stopped for a school bus with its red lights flashing. When may you move off again?

    • When the red lights have stopped flashing and the bus starts to moveCorrect answer
    • As soon as the last child steps down
    • After waiting a slow count of ten
    • As soon as the bus door closes

    Two things have to happen: the red lights stop flashing and the bus begins to move. A closed door on its own is not the signal, because a child may still be crossing in front.

  21. Your vehicle has no automatic daytime running lights. What does Yukon law require of you in daylight?

    • You must drive with your low beam headlights onCorrect answer
    • Nothing, headlights are only for darkness
    • You must use your high beams
    • You must switch on your hazard lights

    On Yukon roadways a vehicle without automatic daytime running lights must be driven with the low beams on during the day. It is a legal requirement, and it makes you far easier to see against snow, dust and a low northern sun.

  22. Which Yukon roads does the territory name as affected by permafrost heaving?

    • The Alaska Highway between Haines Junction and Beaver Creek, the Haines Road, the Dempster, the North and South Klondike and the Robert CampbellCorrect answer
    • The Dempster Highway only
    • The streets inside Whitehorse only
    • Every road that is unpaved

    Ground ice beneath the road thaws and refreezes, leaving waves and dips the territory compares to a roller coaster. The Shakwak section also has cracks, potholes and frequent gravel stretches.

  23. You reach a stretch of highway that rolls and dips from frost heaving. What does Yukon advise?

    • Slow down, scan well ahead and avoid passingCorrect answer
    • Keep your speed up so the vehicle floats over the dips
    • Drive on the shoulder where the surface is flatter
    • Pass slower vehicles quickly to get clear of the section

    Yukon asks drivers to slow down, be cautious, scan the road ahead and avoid passing on heaved sections. A dip you cannot see into can hide oncoming traffic or a vehicle already stopped.

  24. Why does Yukon tell drivers not to pull over on permafrost-damaged highways except in an emergency?

    • Because the shoulders are unstableCorrect answer
    • Because stopping there is a ticketed offence
    • Because there is nothing worth seeing
    • Because the road belongs to a private owner

    The shoulders on these roads can give way under a vehicle. Gravel sections also lengthen your stopping distance, so increase your following distance, and remember that posted speed limits are set for optimal road conditions.

  25. A regulatory sign showing winter tires or chains is posted at the start of a section of road. What does it mean?

    • Winter tires or chains must be used from that point onCorrect answer
    • They are recommended there but remain optional
    • It applies only to commercial trucks
    • It applies only after a snowfall warning is issued

    Where that sign is displayed, winter tires or chains are required, not suggested. Turning back or waiting is the only legal choice if your vehicle is not equipped.

  26. Yukon says every vehicle should carry a winter driving emergency kit. Which of these belongs in it?

    • A shovel, a flashlight, warm clothing, food and booster cablesCorrect answer
    • A spare set of licence plates
    • One litre of windshield washer fluid and nothing more
    • A camera and a notebook for insurance

    The territory's list also includes an ice scraper, a spare tire, a fire extinguisher, a first-aid kit and a source of heat. Where help can be hours away, the kit is what keeps you warm and visible until it arrives.

  27. What does Yukon recommend doing to your vehicle before the winter driving season?

    • Install winter tires and carry an emergency winter driving kitCorrect answer
    • Fit the widest tires you can find
    • Let air out of the tires for extra grip
    • Take the spare tire out to save weight

    Winter tires stay soft and keep gripping at temperatures where all-season rubber goes hard. Yukon pairs that advice with carrying the emergency kit, because the two problems arrive together.

  28. You are about to drive a long, empty stretch of Yukon highway. What does the territory ask you to do first?

    • Tell someone your route, your destination and when you expect to be backCorrect answer
    • Nothing in particular, if the weather looks clear
    • Post your plans on social media
    • Drive only during daylight hours

    Yukon asks drivers to tell someone their travel plans and their estimated time of travel before leaving. If you do not arrive, that is the information that tells searchers where to look.

  29. Where can a Yukon driver check road conditions before setting out?

    • Through 511Yukon, the territory's road report serviceCorrect answer
    • At the vehicle registration counter
    • On a local radio phone-in show only
    • Nowhere official, so you have to judge for yourself

    Yukon publishes road and weather conditions through 511Yukon and asks drivers to check them before travelling. Conditions in the north can change completely between one valley and the next.

  30. What does Yukon suggest taking with you when you travel far from communities?

    • Extra fuel and a satellite phone or CB radioCorrect answer
    • A second driver at all times
    • A trailer loaded with supplies
    • A mobile phone and nothing else

    Distances between services are long and mobile coverage cannot be relied on, so Yukon suggests extra fuel and a satellite phone or CB radio. Both are for the trip that does not go to plan.

About this page

The rules of the road are the other half of the single test Yukon asks you to pass, and this page holds the rules half of our question bank, arranged by chapter.

Several of these rules are genuinely local. The daytime low beams for a vehicle without daytime running lights, the divided highway exception to the school bus rule, the permafrost highways and the zero alcohol rule that binds the co-driver as well as the learner are all specific to this territory, and all of them come from official sources.

What you'll find here

  • Thirty rules questions grouped by chapter, each with the answer marked and explained.
  • Graduated licensing, from the Class 7 learner licence through the Class 5 Novice licence to a full Class 5.
  • Learner and novice restrictions, including the curfew, the passenger limit, the vehicle size cap and the L sign.
  • Demerit points and suspensions, which arrive at seven points for a graduated licensing driver.
  • Northern highways and permafrost, winter driving with the emergency kit, and remote travel with 511Yukon.

Worth remembering

  • On Yukon roadways you must drive with low beam headlights on during the day if your vehicle has no automatic daytime running lights.
  • A graduated licensing driver is suspended at seven demerit points and every other licence holder at fifteen, with a first suspension of one month, three months for a second within a year and six months for a third or more within two years.
  • You stop for a school bus with its upper red lights flashing on a street, an undivided highway or a roadway in any direction, and only drivers approaching from the rear must stop where a median physically divides the highway into two separate roadways.
  • In a school zone you reduce your speed to 30 kilometres an hour.
  • Distracted driving carries a fine of up to six hundred dollars and three demerit points, and learners and novice drivers may not use any electronic device, hands-free included.

Common questions

Do I really have to use my headlights during the day?

Yes, if your vehicle has no automatic daytime running lights. On Yukon roadways you must drive with the low beams on during the day in that case, and it is one of the rules visitors break without knowing it.

Do I stop for a school bus coming the other way?

On a street, an undivided highway or a roadway, yes, in any direction. Where a median physically divides the highway into two separate roadways, only drivers approaching from the rear must stop. Move off only once the red lights have stopped flashing and the bus begins to move.

How many demerit points suspend a new driver?

Seven for a driver in the graduated licensing programme, and fifteen for everybody else. A first suspension lasts one month, a second within a year three months, and a third or more within two years six months.

Are winter tires required in Yukon?

Only where the sign requiring winter tires or chains is displayed. Yukon publishes no territory-wide winter tire law and no studded tire dates, so treat any date you read elsewhere as somebody's guess. The territory does ask every vehicle to carry an emergency winter driving kit.

What should I know before driving the Alaska Highway or the Dempster?

Those roads sit on permafrost and heave into waves and dips. Slow down, scan well ahead, avoid passing, and do not stop or pull over except in an emergency because the shoulders are unstable. Expect longer stopping distances on gravel, increase your following distance, and tell someone your route, your destination and when you plan to return.
