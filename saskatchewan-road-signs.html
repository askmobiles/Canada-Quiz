Saskatchewan Road Signs

This is the half of the Saskatchewan knowledge test SGI calls the Sign test. Signs are grouped by type. Look at the pictures first, then practise the group with real test questions. No timer.

Read the shape and the colour first

Canadian road signs tell you a great deal before you read a single word. A red circle with a slash means you must not do the thing shown; a green circle means you may. A yellow diamond warns about something ahead, and an orange diamond means road work. Green and blue signs give directions and services. Signs are the same right across Canada, so what you learn here travels with you.

Because SGI marks the Sign test separately, this page is worth working through on its own until the shapes are instant. You can pass the Basic test and still be sent back to rewrite this one.

All 30 road sign questions, with every answer explained

Show all 30 questions

  1. Where must you stop at this sign?

    • At the stop line, or before the crosswalk if there is no lineCorrect answer
    • Level with the sign post
    • Anywhere inside the intersection
    • Only if another vehicle is coming

    Stop at the line first. If a snowbank or a parked truck blocks your view from there, creep forward and stop a second time once you can actually see.

  2. What does this sign require?

    • Slow down and give way, stopping only if you mustCorrect answer
    • A full stop every time
    • Speed up to merge
    • Nothing, it is a warning

    Yield means the other traffic goes first. You may keep rolling if the way is genuinely clear, but you must be ready to stop.

  3. What does a sign like this tell you?

    • The maximum speed in good conditionsCorrect answer
    • The speed you must drive
    • The minimum speed
    • A suggested speed for trucks

    A posted limit is a maximum for ideal conditions. On glare ice or in blowing snow the safe speed is far lower, and driving the posted number can still be too fast.

  4. What is about to happen?

    • A new speed limit starts aheadCorrect answer
    • The road ends
    • Speeding is not enforced here
    • The limit you see applies behind you

    This is your warning to be at the new speed by the time you reach the sign, not to start slowing after it.

  5. What does this sign mean?

    • Do not drive in past this pointCorrect answer
    • No parking
    • Road closed to trucks only
    • Enter slowly

    You are looking at the wrong end of a one-way street or an exit ramp. Traffic here is coming at you.

  6. What must you do here?

    • Travel only in the direction of the arrowCorrect answer
    • Choose either direction
    • Stop and wait
    • Use this lane only to turn

    One way means one way. Check the arrow before you turn in, especially downtown where two one-way streets often run side by side.

  7. What is not allowed at this intersection?

    • Turning leftCorrect answer
    • Turning right
    • Going straight through
    • Stopping

    Carry on and turn left further along. Three right turns around a block will get you there legally.

  8. What does this sign prohibit?

    • Passing another vehicleCorrect answer
    • Parking on the shoulder
    • Driving over 60 km/h
    • Trucks in the right lane

    You cannot see far enough ahead to pass safely. On a two-lane highway that is exactly where head-on crashes happen.

  9. What does this sign mean?

    • You may stop briefly to load or drop someone off, but not parkCorrect answer
    • You may not stop for any reason
    • Parking is free here
    • Parking for residents only

    No parking still lets you pause to let a passenger out. No stopping does not — the two signs are different.

  10. How is this different from a no parking sign?

    • You may not stop at all, even to drop someone offCorrect answer
    • It is the same thing
    • It applies only overnight
    • It applies only to trucks

    No stopping means keep moving unless traffic or a police officer makes you stop. These go where a stopped car would be dangerous.

  11. What does this sign forbid?

    • Turning around to go back the way you cameCorrect answer
    • Turning left
    • Reversing
    • Three-point turns only

    U-turns are banned where oncoming drivers would not see you swing across in time.

  12. What must you do when you see this?

    • Pass to the right of the obstacleCorrect answer
    • Pass on either side
    • Stop and wait
    • Turn right at the next street

    Something splits the road — a median, an island, a work zone. Take the right side of it.

  13. What does this lane sign require?

    • Everyone in this lane must turn leftCorrect answer
    • Left turns are allowed but not required
    • Left turns are banned
    • Only buses may turn left

    If you are in this lane you are turning. Change lanes well before the intersection if you meant to go straight.

  14. What must you do at this crossing?

    • Yield to pedestrians and wait until they are safely acrossCorrect answer
    • Sound your horn
    • Speed up to clear the crossing
    • Stop only if a light is flashing

    Wait until the person is fully across, not just past your own bumper.

  15. A school bus ahead has its red lights flashing. What must you do?

    • Stop no closer than 5 metres from the bus and waitCorrect answer
    • Slow to 30 km/h and pass
    • Pass on the left only
    • Stop only if children are visible

    In Saskatchewan you stop no closer than 5 m (15 ft) from the front or the back, from either direction on an undivided highway, until the lights go off and the stop arm folds in.

  16. What does this sign tell you?

    • A school zone is ahead and a lower posted limit may applyCorrect answer
    • School is closed
    • Children may not cross here
    • Parking for school staff

    Watch for the posted speed and the hours on the sign itself. Children step out from between parked cars without looking.

  17. What should you expect after this sign?

    • Children playing near the roadCorrect answer
    • A rest area
    • A sports stadium
    • A parking lot

    A ball rolling onto the road is usually followed by a child. Cover the brake.

  18. What does this marker identify?

    • A railway crossingCorrect answer
    • A crossroads
    • A bridge
    • A hospital

    Look both ways and listen. A long freight train is quieter and far faster than it looks from the side.

  19. You see this sign. What is coming?

    • A railway crossing aheadCorrect answer
    • A level crossing you have already passed
    • A train station
    • A truck route

    Be ready to stop. Never start across unless there is room for your whole vehicle on the far side.

  20. What does this sign warn you about?

    • Deer may cross the road hereCorrect answer
    • A hunting area
    • A farm entrance
    • A wildlife park

    Dawn and dusk are the worst hours, and deer travel in groups — if one crosses, slow right down and expect another.

  21. Why is this animal a particular danger at highway speed?

    • It is tall, so the body comes through the windshieldCorrect answer
    • It always runs away from cars
    • It is only active in summer
    • It is smaller than a deer

    A moose stands so high that the bumper takes out its legs and the body lands on the roof and windshield. Brake hard and aim behind it, never in front.

  22. What does this sign mean?

    • The road may be slippery, especially when wet or icyCorrect answer
    • The road is closed
    • A car wash is ahead
    • Loose gravel ahead

    Ease off before you reach it. Braking or steering hard on a slippery surface is how a skid starts.

  23. What should you do when you see this?

    • Shift to a lower gear and control your speed before the descentCorrect answer
    • Speed up to get it over with
    • Turn off the engine to save fuel
    • Use the shoulder

    Let the engine hold you back. Riding the brakes all the way down heats them until they fade.

  24. What is ahead?

    • A curve to the rightCorrect answer
    • A right turn only lane
    • A road that ends
    • A right hand exit

    Slow down before the curve, not in it. Braking mid-corner unsettles the car.

  25. What is this sign telling you?

    • The road turns sharply in this directionCorrect answer
    • You may pass here
    • The lane is closed
    • There is a picnic area

    Several in a row mark a bend that is much tighter than it looks in the dark.

  26. What does this sign warn about?

    • Traffic is joining your road aheadCorrect answer
    • Your lane ends immediately
    • A passing lane starts
    • A truck stop is ahead

    Merging works when both sides adjust. Make room where you can and match the speed of the traffic you are joining.

  27. How do you drive a roundabout?

    • Yield to traffic already in the circle, then enter and keep right aroundCorrect answer
    • Stop fully before entering
    • Traffic entering has the right of way
    • Drive around it the other way

    Signal your exit, not your entry, and never stop inside the circle.

  28. What should you expect on this road?

    • Slow farm equipment that may be wider than a laneCorrect answer
    • A farmers market
    • Livestock on the road
    • A weigh station

    A combine at 25 km/h closes on you fast at highway speed. Wait for a long clear stretch before passing, and expect it to be wider than it looks.

  29. You see this sign and then a person holding a sign in the road. What must you do?

    • Obey the flagger, even if it contradicts the signs or lightsCorrect answer
    • Ignore them and follow the signs
    • Sound your horn and continue
    • Stop only if the road is blocked

    A traffic control person's direction overrides everything else on the road. Workers are metres from your bumper.

  30. What does a flashing red light mean?

    • Treat it exactly like a stop signCorrect answer
    • Slow down and continue
    • The light is broken, so proceed
    • Stop only if traffic is coming

    Come to a complete stop, then go when it is safe.

About this page

This page is SGI's Sign test: road sign identification, on its own, grouped by type. The pictures are drawn in code rather than photographed, so they load instantly and stay sharp on any screen.

SGI divides the Saskatchewan knowledge test into two parts that are marked separately: a Basic test on driving situations and the rules of the road, and a Sign test on sign identification. If you fail one, you rewrite only that one. That is why this site keeps signs and rules as two separate study pages.

Look at a group first, then practise that group with real test questions. There is no timer, because recognition comes from repetition rather than pressure.

What you'll find here

  • Every sign grouped by type: regulatory, warning, temporary work zone, information and lane markings.
  • Thirty sign questions written for Saskatchewan, with an explanation on every answer.
  • Practice for one group at a time, so you can fix a weak area instead of rereading everything.
  • Signs are national, so what you learn here works in any province you drive in later.
  • No timer and no signup.

Worth remembering

  • Shape and colour carry the meaning. A red circle with a slash forbids; a green circle permits.
  • A yellow diamond warns about the road ahead. An orange diamond means road work and people on foot nearby.
  • A flashing red light is a stop sign. Come to a complete stop, then go when it is safe.
  • A traffic control person's directions override the signs and the lights, every time.
  • Deer and moose signs mark places where animals actually cross, not general countryside decoration.

Common questions

How many questions are on the real Saskatchewan knowledge test?

SGI does not publish that number, and we are not going to invent one. What SGI does state is that the test comes in two parts, Basic and Sign, that they are marked separately, and that you rewrite only the part you fail.

What is the pass mark?

SGI does not publish a pass mark either. Our mock test uses a practice standard of our own so you have something to aim at, and we label it as ours rather than pretending it is the official one.

Can I take the test more than once in a day?

No. SGI allows one attempt per day. A passed written result stays valid for one year.

Where must my supervising driver sit?

In the front passenger seat, at all hours. They need a Class 5 or better licence from Canada or the United States, held for at least 365 days within the past three years.

Can I use a hands-free phone as a new driver?

No. New drivers may not use a cellphone at all, hand-held or hands-free. Hands-free is legal for fully licensed drivers, not at the learner or novice stages.
