The numbers worth memorising

A few facts come up again and again. Keep two seconds of space behind the car in front on a dry road, and more when it is wet. Leave one metre when you pass a cyclist. Stay at least 150 metres behind a fire truck answering a call. Do not park within three metres of a fire hydrant. A G1 or G2 driver, and every driver aged 21 and under, must have zero alcohol in their blood. Demerit points stay on your record for two years from the date of the offence.

  1. While you drive with a G1 licence, how much alcohol may the accompanying driver beside you have in their blood?

    • Below .05Correct answer
    • Zero, the same as your own limit
    • Below .08
    • No limit applies to them

    The accompanying driver must stay below .05. They also need a full class G licence with at least four years of driving experience, and they must sit in the front passenger seat. Your own limit as a G1 driver stays at zero.

  2. The driver who rides with you while you drive on a G1 licence must have how many years of driving experience?

    • At least four yearsCorrect answer
    • Two years
    • One year
    • Ten years

    Your accompanying driver needs a full class G licence or better and at least four years of driving experience. Time held as a G2 counts toward those four years.

  3. Where must your accompanying driver sit while you drive with a G1 licence?

    • In the front passenger seatCorrect answer
    • Anywhere in the vehicle
    • Directly behind you
    • In the back seat

    The accompanying driver must sit beside you at the front so they can help right away. Their own blood alcohol must stay under .05.

  4. With a G1 licence, you are not allowed to drive between which hours?

    • Midnight and 5 a.m.Correct answer
    • 10 p.m. and 6 a.m.
    • 11 p.m. and 4 a.m.
    • Sunset and sunrise

    G1 drivers must stay off the road from midnight to 5 a.m. Those hours bring the most tired drivers and the least light.

  5. Which roads is a G1 driver not allowed to drive on?

    • High-speed 400-series highways and certain named expressways, unless a licensed driving instructor is beside youCorrect answer
    • Any road with a speed limit over 60 km/h
    • Every road outside your own city or town
    • Any road after dark, no matter who is with you

    Normally a G1 driver must stay off 400-series highways posted above 80 km/h, and off roads such as the QEW, the Don Valley Parkway and the Gardiner Expressway. That restriction is lifted only when a licensed driving instructor is with you.

  6. How long must you normally hold a G1 licence before your first road test?

    • 12 months, or 8 months with an approved driver education courseCorrect answer
    • 6 months
    • 3 months
    • 24 months

    The usual wait is 12 months. Finishing a driver education course approved by the government shortens it to 8 months.

  7. How long must you hold a G2 licence before you may take the second road test?

    • At least 12 monthsCorrect answer
    • At least 3 months
    • At least 6 months
    • At least 18 months

    You practise for at least 12 months as a G2. The second road test includes highway driving, so you need real experience at 80 km/h and above.

  8. How long do you have to finish the whole graduated licensing process and earn a full G licence?

    • Five yearsCorrect answer
    • Two years
    • Three years
    • There is no time limit

    You have five years from the day you get your G1. If you do not hold a full G by then, you must start the whole process over again.

  9. In their first six months, a G2 driver aged 19 or under may carry how many passengers aged 19 or under between midnight and 5 a.m.?

    • Only oneCorrect answer
    • Three
    • None
    • As many as there are seat belts

    In the first six months only one young passenger is allowed during those hours; after six months it rises to three, until you turn 20 or earn your full G. Immediate family members, and a fully licensed driver in the front seat, are not counted.

  10. How many road tests must you pass to go from a G1 licence to a full G licence?

    • TwoCorrect answer
    • None, only written tests
    • Three
    • One

    There are two road tests. The first moves you from G1 to G2, and the second gives you the full G licence.

  11. On a dry road, what is the smallest safe following distance behind the vehicle ahead of you?

    • Two secondsCorrect answer
    • One car length
    • Half a second
    • Ten metres at any speed

    Pick a fixed object ahead, such as a pole. When the vehicle in front passes it, you should be able to count two full seconds before you reach it. Leave more room in rain, snow or behind a large truck.

  12. Your mirrors are well adjusted. What else must you do before changing lanes?

    • Turn your head and look over your shoulderCorrect answer
    • Nothing, the mirrors show everything
    • Flash your headlights
    • Speed up first

    Mirrors leave blind spots on both sides of your vehicle. Only a shoulder check shows a car hiding there.

  13. Two vehicles stop at an all-way stop at exactly the same moment. Which one goes first?

    • The one on the rightCorrect answer
    • The larger vehicle
    • The one on the left
    • The one that signals first

    The first vehicle to stop goes first. When two stop together, the driver on the left yields to the driver on the right.

  14. You are stopped at a green light waiting to turn left. What must you do?

    • Yield to oncoming traffic and to people crossing your pathCorrect answer
    • Turn quickly before the oncoming car arrives
    • Hold your wheels turned to the left while you wait
    • Sound your horn and turn

    A left turn crosses the path of oncoming traffic and of people on foot, so they go first. Keep your wheels straight while waiting, so a hit from behind cannot push you into oncoming cars.

  15. How close to a fire hydrant may you park?

    • No closer than three metresCorrect answer
    • One metre
    • Ten metres
    • Five metres

    Stay at least three metres away so firefighters can reach the hydrant fast. Blocking one can get your vehicle ticketed and towed.

  16. You are backing straight out of a parking spot. Where should you look?

    • Turn your body and look back over your right shoulder, checking all around as you moveCorrect answer
    • Only in the rear-view mirror
    • Only at the back-up camera
    • Only over your left shoulder

    A mirror or a camera does not show everything behind and beside you. Turn and look through the back window, and keep checking both sides the whole time you are moving.

  17. In Ontario a child must keep using a booster seat until they reach at least one of three limits. What are they?

    • Age 8, 36 kg or 145 cmCorrect answer
    • Age 6, 25 kg or 120 cm
    • Age 10, 45 kg or 160 cm
    • Age 12, 50 kg or 150 cm

    A booster seat is required until the child turns 8, weighs 36 kg, or is 145 cm tall, whichever comes first. Below that, a seat belt sits in the wrong place on a small body.

  18. A G1 or G2 driver is convicted for the first time of using a hand-held phone while driving. What happens?

    • A 30-day licence suspension and a fineCorrect answer
    • Three demerit points only
    • A warning letter
    • Nothing until the third time

    Novice drivers get no demerit points for this — they get a suspension instead. It is 30 days the first time, 90 days the second, and the licence is cancelled the third time.

  19. There is no speed limit sign posted on the road. What is the maximum speed?

    • 50 km/h in cities, towns and villages, and 80 km/h outside themCorrect answer
    • 60 km/h everywhere
    • 40 km/h in a city and 90 km/h outside
    • Whatever the traffic around you is doing

    When no sign is posted, Ontario sets 50 km/h inside built-up areas and 80 km/h outside them. Watch for signs, because many roads are posted lower.

  20. You switch on your turn signal to move into the next lane. What does that signal do?

    • It only shows others what you plan to do — you must still make sure the way is clearCorrect answer
    • It gives you the right of way
    • It forces other drivers to let you in
    • It lets you move over without checking

    A signal is a message, not a permission. Signal early, then check your mirrors and your blind spot before you move.

  21. When you pass a cyclist, how much space must you leave between your vehicle and the bicycle?

    • At least one metre, where it is possibleCorrect answer
    • Half a metre
    • Two metres
    • Just enough to clear your mirror

    The law asks for a minimum of one metre where practical. Passing too close can bring a fine and two demerit points.

  22. How can you tell whether the driver of a large truck can see you?

    • If you can see the driver's face in the truck's side mirrorCorrect answer
    • If you can see the trailer
    • If your headlights are on
    • If you are within two car lengths

    Large trucks have long blind spots on both sides, in front and behind. If you cannot see the driver's face in the mirror, the driver cannot see you, so do not stay there.

  23. How much road space is a motorcycle entitled to?

    • A full lane, like any other vehicleCorrect answer
    • Half a lane, so a car may share it
    • Only the right edge of the lane
    • The shoulder

    A motorcycle has the right to a whole lane. Never squeeze past one inside the same lane, and remember that many motorcycle signals do not switch off by themselves.

  24. Someone is walking across the road at a pedestrian crossover. When may you drive on?

    • Only once they have finished crossing and are back on the sidewalkCorrect answer
    • As soon as they pass the middle of the road
    • As soon as they are out of your lane
    • After you sound your horn

    At pedestrian crossovers, and at school crossings with a crossing guard, you must wait until the person has cleared the whole road.

  25. A school bus ahead of you turns on its flashing red lights and swings out its stop arm. What must you do?

    • Stop at least 20 metres behind itCorrect answer
    • Slow to 20 km/h and pass
    • Stop only if you can see children
    • Pass on the left carefully

    You must come to a full stop and stay stopped until the red lights stop flashing and the arm folds back. Coming from behind, stop no closer than 20 metres.

  26. You are on a road where a median separates the two directions. A school bus on the other side has its red lights flashing. What do you do?

    • Keep driving — only vehicles behind the bus must stopCorrect answer
    • Stop and wait
    • Stop only if children are visible
    • Change lanes, then stop

    Where a median divides the road, drivers coming toward the bus do not have to stop. Every driver behind the bus still must.

  27. How many demerit points come with failing to stop for a school bus?

    • SixCorrect answer
    • Three
    • Two
    • One

    Failing to stop for a school bus carries six demerit points plus a fine. It is one of the heaviest point penalties in Ontario.

  28. A fire truck is travelling ahead of you with its lights and siren on, answering an alarm. How closely may you follow it?

    • No closer than 150 metresCorrect answer
    • No closer than 30 metres
    • You may drive right behind it to get through traffic
    • No closer than 60 metres

    You must not follow a fire vehicle that is responding to an alarm within 150 metres. Staying well back keeps the road clear for the firefighters and for other emergency vehicles heading to the same call.

  29. A police car and a tow truck are stopped on the shoulder with their lights flashing, on a road with two lanes going your way. What does the law ask of you?

    • Slow down and, if you can do it safely, move over so an empty lane is left between you and themCorrect answer
    • Keep your speed and stay in your lane
    • Come to a full stop until they leave
    • Sound your horn as you pass

    This is the slow down and move over rule. Breaking it brings a fine, three demerit points, and possibly a licence suspension.

  30. A streetcar ahead of you stops and its rear doors open. What must you do?

    • Stop at least two metres behind the rear doors and waitCorrect answer
    • Pass slowly on the left
    • Pass on the right without stopping
    • Stop level with the open doors

    Passengers step straight onto the road. Wait until they are safely on the sidewalk or the island, then pass the streetcar on the right, unless you are on a one-way street.

  31. When does the law say you must turn your headlights on?

    • From half an hour before sunset until half an hour after sunriseCorrect answer
    • Only once it is fully dark outside
    • Only between midnight and 5 a.m.
    • Only on roads with no streetlights

    Ontario sets the rule by the clock, not by how dark it feels. Lights must be on from half an hour before sunset to half an hour after sunrise.

  32. Besides night time, you must also use your headlights when poor weather stops you from clearly seeing people or vehicles less than how far away?

    • 150 metresCorrect answer
    • 50 metres
    • 75 metres
    • 100 metres

    Fog, heavy rain or blowing snow can cut your view sharply. If you cannot clearly see people or vehicles under 150 metres away, your headlights must be on.

  33. You are driving into thick fog. Which lights should you use?

    • Low-beam headlightsCorrect answer
    • High beams, so the light reaches farther
    • Parking lights only
    • Hazard lights while you keep driving at normal speed

    High beams bounce off the tiny water drops in fog and throw the light back at you, so you see less. Low beams aim down at the road and work better.

  34. Your car starts to skid on a slippery road. What should you do first?

    • Ease your foot off the gas or the brake and keep steering where you want to goCorrect answer
    • Press the brake pedal down hard and hold it
    • Turn the wheel quickly in the opposite direction
    • Press the gas to pull the car straight

    Hard braking locks the tires and makes a skid worse. Taking the pressure off lets the tires start rolling and gripping again, and your eyes and hands should stay pointed at the open road ahead.

  35. In heavy rain your steering suddenly feels light, as if the car is floating. What is happening?

    • The tires are riding on a film of water instead of touching the roadCorrect answer
    • The power steering has failed
    • A tire has gone flat
    • The brakes have overheated

    This is hydroplaning. Ease off the gas, do not brake hard and hold the wheel steady until the tires touch the road again. Good tire tread and lower speed are the best protection.

  36. On a cold day, which part of the road is most likely to be icy first?

    • Bridges, overpasses and shaded areasCorrect answer
    • Long straight stretches in full sun
    • The middle of a busy downtown street
    • Gravel roads only

    Cold air passes above and below a bridge, so it freezes before the rest of the road. Shaded spots stay cold too. Black ice looks like shiny black pavement, while safe cold asphalt looks greyish white.

  37. The road is wet, snowy or icy. How should you change the way you follow the car ahead?

    • Leave a much larger gap, because you need far more room to stopCorrect answer
    • Keep the same gap, since brakes work the same in any weather
    • Move closer so you can see their tail lights clearly
    • The gap does not matter as long as you drive slowly

    On a slippery surface your tires need much more distance to bring the car to a stop, so the safe following gap grows a lot compared with a dry road.

  38. How long do demerit points stay on your driving record in Ontario?

    • Two years from the date of the offenceCorrect answer
    • Six months from the date of the offence
    • Five years from the date of the offence
    • They never come off

    Points are recorded from the date the offence happened and drop off two years later. They disappear on their own, but the conviction itself stays on your record longer.

  39. A driver with a full G licence reaches 15 demerit points. What happens?

    • Their licence is suspended for 30 daysCorrect answer
    • Nothing, but they get a warning letter
    • They must retake the road test that week
    • They lose the licence for good

    For a fully licensed driver the ministry sends warning letters on the way up, but 15 points brings a 30-day suspension. After it ends the record is set back to 7 points.

  40. A novice driver (G1 or G2) collects 9 demerit points. What is the result?

    • A 60-day suspensionCorrect answer
    • A warning letter only
    • A 30-day suspension
    • No action until they reach 15 points

    Novice drivers are held to a tighter standard than fully licensed drivers. A warning letter starts at 2 points, a second letter at 6, and 9 points means a 60-day suspension.

  41. How often must an Ontario driver's licence be renewed?

    • Every 5 yearsCorrect answer
    • Every year
    • Every 10 years
    • Only when you move or change your name

    A licence is valid for five years. Renewing late or letting it lapse can mean extra tests, so watch the expiry date printed on the card.

  42. A driver is stopped for stunt driving (street racing). What happens at the roadside, before any court date?

    • An immediate licence suspension and the vehicle is impoundedCorrect answer
    • Only a ticket in the mail
    • A warning for a first offence
    • Nothing until a judge decides

    Stunt driving brings an on-the-spot 30-day licence suspension and a 14-day vehicle impoundment. Going 40 km/h or more over a limit under 80 km/h, 50 km/h or more over any limit, or 150 km/h or more all count as stunt driving.

  43. Your doctor finds a medical condition that could make driving unsafe. What does Ontario law require?

    • The doctor must report it to the Ministry of TransportationCorrect answer
    • Nothing — it stays between you and your doctor
    • You must sell your vehicle
    • You must switch to a G2 licence

    Doctors and nurse practitioners have to report conditions that may make a person unsafe to drive, and optometrists report serious vision problems. A report of a listed condition leads to a licence suspension until the ministry reviews your case.

  44. What is the criminal blood alcohol limit for driving in Canada?

    • 0.08Correct answer
    • 0.02
    • 0.04
    • 0.06

    At 0.08 or more you can be charged under the Criminal Code. In Ontario that also brings an immediate 90-day licence suspension and a 7-day vehicle impoundment before you ever see a courtroom.

  45. A fully licensed driver blows between 0.05 and 0.079 — under the criminal limit. What happens?

    • This is the “warn range” and the licence is suspended right there at the roadsideCorrect answer
    • Nothing, because it is below 0.08
    • A verbal warning only
    • The driver is charged with a criminal offence

    Ontario adds its own rule below the criminal limit. Blowing in the warn range means an immediate roadside suspension that gets longer each time it happens, plus education or treatment programs for repeat occurrences.

  46. How much alcohol may a G1 or G2 driver, or any driver aged 21 and under, have in their blood?

    • None at all — the limit is zeroCorrect answer
    • Up to 0.02
    • Up to 0.05
    • The same 0.08 as everyone else

    Zero tolerance applies to every novice driver and to every driver 21 and under, whatever their licence class. One drink is enough to bring an immediate roadside suspension.

  47. A police officer demands a breath sample and the driver refuses. What is the result?

    • Refusing is treated as seriously as failing the test, with the same suspension and impoundmentCorrect answer
    • The officer must let the driver go
    • The driver may be asked again in a week
    • The driver only gets demerit points

    Refusing or failing to comply with a lawful breath or drug test is itself an offence. It brings the same immediate 90-day licence suspension and 7-day vehicle impoundment as blowing over the criminal limit.

  48. How does Ontario treat driving after using cannabis?

    • The penalties are the same as for driving impaired by alcoholCorrect answer
    • It is allowed as long as you feel fine
    • It is only an issue for commercial drivers
    • It is legal because cannabis itself is legal

    Cannabis being legal to buy does not make it legal to drive after using it. Drivers face the same suspensions, impoundments and charges as with alcohol, and novice and 21-and-under drivers must have none in their system at all.

  49. You are very tired, or you have taken a cold medicine that warns it may cause drowsiness. What should you do?

    • Do not drive — rest, or let someone else driveCorrect answer
    • Drive with the window open and the radio loud
    • Drink coffee and carry on as planned
    • Drive, but stay in the right-hand lane

    Tiredness and some medicines slow your reactions much like alcohol does, and a driver can be charged for being impaired by legal drugs. Fresh air and coffee do not fix it — only rest does.

  50. Are winter tires required by law in Ontario?

    • No, but they grip far better in cold weather and insurers offer a discount for themCorrect answer
    • Yes, on every vehicle from December through March
    • Yes, but only in northern Ontario
    • No, and they make no real difference to safety

    Unlike Quebec, Ontario does not force drivers to fit winter tires. They still stay soft and grip in cold weather when all-season rubber goes hard, which shortens stopping distances.

  51. You are about to leave on a long highway trip. What is the sensible check to make before you go?

    • Tires, lights, signals, wipers, washer fluid and oilCorrect answer
    • Only the fuel level
    • Only the tire pressure
    • Nothing, if the vehicle passed a safety check last year

    A quick walk-around costs a few minutes and catches the small faults that turn into a roadside breakdown far from home. Tires and lights matter most, because both affect whether you can stop and be seen.

  52. Where should the head restraint behind your head be set?

    • So the middle of the restraint sits right behind the back of your headCorrect answer
    • As low as it goes, so it does not block your view
    • Removed, for more room
    • Tilted as far forward as it will go

    A head restraint set too low lets your head snap backwards in a rear-end crash, which is how neck injuries happen. Set at head height it catches your head instead.

  53. You are towing a trailer with safety chains. How should the chains be attached?

    • Crossed underneath the trailer tongue, so the tongue cannot drop to the road if the hitch lets goCorrect answer
    • Left loose and dragging so they do not rub the paint
    • Attached to the trailer only
    • Wrapped around the trailer wheel

    Crossed chains form a cradle that catches the tongue if the hitch fails, keeping the trailer behind you instead of digging into the pavement. Loads on the trailer must also be tied down — a load that can fall off is an offence.

  54. Your brake pedal goes to the floor and the car does not slow down. What should you do?

    • Pump the brake pedal quickly, then apply the parking brake gently while holding its release buttonCorrect answer
    • Switch the engine off at once
    • Steer into the oncoming lane to find space
    • Open the door and drag your foot on the road

    Pumping can build back enough pressure to stop you. If it does not, easing the parking brake on slows the car, and holding the release button lets you let go instantly if the back wheels start to lock.

  55. A front tire blows out at highway speed. What is the right response?

    • Hold the wheel firmly, ease off the gas and steer straight until you slow down, then pull off the roadCorrect answer
    • Brake as hard as you can right away
    • Turn the wheel sharply toward the shoulder
    • Speed up to keep the car balanced

    A blowout pulls the car to one side, and braking hard on top of that can spin you. Let the car slow itself while you steer straight, then leave the road once you are down to a safe speed.

  56. You are in a collision and someone has been hurt. What must you do?

    • Stay at the scene, call the police right away and report the collisionCorrect answer
    • Exchange phone numbers and drive away
    • Report it only if the crash was your fault
    • Wait to see whether the other driver complains

    Any collision with an injury must be reported to police, and leaving the scene is a serious offence. Exchange names, addresses, licence, plate and insurance details with the other driver, and take witnesses' contact information.

  57. You see flashing lights in your mirror and hear a siren. An emergency vehicle is catching up to you from behind. What does the law require you to do?

    • Slow down, signal, bring your car over to the right-hand edge of the road away from any intersection, and stop there until it has gone pastCorrect answer
    • Go faster so that it does not catch up to you
    • Stop in the middle of your lane and wait for it to go around you
    • Stay in your lane and carry on at the same speed until it turns off

    Moving to the right-hand edge opens the quickest path for the emergency vehicle, and keeping out of intersections leaves the crossing free. You also may not follow a fire truck or an ambulance on a call closer than 150 metres.

  58. Your car breaks down on a busy 400-series highway. What is the safest thing to do?

    • Pull onto the right shoulder as far over as you can, put on your hazard lights, and stay buckled in your locked car while you call for helpCorrect answer
    • Stop in the driving lane and turn on your hazard lights
    • Stand behind your car to wave traffic around
    • Walk along the highway to the next exit

    Standing on the shoulder of a fast highway is one of the most dangerous places you can be. Get the car off the driving lanes, then wait inside it with your seatbelt on until help comes.

  59. Your right wheels drop off the pavement onto a soft gravel shoulder. What should you do?

    • Hold the wheel firmly, ease off the gas, and once you have slowed down steer back onto the pavement gentlyCorrect answer
    • Brake hard and turn the wheel sharply back onto the road
    • Keep your speed up and swing back on quickly
    • Let go of the wheel and let the car straighten itself

    Yanking the wheel while a tire is caught on the pavement edge can throw the car across the road. Slow down first, then ease back on when the drop no longer grabs the tire.

  60. Your vehicle stalls on a railway crossing and you cannot restart it. What should you do?

    • Get everyone out and well away from the tracks, then call the railway's emergency number on the sign at the crossing, or 911Correct answer
    • Stay inside and keep trying the ignition
    • Get out and try to push the vehicle clear by yourself
    • Wait beside the car so you can warn the train driver

    A train cannot stop in time and a struck vehicle throws debris a long way, so move people far from the tracks first. The blue sign at the crossing lists the railway's emergency number and the crossing's location so they can stop trains.

  61. How long must you normally hold a G1 licence before you may try the G2 road test?

    • 12 months, or 8 months with an approved driver education courseCorrect answer
    • 3 months
    • 24 months
    • No waiting period

    The usual wait is 12 months. Finishing a ministry-approved driver education course shortens it to 8 months.

  62. Which of these roads may a G1 driver use, assuming the accompanying driver is not an instructor?

    • A city street with a 50 km/h limitCorrect answer
    • The Gardiner Expressway
    • The Queen Elizabeth Way
    • Highway 401

    G1 drivers are kept off 400-series highways with limits over 80 km/h, and off the QEW, Don Valley Parkway, Gardiner Expressway, E.C. Row Expressway and Conestoga Parkway. Ordinary streets are fine.

  63. When may a G1 driver use a 400-series highway?

    • When the accompanying driver is a licensed driving instructorCorrect answer
    • After 6 months
    • Never, under any circumstance
    • On weekends

    The highway restriction is lifted only when a licensed driving instructor is the accompanying driver. A parent or friend with a full G licence is not enough.

  64. How many people may ride in a G1 driver's vehicle?

    • As many as there are working seatbeltsCorrect answer
    • Two passengers maximum
    • No passengers at all
    • Only the accompanying driver

    There is no passenger count limit for G1, but every person in the vehicle must have a working seatbelt, and the accompanying driver must be in the front passenger seat.

  65. A G2 driver aged 18 drives at 1 a.m. in their first six months. How many passengers aged 19 or under may they carry?

    • OneCorrect answer
    • Three
    • Any number
    • None

    In the first six months a G2 driver aged 19 or under may carry only one passenger aged 19 or under between midnight and 5 a.m. Immediate family members are an exception.

  66. What blood alcohol level must every driver aged 21 and under have, no matter which licence they hold?

    • ZeroCorrect answer
    • There is no special rule
    • Under .05
    • Under .08

    Zero, for every driver 21 and under regardless of licence class, and zero for every G1 and G2 driver at any age.

  67. How long do demerit points stay on your record in Ontario?

    • Two years from the date of the offenceCorrect answer
    • Five years
    • Forever
    • Six months

    Points are recorded for two years from the offence date, then drop off. The conviction itself stays on the record longer.

  68. How many demerit points cause a novice driver's licence to be suspended for 60 days?

    • 9 or moreCorrect answer
    • 15 or more
    • 4 or more
    • 6 or more

    For a novice driver the ladder is short: 2 to 5 points brings a warning letter, 6 to 8 a second warning, and 9 or more a 60-day suspension.

  69. How many points does a fully licensed driver need before a 30-day suspension?

    • 15 or moreCorrect answer
    • 9 or more
    • 6 or more
    • 20 or more

    A fully licensed driver gets a warning letter at 6 to 8 points, a second at 9 to 14, and a 30-day suspension at 15.

  70. How many demerit points does careless driving carry?

    • SixCorrect answer
    • None
    • Two
    • Three

    Careless driving is a six-point offence, the same as racing or failing to stop for a school bus. It can also bring a fine, a suspension and even jail.

  71. You are caught driving 20 km/h over the limit. How many demerit points is that?

    • ThreeCorrect answer
    • Six
    • One
    • Four

    Speeding 16 to 29 km/h over is three points. From 30 to 49 km/h over it becomes four, and 50 km/h over is six plus far heavier penalties.

  72. What happens to a novice driver on a third conviction for a four-point offence?

    • The licence is cancelled and they must start the graduated licensing system againCorrect answer
    • A written warning
    • A 7-day suspension
    • Nothing changes

    The escalating sanctions run 30 days, then 90 days, then cancellation. After cancellation you begin the whole G1 process again from the start.

  73. A G1 driver is convicted of distracted driving for the first time. What is the penalty?

    • A 30-day suspension and a fine, but no demerit pointsCorrect answer
    • Three demerit points only
    • A warning letter
    • A 3-day suspension

    Novice drivers get no demerit points for distracted driving — they get suspensions instead: 30 days, then 90 days, then cancellation.

  74. Which use of a phone is still allowed while driving in Ontario?

    • Hands-free, using Bluetooth, an earpiece or a lapel buttonCorrect answer
    • Reading one short text at a red light
    • Holding the phone if you use speakerphone
    • Typing an address into a map app

    Only fully hands-free use is legal. Holding the device at all — even stopped at a light — is distracted driving. Calling 911 in an emergency is the exception.

  75. What is the penalty for a fully licensed driver's first distracted driving conviction?

    • A 3-day suspension, three demerit points and a fineCorrect answer
    • A 30-day suspension
    • Six demerit points
    • A warning only

    A full licence holder gets a 3-day suspension, three demerit points and a fine of $615 out of court, rising to a 7-day suspension on the second conviction.

  76. A driver aged 21 or under is stopped with any alcohol in their blood. What happens immediately?

    • A 24-hour roadside suspensionCorrect answer
    • A warning
    • Nothing until a court date
    • Their vehicle is sold

    The licence is suspended at the roadside for 24 hours. On conviction there is also a fine and a licence suspension of at least 30 days.

  77. Cannabis is legal in Canada. What does that mean for driving?

    • Driving while impaired by cannabis is still a criminal offenceCorrect answer
    • It only matters if you are under 25
    • It is fine as long as you feel alert
    • You may drive after using it

    Legal to buy does not mean legal to drive on. Cannabis slows reaction time and judgement, and impaired driving charges apply the same as with alcohol.

  78. Which of these actually helps a person who has been drinking become safe to drive?

    • Only timeCorrect answer
    • Fresh air and exercise
    • Strong coffee
    • A cold shower

    Nothing speeds up how fast your body clears alcohol. Coffee makes a person a wide-awake impaired driver, which is no safer.

  79. Why can prescription or over-the-counter medicine be a problem for driving?

    • Some medicines cause drowsiness or slow reactions, and driving on them can still be impaired drivingCorrect answer
    • It only matters for drivers over 65
    • It cannot, if a doctor prescribed it
    • Only illegal drugs count

    Read the label and ask the pharmacist. Allergy pills, cold medicine and some painkillers can make you drowsy enough to be charged with impaired driving.

  80. What is the standard following distance in good conditions?

    • Two seconds behind the vehicle aheadCorrect answer
    • One car length
    • Half a second
    • Ten metres at any speed

    Pick a fixed object, and if you reach it less than two seconds after the car ahead you are too close. Double it in rain, snow or fog.

  81. Before you change lanes, what must you check that your mirrors will not show you?

    • Your blind spot, by turning your head and looking over your shoulderCorrect answer
    • The radio
    • The odometer
    • The fuel gauge

    Mirrors leave a blind spot beside and behind you big enough to hide a car or a motorcycle. A quick shoulder check is the only way to see it.

  82. How far ahead of a turn should you normally signal in the city?

    • At least 30 metresCorrect answer
    • Signals are optional
    • Right as you begin the turn
    • After you start turning

    Signal at least 30 metres before the turn so drivers behind can react. Cancel the signal after the turn so you do not confuse anyone.

  83. You want to make a left turn at a green light with oncoming traffic. Where do you wait?

    • Inside the intersection with your wheels straightCorrect answer
    • Behind the stop line until the road is clear
    • On the shoulder
    • Inside the intersection with your wheels turned left

    Wheels straight. If you are hit from behind with the wheels turned, your car is pushed into oncoming traffic instead of straight ahead.

  84. Two vehicles reach an all-way stop at exactly the same moment, side by side. Who goes first?

    • The vehicle on the rightCorrect answer
    • The vehicle on the left
    • The larger vehicle
    • Whoever moves first

    When you arrive together, the driver on the right goes first. Make eye contact and be ready to yield even if the rule is on your side.

  85. What is the default speed limit on a city or town street with no posted sign in Ontario?

    • 50 km/hCorrect answer
    • 80 km/h
    • 40 km/h
    • 60 km/h

    Inside a city, town or village the default is 50 km/h. Outside those built-up areas the default rises to 80 km/h unless a sign says otherwise.

  86. What is the default speed limit outside cities and towns where no sign is posted?

    • 80 km/hCorrect answer
    • 50 km/h
    • 100 km/h
    • 60 km/h

    80 km/h applies on rural roads with no posted limit. A posted sign always overrules the default, in either direction.

  87. At what speed over the limit does a charge move from ordinary speeding into stunt driving territory in an 80 km/h or lower zone?

    • 40 km/h or more overCorrect answer
    • 20 km/h over
    • 60 km/h over
    • 10 km/h over

    On roads posted under 80 km/h it is 40 km/h or more over. On faster roads the threshold is 50 km/h over, and both bring six demerit points.

  88. Why is tailgating dangerous even at low city speeds?

    • You cannot see far enough past the vehicle ahead to spot a hazard in timeCorrect answer
    • It is only rude, not dangerous
    • It wears out your tires
    • It uses more fuel

    Sitting close removes both your stopping distance and your view. Dropping back lets you see the brake lights of cars two or three ahead.

  89. You are being tailgated by an impatient driver. What is the safest response?

    • Increase your own following distance and let them pass when you safely canCorrect answer
    • Speed up well over the limit
    • Turn on your hazard lights and stop
    • Brake sharply to teach them a lesson

    Give yourself more room in front so you never have to brake hard. Move right or pull over when it is safe and let them go.

  90. What should you do if you miss your exit on a highway?

    • Continue to the next exit and turn around thereCorrect answer
    • Reverse along the shoulder to the ramp
    • Cross the grass median
    • Stop and wait for a gap to back up

    Never reverse or cross a median on a highway. Carry on to the next exit — a few extra minutes is far cheaper than a collision or a charge.

  91. On a road divided by a raised median, a school bus stops with red lights flashing on the other side. What must you do?

    • Carry on, because only traffic behind the bus must stopCorrect answer
    • Sound your horn
    • Stop like everyone else
    • Stop only if children are visible

    A median means children will not cross to your side, so only traffic behind the bus stops. With no median, both directions must stop.

  92. Why should you never pass a large truck and then move back in front of it right away?

    • A loaded truck needs much more distance to stop, so cutting in removes its safety cushionCorrect answer
    • It is only impolite
    • Truck drivers cannot see ahead
    • Trucks are slower to accelerate

    A loaded transport can need twice the stopping distance of a car. Pull well ahead before you move back over, so the driver keeps the space they need.

  93. Where are a large truck's blind spots biggest?

    • Directly behind and along both sides, especially the rightCorrect answer
    • Only directly in front
    • Trucks have no blind spots
    • Only above the cab

    If you cannot see the driver's face in their mirror, they cannot see you. The right side is the worst, so never sit beside a truck's right rear wheels.

  94. An emergency vehicle approaches with lights and siren on. What should you do?

    • Pull as far to the right as you safely can and stop until it passesCorrect answer
    • Speed up to get out of its way
    • Stop where you are, in the middle of the lane
    • Follow closely behind it

    Signal, move right and stop clear of the intersection. Never follow an emergency vehicle closely — you may block a second one coming behind.

  95. You are on a highway and an emergency vehicle or tow truck is stopped ahead with its lights flashing. What does the law require?

    • Slow down, and move over a lane if the road has more than one lane your wayCorrect answer
    • Stop completely
    • Sound your horn as you pass
    • Nothing, it is only a courtesy

    Ontario's move over rule protects roadside workers. Slow down and change lanes to leave them a buffer whenever a second lane is available.

  96. Why is opening your door without looking dangerous on a city street?

    • A cyclist riding past can be knocked into trafficCorrect answer
    • It drains the battery
    • It is not dangerous
    • The door may be scratched

    Dooring is a common and serious cycling injury. Check your mirror and look over your shoulder before you open, every time.

  97. A motorcycle is ahead of you. Why should you leave more following distance than for a car?

    • A motorcycle can stop faster and is easily hidden in your blind spotCorrect answer
    • Motorcycles have no brakes
    • There is no reason
    • Motorcycles are slower

    A bike can stop in a shorter distance and is narrow enough to disappear behind your mirror. Give it a full lane and extra space.

  98. When is a wet road usually most slippery?

    • In the first minutes of rain, before the oil is washed awayCorrect answer
    • Only on gravel
    • After several hours of rain
    • Only in freezing weather

    Rain lifts oil and rubber off the pavement first, making a greasy film. Slow down as soon as the first drops fall.

  99. Your car starts to hydroplane on standing water. What is the correct response?

    • Ease off the gas, hold the wheel steady and do not brake suddenlyCorrect answer
    • Accelerate through it
    • Brake hard
    • Steer sharply to one side

    Hydroplaning means the tires are riding on water with no grip. Braking or steering hard does nothing until the tires touch pavement again.

  100. Where does black ice form first on a winter road?

    • On bridges, overpasses and in shaded spotsCorrect answer
    • In the middle of a sunny street
    • Only in parking lots
    • Only on gravel roads

    Bridges and overpasses lose heat from above and below, so they freeze before the road does. Shaded curves are the other classic trap.

  101. Which lights should you use in heavy fog?

    • Low beamsCorrect answer
    • No lights at all
    • Hazard lights while driving
    • High beams

    High beams bounce off the fog and blind you. Low beams sit under the fog layer and light the road ahead.

  102. An oncoming driver leaves their high beams on and dazzles you. What should you do?

    • Look toward the right edge of your lane and slow downCorrect answer
    • Turn on your own high beams
    • Close your eyes briefly
    • Flash your lights repeatedly and keep speed

    Use the white line on your right as a guide and ease off the gas until your eyes recover. Matching their high beams blinds you both.

  103. Your rear wheels begin to skid to the right. Where should you look and steer?

    • Where you want the car to goCorrect answer
    • At the vehicle you might hit
    • To the left, away from the skid
    • Straight down at the road

    Ease off the gas, keep your eyes on your escape path, and steer gently toward it. Your hands follow your eyes, so never stare at the hazard.

  104. Snow is falling and the road is covered. What is the safe speed?

    • Slow enough that you can stop within the distance you can clearly seeCorrect answer
    • Whatever the car in front is doing
    • 10 km/h in every case
    • The posted limit, since it is the law

    The posted limit is a maximum for perfect conditions. In snow you must drive to conditions, and driving too fast for them is a chargeable offence.

  105. Who is responsible for making sure everyone under 16 in the car is buckled up?

    • The driverCorrect answer
    • The vehicle owner
    • The parent, even if not in the car
    • Each passenger themselves

    The driver is responsible for every passenger under 16. Passengers 16 and over are responsible for their own seatbelt.

  106. Why do winter tires help even on a clear, cold winter day?

    • Their rubber stays soft below about 7 °C, so they keep grip when all-seasons hardenCorrect answer
    • They are wider
    • They are only useful in deep snow
    • They make the car quieter

    Grip comes from rubber that can flex. Below roughly 7 °C an all-season tire stiffens and its braking distance grows, even on dry pavement.

  107. What does the ABS light staying on after you start the engine tell you?

    • The anti-lock braking system has a fault and needs serviceCorrect answer
    • You are low on fuel
    • The headlights are on
    • The parking brake is on

    Your brakes still work, but the anti-lock function may not. Have it checked soon, and leave extra following distance until it is fixed.

  108. How often should you check your tire pressure?

    • About once a month and before a long trip, when the tires are coldCorrect answer
    • Only when a tire looks flat
    • Never, sensors do it
    • Once a year

    Underinflated tires wear fast, use more fuel and can overheat. Check monthly when cold, and remember cold weather lowers the pressure.

  109. What must you carry with you whenever you drive in Ontario?

    • Your licence, the vehicle permit and proof of insuranceCorrect answer
    • Only your licence
    • Only proof of insurance
    • Nothing, records are electronic

    All three: driver's licence, vehicle permit (ownership) and the insurance card. An officer may ask for each one at any stop.

  110. Why should you clear snow off the roof of your car, not just the windows?

    • A sheet of snow can slide onto your own windshield or fly into the car behindCorrect answer
    • It looks better
    • It saves fuel
    • It is not necessary

    A slab of roof snow can blind you the moment you brake, or hit the driver behind at highway speed. Clear the whole car, including the lights and plate.

  111. A collision causes damage that looks like more than $2,000 in total. What must you do?

    • Exchange information and report it to policeCorrect answer
    • Nothing, if nobody is hurt
    • Wait a week and call your insurer
    • Only exchange phone numbers

    Report it to police, either at the scene or at a collision reporting centre. Also exchange names, licence, plate and insurance details with everyone involved.

  112. What is the penalty for leaving the scene of a collision?

    • Seven demerit points, plus fines and possible jailCorrect answer
    • Two demerit points
    • A warning letter
    • A parking ticket

    Failing to remain is one of the heaviest offences in the system at seven demerit points, and it can also bring criminal charges.

  113. Your vehicle breaks down on a busy highway. Where should you try to stop?

    • As far onto the right shoulder as possible, well clear of trafficCorrect answer
    • In the left lane
    • On the median
    • In the right lane with hazards on

    Get right off the travelled lanes, put on your hazard lights, and if it is safe, leave the car on the passenger side and wait away from traffic.

  114. A tire blows out at highway speed. What should you do first?

    • Hold the wheel firmly, ease off the gas and let the car slow before steering to the shoulderCorrect answer
    • Brake hard immediately
    • Steer sharply to the shoulder
    • Turn off the engine

    A blowout pulls the car to one side. Grip the wheel, come off the gas gently and only brake once the car is slow and straight.

  115. Your accelerator sticks while you are driving. What is a safe first step?

    • Shift into neutral and brake to a stopCorrect answer
    • Open the door and jump
    • Pump the accelerator harder
    • Turn the key fully off right away

    Neutral cuts the engine's pull while you keep steering and braking. Turning the key fully off can lock the wheel and kill the power steering.

  116. You arrive first at a collision. What should you do before anything else?

    • Make the scene safe — hazard lights on, and call 911Correct answer
    • Take photos for insurance
    • Move the vehicles off the road
    • Move the injured people out of the cars

    Protect the scene and call for help. Do not move an injured person unless there is an immediate danger such as fire.

  117. Why should you turn off your engine and not smoke near a collision?

    • Spilled fuel can catch fire from a spark or flameCorrect answer
    • It is quieter
    • It helps the police
    • It saves fuel

    Leaking fuel and hot parts make fire a real risk. Switch off, keep flames away and keep people back from the vehicles.

  118. Your headlights fail suddenly at night. What is the safest immediate action?

    • Slow down, turn on hazard lights and pull off the road as soon as it is safeCorrect answer
    • Keep driving at the same speed
    • Speed up to reach the next town
    • Use your phone flashlight out the window

    Try the high beams and the hazard lights, then get off the road. Driving on with no lights leaves you invisible to everyone else.

  119. How should you use anti-lock brakes in an emergency stop?

    • Press the pedal down hard and hold it, steering around the hazardCorrect answer
    • Use the parking brake instead
    • Pump the pedal rapidly
    • Press lightly

    ABS pumps for you. The vibration under your foot is normal — keep firm pressure and steer, because ABS lets you brake and steer together.

  120. Why is fatigue treated as seriously as alcohol behind the wheel?

    • Being very tired slows reaction time and judgement much like being impaired, and can cause microsleepsCorrect answer
    • It only matters on long trips
    • Coffee removes the risk
    • It is not, fatigue is harmless

    A driver who has been awake too long makes the same kind of mistakes as an impaired one. The only real fix is to stop and rest.

  121. How long must an Ontario G1 driver wait before the first road test if they take an approved course?

    • 8 monthsCorrect answer
    • 4 months
    • 12 months
    • 24 months

    The G1 stage normally lasts 12 months, but an approved Beginner Driver Education course cuts it to 8.

  122. How much driving experience must an Ontario G1's accompanying driver have?

    • Four yearsCorrect answer
    • One year
    • Two years
    • Ten years

    The accompanying driver needs a full Class G or higher, at least four years of driving experience, a blood alcohol level under .05, and must be the only other person in the front seat.

  123. Which roads may an Ontario G1 driver not use?

    • 400-series highways posted over 80 km/h, and certain named expresswaysCorrect answer
    • All highways outside a city
    • Any road with a centre line
    • Gravel roads

    A G1 may not drive on 400-series highways posted above 80 km/h, nor on the Queen Elizabeth Way, Don Valley Parkway, Gardiner Expressway, E.C. Row Expressway or Conestoga Parkway — unless a certified driving instructor is with them.

  124. Which Ontario licence stage has a midnight to 5 a.m. driving ban?

    • G1Correct answer
    • G2
    • Both G1 and G2
    • Neither

    Only G1 has a driving curfew. A G2 driver may drive at any hour — the midnight to 5 a.m. rule at the G2 stage is about how many young passengers may be in the car, not about driving.

  125. Which drivers in Ontario must have a blood alcohol level of zero?

    • All G1 and G2 drivers, and every driver aged 21 and underCorrect answer
    • Only G1 drivers
    • Only drivers under 18
    • Nobody, the limit is always .08

    Zero applies to every novice driver and to every driver aged 21 and under, whatever licence they hold. So a 20-year-old with a full G is still at zero.

  126. How many demerit points suspend an Ontario novice driver?

    • 9Correct answer
    • 6
    • 12
    • 15

    A novice driver is suspended for 60 days at 9 points. A fully licensed driver is suspended for 30 days at 15.

  127. What does a first distracted driving conviction cost an Ontario NOVICE driver?

    • A fine and a 30-day suspension, with no demerit pointsCorrect answer
    • 3 demerit points and a 3-day suspension
    • A warning
    • Demerit points but no suspension

    A novice gets no demerit points but a 30-day suspension — ten times longer than the three days a fully licensed driver receives. A second offence brings 90 days, a third loses the licence entirely.

  128. What is the speed limit on an Ontario road inside a city, town or village with no sign posted?

    • 50 km/hCorrect answer
    • 40 km/h
    • 60 km/h
    • 80 km/h

    Inside a city, town or village the default is 50 km/h. Everywhere else in Ontario the default is 80 km/h.

  129. Where in Ontario are studded tires allowed?

    • On vehicles registered in Northern OntarioCorrect answer
    • Everywhere in the province
    • Nowhere at all
    • Only on commercial trucks

    Studded tires are for vehicles registered in Northern Ontario. Drivers from other provinces passing through may also use them. Winter tires themselves are not required in Ontario.

  130. When may an Ontario child stop using a booster seat?

    • At age 8, 36 kg or 145 cm — whichever comes firstCorrect answer
    • At age 6
    • At 18 kg
    • At age 12

    The child may move to the seat belt alone once they turn 8, reach 36 kg, or reach 145 cm — whichever happens first. Getting it wrong costs $240 and 2 demerit points.

About this quiz

The rules half of the G1 test asks what you must do, not what a sign looks like. This page splits those rules into eight chapters so you can work through one subject at a time instead of jumping about at random.

Inside a chapter, every question sits behind a tap. Open it to read the correct answer and a short explanation, and once you have been through the chapter, a practice run over the same questions waits underneath.

Nothing is timed and nothing is scored against you here. This is the reading and understanding stage, which is why the questions and answers sit side by side instead of being hidden until the end.

What's inside

  • Graduated Licensing explains the G1, G2 and full G steps and the limits that come with each one.
  • Safe and Responsible Driving covers following distance, turning, passing, parking and how to handle an intersection.
  • Sharing the Road deals with cyclists, pedestrians, school buses, large trucks and emergency vehicles.
  • Driving Conditions looks at rain, snow, ice, fog, night driving and what to do in a skid.
  • Keeping Your Licence covers demerit points, suspensions and what follows certain driving offences.
  • Impaired Driving, Your Vehicle, and Emergencies and Collisions close the set with alcohol rules, upkeep and crash steps.

Good to know

  • Keep two seconds of space behind the vehicle in front on a dry road, and stretch that gap out further when the road is wet.
  • Leave one metre of room when you pass a cyclist, and stay at least 150 metres back from a fire truck answering a call.
  • Parking within three metres of a fire hydrant is not allowed, which is why kerbside spaces beside hydrants stay empty.
  • Drivers holding a G1 or G2 licence, and every driver aged twenty-one and under, must have zero alcohol in their blood.
  • Demerit points stay on your record for two years, counted from the date of the offence rather than the date you paid the fine.

Common questions

How is this study page different from the mock test?

The mock test hides the answers, runs a clock and grades you at the end. Here the answers are visible as you read, there is no timer, and you can stay inside one chapter as long as you wish.

Which chapter is the best place to begin?

Graduated Licensing opens first and makes a sensible start, since it explains what a G1 driver may and may not do. After that, choose whichever chapter felt weakest in your last mock test.

Do I have to finish a chapter in one sitting?

No. Nothing here is graded and nothing counts against a score, so you can read four questions today and come back for the rest tomorrow without losing anything.

Is this an official government study guide?

No. It is unofficial practice with no connection to the Ministry of Transportation, ServiceOntario or DriveTest. The free MTO Driver's Handbook is the official book, and it is worth reading in full.

Can I test myself after reading a chapter?

Yes. A practice run sits under each chapter and shuffles the same questions, so you can find out whether the explanations you just read have genuinely stayed with you.
