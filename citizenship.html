  15. What is a riding, also called an electoral district?

    • A Senate region
    • A geographic area represented by one Member of ParliamentCorrect answer
    • A political party
    • A polling station

    Canada is divided into ridings, and voters in each one elect a single Member of Parliament to the House of Commons.

  16. What is the secret ballot?

    • Only the government sees the results
    • Ballots are counted in secret
    • No one may watch you vote or ask how you votedCorrect answer
    • You may vote more than once

    Your vote is private. It is against the law for anyone to watch you mark your ballot or to demand that you say how you voted.

  17. What normally happens when no party wins a majority of seats in the House of Commons?

    • A new election is held immediately
    • The Senate chooses the government
    • The Governor General governs alone
    • A minority government is formed and needs support from other partiesCorrect answer

    The party with the most seats usually forms a minority government. It must win the support of members from other parties to pass legislation.

  18. What is the voter information card you receive in the mail?

    • A notice telling you where and when to voteCorrect answer
    • Your ballot
    • Proof of citizenship
    • A tax document

    It confirms you are on the voters list and tells you the place and hours for voting. You still need identification at the polling station.

  19. How often must a federal election normally be held in Canada?

    • At least every four yearsCorrect answer
    • Every two years
    • Every six years
    • Only when the Prime Minister resigns

    Fixed-date legislation sets elections about every four years, though one can be called earlier if the government loses the confidence of the House.

  20. Who becomes Prime Minister after a federal election?

    • The leader of the Senate
    • The leader of the party with the most seats in the House of CommonsCorrect answer
    • The candidate with the most personal votes
    • The Governor General's choice from any party

    You do not vote for the Prime Minister directly. You elect a Member of Parliament for your riding, and the party with the most seats normally forms government.

  21. What should you do if you are not on the voters list on election day?

    • You must wait for the next election
    • You must vote by mail only
    • You can still register at the polling station with the right identificationCorrect answer
    • You cannot vote at all

    You may register in person on election day. Bring identification showing your name and address.

  1. What does the 'rule of law' mean?

    • Everyone, including governments, must obey the lawCorrect answer
    • Only citizens obey the law

  2. What is the highest court in Canada?

    • The Provincial Court
    • The Supreme Court of CanadaCorrect answer
    • The House of Commons
    • The Senate

    The Supreme Court of Canada is the highest court in the country.

    • Presumed innocent until proven guiltyCorrect answer

    • The National Police Service
    • The FBI
    • The Royal Canadian Mounted Police (RCMP)Correct answer

    The Royal Canadian Mounted Police (RCMP), the 'Mounties', is the national police force.

  5. What is the main job of a judge in a Canadian court?

    • To apply the law and keep the trial fairCorrect answer
    • To choose the police officers
    • To arrest people who break the law
    • To write the country's laws

    Judges are independent, which means no one can tell them how to decide a case.

  6. How many judges sit on the Supreme Court of Canada?

    • Five
    • NineCorrect answer
    • Twelve
    • Fifteen

    Nine judges hear the country's most important cases, and three of them must come from Quebec.

  7. Most provinces use common law, but Quebec uses a different system called the...

    • Royal Code
    • Common Charter
    • Civil CodeCorrect answer
    • Charter Code

    Quebec's Civil Code comes from French legal tradition and covers matters like contracts and property.

  8. Which court would you use for a small money disagreement, such as unpaid work?

    • A provincial legislature
    • The Supreme Court of Canada
    • A federal appeal court
    • Small claims courtCorrect answer

    Small claims courts are simple and low-cost, and people often go without a lawyer.

  9. What can a person do if they need a lawyer but cannot pay for one?

    • Apply for legal aidCorrect answer
    • Ask the judge to be their lawyer
    • Ask the police for legal advice
    • Wait until the case is closed

    Legal aid offices help people with low incomes get legal advice and a lawyer. ⚖️

  10. What does presumption of innocence mean?

    • A trial must happen within a week
    • A person charged with a crime is considered innocent until proven guiltyCorrect answer
    • Police may not arrest anyone
    • Only judges may accuse someone

    The burden of proof is on the prosecution. This principle protects everyone from being punished without evidence.

  11. Which court is the highest in Canada?

    • The Court of Appeal
    • The Provincial Court
    • The Supreme Court of CanadaCorrect answer
    • The Federal Court

    The Supreme Court of Canada has nine judges and hears final appeals. Its decisions bind every other court in the country.

  12. Which police force serves as the national police service of Canada?

    • The Ontario Provincial Police
    • The Canadian Forces Military Police
    • The Canada Border Services Agency
    • The Royal Canadian Mounted PoliceCorrect answer

    The RCMP enforces federal law across the country and also serves as the provincial or municipal police in many places by contract.

  13. What is the purpose of the court system in Canada?

    • To settle disputes and make sure the law is applied fairly to everyoneCorrect answer
    • To write new laws
    • To collect taxes
    • To elect the government

    Courts resolve disputes and apply the law. A key idea is the rule of law: no person or government is above the law.

  14. Who appoints judges to the Supreme Court of Canada?

    • The Governor General on the advice of the Prime MinisterCorrect answer
    • Voters in a national election
    • The provinces together
    • The Chief Justice alone

    Judges are appointed, not elected. Once appointed they are independent of government, which is what makes the courts impartial.

  15. What does the rule of law mean in Canada?

    • The Prime Minister is above the law
    • Everyone, including governments, must obey the lawCorrect answer
    • Police may make new laws
    • Only citizens must obey the law

    No person and no government is above the law. That principle protects ordinary people from arbitrary power.

  16. What is a jury in a Canadian court?

    • A team of lawyers
    • A group of police officers
    • A group of ordinary citizens who decide the facts of a caseCorrect answer
    • A panel of judges

    Serving on a jury when called is one of the responsibilities of citizenship. Jurors decide what happened; the judge decides the law.

  17. What kind of law does the federal government make?

    • Traffic law in each province
    • School rules
    • Municipal by-laws
    • Criminal law, which is the same across the whole countryCorrect answer

    Criminal law is federal, so it is identical in every province and territory. Provinces handle things like highway traffic acts.

    • Aboriginal, French and Spanish

About this quiz

This page is a full practice run of the Canadian citizenship test: twenty multiple-choice questions, a thirty-minute clock, and a pass mark of fifteen correct. It is made for newcomers who want an honest picture of how ready they really are.

Each answer is marked the moment you tap it, and a short explanation appears underneath so you learn the reason instead of memorising a letter. At the end you see PASS or TRY AGAIN, your score, and every question you missed.

The questions follow the subjects covered in the official Discover Canada study guide. This is unofficial practice built by a small team, not by the Government of Canada, so keep reading the real guide beside it.

What's inside

  • Every attempt draws twenty questions at random from a bank of well over one hundred.
  • A thirty-minute countdown runs beside the question counter and closes the exam when it reaches zero.
  • Topics include Canadian history, government, rights and responsibilities, national symbols, federal elections and the regions.
  • Your browser remembers which questions you have seen, so a repeat attempt brings mostly new ones.
  • The mistake review shows your answer, the correct answer and the explanation together for each miss.

Good to know

  • The real test asks twenty questions and requires at least fifteen correct, a score of seventy-five percent — the same bar used here.
  • Discover Canada, the guide behind these questions, is published free by the Government of Canada and covers history, government and symbols.
  • Canada has ten provinces and three territories, and the guide devotes a chapter to regions far from where most applicants settle.
  • After the test comes the citizenship ceremony, where new citizens take the oath of citizenship together before a presiding official.
