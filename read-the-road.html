🚩 Read the Road

Game

Eighty Canadian road signs, and four ways to learn them. You do not need a licence and you do not need to be taking a test — you need to be someone who crosses a road.

About this quiz

Read the Road takes the eighty Canadian road signs this site already draws and turns them into something you play rather than something you revise. There are four ways in, and you pick one on the page: name the sign, say what you would do, a walking level written for children, or a two-player round where you pass the phone back and forth.

Naming a sign and knowing what to do about it are not the same skill, so they are separate games. The what-would-you-do questions come from the same bank as the licence practice on this site, written in our own words from the free official handbook, and each answer explains itself afterwards. Getting one wrong is the moment the explanation is worth reading, so it appears either way.

The walking level uses twenty-eight signs a child actually meets on the way to school — the stop sign, the school zone, the crossing stripes, the railway crossbuck, the playground, the deer. Each one carries a single plain sentence about what it means, and the game asks the child to say that sentence out loud to whoever is with them. A child who can explain a sign to an adult has learned it properly.

Nothing is saved and there is no account. Wrong answers are drawn from the same family as the right one, so a yellow diamond is never the only warning sign among three blue ones — that would teach colour matching instead of signs. This is free practice and is not affiliated with any government; the rules that count are in your own province's official handbook.

Good to know

  • All eighty signs are drawn in code rather than photographed, so they stay sharp on a phone and on a classroom projector alike.
  • Shape and colour carry meaning before the words do. A red circle with a slash forbids, a green circle permits, a yellow diamond warns and an orange diamond means road work.
  • The railway crossbuck — the big white X — is not a warning that a track is coming. It means the track is here.
