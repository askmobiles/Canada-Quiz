Read the shape and the colour first

Canadian road signs tell you a lot before you even read the words. A red circle with a slash means you must not do the thing shown, while a green circle means you may. A yellow diamond warns you about something ahead, and an orange diamond means road work. Green and blue signs give you directions and services. Signs are the same right across Canada, so what you learn here also travels with you to another province. Once the shapes and colours are second nature, the sign questions become much easier.

    • Oncoming traffic in the lane beside youCorrect answer
    • A toll booth
    • A school zone
    • A gravel surface

    The median is ending. Keep well right and do not begin a pass unless you can see the whole road ahead is clear.

About this quiz

The sign questions on the Alberta Class 7 test are the ones you can study with your eyes. This page shows the signs as clean drawings, sorted into groups, with the name of each one written underneath so the picture and the meaning settle in together.

Nothing here is timed and nothing is scored. Open a group, look through the pictures until they feel familiar, then press the practice button under that group to answer only the questions that use those particular signs.

This is unofficial practice with no link to the Government of Alberta or any registry agent. The free Alberta Basic Licence Driver's Guide is still the book to read, and this page is simply where you drill the pictures until they stay put in your memory.

What's inside

  • Regulatory signs come first, covering stop, yield, speed limits, turn bans, parking rules and lane restrictions.
  • Warning signs form the yellow diamond group for curves, hills, railway crossings, school zones, wildlife and merging traffic.
  • Construction signs make up the orange group, with work zones, flag persons, detours and closed lanes.
  • Information signs are the green and blue ones that point you toward hospitals, fuel, food, exits and highway routes.
  • Traffic lights have their own group, covering red, amber, green, advance arrows and flashing signals.
  • Road markings close the set with solid and broken lines, lane lines, stop lines and crosswalks.

Good to know

  • A red circle with a slash through it bans whatever the picture shows, and a green circle gives you permission for it.
  • Yellow diamonds warn you about what lies ahead, and orange ones carry the same kind of warning but only while road work lasts.
  • Road signs use the same shapes and colours right across Canada, so what you learn here travels with you to another province.
  • The pictures on this page are our own drawings of the standard shapes and colours, made so the page loads quickly on any phone.
  • Learn one group at a time rather than the whole gallery at once, because signs that look alike are much easier to mix up when you rush.
