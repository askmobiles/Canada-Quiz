What Canada Day Is Really About

Canada did not begin with a battle. It began with an agreement, and a lot of meetings.

Before 1867 there was no country called Canada. There were separate colonies, each run from Britain, each with its own government.

Their leaders met and argued for years about joining together. Think of four families deciding to share one house: who pays for what, who decides the rules, and what stays private.

On the 1st of July 1867 they agreed. Ontario, Quebec, Nova Scotia and New Brunswick became one country, called the Dominion of Canada.

The rest joined over the next 132 years. Manitoba, British Columbia, Prince Edward Island, Saskatchewan, Alberta and finally Newfoundland and Labrador in 1949.

Today there are ten provinces and three territories. The newest is Nunavut, in the far north, which was created on the 1st of April 1999.

The holiday was called Dominion Day for over a hundred years. It only became Canada Day in 1982, which means your grandparents remember when it had a different name.

Did you know? Canada is younger than the piano, the postage stamp and the bicycle.
