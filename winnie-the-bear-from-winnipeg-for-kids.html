The Bear from Winnipeg Who Became Winnie-the-Pooh

The most famous bear in the world was real, and she came from Canada.

In August 1914, a young man named Harry Colebourn was on a train. He was an animal doctor from Winnipeg, and he was going east with his regiment, the Fort Garry Horse.

The train stopped at a small town called White River, in Ontario. On the platform, Harry saw a man with a black bear cub. He bought the cub for twenty dollars. It was the 24th of August, 1914.

He named her Winnie, short for Winnipeg, his home city. Winnie went with the soldiers all the way across the ocean to England. She followed Harry around like a puppy.

When Harry had to leave for France, he took Winnie to the London Zoo. That was in December 1914. He meant to come back for her, but Winnie was so gentle and so loved that he left her there for good.

Ten years later, a little boy named Christopher Robin visited the zoo. He was allowed to go right inside and feed her. He loved her so much that he gave his own teddy bear a new name: Winnie.

His father was a writer. In 1926 he published a book about that teddy bear. The book was called Winnie-the-Pooh, and children have been reading it ever since.

Did you know? A bear from a train platform in northern Ontario is the reason a teddy bear in England got the name Winnie.
