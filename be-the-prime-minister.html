🍁 Be the Prime Minister

You lead the Government of Canada for one term. Twelve decisions. The budget, the provinces, the House of Commons and the trust of Canadians all pull against each other.

The people and places in this game are made up. The rules, laws and numbers are real and checked against official Canadian sources.

📖 How to play

Each decision comes from someone who works with a prime minister. Pick one of three answers. Every answer moves four things: the budget, the trust of Canadians, your relations with the provinces, and your support in the House of Commons. If any one reaches zero, your government falls. At the end you will see which parts of the job you leaned on.

🍁

    📚 Study it first: Grade 12 · Canadian and World Politics · Classroom quiz and printable worksheets

    How a prime minister is expected to act

    • Answer to Parliament. A prime minister governs only while they keep the confidence of the House of Commons, and faces questions there in Question Period.
    • Never use the job for personal gain. The Conflict of Interest Act applies to the prime minister, and an independent Commissioner can investigate.
    • Respect independent institutions. Courts, prosecutors and the public service are not there to take political orders.
    • Serve every Canadian, including the people who voted for someone else.

    How to treat a prime minister

    • Disagree respectfully. Criticizing the government and protesting peacefully are protected by the Charter of Rights and Freedoms.
    • Use your voice the proper way: write to your MP, sign a petition to the House of Commons, and vote.
    • Never threaten. Threatening anyone, a prime minister included, is a crime under the Criminal Code.
    • Respect the office, even when you did not vote for the person in it.
    About this game

    Be the Prime Minister puts you at the head of the Government of Canada for one term. Twelve people bring you a decision, you pick one of three answers, and four things move: the budget, the trust of Canadians, your relations with the provinces and your support in the House of Commons.

    The game names no party and no real politician. Every decision is about how the job works: choosing a cabinet, passing a budget, keeping the confidence of the House, working with premiers, responding to emergencies and recommending judges.

    At the end the game shows which part of the work you leaned on and names jobs in Canada that use it. It is a game, not a career test.

    Tips for playing

    • In Canada the Prime Minister is not elected directly. Canadians elect MPs, and the leader who can keep the confidence of the House of Commons becomes Prime Minister.
    • A vote on the budget is a confidence vote. If the government loses one, it usually resigns or the Prime Minister asks the Governor General to dissolve Parliament.
    • Health care is run by the provinces and territories, but Ottawa pays a large share through the Canada Health Transfer.

    Common questions

    Is this game about a real prime minister or party?

    No. It names no party and no politician. It is about how the job works, so it can be played the same way in any classroom.

    Can my government fall?

    Yes. If the budget, trust, the provinces or your support in the House reaches zero, your term ends early, just as a real government can lose a confidence vote.

    Does this tell me what job I should do?

    No, and it says so on the result screen. It shows which kind of decision you enjoyed making today, and which jobs involve a lot of that.

    Is it good for a classroom?

    Yes. There is no signup and nothing is collected, it works on a phone or a projector, and every decision teaches something real about Canadian government that is also on the citizenship test.
