🗺️ Canada's Provinces and Territories: A Simple Guide

An easy guide to Canada's 10 provinces and 3 territories — their names, capitals, and fun facts. Great for kids, students, and newcomers.

Canada is a very big country, and it is divided into 10 provinces and 3 territories. Knowing them is helpful for school, for the citizenship test, and just for understanding the country. Here is a simple guide.

What is the difference between a province and a territory?

A province gets its powers from the Constitution and has more control over its own affairs, like schools and hospitals. A territory gets its powers from the federal (national) government. The three territories are in the north, where fewer people live.

The 10 provinces and their capitals

  • British Columbia — Victoria
  • Alberta — Edmonton
  • Saskatchewan — Regina
  • Manitoba — Winnipeg
  • Ontario — Toronto
  • Quebec — Quebec City
  • New Brunswick — Fredericton
  • Nova Scotia — Halifax
  • Prince Edward Island — Charlottetown
  • Newfoundland and Labrador — St. John's

The 3 territories and their capitals

  • Yukon — Whitehorse
  • Northwest Territories — Yellowknife
  • Nunavut — Iqaluit

A few fun facts

  • Ottawa, in Ontario, is the capital city of all of Canada.
  • Quebec is the largest province by land area, and its main language is French.
  • Nunavut is the newest territory — it was created in 1999.
  • Prince Edward Island is the smallest province.
  • Canada has two official languages: English and French.

Worth remembering too

  • Ottawa sits in Ontario and serves as the capital of the whole country, which is separate from Toronto, the capital of Ontario itself.
  • Quebec is the largest province by land area, and French is the main language spoken across it.
  • Nunavut is the newest territory in Canada. It was created in 1999, long after the rest of the map had settled.
  • Prince Edward Island is the smallest province in Canada, which is why it can be hard to spot on a printed map.
  • Canada has two official languages, English and French, and both appear on federal signs and documents across the country.
  • Four capitals are easy to mix up, namely Regina, Winnipeg, Edmonton and Victoria, so learning them west to east helps.

Common questions

Why does Canada have territories as well as provinces?

The three territories cover the northern part of the country, where far fewer people live. They receive their powers from the federal government rather than from the Constitution, and that is the main legal difference.

Do I really need to memorise all thirteen capitals?

For the citizenship test it helps a great deal, and at school it is often required outright. Learning them in groups of three or four instead of all at once is usually far less painful.
