The Canadian Citizenship Test, As It Actually Is

Twenty questions, forty-five minutes, fifteen right to pass. Everything on this page comes from what Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada publishes itself.

Start with the number almost every practice site gets wrong

You will read on a great many websites that the citizenship test lasts thirty minutes. That was true once. The Government of Canada's current test page says forty-five minutes, and its online test page describes a forty-five minute countdown that cannot be paused, with a warning at five minutes and the test submitted automatically when the time runs out.

The confusion is understandable, because one of the government's own older video pages still says thirty. When two official pages disagree, the live instruction page for the test itself is the one to trust. Practise against forty-five minutes.

Everything on it comes from one free book

Every question is drawn from Discover Canada: The Rights and Responsibilities of Citizenship. It is free from the Government of Canada as a web page, a PDF, a large-print PDF, an eBook and a full audio recording — which matters if reading long English is hard work, or if your commute is the only study time you get.

The government describes the subjects as the rights and responsibilities of being a Canadian citizen, together with Canada's history, geography, economy, government, laws and symbols. In the book itself, geography is the chapter called Canada's Regions — there is no chapter with the word geography in its title, which trips people up when they go looking for it.

Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada puts a warning in the front of that book, and it applies to this website as much as to any other. In its own words: the only official study guide for the citizenship test is Discover Canada, available at no cost, and if you use any other material to prepare, you do so at your own risk.

Taking it at home, and what can go wrong

The test is now normally done online, at home, and the requirements are more particular than people expect:

  • A desktop, laptop or tablet with a webcam. Chrome or Safari only — and specifically not Chrome on an iPad.
  • Any VPN must be turned off.
  • Your permanent resident card, even an expired one, or another valid photo and signature identity document.
  • You must be alone, in good light, with no other devices and no study material. Photographs are taken during the test and compared with the photographs on your application.
  • Do not use the browser back button.

The invitation arrives by email as a PDF, usually one to three months after your acknowledgement of receipt, and at least two to three business days before the test period opens. In-person and video versions still exist, arranged individually as accommodations.

What happens if you fail

You get three chances inside the same thirty-day window. That is worth planning around: it is not three chances spread over months, it is three inside one month.

If all three go badly you are invited to a hearing with a citizenship official, lasting thirty to ninety minutes. At the hearing the knowledge test is the same shape — twenty questions, fifteen to pass — and there is a language assessment of up to nine questions, of which you must pass six. Pass, and you go on to the ceremony. Fail, and the application is refused and you would have to apply and pay again.

Online results are not instant in any meaningful sense. An officer reviews them, which the government says takes a few days or a few weeks.

Language is assessed separately

A point that causes real worry needlessly: the citizenship test itself does not assess your English or French. The language requirement is a separate thing, proven with documents before you get there.

Applicants aged 18 to 54 must show they can speak and listen at Canadian Language Benchmarks level 4 or higher, in English or French. Speaking and listening only — not reading, not writing. Proof can be schooling done in English or French, an approved language test result, or a certificate from certain government-funded language programmes.

The last step

After the test comes the ceremony, where you swear or affirm the Oath of Citizenship, sing O Canada, sign the oath form and receive your citizenship certificate. At a virtual ceremony the form has to be signed the same day you take the oath.

For reference while you plan: an adult grant of citizenship costs 653 dollars in total, which includes the processing fee and the right of citizenship fee, and the physical presence requirement is 1,095 days — three years — inside the five-year eligibility period.

Where these facts come from

Every figure on this page comes from Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada's own pages: the study page, the how-it-works page, the online test page, the invitation page, the results page, the language proof pages, the ceremony page and the fee list.

Some things you will see elsewhere are not on this page because no government page supports them: how many questions exist in total, what share of people pass, and the claim that any practice site has the real questions. Nobody outside the government knows those, and anybody who tells you otherwise is guessing.

About this page

The Canadian citizenship test is 20 questions in 45 minutes, and you need at least 15 right. It is taken by applicants aged 18 to 54, in English or French, and every question comes from the free government study guide Discover Canada.

This page states the 45-minute limit from the government's current test page, notes that an older official video page still says 30, and publishes no question-bank size or pass rate because no official source gives either.

Common questions

How long is the Canadian citizenship test?

45 minutes for 20 questions, counting down without a pause, with a warning at five minutes. Older pages saying 30 minutes are out of date.

How many questions do I need right to pass?

At least 15 of the 20.

What happens if I fail the citizenship test?

You get three chances inside one 30-day window. If all three go badly you are invited to a hearing with a citizenship official lasting 30 to 90 minutes.

Who has to take the test?

Applicants who are between 18 and 54 years old on the day they sign the application.

Does the citizenship test check my English or French?

No. Language is a separate requirement proven with documents beforehand, at Canadian Language Benchmarks level 4 in speaking and listening.
