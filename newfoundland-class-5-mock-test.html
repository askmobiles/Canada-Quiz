Newfoundland and Labrador Class 5 Mock Test

Two halves, signs and rules, with seventeen right needed out of twenty in each. The 85 percent is the province's own published pass mark. The question count and the timer are our practice standard, because Newfoundland and Labrador publishes neither. Questions are picked at random, so every run is a new test.

📖 How to play

Tap an answer to lock it in. You will see straight away whether it was right, with a short explanation, then move on. At the end you get your score for each half, a PASS or TRY AGAIN, and a list of every question you missed.

When you are ready to book the real one

Take this three times on different days and look at where the misses land rather than at the total. If they cluster in the signs, go back to the sign page; if they cluster in the rules, go back to the rules page. Eighty-five percent leaves very little room, so a weak area shows up quickly.

On the day, you can write the real test online through the province's service account, or in person at a Motor Registration Division office. If you write it online, remember that you may not drive until you have attended the appointment for your vision test and your documents.

Where to get the official information

Everything on this page comes from what Newfoundland and Labrador publishes for free. The Driver's Handbook is the book the real test is written from, it is free to download from the province's own site, and there is an official French edition as well. Read it alongside this practice, not instead of it.

This is an unofficial free practice site. It is not affiliated with the Motor Registration Division or the Government of Newfoundland and Labrador, and it cannot issue a licence. Always check the official pages for the current rules before you book.

About this quiz

This is the timed version. Two halves are drawn at random from the Newfoundland and Labrador bank, signs and rules, and each half is scored on its own at the province's published 85 percent.

The question count and the timer are ours, not the province's. Newfoundland and Labrador publishes neither, and inventing one would be the kind of confident wrong number this site exists to avoid.

Because the questions are picked at random each time, every run is a different test. Take it on three separate days before you decide you are ready.

What's inside

  • Twenty road sign questions and twenty rules questions, drawn at random from the sixty-question bank.
  • Seventeen correct answers needed in each half, which is the province's published 85 percent.
  • A countdown timer, clearly labelled as our practice standard rather than the province's.
  • A short explanation after every answer, right or wrong.
  • A pass or try again result for each half, scored separately.
  • A list of every question you missed, so you know which page to go back to.

Good to know

  • Eighty-five percent leaves room for three wrong answers in twenty, which is tighter than most provinces allow.
  • The province does not publish a time limit for the real test, so treat the timer here as practice pressure rather than a rule.
  • The Class 5 Level I licence itself is valid for two years from the day it is issued.
  • Passing the written test online does not let you drive until you have attended the vision test appointment.
  • The province does not publish how long a passed written test stays valid.

Common questions

Is this the real Newfoundland and Labrador test?

No. It is free practice written for this site. The 85 percent pass mark is the province's; the number of questions and the timer are ours.

Why are the two halves scored separately?

Because it shows you where the weakness is. A total can hide a bad half, and going back to the right study page is the whole point.

Can I take it more than once?

As often as you like. The questions are drawn at random each time, so the test is different on every run.

Does it work on a phone?

Yes. There is nothing to install and nothing to sign up for, and there is a full-screen mode for a phone or a tablet.

Where do I go if I fail a half?

Back to the study page for that half — the road signs page or the rules of the road page — and then take this again.
