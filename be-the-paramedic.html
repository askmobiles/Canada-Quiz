🚑 Be the Paramedic

You lead a paramedic crew in a Canadian region for one season. Twelve calls and decisions. Getting there in time, caring for patients, looking after your crew and keeping ambulances free all pull against each other.

📖 How to play

Each decision comes from someone a paramedic works with. Pick one of three answers. Every answer moves four things: time, patient care, your crew's wellbeing, and the number of ambulances available for the next call. If any one reaches zero, your season ends early. At the end you will see which parts of the job you leaned on.

🚑

    How a paramedic is expected to act

    • Ask for consent. A capable adult decides about their own care, including saying no. Paramedics explain the choices and respect the decision.
    • Keep health information private. Patient records are confidential under provincial health privacy laws.
    • Work within your level. Paramedics practise under medical direction from physicians, and in provinces such as Alberta, Saskatchewan and Nova Scotia they are registered with a College of Paramedics.
    • Treat everyone the same. Provincial human rights laws forbid discrimination in services, and every patient gets the same care.

    How to treat paramedics

    • Pull over for an ambulance. In Ontario, move to the right and stop. Failing to slow down or move over for a stopped emergency vehicle costs $400 to $2,000 and 3 demerit points for a first offence.
    • Never follow an ambulance. In Ontario it is illegal to follow within 150 metres of an ambulance responding to a call.
    • Never threaten or hurt a health worker. Since 2021, the Criminal Code makes intimidating health workers a crime, and courts must treat an offence against someone providing health services as more serious.
    • Use 911 for emergencies. For non-urgent health questions, most provinces and territories have 811, so ambulances stay free for people who need them.
    About this game

    Be the Paramedic puts you in charge of a paramedic crew in a Canadian region for one season. Twelve people bring you a decision, you pick one of three answers, and four things move: time, patient care, your crew's wellbeing and the number of ambulances free for the next call.

    The game is calm and made for classrooms. It gives no medical advice and describes no injuries. Every decision is about how the system works: 911 priorities, waiting at a busy hospital, consent, interpreters, home visits, cleaning the ambulance and looking after each other.

    At the end the game shows which part of the work you leaned on and names jobs in Canada that use it. It is a game, not a career test.

    Tips for playing

    • Canada has three main paramedic levels — primary care, advanced care and critical care — plus emergency medical responders.
    • About 60,000 cardiac arrests happen outside hospitals in Canada each year. CPR and a defibrillator can double a person's chance of survival.
    • While paramedics wait to hand a patient over at a busy hospital, their ambulance cannot answer 911 calls. This is called offload delay.

    Common questions

    Does this game give medical advice?

    No. It is about how the paramedic system works. In an emergency, call 911. For non-urgent health questions, most provinces and territories have 811.

    Is it scary?

    No. Nothing graphic is described. The patients in the game are calm and talking, and it is written for players aged 10 and up.

    Can my season end early?

    Yes. If time, patient care, crew wellbeing or ambulances available reaches zero, your season ends, and the game tells you why.

    Is it good for a classroom?

    Yes. There is no signup and nothing is collected, it works on a phone or a projector, and it pairs well with a lesson on community helpers or first aid.
