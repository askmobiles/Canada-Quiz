Nunavut Rules of the Road

The rules of the road and safe driving practices are the second part of the Nunavut examination. Open a question to see the answer and a short explanation, then practise the whole chapter. No timer.

There are no road markings anywhere in Nunavut

This is the most striking thing the territory publishes about driving here, and it is the first thing to understand. There is no painted centre line and there are no lane lines anywhere in Nunavut. The territory's own manual says so, while noting that markings may come one day. Every generic Canadian practice question about solid lines, broken lines and staying inside your lane is describing something that is not on the road in front of you.

What that means in practice is that you judge your position from the edges of the road and you judge oncoming traffic by eye. The lane discipline that a painted line does for you everywhere else, you have to do for yourself here, and you have to do it on a gravel surface and often with dust in the air. It is a skill rather than a rule, which is exactly why it is easy to underestimate.

The speed limits and the surface they apply to

Nunavut publishes two basic speed limits, and a sign is posted wherever a different number applies.

  • 50 km/h inside communities, or as posted.
  • 90 km/h outside communities, day or night, or as posted.
  • In a school or playground zone, the posted speed, and where nothing is posted a reasonable and prudent speed.
  • Every one of those numbers is a ceiling for good conditions rather than a target to reach.

Gravel, dust and the hazard the territory names

Gravel is the standard driving surface here, and the territory's guidance about it is specific rather than general. Do not pass a vehicle that is creating a heavy dust cloud, because you cannot see what is inside it or what is beyond it, and do not travel too fast in loose gravel. Both of those come straight from the official manual.

The territory also names a road hazard that manuals further south do not. Keep a look out for dogs around your vehicle, and stay alert for animals on the road. Dogs are the animal the Nunavut manual names, and a driver who has learned elsewhere is usually watching for something else entirely.

Seat belts are required by law for every driver and every passenger, and child car seats are required for all small children. The manual states it plainly and states no exception to it.

Winter, and getting the vehicle ready for it

Winter preparation is treated in the manual as part of knowing how to drive rather than as an afterthought. Check the battery, make sure the brakes are properly adjusted, and check the windshield wipers, the heater and the defroster before the cold settles in.

The manual also lists what to carry: a window scraper and brush, booster cables, a shovel, a tow chain or strap, a traction mat, sand and a flashlight. On a slippery road, pump the brakes rather than holding them down, watch for patches of black ice, and remember that a snowplow carries flashing orange lights.

The manuals, the languages and the restricted licence

Nunavut publishes four driver manuals and every one of them is free: the Nunavut Driver's Manual, the Restricted Driver's Manual, the Professional Driver's Handbook and the Air Brake Manual. All four are published in English only. The Government of Nunavut website is available in Inuktitut, Inuinnaqtun, English and French, and so is the page that lists the manuals, but the manuals behind that page are the English files. It is a fact about the documents, and it is worth knowing before you go looking for a translation.

Which languages the examination itself is offered in is not published anywhere. The one accommodation the territory does publish is that a driver examiner may give the test aloud to somebody who cannot read and understand the written test, and it is framed as a matter of reading rather than of language choice. We are not going to turn that into a claim about languages.

The Restricted Driver's Manual is worth looking up if you expect to drive only close to home. It covers a licence carrying a B endorsement, which allows driving in Nunavut only, or a G endorsement, which allows driving within 100 kilometres of your home community. No other part of Canada arranges a licence that way, and almost no practice site mentions it.

Where to get the official information

Everything on this page comes from what Nunavut publishes for free. The Nunavut Driver's Manual is the book the knowledge test is written from, and it is free to download from the territory's own site. It is published in English only, as are the territory's three other driver manuals, even though the Government of Nunavut website itself is available in Inuktitut, Inuinnaqtun, English and French. That is why the English title appears in the French place on this site: there is no French edition to name. Read the manual alongside this practice, not instead of it.

This is an unofficial free practice site. It is not affiliated with the Motor Vehicles Division, the Department of Economic Development and Transportation or the Government of Nunavut, and it cannot issue a licence. Always check the official pages for the current rules before you book.

All 40 rules of the road questions, with every answer explained

This is the whole question bank from the test above, written out so you can read straight through it. The right answer is marked, and every question has a short explanation of why it is the right one.

Show all 40 questions

  1. What is the youngest age at which you may hold a Class 7 learner licence in Nunavut?

    • 15 years oldCorrect answer
    • 16 years old
    • 17 years old
    • 18 years old

    Nunavut sets 15 as the minimum age for the Class 7 learner licence. Some practice sites print 16, which is the minimum age for a full Class 5, not for the learner licence.

  2. What does a Class 7 licence allow you to do?

    • Operate any vehicle in Class 5 or Class 6 while you are learning to operate itCorrect answer
    • Drive alone in anything up to a light truck
    • Drive a bus carrying fewer than 24 passengers
    • Drive during daylight hours only

    The Class 7 is a learner licence: it covers the vehicles of Class 5 and Class 6 while you are learning them, and it is used with an accompanying driver rather than on your own.

  3. How is the Nunavut knowledge test put together?

    • Two parts at one sitting: rules of the road and safe driving, and sign identificationCorrect answer
    • One paper covering everything at once
    • Two papers written on separate days
    • A spoken interview only

    Nunavut's manual describes an examination in two parts taken together: one on the rules of the road and safe driving practices, and one on identifying signs.

  4. You pass the sign identification part but fail the other one. What do you have to rewrite?

    • Only the part you failedCorrect answer
    • Both parts, from the beginning
    • Nothing, a pass on one part carries the other
    • A road test instead

    Nunavut has you rewrite only the part that was failed. The part you passed stands, so your studying can go where you actually came up short.

  5. Which stages does a new Nunavut driver pass through after the learner licence?

    • None, because Nunavut has no graduated licensing programme; a Class 7 holder who passes the road test moves to a full Class 5Correct answer
    • A six-month probationary stage with a night curfew
    • Two novice stages of one year each
    • A three-stage programme that ends at age 18

    Graduated licensing with stages, curfews and passenger limits is common elsewhere in Canada, but Nunavut publishes no such programme. Anything you read about Nunavut learner stages has been borrowed from another jurisdiction.

  6. Who may sit with you while you drive on a Class 7 licence?

    • Someone who holds a valid driver's licence and is authorised under the ActCorrect answer
    • Any adult who has driven before
    • Any passenger aged 18 or over
    • Nobody is needed once you have practised enough

    The person beside you must genuinely be licensed and authorised to accompany you, and the examiner may ask to see that licence. Nunavut publishes no minimum age or years of experience for that person.

  7. Your community has no Motor Vehicles Office. How are driver services delivered there?

    • Through the Government Liaison Officer programme, which has an office in every communityCorrect answer
    • You must travel to Iqaluit for every service
    • Everything is handled by mail
    • Only during one yearly visit by an examiner

    Nunavut has Motor Vehicles Offices in Iqaluit, Rankin Inlet and Gjoa Haven, and Government Liaison Offices in all twenty-five communities support the rest of the territory. Some examining is also done by third-party examiners.

  8. Reading the written test is difficult for you. What does Nunavut offer?

    • The examiner may give you the test out loud insteadCorrect answer
    • A shorter version of the test
    • A pass based on the road test alone
    • A friend may write it for you

    Where somebody cannot read and understand the written test, the driver examiner may give a spoken test. It is the same material, asked aloud.

  9. How long does a Nunavut driver's licence run before it has to be renewed?

    • Five yearsCorrect answer
    • One year
    • Two years
    • Three years

    Licences are issued for five years. Renew before the expiry date: a licence that has been expired for more than twelve months cannot simply be renewed and the examinations must be taken again.

  10. You move to Nunavut holding a licence from another province. How long do you have to apply for a Nunavut licence?

    • Thirty daysCorrect answer
    • Fifteen days
    • Six months
    • One year

    New residents have thirty days from the move to apply. Separately, a change of name or address has to be reported to your Motor Vehicle service provider within fifteen days.

  11. Nothing is posted on the street you are driving along inside a community. What speed applies?

    • 50 km/hCorrect answer
    • 30 km/h
    • 40 km/h
    • 60 km/h

    Inside communities the basic limit is 50 km/h unless a sign says otherwise. It is a ceiling for good conditions, not a speed to hold regardless of dust, ice or people on the road.

  12. What is the basic speed limit outside the communities where nothing is posted?

    • 90 km/hCorrect answer
    • 60 km/h
    • 70 km/h
    • 80 km/h

    Outside communities the basic limit is 90 km/h, day or night, unless a sign says otherwise. Loose gravel and poor visibility very often call for far less than that.

  13. What does Nunavut law require about seat belts and child seats?

    • Every driver and passenger must be belted, and small children must be in a child car seatCorrect answer
    • Only the driver must be belted
    • Belts are required outside the communities only
    • Child seats are recommended but not required

    The requirement covers everybody in the vehicle, and small children need a car seat rather than an adult belt across them. It applies on a two-minute trip across the community just as much as on a long one.

  14. Which animal does the Nunavut driver's manual tell you to watch for around your vehicle?

    • DogsCorrect answer
    • Caribou
    • Foxes
    • Ravens

    The manual names dogs. They are often close to parked vehicles and can move without warning, so look around the vehicle before you start off and stay alert for animals on the road.

  15. No speed is posted at a playground in your community. How fast should you drive past it?

    • At a speed that is reasonable and prudent for what is happening thereCorrect answer
    • At the highest speed the street allows
    • At the same speed as the vehicle ahead of you
    • Speed does not matter where nothing is posted

    Where a school or playground zone has a posted speed you drive at it; where nothing is posted the standard is a reasonable and prudent speed, which means slow enough to stop for a child who runs out.

  16. You only ever drive inside your own community, never on a road out of it. Do you still need a licence?

    • Yes, a valid licence is needed to operate any motor vehicle in NunavutCorrect answer
    • No, a licence is only needed between communities
    • No, as long as the vehicle stays off the main street
    • Only if the vehicle is registered

    Nunavut publishes no exemption for short local trips. To legally operate a motor vehicle anywhere in the territory you must hold a valid driver's licence.

  17. The vehicle ahead of you on a gravel road is throwing up a thick cloud of dust. What does the manual tell you to do?

    • Do not pass it, and stay back where you can seeCorrect answer
    • Pass quickly on the left before the dust thickens
    • Sound your horn and pass on the right
    • Switch on your high beams and pass

    You cannot pass what you cannot see around. Inside a dust cloud you can see neither oncoming traffic, nor the edge of the road, nor anybody walking on it, so drop back and wait for a clear stretch.

  18. Why does Nunavut warn drivers not to travel too fast on loose gravel?

    • The tires lose their grip, so braking and steering both take longerCorrect answer
    • Gravel damages the paint on other vehicles
    • Fuel use rises sharply on gravel
    • Gravel roads carry a lower posted limit than paved ones

    Loose stone rolls under the tires. The vehicle takes longer to stop, drifts wide in a turn, and one sudden movement of the wheel can put it sideways.

  19. How much room should you leave behind a vehicle on a dusty gravel road?

    • Enough to stay out of its dust and still see the road aheadCorrect answer
    • One vehicle length, so you do not lose sight of it
    • Close enough to follow its tracks
    • The same distance as on a paved road

    The extra space does two jobs: it keeps you out of the dust so you can see, and it gives you the longer stopping distance that gravel demands.

  20. Which set of checks does the Nunavut manual ask you to make on the vehicle before winter?

    • Battery, brake adjustment, wipers, heater and defrosterCorrect answer
    • Paint, seats and radio
    • Tire colour, mirrors and mud flaps
    • Horn, plate light and spare key

    Those are the parts that decide whether you can see and whether you can stop in the cold. A weak battery and a defroster that cannot clear the glass are winter problems, not summer ones.

  21. What belongs in the kit the manual says to carry in winter?

    • A scraper brush, booster cables, a shovel, a tow strap, a traction mat, sand and a flashlightCorrect answer
    • A spare windshield, an extinguisher and flares
    • A first aid kit and nothing else
    • A second set of keys and a road map

    Every item on that list is there to get a stuck or dead vehicle moving again, or to let you see and be seen while you work on it. Carrying it costs nothing until the day it matters.

  22. The road has turned slippery and your vehicle has no anti-lock brakes. How does the manual say to brake?

    • Pump the brakes rather than locking the wheelsCorrect answer
    • Press hard and hold the pedal down
    • Use the parking brake instead
    • Steer sharply to scrub off speed

    Locked wheels slide and cannot be steered. Pumping keeps them turning, which is what lets you slow down and still point the vehicle where you want it. Watch for patches of black ice, which look like nothing more than wet road.

  23. You come up behind a vehicle showing flashing orange lights while it clears snow. What should you do?

    • Stay well back, because flashing orange marks slow work and thrown snowCorrect answer
    • Pass it on whichever side is clearest
    • Follow it closely so you stay in the cleared track
    • Sound your horn until it pulls over

    Flashing orange marks a vehicle working on the road, not an emergency. It moves slowly, stops without warning and throws snow and stone out to the side, so give it plenty of room.

  24. A Nunavut driver's record reaches twelve demerit points. What happens?

    • The Registrar sends a warning letter, and an interview followsCorrect answer
    • The licence is cancelled at once
    • A fine is added to the next renewal
    • Nothing until the licence expires

    Twelve points brings a warning letter and an interview. It is a chance to change how you drive before the next threshold is reached.

  25. How long is the suspension once a driver reaches fifteen demerit points?

    • One monthCorrect answer
    • One week
    • Three months
    • One year

    At fifteen points the Registrar suspends the licence for one month. Points are added to the record as convictions come in, so they build up quietly.

  26. At what ages must a Class 5, 6 or 7 driver in Nunavut provide a driver's medical examination?

    • At 75, at 80, and every two years after 80Correct answer
    • At 65, then every five years
    • At 70 only
    • Only when a doctor asks for one

    The schedule is set by age: once at 75, again at 80, and every second year from then on. The higher classes, such as buses and tractor-trailers, require a medical whatever the driver's age.

  27. Besides how clearly you see, what else does the Nunavut vision test look at?

    • Colour perception and field of visionCorrect answer
    • How quickly you read a page
    • Your hearing
    • Your reaction time

    Seeing to the sides matters as much as seeing straight ahead, and colour perception matters for signals and signs. If the standard is not met, a vision specialist gives a further test; glasses and contact lenses are allowed and are noted on the licence.

  28. How many painted centre lines and lane lines will you find on Nunavut roads?

    • None at all, because Nunavut roads carry no road markingsCorrect answer
    • They are painted on the main roads of the larger communities
    • Only on the roads between communities
    • Only in summer, once the snow has gone

    Nunavut's own manual states that there are no road markings in the territory, although that may change one day. Practice questions about solid and broken lines are describing roads somewhere else.

  29. With no centre line to follow, how do you keep to your own side of the road?

    • Judge your position from the edges of the road, and keep right of oncoming traffic by eyeCorrect answer
    • Drive down the middle and move over only when a vehicle appears
    • Follow the tracks left by the vehicle before you
    • Stay to the left, because the road is firmest there

    Without paint, the shoulders and the edges of the travelled surface are your reference. Keep inside them, look far ahead for oncoming vehicles, and move over early rather than at the last moment.

  30. You meet a yellow diamond-shaped sign. What kind of message does it carry?

    • A warning about something ahead on the roadCorrect answer
    • An order you can be fined for breaking
    • Directions towards a service
    • A temporary instruction for a work area

    Shape and colour tell you the kind of message before you are close enough to read it. Yellow diamonds warn, red and white signs order, blue signs point to services, and orange signs mark temporary work.

  31. How old must you be to get a Class 7 learner's licence in Nunavut?

    • 15Correct answer
    • 14
    • 16
    • 18

    A Class 7 learner's licence is issued at 15, and a full Class 5 or 6 at 16.

  32. How long must a first-time Nunavut driver practise before taking the driving exam?

    • Four weeks with a learner's permitCorrect answer
    • Six months
    • One year
    • Two years

    Nunavut requires four weeks of practice driving with a learner's permit. Yukon asks six months and the Northwest Territories a full year.

  33. What is the basic speed limit inside a Nunavut community?

    • 50 km/h or as postedCorrect answer
    • 40 km/h
    • 60 km/h
    • 80 km/h

    Inside communities the basic limit is 50 km/h or as posted. Outside communities it is 90 km/h.

  34. What is the speed limit for trucks outside Nunavut communities?

    • 80 km/h, day or nightCorrect answer
    • 90 km/h, the same as cars
    • 100 km/h
    • There is no separate limit

    Nunavut sets a separate truck limit of 80 km/h outside cities and towns, day or night, where cars may do 90.

  35. What does a Nunavut Restricted Driver's Licence allow?

    • Driving only within Nunavut, or within 100 km of your home communityCorrect answer
    • Driving only in summer
    • Driving only during daylight
    • Driving any vehicle anywhere in Canada

    Nunavut publishes a separate manual for this licence, which limits driving to within the territory or within 100 kilometres of the holder's home community. Nothing like it exists in Yukon or the Northwest Territories.

  36. What is the first rule for driving on a loose gravel road in Nunavut?

    • Do not travel too fastCorrect answer
    • Use your high beams
    • Stay in the middle of the road
    • Brake hard on curves

    Speed is the danger on gravel: traction is poor, stones fly up, and dust hides what is ahead. The first rule is simply not to go too fast.

  37. What should you not do behind a vehicle raising a heavy dust cloud in Nunavut?

    • Try to pass itCorrect answer
    • Slow down
    • Turn on your headlights
    • Increase your following distance

    Do not try to pass a vehicle creating a heavy dust cloud. You cannot see what is coming the other way until you are already committed.

  38. What should a Nunavut driver watch for when parking or backing up in a community?

    • Loose dogs around the vehicleCorrect answer
    • Falling ice
    • Low aircraft
    • Nothing in particular

    The driver's manual asks drivers to look out for dogs around the car when parking, backing up or driving through town, as well as for wildlife on the road.

  39. When may a Nunavut motorcycle learner not ride?

    • After dark, or where the limit is 70 km/h or moreCorrect answer
    • In the rain
    • On weekends
    • There is no restriction

    A motorcycle learner may not ride in the dark, or in speed zones of 70 km/h or greater, and must have a Class 6 licence holder with them.

  40. What is the legal blood alcohol limit for a fully licensed Nunavut driver?

    • 0.08Correct answer
    • 0.05
    • Zero
    • 0.10

    The Nunavut driver's manual states the limit as 0.08. As everywhere in Canada, the safest amount before driving is none.

About this page

The rules of the road and safe driving practices are the second part of the Nunavut examination. This page holds the rules half of our question bank, arranged by chapter, with the answer and a short explanation on every question.

Several of these rules are genuinely local. Roads with no markings at all, the basic limits of 50 inside a community and 90 outside it, the guidance on gravel and dust, and the dogs the manual names as a hazard are all specific to the territory, and all of them come from official sources.

What you'll find here

  • Thirty rules questions grouped by chapter, each with the answer marked and explained.
  • Your licence and the test, and applying, testing and renewing.
  • Driving in the communities, on roads that carry no painted markings at all.
  • Gravel roads and dust, and the speed and spacing the territory asks for on them.
  • Winter and cold, seat belts and child car seats, and safe and responsible driving.

Worth remembering

  • The basic speed limit is 50 km/h inside communities and 90 km/h outside them, day or night, or as posted.
  • Seat belts are required by law for all drivers and passengers, and child car seats for all small children.
  • Twelve or more demerit points brings a warning letter and an interview, and fifteen or more brings a one-month suspension.
  • Do not pass a vehicle that is creating a heavy dust cloud, and do not travel too fast in loose gravel.
  • A driver's medical examination is required at seventy-five, at eighty, and every two years after eighty.

Common questions

What is the speed limit if there is no sign?

50 km/h inside a community and 90 km/h outside one, day or night. In a school or playground zone it is the posted speed, and where nothing is posted, a reasonable and prudent speed.

Where are the lane markings?

There are none. The territory's own manual states that there are no road markings in Nunavut, so you judge your position from the edges of the road and judge oncoming traffic by eye.

How many demerit points before I lose my licence?

Fifteen or more brings a suspension of one month, and twelve or more brings a warning letter and an interview first. The points attached to each individual offence are not obtainable through any accessible official channel, so we do not list them.

Is the driver's manual available in French or Inuktitut?

No. All four of the territory's driver manuals are published in English only, even though the Government of Nunavut website is available in Inuktitut, Inuinnaqtun, English and French. Which languages the test itself is offered in is not published.

What is the restricted licence with a B or G endorsement?

It is a licence for people who will drive only inside the territory, or only within 100 kilometres of their home community, and Nunavut publishes a separate Restricted Driver's Manual for it that is worth looking up.
