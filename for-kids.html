For grown-ups and teachers

Every one of these stories is true, and every fact was checked against an official source: a museum, a national park, a government page or a university archive. Where the popular version of a story disagreed with the record, we used the record and dropped the popular version.

There is a Read aloud button at the bottom of each story's questions. Tap it and the phone reads the question and the answers out loud, so a child who cannot read yet can still play. It uses the voice already on the device, so it costs nothing and works without internet.

Two of these pages are not only cheerful. The railway story includes the workers who died building it and the tax Canada charged Chinese families for decades afterwards. The page about First Nations, Inuit and Métis peoples mentions residential schools. Both are written plainly and briefly, at a level a child can hold, because a version that leaves them out is not a shorter truth but a different story.

Nothing here asks a child to sign up, log in or type their name. There is no chat, no messaging and nothing to install. Teachers are welcome to put these on a projector and read them to a class.

About this quiz

This page gathers twelve true Canadian stories written for children aged six to nine. Each one has a picture drawn for this site, short sentences, and three questions at the end.

They were written because everything else on this site is taught through written paragraphs, which locks out the youngest readers. These pages work with the Read aloud button, so a child who cannot read yet can still take part.
