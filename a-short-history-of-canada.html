📜 A Short History of Canada: From First Peoples to Today

A simple, easy-to-read history of Canada — from the first Indigenous peoples to Confederation and modern Canada.

Canada has a long and rich history. This short guide walks through the main chapters, from the very first peoples to the country we know today.

The first peoples

Long before Europeans arrived, Indigenous peoples — First Nations, Inuit, and later the Métis — lived across the land for thousands of years. They had their own languages, cultures, governments, and trade routes, and their history is a vital part of Canada.

European explorers and the fur trade

About 500 years ago, explorers from Europe began arriving. French and British settlers built colonies, and the fur trade became very important to the early economy. The beaver, whose fur was highly valued, became a lasting symbol of the country.

Confederation in 1867

On July 1, 1867, several British colonies joined together to create the Dominion of Canada. This is called Confederation, and it is why we celebrate Canada Day. Canada started with four provinces: Ontario, Quebec, Nova Scotia, and New Brunswick.

Building a country from coast to coast

Over the following years, more provinces and territories joined, and a railway was built to connect the country from the Atlantic to the Pacific. This helped people and goods travel across the huge land.

Modern Canada

Through the 1900s, Canada grew into an independent, modern nation. Canadians served bravely in both World Wars, welcomed newcomers from around the world, and built one of the most diverse and peaceful countries on Earth. Today Canada has two official languages, English and French, and people from many cultures call it home.
