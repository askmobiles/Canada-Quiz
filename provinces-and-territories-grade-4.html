📖 Learn: ten provinces, three territories

Canada is divided into ten provinces and three territories. Each one has a capital city, where its government meets. The capital of the whole country is Ottawa, in Ontario.

Provinces run many of their own affairs. Territories are in the far north, have far fewer people, and get more of their powers from the federal government in Ottawa.

🗺️ Learn: the seven physical regions

Provinces and territories are political regions: people drew the lines. Canada also has physical regions, made by nature — the land itself, from mountains to plains to old rock.

  • Western Cordillera. The mountains of British Columbia and Yukon. Tall, young and still growing.
  • Interior Plains. The flat land of Alberta, Saskatchewan and Manitoba. Canada's farmland, and oil and gas underneath.
  • Canadian Shield. Old, hard rock with thin soil, thousands of lakes and forest. It covers about half of Canada.
  • Hudson Bay Lowlands. Flat, wet land along the south of Hudson Bay, in Ontario and Manitoba. Very few people live there.
  • Great Lakes and St. Lawrence Lowlands. The smallest region, in southern Ontario and Quebec. More than half of all Canadians live here.
  • Appalachian Region. Old, rounded hills in the Atlantic provinces and eastern Quebec. Some of the oldest mountains in the world, worn down by time.
  • Arctic Lowlands and Innuitian Mountains. The islands of the far north, in Nunavut. Frozen most of the year, with no trees at all.

Good to know

  • Canada has ten provinces and three territories. The territories are Yukon, the Northwest Territories and Nunavut, and together they cover about two fifths of the country's land but hold fewer people than most single cities.
  • The Canadian Shield covers about half of Canada. Its rock is among the oldest on Earth, its soil is thin, and it holds thousands of lakes.
  • More than half of all Canadians live in the smallest physical region, the Great Lakes and St. Lawrence Lowlands, in southern Ontario and Quebec.
