🏛️ How Canada's Government Works, in Plain English

A simple explanation of how Canada's government works — the three levels, Parliament, the Prime Minister, and how elections work.

Understanding how Canada is governed is helpful for school and important for the citizenship test. Here is a simple, plain-English explanation.

Canada is a democracy

Canada is a democracy, which means the people choose their leaders by voting in elections. It is also a constitutional monarchy, so the King is Canada's head of state, represented in Canada by the Governor General.

Three levels of government

Canada has three levels of government, and each one takes care of different things:

  • Federal (national) — handles things for the whole country, like defence, money, and citizenship. Led by the Prime Minister.
  • Provincial and territorial — handles things like schools, hospitals, and highways. Led by a Premier.
  • Municipal (local) — your city or town, handling things like parks, garbage, and local roads. Led by a Mayor.

Parliament and how laws are made

The federal Parliament has three parts: the Sovereign (the King), the Senate, and the House of Commons. Canadians elect Members of Parliament (MPs) to the House of Commons. New laws must be discussed and approved before they can take effect.

The Prime Minister

The Prime Minister is the head of the government and usually the leader of the political party with the most seats in the House of Commons. The Prime Minister chooses ministers to help run the country.

How elections work

In a federal election, Canadians vote for the person they want to represent their local area (their riding). The party that wins the most ridings usually forms the government. Every citizen 18 or older has the right to vote.
