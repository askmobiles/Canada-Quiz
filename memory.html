About this game

Canada Memory Match is the classic pairs game with a Canadian face on it. All the cards begin face down. You turn over two at a time, and when the pictures match they stay open. When they do not, they flip back and you try to remember where they were.

There is no reading and no time pressure, so it works for a preschooler and still gives a grandparent something to think about. Everyone in the room can play the same board and simply compare how many moves each of them needed.

Three board sizes let the game grow with the player. A small board is a two-minute warm-up, and the largest one is a genuine memory test that rewards a calm, orderly search instead of random tapping.

How it works

  • A level menu offers easy with six pairs, medium with eight pairs, or hard with ten pairs.
  • The card pictures are Canadian: a maple leaf, a hockey stick, a beaver, a bear, a moose, an owl and a canoe.
  • More pictures include a skier, a mountain, a wolf, an eagle, an evergreen, a snowflake, a bison and a fish.
  • Three counters run along the top of the board: your moves, your matched pairs, and the seconds elapsed.
  • The clock only starts when you flip your first card, so nobody is rushed while choosing a level.
  • Finishing all the pairs shows a winning message with the number of moves and the time you took.

Tips for playing

  • Work along one row at a time instead of tapping about. An orderly search tells you where a picture is not, which is nearly as useful as where it is.
  • Say each picture out loud as you turn it over. Hearing yourself name the moose or the canoe fixes it in memory far better than looking alone.
  • Unmatched cards stay visible for a short moment before flipping back, so pause and look rather than reaching for the next card immediately.
  • Moves count each pair of flips, not each card, so two cards turned over together count as one single move.
  • The pictures are chosen fresh from a pool of sixteen every game, so a new board can hold animals you did not see last time.
  • For a family contest, keep the level the same for everybody and compare moves rather than seconds, which is fairer to younger fingers.

Common questions

Can two people play against each other on one screen?

The board tracks one player at a time, so the usual way is to take turns. One person finishes a board, notes their moves and time, then presses New Game and hands the device over.

What is a good number of moves to aim for?

On the eight pair board, finishing under twenty moves is strong and under sixteen is close to perfect. Beginners often need thirty or more, and that number drops quickly with practice.

Does changing the level restart my board?

Yes. Picking a new level deals a fresh set of cards immediately and resets the moves, pairs and clock to zero, so finish the board you are on before you switch.

Is this game any good for a child who cannot read yet?

It is one of the best on the site for them. Every card is a picture, the only action is tapping, and there is nothing to type, so a three-year-old can play with a little help.

Why does the timer sometimes show zero when I start?

It waits for your first flip before it begins counting. That way you can read the level menu, explain the rules to a child, or set the phone down without losing seconds.
