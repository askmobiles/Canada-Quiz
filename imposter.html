🕵️ Imposter

Everyone gets the secret word… except one sneaky imposter!

📖 How to play

Play with 3–10 people and ONE phone. Everyone taps to see their secret card, one at a time, then passes the phone on. Everybody gets the same secret word — except one person, the Imposter, who only sees the category. Then talk! Each person says one word or clue about the secret word. The imposter has to fake it and blend in. After a round of talking, everyone votes on who they think the imposter is — then reveal to see if you caught them!

About this game

Imposter is a talking game for three to ten people sharing a single phone. Everyone sees the same secret word, except one player who sees only the category. That player has to bluff their way through the conversation without ever being sure what the group is describing.

The fun sits in the clues. Each person says one word about the secret, so the honest players must prove they know it without handing it to the imposter. Say too little and you look guilty; say too much and you give the whole thing away.

It works at a kitchen table, on a bus ride, at a family reunion or in a classroom where students already know each other. No writing, no cards, no pieces to lose — just one phone travelling around the circle and a few minutes of good argument.

How it works

  • A stepper on the setup screen sets the group size anywhere from three players up to ten players.
  • Three difficulty levels, Easy, Medium and Hard, each hold one hundred words spread across five categories.
  • Easy words come from animals, food, objects, places and nature, so children can follow the conversation.
  • Medium mixes jobs, sports, entertainment, transport and famous places, while Hard reaches into science, emotions and big ideas.
  • The phone passes one player at a time, and each person taps once to reveal their own private card.
  • A discussion timer offers one, two or three minutes before the reveal screen names the imposter.

Tips for playing

  • The imposter is told the category, so a clue that fits the category but not the exact word is their best disguise.
  • Speaking last is a big advantage for the imposter, because every earlier clue narrows the field before they open their mouth.
  • Honest players often protect themselves by choosing a clue that is true but slightly vague, then watching who copies the idea.
  • Change who speaks first between rounds. If the same person always opens, that seat carries an unfair burden all evening.
  • With ten players a single round of clues takes a while, so the one-minute timer usually suits small groups better.
  • The New Round button keeps the same players and draws a fresh word, which is the quickest way to play a long series.

Common questions

Does every player need their own phone?

No, one device is the whole point. It travels around the group and each person looks at their card alone, then hides it and hands the phone to the next player before anything is discussed.

What happens if somebody sees another player's card?

Start the round again with the New Round button. It picks a brand new secret word and chooses a new imposter, so nothing from the spoiled round can be used against anyone.

How long does one full round take?

Passing the phone takes about a minute for a group of five, then you choose a one, two or three minute discussion. Most rounds finish, votes and all, in under five minutes.

Which difficulty should a family with kids choose?

Start on Easy. The words are things children touch and see every day, so a young player can give a clue with confidence. Move up to Medium once the group finds the imposter too quickly.

Does the app decide who wins the argument?

No, your group votes out loud however it likes. The screen only reveals which player number was the imposter and what the secret word was, so you can see whether the vote was right.
