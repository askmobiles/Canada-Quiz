❌⭕ Tic-Tac-Toe

Play with a friend, or beat the computer. Pick a level!

📖 How to play

Players take turns placing X and O on the grid. Get three of your marks in a row — across, down, or diagonally — to win. Play against a friend (2 Players) or try to beat the computer on Easy or Hard.

About this game

Tic-Tac-Toe fits in a single screen and takes about a minute. Two players take turns placing X and O on a three-by-three grid, and the first to line up three marks in a row wins the round.

The page can also play against you. A dropdown offers a friend on the same device, an easy computer that makes mistakes, or a hard computer that does not. Beating the hard setting is a genuine challenge.

It makes a good first game for a young child. The rules take ten seconds to explain, the squares are big enough for small fingers, and a fresh round starts with a single button. A whole game fits into the wait for a bus.

How it works

  • The mode dropdown holds three choices: two players, computer on easy, and computer on hard.
  • Hard is the setting chosen when the page opens, so switch it if you want a gentler opponent.
  • A line of text above the grid always tells you whose turn it is and announces the result.
  • Tap any empty square to place your mark; the computer replies right away in the same grid.
  • The new game button clears the board without reloading the page or forgetting the mode you chose.

Tips for playing

  • The centre square belongs to more winning lines than any other square, so taking it first is the strongest opening move.
  • Corners come next. A player holding two opposite corners often ends up with two different ways to win at once.
  • If both players play perfectly, every game ends in a draw. That is exactly why the hard computer is so difficult to beat.
  • Before planning your own line, check whether your opponent already has two in a row. Blocking always comes first.
  • The easy computer is useful for children, because it sometimes misses an obvious block and lets them enjoy a real win.

Common questions

Can I play when nobody else is around?

Yes. Pick one of the two computer settings from the dropdown and it takes every second turn. Easy makes mistakes on purpose, while hard plays as well as the game allows.

What exactly counts as a win?

Three of your marks in a straight line — across a row, down a column, or along either diagonal. The text above the board says who got there first.

Why do so many games end in a draw?

With such a small grid there are only a few lines to make, and two careful players simply block each other. A draw against the hard computer is a good outcome.

Does it work on a tablet?

Yes. The squares are large and respond to a tap, so a tablet propped between two people suits the two-player mode nicely. Nothing needs to be installed first.
