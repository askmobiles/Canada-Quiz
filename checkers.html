🔴 Checkers

Play against the computer, or a friend on the same device!

📖 How to play

You are the red pieces at the bottom. Tap one of your pieces, then tap a highlighted green dot to move it diagonally. Jump over an opponent's piece to capture it — and if you can jump, you must! Reach the far side to become a King 👑, which can move both forwards and backwards. Capture all the other player's pieces (or block them so they can't move) to win!

About this game

Checkers, also called draughts, is here in its full form: an eight-by-eight board, forced jumps and kings that move in every direction. You play the red pieces at the bottom and move by tapping a piece and then a highlighted square.

Two modes cover most situations. Take on the computer when you are alone and want a quick game, or pass the phone back and forth with a friend sitting next to you in the two-player mode.

It is one of the easiest games to teach a child. The rules fit into a sentence, every move is diagonal, and a beginner can win a genuine game after one afternoon of practice.

How it works

  • You control the red pieces at the bottom, and green dots appear on every square the selected piece can reach.
  • Jumping over an opponent's piece captures it, and whenever a jump is available you are required to take it.
  • A piece that reaches the far row becomes a King, marked with a crown, and may then move backwards as well.
  • Buttons above the board switch between playing the computer and playing a friend on the same device.
  • A status line names whose turn it is and lets you know when the computer is thinking about its reply.
  • The New Game button clears the board at once, so a poor opening costs you nothing more than a tap.

Tips for playing

  • Because jumps are forced, offering a piece on purpose can steer your opponent into a move they would rather not make.
  • Keeping your back row intact as long as possible stops the other side from crowning kings on your half of the board.
  • Pieces on the edges cannot be jumped from both sides, which makes them safer, though they also have fewer moves available.
  • Trading pieces one for one helps whoever is already ahead, so avoid straight swaps when you are the player behind.
  • A double or triple jump often hides where a single one looks obvious, so check one square further before you tap.

Common questions

Is the checkers computer hard to beat?

It looks for the biggest capture and likes moves that crown a king, so it punishes careless play. It does not plan many moves ahead, and beginners beat it regularly.

Can two people play checkers on one phone?

Yes. Choose the two-player mode and pass the device after each turn. Red always moves first and the line above the board says whose turn it is.

What happens if a player has no move left?

Blocking your opponent so that no legal move remains wins the game, just as capturing all their pieces does. The board recognises both endings on its own.

Do I need to know the rules before I start?

No. The how-to-play panel at the top explains tapping, jumping and kings in one short paragraph, and the green dots show you the legal moves as you go along.
