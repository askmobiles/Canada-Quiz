Saskatchewan Rules of the Road

This is the half of the Saskatchewan knowledge test SGI calls the Basic test: driving situations and the rules of the road, chapter by chapter. Open a question to see the answer and a short explanation, then practise the whole chapter. No timer.

The Saskatchewan numbers worth memorising

Stop no closer than 5 metres — about fifteen feet — from a school bus showing flashing red lights, from the front or the back. On an undivided highway traffic in both directions stops; on a divided highway only the traffic behind the bus stops. Wait until the lights go off and the stop arm folds in.

Slow to 60 km/h to pass a stopped emergency vehicle or tow truck with its lights flashing. On an undivided highway that applies from both directions; on a divided highway it does not apply to traffic going the other way. If an emergency vehicle is coming toward you with lights and siren, pull as close as possible to the right edge, stop, and stay out of the next intersection until it has passed.

Leave at least two seconds behind the car ahead on a dry road, and far more on snow or ice, where stopping distance can grow many times over. Give a cyclist plenty of room and treat them as traffic. Watch the posted sign for school zone speeds and hours rather than assuming a number, because it is posted on the sign for a reason.

Winter is on the test because winter is the road here

Bridges and overpasses freeze before the road either side of them, because cold air passes underneath as well as over. Black ice is a thin clear layer you often cannot see at all, and the first hint is usually the steering going light.

In blowing snow, high beams make things worse — the light bounces back off the snow in the air. Drop to low beams, increase your following distance and slow down. Never follow the tail lights in front as a guide: that is how a chain collision starts. If it gets bad enough that you must stop, get right off the road and put your hazard lights on, because a vehicle stopped in a driving lane in a whiteout will be hit.

Clear the snow off the whole vehicle, not a porthole in the windscreen. A slab of snow leaving your roof at highway speed can blind the driver behind you completely. Roof, lights, mirrors, all the glass.

Grid roads and highway driving

Much of Saskatchewan's road network is gravel grid road, and it drives differently. Loose gravel lengthens every stop. A dust cloud from another vehicle can hide a whole truck. Crossroads are often uncontrolled, so slow down and look both ways even when nothing suggests you should.

Farm machinery is on the highway through seeding and harvest. A combine doing 25 km/h closes on you far faster than you expect at highway speed, and it is usually wider than the lane it is in. Wait for a genuinely long clear stretch before passing.

Deer move at dawn and dusk and rarely travel alone — if one crosses in front of you, expect another. A moose is a different problem: it stands so tall that the bumper takes its legs and the body comes through the windscreen. Brake hard and aim for where the animal has been, never for where it is going.

About this page

This page is SGI's Basic test: driving situations and the rules of the road, chapter by chapter. Open any question to see the answer and a short explanation, then practise the whole chapter.

SGI divides the Saskatchewan knowledge test into two parts that are marked separately: a Basic test on driving situations and the rules of the road, and a Sign test on sign identification. If you fail one, you rewrite only that one. That is why this site keeps signs and rules as two separate study pages.

It also gathers the numbers that are specific to Saskatchewan, along with the winter and grid road driving that a knowledge test in this province genuinely has to cover.

What you'll find here

  • Thirty rules questions grouped by chapter, with an explanation on every answer.
  • The graduated licence in full: nine months at Class 7, six at Novice 1, twelve at Novice 2.
  • The five-metre school bus rule, and how it differs on a divided highway.
  • The 60 km/h speed for passing a stopped emergency vehicle or tow truck with its lights on.
  • Winter driving and gravel grid roads, which is most of the province's road network.

Worth remembering

  • Bridges and overpasses freeze before the road either side of them, because cold air passes underneath as well as over.
  • High beams make blowing snow worse. The light bounces back off the snow in the air.
  • Never follow the tail lights ahead as a guide in a whiteout. That is how chain collisions start.
  • Clear the snow off the roof too. A slab leaving your roof at speed can blind the driver behind you.
  • A combine at 25 km/h closes on you very fast at highway speed, and is usually wider than its lane.

Common questions

How many questions are on the real Saskatchewan knowledge test?

SGI does not publish that number, and we are not going to invent one. What SGI does state is that the test comes in two parts, Basic and Sign, that they are marked separately, and that you rewrite only the part you fail.

What is the pass mark?

SGI does not publish a pass mark either. Our mock test uses a practice standard of our own so you have something to aim at, and we label it as ours rather than pretending it is the official one.

Can I take the test more than once in a day?

No. SGI allows one attempt per day. A passed written result stays valid for one year.

Where must my supervising driver sit?

In the front passenger seat, at all hours. They need a Class 5 or better licence from Canada or the United States, held for at least 365 days within the past three years.

Can I use a hands-free phone as a new driver?

No. New drivers may not use a cellphone at all, hand-held or hands-free. Hands-free is legal for fully licensed drivers, not at the learner or novice stages.
