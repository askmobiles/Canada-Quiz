Saskatchewan Rules of the Road

This is the half of the Saskatchewan knowledge test SGI calls the Basic test: driving situations and the rules of the road, chapter by chapter. Open a question to see the answer and a short explanation, then practise the whole chapter. No timer.

The Saskatchewan numbers worth memorising

Stop no closer than 5 metres — about fifteen feet — from a school bus showing flashing red lights, from the front or the back. On an undivided highway traffic in both directions stops; on a divided highway only the traffic behind the bus stops. Wait until the lights go off and the stop arm folds in.

Slow to 60 km/h to pass a stopped emergency vehicle or tow truck with its lights flashing. On an undivided highway that applies from both directions; on a divided highway it does not apply to traffic going the other way. If an emergency vehicle is coming toward you with lights and siren, pull as close as possible to the right edge, stop, and stay out of the next intersection until it has passed.

Leave at least two seconds behind the car ahead on a dry road, and far more on snow or ice, where stopping distance can grow many times over. Give a cyclist plenty of room and treat them as traffic. Watch the posted sign for school zone speeds and hours rather than assuming a number, because it is posted on the sign for a reason.

Winter is on the test because winter is the road here

Bridges and overpasses freeze before the road either side of them, because cold air passes underneath as well as over. Black ice is a thin clear layer you often cannot see at all, and the first hint is usually the steering going light.

In blowing snow, high beams make things worse — the light bounces back off the snow in the air. Drop to low beams, increase your following distance and slow down. Never follow the tail lights in front as a guide: that is how a chain collision starts. If it gets bad enough that you must stop, get right off the road and put your hazard lights on, because a vehicle stopped in a driving lane in a whiteout will be hit.

Clear the snow off the whole vehicle, not a porthole in the windscreen. A slab of snow leaving your roof at highway speed can blind the driver behind you completely. Roof, lights, mirrors, all the glass.

Grid roads and highway driving

Much of Saskatchewan's road network is gravel grid road, and it drives differently. Loose gravel lengthens every stop. A dust cloud from another vehicle can hide a whole truck. Crossroads are often uncontrolled, so slow down and look both ways even when nothing suggests you should.

Farm machinery is on the highway through seeding and harvest. A combine doing 25 km/h closes on you far faster than you expect at highway speed, and it is usually wider than the lane it is in. Wait for a genuinely long clear stretch before passing.

Deer move at dawn and dusk and rarely travel alone — if one crosses in front of you, expect another. A moose is a different problem: it stands so tall that the bumper takes its legs and the body comes through the windscreen. Brake hard and aim for where the animal has been, never for where it is going.

All 40 rules of the road questions, with every answer explained

This is the whole question bank from the test above, written out so you can read straight through it. The right answer is marked, and every question has a short explanation of why it is the right one.

Show all 40 questions

  1. How long must you hold a Class 7 learner licence in Saskatchewan before moving on?

    • Nine monthsCorrect answer
    • Three months
    • One year
    • Six weeks

    The learner stage lasts nine months. Time does not count while your licence is suspended, refused or not renewed.

  2. Where must your supervising driver sit while you drive on a Class 7 licence?

    • In the front passenger seat, at all hoursCorrect answer
    • Anywhere in the vehicle
    • In the back seat
    • They do not have to be in the vehicle

    Front passenger seat, every trip, day or night. From there they can see what you see and reach the wheel if they must.

  3. Who is allowed to be your supervising driver?

    • Someone with a Class 5 or better licence held for at least 365 days in the past three yearsCorrect answer
    • Any licensed driver over 18
    • Any adult in the car
    • A driving instructor only

    The licence must be from Canada or the United States, at least Class 5, and held for 365 days or more within the preceding three years.

  4. What is the minimum age for a Class 7 learner licence in Saskatchewan?

    • 16, or 15 with high school driver educationCorrect answer
    • 14
    • 17
    • 18

    Sixteen normally, and fifteen if you are taking driver education through a high school.

  5. Between midnight and 5 a.m. on a Class 7 licence, who may be in your vehicle?

    • Your supervising driver and immediate family membersCorrect answer
    • Anyone at all
    • Nobody but you
    • Up to three friends

    Late at night your passengers must be immediate family, apart from the supervising driver who has to be there anyway.

  6. How many passengers may ride with you on a Class 7 licence?

    • No more than the number of working seatbeltsCorrect answer
    • As many as will fit
    • Only one
    • Six

    Every person needs a working seatbelt of their own. That is the limit, whatever the vehicle.

  7. Which is true about cellphones for a new driver in Saskatchewan?

    • You cannot use any cellphone, hand-held or hands-freeCorrect answer
    • Hands-free is allowed
    • You may use one while stopped at a red light
    • You may use one on rural roads

    New drivers get no phone at all. Hands-free is legal for fully licensed drivers, not for you.

  8. How much alcohol may a learner or novice driver have in their blood?

    • None at allCorrect answer
    • Under 0.05
    • Under 0.08
    • One drink is allowed

    Zero. The rule runs right through the learner and both novice stages.

  9. After the learner stage, how long do the two novice stages last?

    • Six months for Novice 1, then twelve months for Novice 2Correct answer
    • Three months each
    • Twelve months for each
    • There are no novice stages

    Novice 1 is six months and Novice 2 is twelve, so the whole programme runs about twenty-seven months from your first learner day.

  10. Can you act as a supervising driver for someone else while you hold a Class 7?

    • NoCorrect answer
    • Yes, if you are over 18
    • Yes, for a family member
    • Yes, in daylight

    A learner cannot supervise a learner. You also cannot get a commercial licence or a school bus endorsement at this stage.

  11. The Saskatchewan knowledge test comes in how many parts?

    • Two — a Basic test and a Sign testCorrect answer
    • One test covering everything
    • Three parts
    • Four parts

    SGI runs two: the Basic test on driving situations and the rules of the road, and the Sign test on sign identification.

  12. You pass the Sign test but fail the Basic test. What happens?

    • You rewrite only the part you failedCorrect answer
    • You rewrite both parts
    • You wait six months
    • You must take a driving course

    Only the failed part is rewritten. This is why it is worth studying signs and rules separately.

  13. How often may you write the knowledge test?

    • Once per dayCorrect answer
    • Twice per day
    • Once per week
    • As often as you like

    One attempt a day. Use the gap to study the part you missed rather than guessing again.

  14. How long does a passed written exam result stay valid?

    • One yearCorrect answer
    • Thirty days
    • Three months
    • Forever

    A successful result is good for one year, so do not let too long pass before the next step.

  15. You do not understand what a test question is asking. What can you do?

    • Ask the examiner to explain what the question meansCorrect answer
    • Skip it and come back later only
    • Use your phone to look it up
    • Guess, and nothing else

    You may ask the examiner about the meaning of a question. You may not use a phone or any electronic device in the test area.

  16. An emergency vehicle or tow truck is stopped ahead with its lights flashing. What speed must you pass at?

    • 60 km/hCorrect answer
    • The posted limit
    • 80 km/h
    • 30 km/h

    Slow to 60 km/h to pass. On an undivided highway this applies from both directions; on a divided highway it does not apply to traffic going the other way.

  17. An emergency vehicle is coming toward you with lights and siren. What do you do?

    • Pull as close as possible to the right edge of the road and stopCorrect answer
    • Speed up to clear the way
    • Stop where you are in the lane
    • Turn left out of the way

    Right edge, stop, and do not enter the next intersection until it has gone by.

  18. How far from a school bus with flashing red lights must you stop?

    • No closer than 5 metresCorrect answer
    • No closer than 20 metres
    • Right behind it
    • There is no set distance

    Five metres, about fifteen feet, from the front or the back. Wait for the lights to stop and the stop arm to fold in.

  19. On a divided highway, when must you stop for a school bus with flashing red lights?

    • Only when you are approaching it from behindCorrect answer
    • From both directions always
    • Never on a divided highway
    • Only if children are crossing

    A median between you and the bus means only traffic behind it has to stop. On an undivided road, both directions stop.

  20. How should you pass a cyclist?

    • Like any other vehicle, giving them plenty of roomCorrect answer
    • As close as you can to save time
    • By sounding your horn first
    • Only on the right

    Treat a cyclist as traffic. Wait for room, then leave a wide gap — wind from a passing vehicle can push a bike sideways.

  21. Snow is blowing across the highway and you cannot see far. What is the safest thing to do?

    • Slow down, increase your following distance, and pull well off the road if it gets worseCorrect answer
    • Turn on your high beams and keep your speed
    • Stop in the driving lane
    • Follow the tail lights ahead closely

    Following someone's tail lights in blowing snow is how chain collisions start. If you must stop, get right off the road and put your hazards on.

  22. Why are high beams a bad idea in falling snow or fog?

    • The light bounces back off the snow or fog and blinds youCorrect answer
    • They use too much power
    • They are illegal in winter
    • They melt the snow

    Bright light reflects off the particles in the air and reduces what you can see. Low beams do better.

  23. What is black ice and where does it form first?

    • A thin clear layer of ice, forming first on bridges and shaded spotsCorrect answer
    • Ice mixed with gravel, on hills
    • Ice only found in parking lots
    • Snow packed down by traffic

    Bridges and overpasses freeze first because cold air passes underneath them as well as over.

  24. Your back wheels start to slide out on an icy corner. What do you do?

    • Ease off the accelerator and steer where you want to goCorrect answer
    • Brake hard
    • Steer the opposite way
    • Pull the parking brake

    Look and steer where you want the car to end up. Hard braking mid-skid takes away what little grip you have left.

  25. On a gravel grid road, what changes about the way you drive?

    • Stopping takes longer and dust can hide oncoming trafficCorrect answer
    • Nothing changes
    • You can drive faster because there is no traffic
    • Signals are not needed

    Loose gravel lengthens every stop, and a dust cloud from another vehicle can hide a whole truck. Slow down at every uncontrolled crossroad.

  26. What is a safe following distance in good conditions, and what about on ice?

    • At least two seconds in good conditions, and far more on iceCorrect answer
    • One car length at any speed
    • Two seconds always, whatever the surface
    • Half a second

    Two seconds is the dry-road minimum. On snow or ice you may need three or four times that, because stopping distance can grow tenfold.

  27. Who is responsible for making sure everyone in the vehicle is buckled up?

    • The driver is responsible for passengers under 16Correct answer
    • Each passenger only
    • Nobody, it is a personal choice
    • The front seat passenger

    You are responsible for younger passengers, and everyone needs their own working belt.

  28. You are tired on a long drive across the prairies. What actually works?

    • Stop and rest or swap driversCorrect answer
    • Open the window and turn up the radio
    • Drink coffee and keep going
    • Drive faster to arrive sooner

    Only sleep fixes tiredness. Long straight roads and an unchanging horizon make drowsiness worse, not better.

  29. Why clear snow from the whole vehicle and not just a patch of windshield?

    • Snow blows off onto the driver behind and blocks your own viewCorrect answer
    • It is only about appearance
    • It saves fuel
    • It is not required in Saskatchewan

    A slab of snow off a roof can blind the car behind you completely. Clear the roof, lights, mirrors and all the glass.

  30. You are in a collision with injuries. What is the first thing to do?

    • Call for help and do not move injured people unless they are in dangerCorrect answer
    • Move all vehicles off the road first
    • Leave to find a phone
    • Take photographs first

    Get help coming, warn other traffic, and leave injured people where they are unless there is fire or another immediate danger.

  31. How long must a Saskatchewan learner hold a Class 7 licence before moving on?

    • 9 monthsCorrect answer
    • 3 months
    • 12 months
    • 24 months

    The Class 7 learner stage lasts 9 months, then Novice 1 for 6 months and Novice 2 for 12 months before a full Class 5.

  32. Who must have zero alcohol in Saskatchewan, besides new drivers?

    • Every driver aged 21 and underCorrect answer
    • Drivers over 65
    • Only commercial drivers
    • Nobody else

    Zero tolerance for alcohol and drugs applies to new drivers and to every driver aged 21 and under, whatever licence they hold.

  33. Between midnight and 5 a.m., who may ride with a Saskatchewan learner or Novice 1 driver?

    • Only immediate family membersCorrect answer
    • Anybody, up to the number of seat belts
    • Nobody at all
    • Only other new drivers

    Between midnight and 5 a.m. every passenger must be an immediate family member. Saskatchewan restricts who is in the car rather than banning night driving outright.

  34. How many non-family passengers may a Saskatchewan Novice 1 driver carry?

    • OneCorrect answer
    • None
    • Three
    • As many as there are seat belts

    A Novice 1 driver may carry one passenger who is not immediate family. Other passengers must be immediate family, up to the number of seat belts.

  35. Which phones may a Saskatchewan learner or novice driver use?

    • None — hand-held and hands-free are both bannedCorrect answer
    • Hands-free only
    • Any phone at a red light
    • A phone used for navigation

    Saskatchewan learners and novices may not use a cellphone at all, hand-held or hands-free. Only British Columbia is as strict with new drivers.

  36. What does a first distracted driving ticket cost in Saskatchewan?

    • $580 and 4 demerit pointsCorrect answer
    • $150
    • $280
    • $1,400

    A first offence is $580 and 4 demerit points. A second within twelve months rises to $1,400 and the vehicle is seized for seven days.

  37. What kind of vehicle may a Saskatchewan learner or Novice 1 driver not drive?

    • A right-hand-drive vehicleCorrect answer
    • A pickup truck
    • A vehicle with a trailer hitch
    • A two-door car

    Saskatchewan does not let a learner or Novice 1 driver operate a right-hand-drive vehicle. No neighbouring province has this rule.

  38. What must a Saskatchewan learner's supervising driver hold?

    • A Class 5 held at least 12 months in the last 3 yearsCorrect answer
    • Any valid licence
    • A commercial licence
    • A licence held for 10 years

    The supervising driver must hold a valid Class 5, held for at least 12 months in the last three years, and must sit in the front passenger seat at all hours.

  39. What is the default speed limit on a Saskatchewan rural road with no posted sign?

    • 80 km/hCorrect answer
    • 60 km/h
    • 100 km/h
    • 110 km/h

    Where no sign is posted on a rural road the limit is 80 km/h. In towns and cities the limit is set locally, so watch for the sign at the edge of the community.

  40. Until roughly what age must a child in Saskatchewan use a car seat or booster?

    • Under 7Correct answer
    • Under 3
    • Under 5
    • Under 12

    Children under seven must ride in an approved car seat or booster sized to them. A child may move to the seat belt alone once over 36 kg.

About this page

This page is SGI's Basic test: driving situations and the rules of the road, chapter by chapter. Open any question to see the answer and a short explanation, then practise the whole chapter.

SGI divides the Saskatchewan knowledge test into two parts that are marked separately: a Basic test on driving situations and the rules of the road, and a Sign test on sign identification. If you fail one, you rewrite only that one. That is why this site keeps signs and rules as two separate study pages.

It also gathers the numbers that are specific to Saskatchewan, along with the winter and grid road driving that a knowledge test in this province genuinely has to cover.

  • The 60 km/h speed for passing a stopped emergency vehicle or tow truck with its lights on.
  • Winter driving and gravel grid roads, which is most of the province's road network.

Common questions

How many questions are on the real Saskatchewan knowledge test?

SGI does not publish that number, and we are not going to invent one. What SGI does state is that the test comes in two parts, Basic and Sign, that they are marked separately, and that you rewrite only the part you fail.

What is the pass mark?

SGI does not publish a pass mark either. Our mock test uses a practice standard of our own so you have something to aim at, and we label it as ours rather than pretending it is the official one.

Can I take the test more than once in a day?

No. SGI allows one attempt per day. A passed written result stays valid for one year.

Where must my supervising driver sit?

In the front passenger seat, at all hours. They need a Class 5 or better licence from Canada or the United States, held for at least 365 days within the past three years.

Can I use a hands-free phone as a new driver?

No. New drivers may not use a cellphone at all, hand-held or hands-free. Hands-free is legal for fully licensed drivers, not at the learner or novice stages.
