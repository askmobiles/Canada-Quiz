The Town Where Polar Bears Walk Down the Street

There is a town in Manitoba where the school has bear drills the way other schools have fire drills.

A town you cannot drive to

Churchill sits on the edge of Hudson Bay, in the far north of Manitoba. About 900 people live there.

There is no road to Churchill. None. You cannot drive there from anywhere. You go by train, which takes about two days from Winnipeg, or you fly, which takes about two hours.

Everything the town needs arrives on that train or that plane. Bananas, bicycles, school books, all of it.

Why the bears come

Polar bears hunt seals, and they hunt them out on the sea ice. In summer the ice melts, so the bears have to come ashore and wait, eating almost nothing for months.

The water near Churchill freezes earlier than the rest of Hudson Bay. So every autumn the bears gather there, waiting for the ice to come back.

From the middle of October to the middle of November, hundreds of polar bears are near that little town. Some years it is close to a thousand.

They are hungry and they are enormous. A big male can weigh as much as six grown-ups put together.

How the town stays safe

Churchill has a Polar Bear Alert programme. Warning signs stand along the shore, and if anybody sees a bear near town they ring a special number.

Officers come and put the bear safely to sleep for a little while. Then the bear is taken to a building on the edge of town, which everybody calls the polar bear jail.

The bears are not given food there. That sounds unkind, but it is not: at that time of year a polar bear is naturally going without food anyway, and feeding them would teach them that people mean dinner.

When the bay finally freezes, the bears are flown out by helicopter and let go on the ice, where they wanted to be all along. This programme replaced what used to happen, which was that bears near town were shot.

Watching them properly

People come from all over the world to see the bears. They travel in tundra buggies, which are huge vehicles with wheels taller than a person, so everybody sits well above the ground.

There is one worry, and it is worth telling the truth about. Scientists counting the bears around western Hudson Bay have found fewer of them than there used to be, because the ice now melts earlier and freezes later, which means longer without food.

Words to know

Tundra
Cold, flat, treeless land in the far north where the ground stays frozen underneath.
Sea ice
Frozen sea water. Polar bears walk and hunt on it.
Tranquillise
To give an animal medicine that makes it sleep safely for a while.
Did you know? The safest way to look at a polar bear is from a vehicle with wheels taller than you are.
