🏡 Grade 1 Social Studies: Me and My Community

A Grade 1 lesson to read together, about me, my family, the people who help us and the places in my community, with an easy quiz, endless practice, a challenge round, and printable worksheets with answer sheets. No accounts, nothing to download.

Curriculum: Ontario Social Studies, Grade 1, revised in 2023: Strand A, Heritage and Identity: Our Changing Roles and Responsibilities, and Strand B, People and Environments: The Local Community. Unofficial practice, not approved by any school board. Read the official curriculum.

📖 For teachers and parents

Six tabs, one topic. Learn is a short lesson with a timeline. Quiz is ten questions with an explanation after each answer. Practice never ends and keeps no score. Challenge is twelve harder questions. Worksheet and Answers print — pick the same letter for both, and the answer sheet matches the worksheet. Three versions, so a class can have three different sheets side by side.

📖 Me, my family and my roles

Everyone is special. We all have roles and jobs at home and at school. As we grow, our roles change. Everyone should be treated fairly and with respect.

Me

I am special

Everyone is different. I have my own name, my own face and things I like to do. Other kids are special too.

Family

Families are different

Some families are big and some are small. Some children live with one parent, some with two, some with grandparents. Every family is special.

Roles

My roles

A role is a part you play. At home I can be a son or a daughter, and a big brother or a little sister. At school I am a student and a friend.

Responsibilities

My jobs

A responsibility is a job I am trusted to do. I can put away my toys, feed my pet, help set the table and hang up my coat.

Rules

Rules at school

Rules keep us safe and help us be fair. We walk in the halls, raise our hand, take turns and keep our hands to ourselves.

Respect

Kind and fair

We share, take turns, listen and use kind words. Everyone should be treated fairly, whatever language they speak and wherever their family comes from.

Changes

Things change as we grow

When I was a baby, I needed help with everything. Now I can do many things by myself. A new baby, a new school or a new home can change my roles too.

Indigenous peoples

First Nations, Inuit and Métis families

First Nations, Inuit and Métis are the Indigenous peoples of Canada. Family is very important to them. Grandparents and Elders share stories, language and teachings, and families have kept their cultures strong, even when it was hard.

📖 People who help us

Many people work to keep our community safe, healthy and clean. We can help too, by following safety rules.

Helpers

Community helpers

Many people have jobs that help everyone: teachers, doctors, nurses, bus drivers, mail carriers, store clerks and many more.

Fire

Firefighters

Firefighters put out fires and rescue people. They teach us fire safety: if there is a fire, get out and stay out. Smoke alarms beep loudly when there is smoke. In Ontario, every home must have one on every floor.

Emergency

Call 911

In an emergency, like a fire or someone badly hurt, call 911. Police officers, firefighters or paramedics will come. Never call 911 for a joke.

Library

Librarians

At the public library you can borrow books for free and bring them back later. Librarians help you find books, and many libraries have story time.

Crossing

Crossing guards

Near many schools, a crossing guard holds up a stop sign so cars stop and children can cross the street safely.

Safety

Safe on the street

Red means stop and green means go. Before you cross, stop and look both ways. In Ontario, everyone under 18 must wear a helmet when riding a bike.

Clean

A clean community

Workers pick up garbage and recycling. Recycling turns old cans, bottles and paper into new things. Many towns in Ontario use a blue box or bin for recycling and a green bin for food scraps.

📖 Places, maps and symbols

My community has places made by nature and places built by people. A map shows where they are. Canada has symbols we see every day.

Nature

Natural features

Some things are made by nature: rivers, lakes, hills, trees and rocks.

Built

Built features

Some things are built by people: houses, schools, roads, bridges and playgrounds.

Places

Important places

My home, my school, the park, the library, the community centre, the store and places of worship are important places in my community.

Maps

Reading a map

A map is a drawing of a place seen from above, like a bird would see it. Little pictures called symbols show things like trees or schools. The legend, or key, tells what each symbol means.

Directions

North, south, east and west

North, south, east and west are the four directions. A compass rose on a map shows them. On most maps, north is at the top.

Land

Caring for the land

We can care for our community: put garbage in the bin, save water and plant trees. Many schools say a land acknowledgement, to thank the Indigenous peoples who have cared for this land for a very long time.

Flag

Our flag

Canada's flag is red and white, with a red maple leaf in the middle. The leaf has 11 points. The flag was raised for the first time on February 15, 1965.

Symbols

Symbols of Canada

The beaver is an official symbol of Canada. A loon is on the one-dollar coin, the loonie, and a polar bear is on the two-dollar coin, the toonie. Our anthem is O Canada. Canada has two official languages, English and French.

Celebrations

Special days

Canada Day, on July 1, is Canada's birthday: Canada began on July 1, 1867. June 21 is National Indigenous Peoples Day. On September 30, many people wear orange to say that every child matters.

🗓️ Timeline

  • 1867 Canada begins on July 1
  • 1879 July 1 becomes a holiday, then called Dominion Day
  • 1965 The maple leaf flag is raised for the first time
  • 1969 A law makes English and French Canada's official languages
  • 1975 The beaver becomes an official symbol of Canada
  • 1980 O Canada becomes our national anthem
  • 1982 Dominion Day gets a new name: Canada Day
  • 1987 The loonie coin comes out
  • 1994 Hockey and lacrosse become Canada's national sports
  • 1996 The first National Indigenous Peoples Day, on June 21
  • 1999 Nunavut becomes a territory
  • 2013 The first Orange Shirt Day
  • 2017 Canada turns 150
  • 2021 The first National Day for Truth and Reconciliation, on September 30

About this quiz

This page covers Grade 1 Social Studies for young children to read with a teacher or parent: my roles and responsibilities at home and at school, how they change as we grow, fairness and respect, First Nations, Inuit and Métis families; the community helpers who keep us safe; and the places, maps and symbols of my community and of Canada. It follows Ontario's Grade 1 curriculum, revised in 2023.

It works six ways. Read the lesson and its timeline, take the ten-question quiz, practise for as long as you like with no score, try the harder challenge round, then print a worksheet and its answer sheet. There are three versions, lettered A, B and C, and the answer sheet with the same letter matches its worksheet exactly.

Good to know

  • The maple leaf on Canada's flag has 11 points. The flag was raised for the first time on February 15, 1965.
  • The loonie, Canada's one-dollar coin, came out on June 30, 1987. It is named after the loon on it.
  • In Ontario, everyone under 18 must wear a helmet when riding a bike.

Common questions

Do the worksheet and the answer sheet really match?

Yes. Worksheet A always has the same questions in the same order, and Answer Sheet A lists those answers, including the order of the timeline events and what a good written answer should mention.
