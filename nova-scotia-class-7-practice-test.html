What the real Nova Scotia knowledge test looks like

Nova Scotia publishes the whole format, which is unusual and very helpful. There are two tests. The Rules of the Road test has 20 multiple-choice questions. The Road Sign Recognition test has 20 more. You need 16 right on each one, which is eighty percent, and you get 30 minutes for each.

You can sit it online with a desktop or laptop and a webcam, or in person at an Access Nova Scotia or Registry of Motor Vehicles office. A phone or a tablet will not work for the online version. It costs $15.03, and you also need an eye test.

If you fail in an office you wait until the next day to try again. Online there is no waiting period, though you pay again either way. Because Nova Scotia publishes all of it, our mock test below is not a standard of ours: it is the real format.

Study the rules

Nova Scotia's three stages, one at a time

You start at 16, and if you are 16 or 17 a parent or guardian signs the application. Pass both written tests and an eye test and you hold a Learner's Licence, Class 7. You keep it for at least 12 full months before the road test, or 9 months if you finish an approved driver-training course.

Then comes the Newly Licensed Driver stage, for at least two years, with one restriction people forget: no driving between midnight and 5 a.m. An exemption can be granted for work, and if you are under 18 a parent has to consent to it.

After that comes a third stage that almost no other province has: Restricted Individual, marked Condition 47 on the licence, for two more years. From the day you first hold a learner's licence to a full Class 5 is therefore at least five years.

The rules that catch learners out

Your blood alcohol must be zero, and it stays zero through all three stages, not only while you are learning. That is stricter than most of the country and it surprises people who have already passed their road test.

The person beside you must hold a valid licence for the vehicle, must have at least two years of driving experience, and must not be in the graduated programme themselves. A friend who is a Newly Licensed Driver cannot supervise you, even though they hold a licence. They sit in the front passenger seat.

Demerit points bite quickly here. Two points bring a warning letter, and four points suspend a learner for six months. A hand-held phone conviction alone carries four points.

Where to get the official information

Everything on this page comes from what Nova Scotia publishes for free. The Nova Scotia Driver's Handbook is the book the real test is written from, and it is free to read on the province's own site. Read it alongside this practice, not instead of it.

This is an unofficial free practice site. It is not affiliated with Access Nova Scotia, the Registry of Motor Vehicles or the Government of Nova Scotia, and it cannot issue a licence. Always check the official pages for the current rules before you book.

Road Sign Recognition Test

6 of 30

  1. What must you do at this sign?

    • Come to a complete stop, then go when it is safeCorrect answer
    • Slow down and keep rolling
    • Stop only if another car is coming
    • Sound your horn and continue

    A full stop means the wheels stop turning. Stop at the line, or before the crosswalk if there is no line, then move off when the way is clear.

  2. What does this sign mean?

    • Do not pass another vehicle hereCorrect answer
    • No parking
    • No trucks
    • No turning

    Passing is banned on this stretch, usually because you cannot see far enough ahead to get back in safely.

  3. What must you do at a marked pedestrian crossover?

    • Stop and let people cross the whole way, then goCorrect answer
    • Sound your horn
    • Drive around them
    • Speed up before they step out

    Stop and wait. Do not creep forward behind somebody who is still crossing, and never pass another vehicle stopped at a crossover — there is a person you cannot see.

  4. What does this yellow diamond warn about?

    • The road may be slippery when wetCorrect answer
    • A car wash ahead
    • Loose gravel
    • A steep hill

    Slippery when wet. Ease off the accelerator, avoid sudden steering, and leave far more room than usual.

  5. What is about to happen?

    • The divided road ends and traffic comes towards you againCorrect answer
    • The road widens
    • A rest area begins
    • The speed limit rises

    The median is ending. This is a common place for head-on collisions because drivers keep left out of habit.

  6. How do you drive through a roundabout?

    • Yield to traffic already in it, then go around to the rightCorrect answer
    • Stop at the entrance every time
    • Go left around the circle
    • Take the first exit always

    Yield on entry to whoever is already going round, then travel to the right, around the circle, and signal before your exit.

Rules of the Road Test

6 of 40

  1. What is the first stage of Nova Scotia's graduated licensing programme called?

    • The Learner's Licence, Class 7Correct answer
    • The Newly Licensed Driver's Licence
    • The Restricted Individual licence
    • A full Class 5 licence

    Nova Scotia has three stages before a full licence: Learner's Licence (Class 7), then Newly Licensed Driver, then Restricted Individual with Condition 47.

  2. You fail the test at an office. When can you try again?

    • The next dayCorrect answer
    • The same hour
    • After one month
    • After one year

    In person you wait until the next day. If you take it online there is no waiting period before you try again, but you pay again either way.

  3. How many demerit points will suspend a learner in Nova Scotia?

    • 4 pointsCorrect answer
    • 10 points
    • 15 points
    • 2 points

    Four points brings a six-month suspension for a learner. Two points already brings a warning letter, so the margin is very small.

  4. What does the law say about a hand-held cellphone while driving?

    • You may not use one, and texting is bannedCorrect answer
    • You may use it at a red light
    • You may use it below 50 km/h
    • There is no rule

    Hand-held use and texting are banned while driving. A conviction brings four demerit points as well as a fine, and the fines rise steeply for repeat offences.

  5. What should you do before driving in winter weather?

    • Clear the glass, lights and mirrors and check the wipers and defrosterCorrect answer
    • Only clear a small hole in the windscreen
    • Nothing, the heater will do it
    • Drive slowly with the window open

    Clear all the glass, the lights and the mirrors, and make sure wipers, washers and defrosters work. You cannot avoid what you cannot see.

  6. Why is one phone ticket so serious for a Nova Scotia learner?

    • It carries 4 demerit points, and a learner is suspended at 4Correct answer
    • It doubles the insurance premium
    • It adds a year to the learner stage
    • It carries a $1,000 fine

    A hand-held phone offence is 4 demerit points, and a Nova Scotia learner is suspended at 4. One ticket ends the learner's licence outright.

About this page

Nova Scotia splits its knowledge test into two parts that are scored separately: a Rules of the Road test and a Road Sign Recognition test. Each has twenty multiple-choice questions, each needs sixteen right, and each is allowed thirty minutes. That is why this site keeps signs and rules as two separate study pages.

Nova Scotia publishes its whole test format, which is unusual. Several other provinces publish none of it, and on those pages we say so rather than invent a number. Here we did not have to: the numbers on this page are the province's own.

Common questions

How many questions are on the real Nova Scotia knowledge test?

Forty in total, in two tests of twenty. One is on the rules of the road and one is on road sign recognition, and you need sixteen right on each.

How long do I get?

Thirty minutes for each of the two tests.

Can I take it online?

Yes, with a desktop or laptop and a webcam. A phone or tablet will not work. You can also sit it in person at an Access Nova Scotia office. It costs $15.03.

How long must I hold the learner's licence?

Twelve full months before the road test, or nine if you finish an approved driver-training course.

Who can sit beside me while I practise?

Someone with a valid licence for the vehicle and at least two years of driving experience, who is not in the graduated programme themselves. They sit in the front passenger seat.
