About this quiz

The Fun Canada Quiz is the light-hearted one. Maple syrup, hockey, poutine, the loonie, toques and the northern lights: the kind of questions that make a newcomer smile and make someone born here nod along.

There is no timer and no pass mark. A round is eight questions, short enough to finish while the kettle boils, and every answer ends with a small fact you can repeat at the dinner table.

It makes a friendly first stop for anyone new to Canada who wants everyday culture rather than the textbook version, and a quick game for families who already know the answers and want to prove it.

What's inside

  • Each round is eight questions drawn from a bank of about one hundred and fifty.
  • The quiz remembers which questions you have already seen and serves you unseen ones first.
  • Topics cover food, hockey, wildlife, coins, slang, cities, weather and national symbols.
  • Answer choices are shuffled each time, so remembering the position of a right answer will not help you.
  • A score pill and a progress bar update after every tap, so you always know where you stand.
  • The results screen offers a replay button and a shortcut across to the family games.

Good to know

  • The beaver appears on the Canadian five cent coin, and the loon on the one dollar coin, which is why it is nicknamed the loonie.
  • A toque is the knitted winter hat Canadians pull on the moment the temperature starts to drop.
  • Poutine, made of fries, cheese curds and gravy, began in Quebec and is now eaten right across the country.
  • In Canada the last letter of the alphabet is usually said as zed rather than zee.
  • The northern lights, also called the aurora, glow in bands of colour across the skies of Canada's north.
  • Most of the world's maple syrup comes from Canada, and most of that comes from Quebec.
