Which Canadian Food Are You?

Answer seven questions about how you eat, rest and travel, and find the Canadian dish that fits.

📖 How to play

Tap the choice that feels most like you. There are seven questions, no right answers and nothing to study. At the end you get one result, plus the one that came second, and a button to run the whole thing again.

About this quiz

Seven quick questions about how you eat, rest and travel, and one Canadian dish at the end. It is a personality quiz rather than a food test, so there is nothing to get wrong.

Behind the fun there is a small tour of the country. Poutine and tourtière come from Quebec, the butter tart from Ontario, the Nanaimo bar from British Columbia, and maple syrup from the sugar bush in late winter.

It takes about a minute, it is free, and there is no signup. Families run it together and argue happily about who got which dish.

What's inside

  • Seven questions, four choices each, with a progress bar across the top.
  • Seven possible dishes: poutine, maple syrup, the butter tart, tourtière, the Nanaimo bar, the Montreal bagel and ketchup chips.
  • Each result has a short description of the character behind the dish rather than a recipe.
  • A Try again button restarts everything instantly.
  • Nothing is saved and no account is needed.

Good to know

  • It takes roughly forty litres of maple sap to boil down into one litre of syrup.
  • Quebec produces the large majority of the world's maple syrup.
  • Poutine grew up in rural Quebec in the late 1950s and is now sold coast to coast.
  • The Nanaimo bar is named after the city of Nanaimo on Vancouver Island and needs no baking.
  • Montreal bagels are boiled in honey water and baked in a wood-fired oven, which makes them sweeter and denser than the New York kind.

Common questions

Is this a cooking quiz?

No. It asks about your habits, not your kitchen skills. Every choice simply adds points to different dishes.

How long does it take?

About a minute, seven questions, one tap each.

Can I get a different dish?

Yes. Press Try again and change a couple of answers and the totals move.

Are these all really Canadian?

All seven are strongly associated with Canada and most were created here. Food travels, so several have relatives elsewhere, but these are the versions Canadians grew up with.
