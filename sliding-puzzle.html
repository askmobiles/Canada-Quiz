🧩 Sliding Puzzle

Slide the tiles until the numbers are in order!

📖 How to play

There's one empty space. Tap any tile next to the empty space and it slides over. Keep sliding until all the numbers are in order — 1, 2, 3… with the empty space at the bottom right. Pick a size: 3×3 is easy, 5×5 is a real challenge. Try to solve it in as few moves as you can!

About this game

One tile is missing from the board, and that gap is the only thing that lets the numbers move. Tap a tile beside the space and it slides over. Keep going until the numbers sit in order with the gap at the bottom right.

It is the little plastic puzzle from a childhood toy box, rebuilt for a screen. Good for a waiting room, a bus ride, or a quiet ten minutes when you want something to occupy your hands and nothing to read.

How it works

  • Three board sizes: 3x3 for a gentle start, 4x4 for a real puzzle, and 5x5 for a long sitting.
  • Tap any tile that touches the empty square and it slides in, while tiles further away stay exactly where they are.
  • Tiles already sitting in their finished position are highlighted, so your progress is easy to see at a glance.
  • A move counter and a clock start on your very first slide, letting you chase a tidier result next time.
  • The shuffle deals a new board by making random legal moves, which means every puzzle can definitely be solved.

Tips for playing

  • Half of all random tile arrangements cannot be solved at all, which is exactly why this board is mixed using legal moves instead.
  • Experienced solvers finish the top row first, then the left column, shrinking the puzzle until only a small square remains.
  • The last two tiles of a row are the hard part, and most people place that pair together rather than one tile at a time.
  • A 4x4 board has more than ten trillion solvable arrangements, so a shuffle almost never hands you the same start twice.
  • Undoing part of a finished row is sometimes necessary, and unpicking three tiles to fix one is a normal step, not a mistake.

Common questions

Which board size should a young child start with?

The 3x3 board is friendly for young children and usually falls in a couple of minutes. Move up to 4x4 once that first size stops feeling like a challenge.

Can I change the size in the middle of a game?

Yes, but choosing another size deals a fresh board immediately, so your current attempt ends there. Finish first if the moves and the time on screen matter to you.

Is there a hint button or an automatic solver?

No. The only controls are the three sizes and the shuffle. Working it out yourself is the whole game, and a completely fresh board is always one tap away.

Does this work properly on a small phone screen?

It does. The board resizes to fit the screen and every tile responds to a tap, so no keyboard and no mouse are needed anywhere in the game.
