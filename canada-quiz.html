About this quiz

The Canada Quiz gathers the country into eight topics and lets you play whichever one you fancy. Choose a century of history, or food, or hockey, and answer the whole set of questions in that topic before your final score appears.

It was built for a wide crowd: newcomers filling gaps before the citizenship test, students revising a social studies unit, and families who mostly want to argue about who knows the country best.

Every question ends with a short explanation of the right answer, so the quiz teaches as it goes. Wrong choices are marked clearly rather than glossed over, and the correct one is shown before you move on.

What's inside

  • Three history topics split the past by century, covering the 1700s, the 1800s and the 1900s one at a time.
  • Culture and Food asks about the traditions and dishes that Canadians recognise straight away.
  • Geography holds twenty questions about the land itself, from provinces and capitals through to rivers and coasts.
  • Famous Canadians, Hockey and Sports, and Canadian Inventions make up the three remaining topic cards.
  • Unlike shorter quizzes, a topic here plays through its full question set rather than stopping after ten.
  • A question counter, a score pill and a progress bar sit at the top so you always know how far you have come.

Sample questions

  1. Halifax, founded in 1749, is in which province?

    Answer: Nova Scotia

    Halifax became Britain's main naval base on Canada's Atlantic coast.

  2. Laura Secord became famous in 1813 for walking far to deliver what?

    Answer: A warning

    Laura Secord walked about 30 km to warn British and Indigenous troops of an attack.

  3. In 2008, Canada gave a formal apology to Indigenous Peoples about what?

    Answer: Residential schools

    In 2008 Canada formally apologized for the residential school system and began reconciliation work.

  4. At a Canadian coffee shop, what is a 'double-double'?

    Answer: Coffee with two creams and two sugars

    A double-double means two creams and two sugars, a phrase heard across Canada.

  5. Which province lies directly east of Alberta?

    Answer: Saskatchewan

    Saskatchewan sits between Alberta and Manitoba on the Canadian prairies.

Good to know

  • Splitting history by century makes it easier to remember, because events feel less tangled when you meet them a hundred years at a time.
  • Canadian inventions are a common weak spot in quizzes, so that topic tends to teach people the most on a first attempt.
  • A wrong answer here is worth more than a lucky guess, since the explanation stays on screen until you press next.
  • Geography questions overlap heavily with citizenship study, which makes that topic a useful warm-up before serious revision.
  • Playing a full topic gives a truer picture of what you know, because a single lucky streak cannot carry the whole score.

Common questions

How many questions will I face inside one topic?

It depends on the topic. Each one runs from roughly seventeen to twenty questions and you play the whole set. The card on the start screen tells you the exact number before you begin.

Which topic should a newcomer start with?

Geography and the history topics line up most closely with citizenship study. Culture, hockey and inventions are lighter, and they make a good way to finish a session once the serious work is done.

Is the Canada Quiz free and open to anyone?

Completely. There is no sign-up, no email address to hand over and no paid version hiding behind the free one. You open the page, pick a topic and start playing.

Can a group play this quiz together?

Nothing stops you. Families often read the question aloud, let everyone say an answer, then tap the one they agree on. The explanation afterwards usually starts a conversation of its own.

Do the questions ever change between games?

The order is shuffled and the answer choices are rearranged each time, so a repeat game never follows the same path even when you choose the same topic twice.
