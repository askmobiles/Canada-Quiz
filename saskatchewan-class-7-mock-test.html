Saskatchewan Class 7 Mock Test

Thirty questions with a 30-minute timer, signs and rules mixed together. SGI does not publish a question count, a pass mark or a time limit, so this is a clearly stated practice standard of ours rather than a copy of the real test. Questions are picked at random, so every run is a new test.

📖 How to play

Tap an answer to lock it in. You will see straight away whether it was right, with a short explanation, then move on to the next question. At the end you get your score, a PASS or TRY AGAIN, and a list of every question you missed.

Remember that the real test is two tests

SGI marks the Basic test and the Sign test separately, and you rewrite only the part you fail. This mock mixes both so you get used to switching between them, but when you study, study them apart.

You may write only once per day, so an attempt you were not ready for costs you a day rather than an hour. A passed result stays valid for one year.

When you are ready to book the real one

Take this mock test three times, on different days, and look at where the misses land rather than at the score. If they cluster in signs, go back to the sign page; if they cluster in rules, go back to the rules page. Because SGI marks the two parts separately, a lopsided result here predicts a lopsided result there.

On the day: leave your phone out of the test area, because electronic devices are not allowed. If the wording of a question confuses you, ask the examiner what it means — you are allowed to, and it is better than guessing. And remember you get one attempt per day, so there is no advantage in sitting it before you are ready.

About this page

A timed practice exam for the Saskatchewan Class 7 knowledge test: thirty questions in thirty minutes, road signs and rules mixed together, drawn at random from our sixty so no two runs are the same.

SGI does not publish how many questions the test has, what the pass mark is, or how long you get. Other practice sites state those numbers confidently and contradict each other. We would rather tell you what is actually published than invent a number that decides whether somebody books a test.

At the end you get a score, a clear pass or try again, and a list of every question you missed with its explanation. Reading that list is where most of the learning happens.

What you'll find here

  • Thirty questions with a thirty-minute timer, our own stated practice standard.
  • Signs and rules mixed, the way they come at you on the day.
  • A different selection every run, drawn at random from the full bank.
  • A review list at the end of every question you got wrong, with the explanation.
  • No signup, no account, nothing saved about you.

Worth remembering

  • Time yourself honestly. Rushing the last five questions is the commonest way to lose marks you already knew.
  • A passed written result at SGI is valid for one year, so do not sit it long before you are ready to go on.
  • Because the two real tests are marked apart, note whether your misses cluster in signs or in rules.
  • Phones are not allowed in the test area, so practise without one beside you.
  • Run it more than once. A single good score can be luck; three in a row is readiness.

Common questions

How many questions are on the real Saskatchewan knowledge test?

SGI does not publish that number, and we are not going to invent one. What SGI does state is that the test comes in two parts, Basic and Sign, that they are marked separately, and that you rewrite only the part you fail.

What is the pass mark?

SGI does not publish a pass mark either. Our mock test uses a practice standard of our own so you have something to aim at, and we label it as ours rather than pretending it is the official one.

Can I take the test more than once in a day?

No. SGI allows one attempt per day. A passed written result stays valid for one year.

Where must my supervising driver sit?

In the front passenger seat, at all hours. They need a Class 5 or better licence from Canada or the United States, held for at least 365 days within the past three years.

Can I use a hands-free phone as a new driver?

No. New drivers may not use a cellphone at all, hand-held or hands-free. Hands-free is legal for fully licensed drivers, not at the learner or novice stages.
