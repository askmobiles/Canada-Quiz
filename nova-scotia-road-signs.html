Nova Scotia Road Signs

This is the half of the Nova Scotia knowledge test called the Road Sign Recognition test: twenty questions, sixteen to pass. Signs are grouped by type. Look at the pictures first, then practise the group with real test questions. No timer.

Advertisement

Read the shape and the colour first

Canadian road signs tell you a great deal before you read a single word. A red circle with a slash means you must not do the thing shown; a green circle means you may. A yellow diamond warns about something ahead, and an orange diamond means road work. Green and blue signs give directions and services.

Because this half is scored on its own, it is worth working through until the shapes are instant. You can do well on the rules and still be sent back to rewrite this one.

When the signs feel automatic, move on to the rules half, then put the two together in the timed mock test.

Where to get the official information

Everything on this page comes from what Nova Scotia publishes for free. The Nova Scotia Driver's Handbook is the book the real test is written from, and it is free to read on the province's own site. Read it alongside this practice, not instead of it.

This is an unofficial free practice site. It is not affiliated with Access Nova Scotia, the Registry of Motor Vehicles or the Government of Nova Scotia, and it cannot issue a licence. Always check the official pages for the current rules before you book.

All 30 road sign questions, with every answer explained

This is the whole question bank from the test above, written out so you can read straight through it. The right answer is marked, and every question has a short explanation of why it is the right one.

Show all 30 questions

  1. What must you do at this sign?

    • Come to a complete stop, then go when it is safeCorrect answer
    • Slow down and keep rolling
    • Stop only if another car is coming
    • Sound your horn and continue

    A full stop means the wheels stop turning. Stop at the line, or before the crosswalk if there is no line, then move off when the way is clear.

  2. What does a yield sign ask of you?

    • Let traffic and pedestrians already there go firstCorrect answer
    • Stop every time
    • Speed up to merge
    • Turn right only

    Yield means give way. Slow down, be ready to stop, and only go when you will not force anybody else to brake or swerve.

  3. You see this sign at the mouth of a street. What does it mean?

    • Do not enter, traffic comes the other wayCorrect answer
    • Entry allowed after 6 p.m.
    • Trucks only
    • Parking area ahead

    This is a one-way street facing you. Entering it puts you head-on against traffic, which is one of the most dangerous mistakes a new driver makes.

  4. What is this sign telling you?

    • Traffic on this street moves in the direction of the arrow onlyCorrect answer
    • You may drive either way
    • The road ends
    • Passing is allowed

    One way. Every lane goes the same direction, so when you turn onto it you must end up going the way the arrow points.

  5. What does this sign forbid?

    • Turning around to go back the other wayCorrect answer
    • Turning left
    • Stopping
    • Passing

    No U-turn. Turning around here is banned because drivers coming towards you cannot see you doing it in time.

  6. What does this sign mean?

    • Do not pass another vehicle hereCorrect answer
    • No parking
    • No trucks
    • No turning

    Passing is banned on this stretch, usually because you cannot see far enough ahead to get back in safely.

  7. What must you do when you see this sign?

    • Pass to the right of the island or obstacleCorrect answer
    • Stop and wait
    • Pass on either side
    • Turn around

    Keep right. There is something in the road ahead — an island, a median, a work area — and your side of it is the right side.

  8. What is not allowed where this sign is posted?

    • Stopping at all, even for a momentCorrect answer
    • Parking overnight only
    • Loading passengers only
    • Nothing, it is a warning

    No stopping is the strictest of the three. You may not stop for any reason, including to let somebody out. No parking and no standing are less strict than this.

  9. What does this sign allow you to do?

    • Stop briefly to let someone in or out, but not parkCorrect answer
    • Park for up to an hour
    • Nothing at all
    • Park only at night

    No parking still lets you stop for a moment while somebody gets in or out. If it said no stopping, even that would be banned.

  10. Who may use the lane marked by this sign?

    • Vehicles carrying the number of people shown, and busesCorrect answer
    • Anyone in a hurry
    • Trucks only
    • Nobody, it is closed

    A high-occupancy vehicle lane is for vehicles carrying at least the posted number of people. Driving alone in it is an offence.

  11. What must you do at a marked pedestrian crossover?

    • Stop and let people cross the whole way, then goCorrect answer
    • Sound your horn
    • Drive around them
    • Speed up before they step out

    Stop and wait. Do not creep forward behind somebody who is still crossing, and never pass another vehicle stopped at a crossover — there is a person you cannot see.

  12. A school bus has its red lights flashing. What must you do in Nova Scotia?

    • Stop, whichever direction you are coming from, even on a divided highwayCorrect answer
    • Stop only if you are behind it
    • Slow to 30 km/h and pass
    • Nothing, if there is a median

    Nova Scotia is stricter than most provinces here. Traffic stops from both directions, and there is no exception for a divided highway or a boulevard. Wait until the red lights stop flashing.

  13. What is this yellow sign warning you about?

    • A school bus stop is aheadCorrect answer
    • A school is closed
    • Buses are banned
    • A bus terminal

    A school bus stop ahead. Expect a bus to be stopped with children getting on or off, often just over a rise or around a bend.

  14. What is different inside a zone marked by this sign?

    • Fines for driving offences are higherCorrect answer
    • The speed limit is always 30 km/h
    • Parking is free
    • Only local traffic may enter

    This is an area where extra care is expected, usually near a school or a park, and the penalties for offences committed inside it are increased.

  15. What does this sign tell you?

    • A new speed limit starts hereCorrect answer
    • The speed limit ended
    • A speed camera is ahead
    • Slow vehicles must exit

    The posted limit changes from this point. It is not advice: the new number applies as soon as you pass the sign.

  16. What does this yellow diamond warn about?

    • The road may be slippery when wetCorrect answer
    • A car wash ahead
    • Loose gravel
    • A steep hill

    Slippery when wet. Ease off the accelerator, avoid sudden steering, and leave far more room than usual.

  17. What is ahead?

    • A bridge narrower than the road you are onCorrect answer
    • A bridge that is closed
    • A weight limit
    • A tunnel

    The bridge is narrower than the road. Slow down, check for oncoming traffic, and do not try to pass on it.

  18. What does this sign mean?

    • Traffic now comes towards you as wellCorrect answer
    • The road becomes one way
    • Two lanes join
    • A passing lane starts

    Two-way traffic ahead. A divided road is ending, so from here there is oncoming traffic on the other side of the line.

  19. What should you do when you see this sign?

    • Expect traffic joining, and adjust your speed or laneCorrect answer
    • Stop and wait
    • Speed up so nobody gets in front
    • Turn right

    Traffic is joining from the side. Merging works when both drivers adjust: match speeds and leave a gap rather than racing for the same space.

  20. What is starting ahead?

    • A divided road with a median between directionsCorrect answer
    • A tunnel
    • A toll section
    • A gravel road

    A divided highway begins. Traffic going the other way is now separated from you by a median.

  21. What is about to happen?

    • The divided road ends and traffic comes towards you againCorrect answer
    • The road widens
    • A rest area begins
    • The speed limit rises

    The median is ending. This is a common place for head-on collisions because drivers keep left out of habit.

  22. Why does this sign exist?

    • A stop sign is coming that you may not see in timeCorrect answer
    • You must stop here
    • Traffic lights ahead
    • The road is closed

    It gives you room to slow down for a stop sign hidden by a hill, a bend or trees.

  23. You see this sign on a Nova Scotia highway at dusk. What should you do?

    • Slow down and scan both shouldersCorrect answer
    • Sound your horn continuously
    • Speed up to get past the area
    • Drive on the shoulder

    A moose stands so tall that in a collision the bumper takes its legs and the body comes through the windscreen. Dawn and dusk are the worst times.

  24. What should you expect where this sign is posted?

    • Deer crossing, often more than oneCorrect answer
    • A petting zoo
    • A hunting area
    • A farm entrance

    Deer rarely travel alone. If one crosses in front of you, slow right down and expect another behind it.

  25. What does this X-shaped sign mark?

    • A railway crossingCorrect answer
    • A crossroads
    • A hospital
    • A dead end

    A railway crossing. Look both ways, and never start across unless you can clear the whole crossing without stopping.

  26. How do you drive through a roundabout?

    • Yield to traffic already in it, then go around to the rightCorrect answer
    • Stop at the entrance every time
    • Go left around the circle
    • Take the first exit always

    Yield on entry to whoever is already going round, then travel to the right, around the circle, and signal before your exit.

  27. What is this black and yellow checkerboard telling you?

    • The road ends or turns sharply hereCorrect answer
    • A construction zone
    • A taxi stand
    • A speed change

    The road you are on does not continue straight. Slow down well before it, because it is placed where drivers are most likely to keep going.

  28. What do a line of these arrow signs mark?

    • The direction a sharp curve goesCorrect answer
    • The edge of the province
    • A passing lane
    • A bus route

    Chevrons follow the outside of a bend and show you where the road goes when the headlights cannot.

  29. You see this orange sign, then a person holding a sign in the road. What must you do?

    • Obey the flagger, even over a traffic light or signCorrect answer
    • Ignore them and follow the signs
    • Drive around them
    • Stop and wait for the police

    A traffic control person at a work zone directs you, and their instruction comes before the ordinary signs and signals.

  30. What does a solid yellow line beside your lane mean?

    • Do not cross it to passCorrect answer
    • Passing is allowed
    • Parking is allowed
    • The lane is closed

    Solid means do not cross to pass. A broken yellow line on your side means passing is allowed when the way is clear.

About this page

Nova Scotia splits its knowledge test into two parts that are scored separately: a Rules of the Road test and a Road Sign Recognition test. Each has twenty multiple-choice questions, each needs sixteen right, and each is allowed thirty minutes. That is why this site keeps signs and rules as two separate study pages.

Nova Scotia publishes its whole test format, which is unusual. Several other provinces publish none of it, and on those pages we say so rather than invent a number. Here we did not have to: the numbers on this page are the province's own.

Common questions

How many questions are on the real Nova Scotia knowledge test?

Forty in total, in two tests of twenty. One is on the rules of the road and one is on road sign recognition, and you need sixteen right on each.

How long do I get?

Thirty minutes for each of the two tests.

Can I take it online?

Yes, with a desktop or laptop and a webcam. A phone or tablet will not work. You can also sit it in person at an Access Nova Scotia office. It costs $15.03.

How long must I hold the learner's licence?

Twelve full months before the road test, or nine if you finish an approved driver-training course.

Who can sit beside me while I practise?

Someone with a valid licence for the vehicle and at least two years of driving experience, who is not in the graduated programme themselves. They sit in the front passenger seat.
