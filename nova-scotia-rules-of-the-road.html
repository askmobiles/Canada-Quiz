Nova Scotia Rules of the Road

This is the half of the Nova Scotia knowledge test called the Rules of the Road test: twenty questions, sixteen to pass. Open a question to see the answer and a short explanation, then practise the whole chapter. No timer.

The school bus rule is stricter here than almost anywhere

When a school bus has its alternating red lights flashing, traffic stops from both directions. In Nova Scotia that applies even on a divided highway or a boulevard. There is no median exception. Most provinces excuse traffic on the far side of a median; this one does not.

Amber lights mean the bus is about to stop. You may still pass, carefully. Red lights mean stop and stay stopped until they go out and the bus moves off.

The other rule worth memorising is Slow Down Move Over. Pass a stopped emergency vehicle or a tow truck with its lights flashing at 60 km/h, or at the posted limit if that is lower, and move over a lane where there are two or more in your direction and it is safe. It does not apply if the vehicle is across a median from you.

Numbers worth learning by heart

  • 50 km/h is the default in a business or residential area unless a sign says otherwise.
  • 80 km/h is the general maximum outside a built-up area. Where a highway is posted higher, the sign is what counts.
  • 30 km/h in a school zone where the approaching limit is 50, and 50 where the approaching limit is higher, when children are present.
  • 60 km/h passing a stopped emergency vehicle or tow truck with its lights flashing, or the posted limit if lower.
  • Two seconds is the following distance the handbook asks for on a good road, and far more on snow or ice.
  • 145 cm tall or nine years old is when a child may stop using a booster seat, whichever comes first.

Where to get the official information

Everything on this page comes from what Nova Scotia publishes for free. The Nova Scotia Driver's Handbook is the book the real test is written from, and it is free to read on the province's own site. Read it alongside this practice, not instead of it.

This is an unofficial free practice site. It is not affiliated with Access Nova Scotia, the Registry of Motor Vehicles or the Government of Nova Scotia, and it cannot issue a licence. Always check the official pages for the current rules before you book.

All 40 rules of the road questions, with every answer explained

This is the whole question bank from the test above, written out so you can read straight through it. The right answer is marked, and every question has a short explanation of why it is the right one.

Show all 40 questions

  1. What is the first stage of Nova Scotia's graduated licensing programme called?

    • The Learner's Licence, Class 7Correct answer
    • The Newly Licensed Driver's Licence
    • The Restricted Individual licence
    • A full Class 5 licence

    Nova Scotia has three stages before a full licence: Learner's Licence (Class 7), then Newly Licensed Driver, then Restricted Individual with Condition 47.

  2. What is the youngest age you can get a Nova Scotia learner's licence?

    • 16Correct answer
    • 14
    • 18
    • 21

    Sixteen. If you are 16 or 17 you also need a parent or guardian to sign the application.

  3. How is the Nova Scotia knowledge test organised?

    • Two tests: Rules of the Road and Road Sign RecognitionCorrect answer
    • One long test
    • Three tests
    • A spoken interview only

    Two separate tests. Each has 20 multiple-choice questions and you need 16 right on each one, with 30 minutes for each.

  4. How many questions must you get right on each part of the Nova Scotia test?

    • 16 out of 20Correct answer
    • 10 out of 20
    • 20 out of 20
    • 12 out of 20

    Sixteen out of twenty on each part, which is eighty percent. You can only afford four mistakes on each side.

  5. How long do you get for each part of the knowledge test?

    • 30 minutesCorrect answer
    • 10 minutes
    • 2 hours
    • There is no time limit

    Thirty minutes for each part. Most people finish well inside that, but the clock is real, so do not linger on one question.

  6. Where can you take the Nova Scotia knowledge test?

    • Online with a webcam, or in person at Access Nova ScotiaCorrect answer
    • Only in person
    • Only online
    • At any driving school

    Both. The online test needs a desktop or laptop with a webcam, not a phone or tablet. In person, you go to Access Nova Scotia or a Registry of Motor Vehicles office.

  7. You fail the test at an office. When can you try again?

    • The next dayCorrect answer
    • The same hour
    • After one month
    • After one year

    In person you wait until the next day. If you take it online there is no waiting period before you try again, but you pay again either way.

  8. What is checked besides your knowledge before you are given a learner's licence?

    • Your eyesightCorrect answer
    • Your typing speed
    • Your school marks
    • Your height

    A vision test. Nova Scotia checks colour perception, a field of vision of at least 120 degrees, and visual acuity of at least 6/12.

  9. How long must you normally hold the learner's licence before the road test?

    • 12 months, or 9 with an approved driver-training courseCorrect answer
    • 3 months
    • 6 months always
    • 24 months

    Twelve full months, or nine if you complete an approved driver-training course. An older rule said six months; that changed in 2016.

  10. How much alcohol may a driver in the graduated programme have?

    • None at allCorrect answer
    • Under .05
    • Under .08
    • One drink only

    Zero, and it stays zero through all three stages, not only while you are a learner. That is stricter than many provinces.

  11. Who may sit beside you while you drive on a learner's licence?

    • A driver with at least two years' experience who is not in the graduated programmeCorrect answer
    • Anyone over 16
    • Any licensed driver
    • A friend with a learner's licence

    They need a valid licence for the vehicle, at least two years of experience, and they must not be a learner, a newly licensed driver or a restricted individual themselves. They sit in the front passenger seat.

  12. Where must the supervising driver sit?

    • In the front passenger seatCorrect answer
    • Anywhere in the vehicle
    • Directly behind you
    • In another car following you

    The front passenger seat, so they can see what you see and reach the wheel or the handbrake if they have to.

  13. A Newly Licensed Driver has one restriction about time of day. What is it?

    • No driving between midnight and 5 a.m.Correct answer
    • No driving before 7 a.m.
    • No driving on Sundays
    • No driving after dark at all

    No driving between midnight and five in the morning. An exemption can be granted for work, and if you are under 18 a parent must consent to it.

  14. How many demerit points will suspend a learner in Nova Scotia?

    • 4 pointsCorrect answer
    • 10 points
    • 15 points
    • 2 points

    Four points brings a six-month suspension for a learner. Two points already brings a warning letter, so the margin is very small.

  15. What is the default speed limit in a built-up area unless a sign says otherwise?

    • 50 km/hCorrect answer
    • 40 km/h
    • 60 km/h
    • 80 km/h

    Fifty in business and residential areas. Outside a built-up area the general maximum is eighty unless the road is posted differently.

  16. The road outside a built-up area has no speed sign. What is the maximum?

    • 80 km/hCorrect answer
    • 50 km/h
    • 100 km/h
    • There is no maximum

    Eighty. Where a highway is posted higher, the sign is what counts, so read the signs rather than assuming a number.

  17. In a school zone where the approaching limit is 50 km/h, what is the limit when children are present?

    • 30 km/hCorrect answer
    • 50 km/h
    • 40 km/h
    • 20 km/h

    Thirty. Where the approaching road is posted higher than fifty, the school zone limit is fifty instead.

  18. You are passing a stopped ambulance with its lights flashing. What speed applies?

    • 60 km/h, or the posted limit if it is lowerCorrect answer
    • 80 km/h
    • The normal limit
    • 30 km/h

    Slow to sixty, or to the posted limit if that is lower, and move over a lane if there are two or more in your direction and it is safe. The same applies to a tow truck with its lights on.

  19. The emergency vehicle is stopped on the other side of a divided highway. Does the slow-down rule apply?

    • No, not when a median separates youCorrect answer
    • Yes, always
    • Only at night
    • Only if you can see it

    Not when there is a median between you. On an undivided road it applies from both directions.

  20. How much space does the Nova Scotia handbook say to leave behind the vehicle ahead?

    • Two secondsCorrect answer
    • One car length
    • Half a second
    • Ten metres

    Two seconds on a good road. Pick a fixed object, and if you reach it before you finish counting, you are too close. On snow or ice you need far more.

  21. What does the law say about a hand-held cellphone while driving?

    • You may not use one, and texting is bannedCorrect answer
    • You may use it at a red light
    • You may use it below 50 km/h
    • There is no rule

    Hand-held use and texting are banned while driving. A conviction brings four demerit points as well as a fine, and the fines rise steeply for repeat offences.

  22. Who must wear a seatbelt in Nova Scotia?

    • Everyone aged 16 and over, and the driver is responsible for younger passengersCorrect answer
    • Only the driver
    • Only people in the front
    • Only on the highway

    Everyone sixteen and over must wear one where a belt is available, and the driver is responsible for every passenger under sixteen.

  23. When may a child stop using a booster seat in Nova Scotia?

    • At 145 cm tall or age nine, whichever comes firstCorrect answer
    • At age five
    • At 20 kg
    • When they ask to

    A booster is used above 18 kg until the child is 145 centimetres tall or nine years old. A seatbelt alone does not sit correctly on a smaller child.

  24. Which way should an infant's car seat face?

    • Backwards, until the child outgrows the seatCorrect answer
    • Forwards from birth
    • Sideways
    • It does not matter

    Rear-facing, from birth until the child grows past the seat's limit. A rear-facing seat spreads the force of a crash across the whole back and head.

  25. A cyclist is riding in your lane on a narrow Nova Scotia road. What should you do?

    • Stay back and pass only when you can leave plenty of roomCorrect answer
    • Sound your horn to move them
    • Squeeze past in the same lane
    • Follow closely so they hurry

    A bicycle is a vehicle and has a right to the lane. Wait for room and pass wide. Squeezing past inside the lane is how cyclists get hurt.

  26. Fog is thick on a coastal road. Which lights should you use?

    • Low beamsCorrect answer
    • High beams
    • Hazard lights while moving
    • No lights

    Low beams. High beams bounce off the fog and make it harder to see, exactly like blowing snow.

  27. What should you do before driving in winter weather?

    • Clear the glass, lights and mirrors and check the wipers and defrosterCorrect answer
    • Only clear a small hole in the windscreen
    • Nothing, the heater will do it
    • Drive slowly with the window open

    Clear all the glass, the lights and the mirrors, and make sure wipers, washers and defrosters work. You cannot avoid what you cannot see.

  28. Your brakes have been through deep water. What should you do?

    • Test them gently at low speed while it is safeCorrect answer
    • Drive faster to dry them
    • Nothing, they are fine
    • Pump the accelerator

    Wet brakes grip poorly. Test them lightly at low speed where nobody is behind you, and keep testing until they feel normal again.

  29. You are in a collision where somebody is hurt. What must you do first?

    • Stop, and call for helpCorrect answer
    • Drive to the nearest garage
    • Exchange numbers and leave
    • Move all vehicles immediately

    Stop. Leaving the scene of a collision is a serious offence. Call for help, and do not move an injured person unless they are in danger where they are.

  30. A moose steps onto the highway in front of you. What is the safest response?

    • Brake hard and steer for where the animal came fromCorrect answer
    • Swerve into the oncoming lane
    • Accelerate past it
    • Close your eyes and hold the wheel

    Brake as hard as you can and aim behind the animal, where it has already been. Swerving into oncoming traffic turns one problem into a worse one.

  31. What are the three stages of the Nova Scotia graduated programme?

    • Learner, Newly Licensed and Restricted IndividualCorrect answer
    • L, N and full
    • G1, G2 and G
    • Level 1 and Level 2 only

    Nova Scotia has an extra stage most provinces do not: after Newly Licensed comes Restricted Individual, which still carries conditions.

  32. Who must have zero alcohol in Nova Scotia?

    • Learners, newly licensed AND restricted driversCorrect answer
    • Learners only
    • Drivers under 19
    • Learners and newly licensed drivers

    Zero alcohol runs right through all three stages, including the Restricted Individual stage that comes after the newly licensed years. Any detectable alcohol means a six-month suspension.

  33. How many demerit points suspend a Nova Scotia learner?

    • 4Correct answer
    • 2
    • 6
    • 10

    A learner is suspended at 4 points, a newly licensed driver at 6, and a fully licensed driver at 10 — each for six months.

  34. Why is one phone ticket so serious for a Nova Scotia learner?

    • It carries 4 demerit points, and a learner is suspended at 4Correct answer
    • It doubles the insurance premium
    • It adds a year to the learner stage
    • It carries a $1,000 fine

    A hand-held phone offence is 4 demerit points, and a Nova Scotia learner is suspended at 4. One ticket ends the learner's licence outright.

  35. What is the speed limit on any Nova Scotia highway with no posted sign?

    • 80 km/hCorrect answer
    • 90 km/h
    • 100 km/h
    • 50 km/h

    Unless otherwise posted the maximum on any Nova Scotia highway is 80 km/h. In business and residential districts it is 50 km/h.

  36. When does a Nova Scotia school zone speed limit apply?

    • Whenever children are present, any time of day, all yearCorrect answer
    • School mornings only
    • 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. on school days
    • Only in September

    Nova Scotia ties the school zone to children being present rather than to clock hours, so it applies any time of day, 365 days a year.

  37. Who may ride in the front with a Nova Scotia newly licensed driver?

    • One passengerCorrect answer
    • Nobody
    • Two passengers
    • Anyone, if belted

    Only one passenger may sit in the front. Rear passengers are limited to the number of working seat belts.

  38. What must a Nova Scotia newly licensed driver do to reach the next stage?

    • Complete a driver improvement courseCorrect answer
    • Simply wait two years
    • Pass a written test
    • Buy winter tires

    Waiting is not enough. Nova Scotia requires a driver improvement course — a second course after the one many take to shorten the learner stage.

  39. When are studded tires allowed in Nova Scotia?

    • 15 October to 30 AprilCorrect answer
    • All year
    • 1 November to 1 May
    • They are banned

    Studded tires may be used from 15 October to 30 April. Winter tires are recommended but not required by Nova Scotia law.

  40. When may a Nova Scotia newly licensed driver not drive?

    • Midnight to 5 a.m., unless supervised or exempt for workCorrect answer
    • 10 p.m. to 6 a.m.
    • There is no restriction
    • Only on holidays

    The midnight to 5 a.m. restriction can be lifted by a supervising driver in the front seat or by a written employment exemption. Unlike some provinces it is not limited by age.

About this page

Nova Scotia splits its knowledge test into two parts that are scored separately: a Rules of the Road test and a Road Sign Recognition test. Each has twenty multiple-choice questions, each needs sixteen right, and each is allowed thirty minutes. That is why this site keeps signs and rules as two separate study pages.

Nova Scotia publishes its whole test format, which is unusual. Several other provinces publish none of it, and on those pages we say so rather than invent a number. Here we did not have to: the numbers on this page are the province's own.

Common questions

How many questions are on the real Nova Scotia knowledge test?

Forty in total, in two tests of twenty. One is on the rules of the road and one is on road sign recognition, and you need sixteen right on each.

How long do I get?

Thirty minutes for each of the two tests.

Can I take it online?

Yes, with a desktop or laptop and a webcam. A phone or tablet will not work. You can also sit it in person at an Access Nova Scotia office. It costs $15.03.

How long must I hold the learner's licence?

Twelve full months before the road test, or nine if you finish an approved driver-training course.

Who can sit beside me while I practise?

Someone with a valid licence for the vehicle and at least two years of driving experience, who is not in the graduated programme themselves. They sit in the front passenger seat.
