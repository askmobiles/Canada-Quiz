🎯 Carrom

Flick the striker, pocket the men, and take the red queen. Play on your own against the computer, or pass one screen around with up to four people.

📖 How to play

Slide the bar under the board to move your striker along your own line. Then press on the board and pull backwards, like a slingshot — a longer pull means a harder shot. Let go to fire.

Every carrom man you pocket is worth 1 point, and the red queen is worth 3. Pocket something and you get another shot. If your striker falls in a pocket, you lose a point and one of your men goes back to the middle.

When the board is empty, the highest score wins.

One player is you against the computer. Two, three or four players share one screen — no computer.

Carrom in one minute

Carrom is played all over India, Sri Lanka, Bangladesh, Pakistan and Nepal, and wherever families from those countries have settled — which is a great many kitchen tables in Canada. It is sometimes called finger billiards, and that is a fair description: a smooth wooden board, four corner pockets, and small flat discs you flick with one finger.

A real board has nine white men, nine black men and one red disc called the queen. On this screen the men are all worth the same, so anybody can play without learning whose colour is whose — good for a family where one person has played all their life and another has never touched a board.

The one piece of real carrom we kept is the queen. She is the red one in the middle, she is worth three, and everybody wants her.

Playing with one, two, three or four

Choose how many people are playing before you start. With one player you are up against the computer, which takes its own shots after yours. With two, three or four players nobody plays the computer — you simply pass the phone or tablet to the next person when your turn ends.

Each player shoots from their own side of the board. With two players that is the bottom and the top; with three or four the sides come in as well, and the board tells you whose line is live by lighting it up.

Because scoring is by points rather than by colour, a game with three players works exactly as well as a game with four, which is not true of a real board.

About this page

Carrom is a board game played across South Asia and wherever families from there have settled. A smooth board, four corner pockets, small flat discs called men, and a heavier disc called the striker that you flick with one finger. This version plays on a phone, a tablet or a computer, with no signup and nothing to install.

One player takes on the computer. Two, three or four players share one screen and pass it around, with no computer involved. Every man is worth a point, the red queen is worth three, and the highest score when the board is empty wins.

Common questions

Can four people play carrom on one phone?

Yes. Choose 4 players before you start and each person gets their own side of the board. Pass the phone along when your turn ends. The computer only joins in when one person is playing alone.

How do I shoot?

Move your striker along your line with the slider, then press on the board and pull backwards like a slingshot. The further you pull, the harder the shot. Let go to fire.

What is the red disc worth?

The red disc is the queen and she is worth three points instead of one. She starts in the middle of the board.

What happens if my striker goes in a pocket?

You lose one point and one pocketed man goes back to the middle of the board. Your turn then passes to the next player.

Is the carrom computer hard to beat?

It aims properly at the easiest pocket and it goes for the queen when it can, but it misses on purpose now and then. A careful player beats it.
