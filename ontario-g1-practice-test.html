What the real G1 test looks like

You need 80 percent to pass. That is the number to aim at, it is DriveTest's own, and it is the one thing worth memorising before you go. On a section of twenty questions, 80 percent is sixteen right, so our mock test asks for sixteen out of twenty in each half.

The test is split into sections, normally one on road signs and one on the rules of the road, and they are marked separately. If you fail, you rewrite only the sections you failed, not the whole test. That is why this site keeps signs and rules as two study pages.

Two other things DriveTest states. The real test is not timed, so take as long as you need; most people finish in twenty to thirty minutes. And the number of questions is not published anywhere we could find, which is why you will not see one stated on this site even though almost every other practice site states one. Our mock runs forty questions against a 30-minute clock because practising under a little pressure helps, not because the real test does.

You take the test at a DriveTest centre, with an eye test and your identification, and no appointment is needed. Once you pass, you hold the G1 licence for twelve months before your first road test, or eight months if you finish an approved driving course.

How to pass on your first try

Read the official handbook once from start to finish, then use practice questions to find your weak spots. Most people fail on the signs half, not the rules half, because there are a lot of signs and they look similar. Study the signs in small groups rather than all at once, and pay attention to shape and colour: a red circle with a line through it always means you must not do something, a yellow diamond always warns you about what is coming, and an orange sign always means road work.

Take the mock test more than once. The questions are picked at random each time, so you will see a different test on every run. When you can pass three times in a row, you are ready to book the real one.

Road Signs

6 of 120

  1. What must you do at this sign, even when the road looks empty?

    • Come to a full stop every time, then go when it is safeCorrect answer
    • Slow down and roll through if nothing is coming
    • Stop only during busy hours
    • Stop only if a pedestrian is waiting

    A full stop is needed every time. Stop at the line, look both ways, and move ahead only when the road is clear.

  2. What is the best thing to do when you see this sign?

    • Move into the open lane early, before the closed lane runs outCorrect answer
    • Drive to the very end of the closing lane and push your way in
    • Stop and wait for someone to wave you through
    • Use the shoulder to get past the line of cars

    Merging early keeps traffic moving and avoids a last-second squeeze. Signal, check your mirrors and blind spot, then move over.

  3. You see this sign on a quiet street. What does it tell you?

    • Children play nearby, so drive slowly and be ready for one to run onto the roadCorrect answer
    • Children are not allowed to cross here
    • The street ahead is closed to cars
    • You may park here while children play

    A ball rolling onto the road is often followed by a child. Keep your foot near the brake, hold your speed down and look between parked cars. This warning applies every day, not only during school hours.

  4. You reach this sign and a person is walking across. When may you drive on?

    • Once they are completely across and back on the sidewalkCorrect answer
    • As soon as they are out of your lane
    • Right away, if you steer around them
    • As soon as they pass the middle of the road

    At a pedestrian crossover you must wait until the person is all the way across and safely on the sidewalk. Not yielding here costs up to $1,000 and four demerit points.

  5. What is this black and yellow striped marker telling you?

    • Pass on the side the stripes point down towardCorrect answer
    • Stop completely
    • This lane is for buses
    • Parking is allowed here

    The stripes slope down toward the side you should pass on. It marks an object close to the road, such as a bridge support or an island.

  6. A person has stepped onto these painted lines. What must you do?

    • Yield and wait for them to finish crossingCorrect answer
    • Drive around behind them
    • Flash your lights and continue
    • Reverse

    Painted crosswalk lines mark where people cross. Once someone has stepped on, you wait — turning across in front of them is both dangerous and an offence.

Rules of the Road

6 of 130

  1. While you drive with a G1 licence, how much alcohol may the accompanying driver beside you have in their blood?

    • Below .05Correct answer
    • Zero, the same as your own limit
    • Below .08
    • No limit applies to them

    The accompanying driver must stay below .05. They also need a full class G licence with at least four years of driving experience, and they must sit in the front passenger seat. Your own limit as a G1 driver stays at zero.

  2. How can you tell whether the driver of a large truck can see you?

    • If you can see the driver's face in the truck's side mirrorCorrect answer
    • If you can see the trailer
    • If your headlights are on
    • If you are within two car lengths

    Large trucks have long blind spots on both sides, in front and behind. If you cannot see the driver's face in the mirror, the driver cannot see you, so do not stay there.

  3. What is the criminal blood alcohol limit for driving in Canada?

    • 0.08Correct answer
    • 0.02
    • 0.04
    • 0.06

    At 0.08 or more you can be charged under the Criminal Code. In Ontario that also brings an immediate 90-day licence suspension and a 7-day vehicle impoundment before you ever see a courtroom.

  4. What blood alcohol level must every driver aged 21 and under have, no matter which licence they hold?

    • ZeroCorrect answer
    • There is no special rule
    • Under .05
    • Under .08

    Zero, for every driver 21 and under regardless of licence class, and zero for every G1 and G2 driver at any age.

  5. At what speed over the limit does a charge move from ordinary speeding into stunt driving territory in an 80 km/h or lower zone?

    • 40 km/h or more overCorrect answer
    • 20 km/h over
    • 60 km/h over
    • 10 km/h over

    On roads posted under 80 km/h it is 40 km/h or more over. On faster roads the threshold is 50 km/h over, and both bring six demerit points.

  6. What must you carry with you whenever you drive in Ontario?

    • Your licence, the vehicle permit and proof of insuranceCorrect answer
    • Only your licence
    • Only proof of insurance
    • Nothing, records are electronic

    All three: driver's licence, vehicle permit (ownership) and the insurance card. An officer may ask for each one at any stop.
