Sweet Water, Boiling Pots, and the Great Syrup Robbery

Maple syrup starts out as water that runs inside a tree, and it once got stolen by the barrel-load.

Water that runs inside a tree

All winter a sugar maple sleeps with food stored inside it. Then late in winter something very particular has to happen: freezing nights, and days warm enough to thaw.

That freeze and thaw makes the tree's sap move. Sap is not syrup. It is thin and clear, like slightly sweet water, and if you tasted it you might not even notice the sugar.

A farmer drills a small hole and puts in a spout, and the sap drips out into a bucket or runs down a pipe. A healthy tree does not mind. Only a small part of its sap is taken, and the same trees are tapped year after year for a lifetime.

The whole season is short. A few weeks, and it is over until next year.

Forty buckets to make one bottle

Now the sap is boiled, usually in a long flat pan in a little wooden building called a sugar shack. Steam pours out of the roof for hours.

Boiling drives the water off and leaves the sugar behind. This is the number worth remembering: it takes about 40 litres of sap to make 1 litre of syrup.

So the bottle on your breakfast table started out as forty bottles of tree water, and somebody stood over a hot pan while thirty-nine of them turned into steam.

Quebec makes around seventy percent of all the maple syrup in the world. Not seventy percent of Canada's: seventy percent of the whole planet's.

A warehouse full of barrels

Because a bad year would leave everybody short, Quebec's syrup producers keep an enormous store of syrup in barrels in a warehouse. It is a bit like a piggy bank for a whole industry.

In 2011 and 2012, thieves worked out how to steal from it. They took barrels out, emptied the syrup, and put the barrels back filled with water.

A barrel of water and a barrel of syrup look exactly the same from the outside. Nobody noticed for months.

When somebody finally climbed up to check the barrels in the summer of 2012, one wobbled. It was full of water. About 2,700 tonnes of syrup were gone, worth close to eighteen million dollars.

The police caught them. The man who organised it went to prison for nearly eight years, and a few hundred tonnes of syrup were found and brought back.

Words to know

Sap
The watery juice that moves inside a tree, carrying its food.
Tap
To drill a small hole in a maple and fit a spout so the sap can drip out.
Sugar shack
The little building where the sap is boiled down into syrup.
Did you know? The thieves were caught partly because a barrel wobbled when somebody leaned on it. Syrup is heavy. Water is not heavy enough.
