About this quiz

This quiz asks about the world around you: the planets overhead, the animals in the woods, the body you live in, and the machines and weather that fill an ordinary day. Choose a topic, answer ten questions, and see how much you really know.

It suits curious kids, families sharing a screen after supper, and teachers who want a quick warm-up. Every answer comes with a short explanation, so even a wrong guess teaches you something before the next question arrives.

What's inside

  • Six topics to choose from: Space and Planets, Animals and Nature, The Human Body, How Things Work, Weather and Earth, and Numbers and Math.
  • Each round gives you ten questions with four answer choices, and a single tap locks in your answer.
  • A short explanation appears the moment you answer, telling you why the right answer is right.
  • A question counter and a progress bar show where you are and how many points you have collected.
  • The page remembers which questions you have already seen, so playing a topic again brings different ones.
  • At the end you get your score out of ten and can pick another topic straight away.

Sample questions

  1. Why does the Moon appear to shine?

    Answer: It reflects sunlight

    The Moon makes no light of its own; it bounces sunlight back to us.

  2. Which tree keeps its green needles all winter?

    Answer: Pine

    Pines are evergreens, so they stay green while maples and birches lose their leaves.

  3. How many senses do people traditionally count?

    Answer: Five

    The five classic senses are sight, hearing, smell, taste and touch.

  4. What makes sound?

    Answer: Vibrations

    Sound is made by vibrations travelling through air, water or solids.

  5. Which instrument measures air pressure?

    Answer: Barometer

    When a barometer reading falls, clouds and rain are often on the way.

Good to know

  • Sunlight takes roughly eight minutes to cross the space between the Sun and Earth, so you always see the Sun as it looked minutes ago.
  • Venus, not Mercury, is the hottest planet. Its thick clouds trap heat like a heavy blanket, even though Mercury sits closer to the Sun.
  • An octopus has three hearts and blue blood, which is one of the odder surprises hiding in the animals and nature round.
  • The Canadarm was a robotic arm built in Canada for the space shuttle, and Canadian astronaut Chris Hadfield later filmed and sang from the space station.
  • A light-year sounds like a length of time but actually measures distance: how far light travels in a single year.

Common questions

How long does one round of ten science questions take?

Most people finish in about four or five minutes. You can stop whenever you like, since nothing is submitted anywhere and closing the page costs you nothing but that round.

Is this quiz written for children or for adults?

The questions suit roughly age eight and up, and plenty of grown-ups still miss a few. Parents often play beside younger children and read the explanations out loud together.

Can I play the same topic more than once?

Yes, and it is worth doing. The page keeps track of the questions you have answered and offers unseen ones first, so a second run through space and planets feels new.

Do I need to sign in or install anything first?

No. The quiz runs in the browser you already have, on a phone, a tablet or a computer. There is no account to make, no download, and no email address to type.
