What Yukon publishes, and what it does not

Yukon publishes its pass mark, which many places do not. The Motor Vehicles Regulations set it at 80 percent, and the same line says that figure applies to every class of licence, from a Class 7 learner to a commercial licence. The regulations also describe what you sit as one combined written and sign recognition test, a single test covering both, rather than two exams taken one after the other.

What Yukon does not publish is how many questions the test has or how long you get. The regulations leave the content of the basic test to the registrar and set no count for it, and no page on yukon.ca gives one either. So the question count on our mock test, the timer on it and our decision to split the practice into a signs half and a rules half are all our own. One commercial site states that the exam comes in two parts of twenty questions each; nothing in the regulation supports that, and the regulation describes a single combined test.

The rest of the process is published plainly and is worth knowing. The test costs twenty dollars, you sit it in person at a Motor Vehicles office, and you are told whether you passed right away. If it does not go your way you may take it again as early as the following day, and you pay another twenty dollars for that attempt. Yukon's online service covers booking a road test, not the written test.

Study the rules

The three stages, one at a time

You start at 15, which is younger than most of the country allows. Pass the written test and a vision test and you hold a Class 7 learner licence, the first stage of Yukon's graduated programme. You keep it for at least six months and you drive at least fifty hours with a co-driver before you may apply to move up.

Those fifty hours are not an honour system. They are recorded on the official GDL Driver Record and Declaration form and co-signed by a parent, a guardian or another valid co-driver, and the form goes in with your application. Start filling it in from your first drive rather than reconstructing it six months later.

The second stage is the Class 5 Novice licence, which you reach at 16 at the earliest and only after a road test. You hold it for at least eighteen months before a full Class 5. Yukon publishes no driver-education course that shortens either stage, so do not count on one. It does publish the opposite: if you commit a graduated licensing violation, the time you have already held the licence does not count towards moving up, and the clock starts again.

The rules that catch learners out

The co-driver rule has a Yukon twist that people miss. Your co-driver must have held at least a Class 5 licence for two years and must be with you every time you drive, which is ordinary enough. What is not ordinary is that the co-driver must have no alcohol in their blood either. The zero-alcohol rule binds the person in the passenger seat exactly as it binds you.

The learner restrictions themselves are specific. You may drive only between five in the morning and midnight. You may carry no more than one passenger besides your co-driver, unless the extra passengers are a parent, a grandparent, a brother, a sister or a child. You may drive only a car or light truck of up to 4,000 kilograms registered gross vehicle weight with no more than seven seats including the driver, and that seat limit rises to nine at the novice stage. An L sign goes on the back of the vehicle every time you drive, where drivers coming up behind you can see it.

The phone rule is stricter here than most people assume. A learner and a novice driver may not use any electronic device at all, and that includes a hands-free set-up: only full licence holders may use a device set up and used hands-free. Distracted driving carries a fine of up to six hundred dollars and three demerit points, and for a graduated licensing driver three points is nearly half the way to a suspension.

Where to get the official information

Everything on this page comes from what Yukon publishes for free. The territory writes its own book, the Yukon Driver's Basic Handbook, and it is free to download from yukon.ca, so any site telling you that Yukon borrows another jurisdiction's handbook is simply wrong. There is an official French edition as well, the Guide de la route du Yukon. Whether the test itself can be sat in French is not published anywhere, even though the handbook and the licensing pages are bilingual, so we do not claim that it can. Read the handbook alongside this practice, not instead of it.

This is an unofficial free practice site. It is not affiliated with Yukon Motor Vehicles, with Highways and Public Works or with the Government of Yukon, and it cannot issue a licence. Always check the official pages for the current rules and fees before you book.

Road Sign Recognition

30 questions

  1. You reach this sign at a Yukon intersection. What does it require?

    • A complete stop, then go on only when the way is clearCorrect answer
    • A rolling stop if nothing is coming
    • Stopping only when a pedestrian is waiting
    • Flashing your headlights and carrying on

    A stop means the wheels stop turning. Stop at the stop line, or before the crosswalk if none is painted, and move off only when you can do it without forcing anyone else to slow down.

  2. What does this red and white triangle require of you?

    • Give way to the traffic and pedestrians already using the roadCorrect answer
    • Stop and stay stopped until someone waves you on
    • Speed up so you fit into the gap
    • Turn right without checking

    Yield means give way. Slow right down, be ready to stop, and join only when you will not make another driver or a pedestrian change what they are doing.

  3. This sign faces you at the mouth of a street. What does it mean?

    • You must not enter, because traffic here comes towards youCorrect answer
    • Local traffic only
    • Entry allowed outside busy hours
    • Heavy vehicles must find another route

    The street runs the other way. Driving past this sign puts you head-on into traffic that has no reason to expect you.

  4. A sign like this is posted beside a Yukon road. What does the number mean?

    • The highest speed allowed, and only when conditions are goodCorrect answer
    • A speed suggested only for good weather
    • The slowest speed you may legally travel
    • An average speed to hold over the whole trip

    A posted limit is a ceiling, never a target. Yukon states that speed limits are set for optimal road conditions, so on gravel, frost heaves, ice or poor visibility the safe speed is lower than the number on the sign.

  5. What does this regulatory sign prohibit?

    • Passing another vehicle along this stretchCorrect answer
    • Stopping on the shoulder
    • Parking overnight
    • Towing a trailer

    Passing is banned here, normally because you cannot see far enough ahead to pull back in safely. On a northern highway that view can also be blocked by dust or blowing snow.

  6. Where this sign is posted, what are you still allowed to do?

    • Stop briefly while somebody gets in or outCorrect answer
    • Leave the vehicle for a few minutes
    • Park if you switch on your hazard lights
    • Nothing at all is permitted here

    No parking still lets you pause while a passenger gets in or out. It is the sign that bans stopping altogether that takes even that away.

  7. This sign goes further than a no-parking sign. In what way?

    • It bans stopping for any reason, even to let someone outCorrect answer
    • It applies only during the winter months
    • It applies only to trucks
    • It bans parking but still allows loading

    This is the strictest of the stopping signs. You may not halt here at all, so plan to drop a passenger somewhere else.

  8. A traffic island lies ahead with this sign on it. What must you do?

    • Pass to the right of the islandCorrect answer
    • Pass on whichever side is clear
    • Stop before the island and wait
    • Reverse and find another route

    Keep right of the island. The sign is placed where the road splits around something, and your half of the road is the right-hand half.

  9. Which manoeuvre does this sign forbid?

    • Turning around to head back the way you cameCorrect answer
    • Turning left at the next street
    • Reversing into a driveway
    • Passing a slower vehicle

    No U-turn. Turning right around is banned here because oncoming drivers cannot see what you are doing early enough to react.

  10. This yellow diamond stands beside a Yukon highway. What is it telling you?

    • Deer may be on or near the road aheadCorrect answer
    • Hunting is permitted beside the road
    • A wildlife viewing area is ahead
    • Livestock may be loose on the road

    The handbook uses this sign to mean watch for deer ahead. Ease off, keep scanning both shoulders and be ready for an animal to step out where you cannot see far.

  11. What hazard does this warning sign describe?

    • Rock may fall onto the road, and rock may already be lying on itCorrect answer
    • A quarry entrance is ahead
    • Loose gravel has just been spread on the surface
    • A rock crusher is working nearby

    The handbook wording is to beware of falling rock and to watch for rock already on the road ahead. Keep your eyes well down the road rather than on the cliff face.

  12. You meet this sign on a northern highway. What is ahead?

    • The paved surface ends and the road turns to gravelCorrect answer
    • A bridge deck
    • A closed section of road
    • A weigh station

    Gravel gives less grip than pavement, and Yukon warns that stopping distances get longer on it. Slow down before the surface changes and leave far more room to the vehicle ahead.

  13. What is this warning sign telling you about the road surface?

    • The surface is uneven, so slow down before you reach itCorrect answer
    • A speed hump has been built to slow traffic
    • A cattle guard crosses the road
    • Road work is under way

    Yukon roads built over permafrost can settle into waves and dips that the territory describes as feeling like a roller coaster. Take your speed off before the rough part, not in the middle of it.

  14. What is this yellow diamond warning you to expect?

    • The road can turn slippery, so brake and steer gentlyCorrect answer
    • A car wash entrance
    • A river crossing
    • A section closed each winter

    Grip can disappear without warning on a wet or icy surface. Smooth inputs keep the tires working; sudden braking or steering is what breaks them loose.

  15. You meet this sign at the top of a long grade. What is the safe way down?

    • Pick a lower gear before the descent and hold your speed downCorrect answer
    • Coast down in neutral to save fuel
    • Speed up so you do not lose momentum
    • Run your hazard lights the whole way down

    A lower gear lets the engine do part of the braking. Riding the brakes the whole way down heats them until they stop working well, which matters most with a loaded vehicle.

  16. What does this yellow sign say about the road in front of you?

    • There is a series of bends, so lose speed before the first oneCorrect answer
    • The road ends shortly
    • There is one sharp bend and then it straightens
    • The road narrows to a single lane

    Braking is for the straight before the bend, not for the bend itself. Set your speed early, then steer smoothly through each curve.

  17. This round yellow sign appears beside the road. What is coming up?

    • A railway crossingCorrect answer
    • A roundabout
    • A rest area
    • A tunnel entrance

    A railway crossing is coming. Slow down, look and listen both ways, and never start across unless you can clear the far side without stopping.

  18. This sign marks the start of what?

    • A school zone, where you must drop to 30 km/hCorrect answer
    • A bus terminal
    • A playground with no road beside it
    • A crossing used only in summer

    Yukon asks drivers to reduce speed to 30 kilometres an hour in school zones. Children step out from between parked cars, and 30 is the speed that leaves you time to stop.

  19. What does this diamond warn you about?

    • A bridge narrower than the road you are onCorrect answer
    • A bridge closed to all traffic
    • A ferry landing
    • A culvert with a weight restriction

    The bridge is tighter than the road leading to it. Check for oncoming traffic, hold your lane position and never try to pass on the structure.

  20. An orange diamond like this stands beside the highway. What does the orange colour mean?

    • The conditions are temporary, such as road work aheadCorrect answer
    • The warning is permanent
    • The sign applies only after dark
    • The sign marks a scenic route

    Orange means temporary. Work zones on northern highways often involve loose gravel and equipment crossing the road, so the posted work-zone speed applies whether or not you can see a crew.

  21. What does this orange sign tell you to expect?

    • A traffic control person directing traffic aheadCorrect answer
    • A police checkpoint
    • A weigh scale
    • A closed rest area

    Someone will be controlling traffic through the work zone. Slow right down, be ready to stop, and do exactly what they signal, even when the road ahead looks empty.

  22. What is this orange sign asking you to do?

    • Follow the marked route around a closed piece of roadCorrect answer
    • Stop and turn back the way you came
    • Use the shoulder to get past the work
    • Report to the work crew before continuing

    A detour sends you along a signed alternative. Keep following the detour markers rather than guessing a shortcut, because the side roads may be gravel or dead ends.

  23. What service does this blue sign point you to?

    • A hospitalCorrect answer
    • A pharmacy
    • A first-aid post at a rest stop
    • A veterinary clinic

    Blue signs point to services rather than telling you a rule. Knowing where the hospital sign is on your route matters more in a territory where the next community can be far away.

  24. What is this blue information sign telling you?

    • Fuel is available at the turn or exit aheadCorrect answer
    • A vehicle inspection station is ahead
    • A truck route begins here
    • Parking here is free

    On long Yukon highways the gap between fuel stops can be very large, so fill up when you get the chance. The territory also suggests carrying extra fuel when you travel in remote areas.

  25. What is this blue sign showing you?

    • Food services are available aheadCorrect answer
    • A picnic shelter with no services
    • A grocery store only
    • A weigh station

    The symbol marks a place to eat. On a long northern drive, planning stops around fuel and food is part of preparing the trip, not a luxury.

  26. This sign is painted on a parking space outside a store. What does it reserve the space for?

    • Only a vehicle displaying a valid accessible parking permitCorrect answer
    • Anyone stopping for less than five minutes
    • Delivery vehicles
    • Anyone, once the business has closed

    The space is reserved for a permit holder. Taking it, even briefly, can leave someone with no way to reach the door.

  27. The light turns amber as you come up to the intersection. What does that require?

    • Stop if you can do it safely, and carry on only if stopping would be dangerousCorrect answer
    • Always stop, whatever your speed
    • Always continue through
    • Sound your horn and keep going

    Amber means the red is coming. The decision is made by where you already are: close enough that hard braking would be dangerous, keep going; far enough back, stop.

  28. A signal at an intersection is flashing red. What does it require of you?

    • Come to a full stop, then go when the way is clearCorrect answer
    • Treat it exactly like a green light
    • Slow down but keep rolling through
    • Stop and wait there until it turns green

    A flashing red works like a stop sign. Stop completely, look both ways, and go only when the intersection is clear. It will not change colour.

  29. You are behind a slow truck and a double solid yellow line separates you from the oncoming lane. What does that line mean?

    • Traffic in each direction must stay on its own side and must not cross to passCorrect answer
    • You may cross it because the truck is holding up traffic
    • You may cross it in daylight only
    • It marks the outer edge of the travelled road

    Double solid yellow lines separate opposing traffic where passing is not safe for either side. Wait for a passing lane or a broken line rather than gambling on the view ahead.

  30. The centre line beside your lane is a broken yellow line. What does that allow?

    • Passing when the way ahead is clear and it is safe to do itCorrect answer
    • Parking along the centre of the road
    • Driving in either lane whenever you like
    • Passing, but only for large vehicles

    A broken yellow line permits passing; it does not promise that passing is a good idea. You still need clear sight distance, a gap long enough to get back in, and a surface with enough grip.

Rules of the Road

30 questions

  1. What is the youngest age at which you can apply for a Yukon learner licence?

    • 15Correct answer
    • 14
    • 16
    • 17

    Yukon sets 15 as the minimum age for a Class 7 learner licence. You must be at least 16 before you can move up to the Class 5 Novice licence.

  2. Before a Yukon learner can move on to the next stage, what does the programme ask for?

    • At least six months holding the learner licence and 50 hours of drivingCorrect answer
    • Three months and 20 hours of driving
    • One full year and 100 hours of driving
    • No minimum period, only a passing road test

    The learner licence must be held continuously for six months, and 50 hours of driving must be completed with a co-driver before you apply for the road test.

  3. Where are those practice driving hours written down?

    • On the GDL Driver Record and Declaration, signed by you and your co-driverCorrect answer
    • In any notebook you keep for yourself
    • On the back of the learner licence
    • On a form that only a driving school may complete

    Yukon publishes the GDL Driver Record and Declaration for exactly this purpose. A parent, guardian or other valid co-driver signs it, so fill it in as you go rather than the night before you apply.

  4. How long must the novice stage be held before you can apply for a full Class 5 licence in Yukon?

    • Eighteen monthsCorrect answer
    • Six months
    • Twelve months
    • Three years

    The Class 5 Novice licence must be held continuously for eighteen months before the application for a full licence. Counting from the day you started your learner licence, the whole programme takes a minimum of two years.

  5. Who is allowed to sit beside you as your co-driver while you hold a Yukon learner licence?

    • Someone who has held at least a Class 5 licence for two years or moreCorrect answer
    • Any adult with a valid licence of any class
    • Any driver over the age of 25
    • Anyone who has passed a road test at some point

    The co-driver must hold a valid licence of at least Class 5, and must have held it for at least two years. They have to be with you every single time you drive.

  6. Your co-driver has had one beer with dinner and feels completely normal. May they supervise you?

    • No, because your co-driver must have no alcohol in their bloodCorrect answer
    • Yes, as long as they stay under the criminal limit
    • Yes, if the drive is short
    • Yes, if you keep to quiet streets

    Yukon's zero tolerance binds the supervisor as well as the learner. The co-driver must have no alcohol in their blood and must not be impaired by drugs or any other substance.

  7. During which hours may a Yukon learner drive?

    • Between 5 a.m. and midnightCorrect answer
    • Any hour, provided the co-driver is present
    • In daylight only
    • Between 6 a.m. and 10 p.m.

    The learner stage carries a curfew: driving is allowed between 5 a.m. and midnight. A co-driver beside you does not lift it.

  8. Apart from your co-driver, how many passengers may a Yukon learner carry?

    • One, unless the additional passengers are a parent, grandparent, brother, sister or childCorrect answer
    • None at all
    • Two, with no exceptions
    • One for every working seat belt

    The limit is one passenger in addition to the co-driver. Close family members named in the rule do not count against that limit, so a full car of siblings is allowed while a full car of friends is not.

  9. What sort of vehicle may a Yukon learner drive?

    • A car or light truck up to 4,000 kilograms registered gross vehicle weight, seating no more than seven including the driverCorrect answer
    • Any vehicle the co-driver is licensed for
    • Any vehicle under 10,000 kilograms
    • A passenger van of any size

    The weight cap stays at 4,000 kilograms at the novice stage, but the seating limit rises to nine including the driver. Learners and novice drivers may not tow a trailer.

  10. What has to be shown on the vehicle every time a Yukon learner drives?

    • An L sign on the back, where drivers behind you can see itCorrect answer
    • A green sticker on the windshield
    • A sign on each front door
    • Nothing extra, the licence in your pocket is enough

    The learner L sign goes on the rear of the vehicle every time you drive, visible to drivers approaching from behind. It tells the traffic behind you to expect a driver who is still learning.

  11. What score does Yukon require to pass the knowledge test for a driver's licence?

    • 80 per centCorrect answer
    • 70 per cent
    • 85 per cent
    • 90 per cent

    The Motor Vehicles Regulations set a passing score of 80 per cent, and the same figure applies to every class of licence. The regulations describe a single combined written and sign-recognition test rather than two separate exams.

  12. You do not pass the Yukon knowledge test. When can you try again, and what does it cost?

    • As early as the following day, for another 20 dollarsCorrect answer
    • After seven days, at no charge
    • After thirty days, for 20 dollars
    • Only once more, and only on the same day

    The test fee is 20 dollars, and a failed attempt can be repeated as early as the next day for another 20 dollars. Coming back the following morning with the handbook read properly is cheaper than guessing twice.

  13. What else is checked when you apply for a Yukon driver's licence?

    • Your eyesightCorrect answer
    • Your hearing
    • A short drive around the parking lot
    • A first-aid certificate

    A vision test is part of the process. If you do not pass it you are sent for further testing by an optometrist or ophthalmologist, and if you need glasses or contact lenses to meet the standard, that condition is recorded on your licence.

  14. How many demerit points bring a suspension for a driver in the graduated licensing programme in Yukon?

    • SevenCorrect answer
    • Five
    • Ten
    • Fifteen

    Graduated licensing drivers are suspended at seven demerit points. Every other licence holder is suspended at fifteen, so a new driver has less than half the margin.

  15. May a Yukon learner or novice driver use a phone mounted in a hands-free holder?

    • No, learners and novice drivers may not use any electronic device, hands-free includedCorrect answer
    • Yes, hands-free use is always allowed
    • Yes, but only for navigation
    • Yes, once the vehicle is moving at a steady speed

    Only full licence holders may use a device that is set up and used in a hands-free way. For learners and novice drivers the ban covers hand-held and hands-free alike, so the phone goes away before you start the engine.

  16. What can a distracted driving conviction cost a driver in Yukon?

    • A fine of up to 600 dollars and three demerit pointsCorrect answer
    • A written warning only
    • A fine but never any points
    • One demerit point and no fine

    Distracted driving carries a fine of up to 600 dollars and three demerit points, for full licence holders and graduated licensing drivers alike. For a graduated licensing driver those three points are almost half the way to a suspension.

  17. What happens to your graduated licensing time if you are convicted of a graduated licensing violation?

    • The time already served stops counting towards the next stage, so the clock starts againCorrect answer
    • Nothing changes, the months keep counting
    • You move up one month later than planned
    • Only your insurance is affected

    Yukon's regulations state that the time the licence was held before the violation does not count towards the total required for the next stage. One conviction can therefore cost you the whole six or eighteen months you had already built up.

  18. You are on an undivided Yukon highway and a school bus ahead stops with its upper red lights flashing. What must you do?

    • Stop, whichever direction you are travelling inCorrect answer
    • Pass slowly on the left
    • Stop only if you can see children
    • Sound your horn and keep going

    On a street, an undivided highway or a roadway, traffic in every direction must stop for a stopped school bus showing flashing upper red lights. Children may be crossing in front of the bus where you cannot see them.

  19. The same bus is stopped on a highway that is physically divided by a median into two separate roadways. Who has to stop?

    • Only the drivers approaching the bus from behindCorrect answer
    • Everybody, from both directions
    • Nobody, because the median keeps children away
    • Only the drivers in the left lane

    Where a median physically divides the highway into two roadways, only drivers coming up behind the bus must stop. On any street or undivided road, both directions stop.

  20. You have stopped for a school bus with its red lights flashing. When may you move off again?

    • When the red lights have stopped flashing and the bus starts to moveCorrect answer
    • As soon as the last child steps down
    • After waiting a slow count of ten
    • As soon as the bus door closes

    Two things have to happen: the red lights stop flashing and the bus begins to move. A closed door on its own is not the signal, because a child may still be crossing in front.

  21. Your vehicle has no automatic daytime running lights. What does Yukon law require of you in daylight?

    • You must drive with your low beam headlights onCorrect answer
    • Nothing, headlights are only for darkness
    • You must use your high beams
    • You must switch on your hazard lights

    On Yukon roadways a vehicle without automatic daytime running lights must be driven with the low beams on during the day. It is a legal requirement, and it makes you far easier to see against snow, dust and a low northern sun.

  22. Which Yukon roads does the territory name as affected by permafrost heaving?

    • The Alaska Highway between Haines Junction and Beaver Creek, the Haines Road, the Dempster, the North and South Klondike and the Robert CampbellCorrect answer
    • The Dempster Highway only
    • The streets inside Whitehorse only
    • Every road that is unpaved

    Ground ice beneath the road thaws and refreezes, leaving waves and dips the territory compares to a roller coaster. The Shakwak section also has cracks, potholes and frequent gravel stretches.

  23. You reach a stretch of highway that rolls and dips from frost heaving. What does Yukon advise?

    • Slow down, scan well ahead and avoid passingCorrect answer
    • Keep your speed up so the vehicle floats over the dips
    • Drive on the shoulder where the surface is flatter
    • Pass slower vehicles quickly to get clear of the section

    Yukon asks drivers to slow down, be cautious, scan the road ahead and avoid passing on heaved sections. A dip you cannot see into can hide oncoming traffic or a vehicle already stopped.

  24. Why does Yukon tell drivers not to pull over on permafrost-damaged highways except in an emergency?

    • Because the shoulders are unstableCorrect answer
    • Because stopping there is a ticketed offence
    • Because there is nothing worth seeing
    • Because the road belongs to a private owner

    The shoulders on these roads can give way under a vehicle. Gravel sections also lengthen your stopping distance, so increase your following distance, and remember that posted speed limits are set for optimal road conditions.

  25. A regulatory sign showing winter tires or chains is posted at the start of a section of road. What does it mean?

    • Winter tires or chains must be used from that point onCorrect answer
    • They are recommended there but remain optional
    • It applies only to commercial trucks
    • It applies only after a snowfall warning is issued

    Where that sign is displayed, winter tires or chains are required, not suggested. Turning back or waiting is the only legal choice if your vehicle is not equipped.

  26. Yukon says every vehicle should carry a winter driving emergency kit. Which of these belongs in it?

    • A shovel, a flashlight, warm clothing, food and booster cablesCorrect answer
    • A spare set of licence plates
    • One litre of windshield washer fluid and nothing more
    • A camera and a notebook for insurance

    The territory's list also includes an ice scraper, a spare tire, a fire extinguisher, a first-aid kit and a source of heat. Where help can be hours away, the kit is what keeps you warm and visible until it arrives.

  27. What does Yukon recommend doing to your vehicle before the winter driving season?

    • Install winter tires and carry an emergency winter driving kitCorrect answer
    • Fit the widest tires you can find
    • Let air out of the tires for extra grip
    • Take the spare tire out to save weight

    Winter tires stay soft and keep gripping at temperatures where all-season rubber goes hard. Yukon pairs that advice with carrying the emergency kit, because the two problems arrive together.

  28. You are about to drive a long, empty stretch of Yukon highway. What does the territory ask you to do first?

    • Tell someone your route, your destination and when you expect to be backCorrect answer
    • Nothing in particular, if the weather looks clear
    • Post your plans on social media
    • Drive only during daylight hours

    Yukon asks drivers to tell someone their travel plans and their estimated time of travel before leaving. If you do not arrive, that is the information that tells searchers where to look.

  29. Where can a Yukon driver check road conditions before setting out?

    • Through 511Yukon, the territory's road report serviceCorrect answer
    • At the vehicle registration counter
    • On a local radio phone-in show only
    • Nowhere official, so you have to judge for yourself

    Yukon publishes road and weather conditions through 511Yukon and asks drivers to check them before travelling. Conditions in the north can change completely between one valley and the next.

  30. What does Yukon suggest taking with you when you travel far from communities?

    • Extra fuel and a satellite phone or CB radioCorrect answer
    • A second driver at all times
    • A trailer loaded with supplies
    • A mobile phone and nothing else

    Distances between services are long and mobile coverage cannot be relied on, so Yukon suggests extra fuel and a satellite phone or CB radio. Both are for the trip that does not go to plan.

About this quiz

This page is free practice for the Yukon written and sign recognition test, the one test you pass before you may drive with a co-driver beside you. Yukon calls the licence a Class 7 learner licence, and it is the first stage of the territory's graduated licensing programme.

Yukon publishes its pass mark, which is more than many places do. The Motor Vehicles Regulations set it at 80 percent and say that mark applies to every class of licence. The same regulations describe one combined written and sign recognition test rather than two separate exams.

What is not published is the number of questions or a time limit, so the count on our mock test, the timer and our split into a signs half and a rules half are a clearly stated practice standard of our own. A commercial site states that the exam comes in two parts of twenty questions each, and the regulation does not support it.

Every question was written for this site from the free Yukon Driver's Basic Handbook, the driver licensing and road safety pages on yukon.ca, and the Motor Vehicles Regulations. Nothing was copied from a handbook, from a real test, or from another practice site.

Good to know

  • The pass mark is 80 percent, it is set in the Motor Vehicles Regulations, and it applies to every class of licence.
  • The test costs twenty dollars, you are told the result immediately, and you may take it again as early as the following day for another twenty dollars.
  • A learner licence starts at 15, and the learner stage lasts at least six months with fifty logged driving hours.
  • The co-driver must have held at least a Class 5 licence for two years and must have no alcohol in their blood.
  • A graduated licensing driver is suspended at seven demerit points, while every other licence holder is suspended at fifteen.

Common questions

How many questions are on the real Yukon knowledge test?

Yukon does not publish a number. The regulations leave the basic test to the registrar and set no count for it. A commercial site states two parts of twenty questions each, which the regulation does not support, and our mock test uses a count of our own and says so.

What is the pass mark?

Eighty percent. That one is official: the Motor Vehicles Regulations set it, and the same line says it applies to every class of licence.

Are signs and rules two separate tests?

No. The regulations describe one combined written and sign recognition test. We keep signs and rules as two study pages because it is a better way to learn, not because Yukon runs two exams.

Where do I write the test, and can I write it online?

In person at a Motor Vehicles office. Yukon's online service covers booking a road test, and no official page offers the written test online.

Can I take the test in French?

Yukon does not publish an answer either way. The handbook and the licensing pages are bilingual, and the Guide de la route du Yukon is an official edition, but no official page says which languages the examination itself is given in. Ask the Motor Vehicles office before you go.

Who can sit beside me while I practise?

A co-driver who has held at least a Class 5 licence for two years, who must be with you every time you drive and who must have no alcohol in their blood. Your own blood alcohol must be zero as well.
