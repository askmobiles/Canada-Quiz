What Yukon publishes, and what it does not

Yukon publishes its pass mark, which many places do not. The Motor Vehicles Regulations set it at 80 percent, and the same line says that figure applies to every class of licence, from a Class 7 learner to a commercial licence. The regulations also describe what you sit as one combined written and sign recognition test, a single test covering both, rather than two exams taken one after the other.

What Yukon does not publish is how many questions the test has or how long you get. The regulations leave the content of the basic test to the registrar and set no count for it, and no page on yukon.ca gives one either. So the question count on our mock test, the timer on it and our decision to split the practice into a signs half and a rules half are all our own. One commercial site states that the exam comes in two parts of twenty questions each; nothing in the regulation supports that, and the regulation describes a single combined test.

The rest of the process is published plainly and is worth knowing. The test costs twenty dollars, you sit it in person at a Motor Vehicles office, and you are told whether you passed right away. If it does not go your way you may take it again as early as the following day, and you pay another twenty dollars for that attempt. Yukon's online service covers booking a road test, not the written test.

Study the rules

The three stages, one at a time

You start at 15, which is younger than most of the country allows. Pass the written test and a vision test and you hold a Class 7 learner licence, the first stage of Yukon's graduated programme. You keep it for at least six months and you drive at least fifty hours with a co-driver before you may apply to move up.

Those fifty hours are not an honour system. They are recorded on the official GDL Driver Record and Declaration form and co-signed by a parent, a guardian or another valid co-driver, and the form goes in with your application. Start filling it in from your first drive rather than reconstructing it six months later.

The second stage is the Class 5 Novice licence, which you reach at 16 at the earliest and only after a road test. You hold it for at least eighteen months before a full Class 5. Yukon publishes no driver-education course that shortens either stage, so do not count on one. It does publish the opposite: if you commit a graduated licensing violation, the time you have already held the licence does not count towards moving up, and the clock starts again.

The rules that catch learners out

The co-driver rule has a Yukon twist that people miss. Your co-driver must have held at least a Class 5 licence for two years and must be with you every time you drive, which is ordinary enough. What is not ordinary is that the co-driver must have no alcohol in their blood either. The zero-alcohol rule binds the person in the passenger seat exactly as it binds you.

The learner restrictions themselves are specific. You may drive only between five in the morning and midnight. You may carry no more than one passenger besides your co-driver, unless the extra passengers are a parent, a grandparent, a brother, a sister or a child. You may drive only a car or light truck of up to 4,000 kilograms registered gross vehicle weight with no more than seven seats including the driver, and that seat limit rises to nine at the novice stage. An L sign goes on the back of the vehicle every time you drive, where drivers coming up behind you can see it.

The phone rule is stricter here than most people assume. A learner and a novice driver may not use any electronic device at all, and that includes a hands-free set-up: only full licence holders may use a device set up and used hands-free. Distracted driving carries a fine of up to six hundred dollars and three demerit points, and for a graduated licensing driver three points is nearly half the way to a suspension.

Where to get the official information

Everything on this page comes from what Yukon publishes for free. The territory writes its own book, the Yukon Driver's Basic Handbook, and it is free to download from yukon.ca, so any site telling you that Yukon borrows another jurisdiction's handbook is simply wrong. There is an official French edition as well, the Guide de la route du Yukon. Whether the test itself can be sat in French is not published anywhere, even though the handbook and the licensing pages are bilingual, so we do not claim that it can. Read the handbook alongside this practice, not instead of it.

This is an unofficial free practice site. It is not affiliated with Yukon Motor Vehicles, with Highways and Public Works or with the Government of Yukon, and it cannot issue a licence. Always check the official pages for the current rules and fees before you book.

Road Sign Recognition

6 of 30

  1. You reach this sign at a Yukon intersection. What does it require?

    • A complete stop, then go on only when the way is clearCorrect answer
    • A rolling stop if nothing is coming
    • Stopping only when a pedestrian is waiting
    • Flashing your headlights and carrying on

    A stop means the wheels stop turning. Stop at the stop line, or before the crosswalk if none is painted, and move off only when you can do it without forcing anyone else to slow down.

  2. Where this sign is posted, what are you still allowed to do?

    • Stop briefly while somebody gets in or outCorrect answer
    • Leave the vehicle for a few minutes
    • Park if you switch on your hazard lights
    • Nothing at all is permitted here

    No parking still lets you pause while a passenger gets in or out. It is the sign that bans stopping altogether that takes even that away.

  3. What hazard does this warning sign describe?

    • Rock may fall onto the road, and rock may already be lying on itCorrect answer
    • A quarry entrance is ahead
    • Loose gravel has just been spread on the surface
    • A rock crusher is working nearby

    The handbook wording is to beware of falling rock and to watch for rock already on the road ahead. Keep your eyes well down the road rather than on the cliff face.

  4. What does this yellow sign say about the road in front of you?

    • There is a series of bends, so lose speed before the first oneCorrect answer
    • The road ends shortly
    • There is one sharp bend and then it straightens
    • The road narrows to a single lane

    Braking is for the straight before the bend, not for the bend itself. Set your speed early, then steer smoothly through each curve.

  5. What does this orange sign tell you to expect?

    • A traffic control person directing traffic aheadCorrect answer
    • A police checkpoint
    • A weigh scale
    • A closed rest area

    Someone will be controlling traffic through the work zone. Slow right down, be ready to stop, and do exactly what they signal, even when the road ahead looks empty.

  6. This sign is painted on a parking space outside a store. What does it reserve the space for?

    • Only a vehicle displaying a valid accessible parking permitCorrect answer
    • Anyone stopping for less than five minutes
    • Delivery vehicles
    • Anyone, once the business has closed

    The space is reserved for a permit holder. Taking it, even briefly, can leave someone with no way to reach the door.

Rules of the Road

6 of 40

  1. What is the youngest age at which you can apply for a Yukon learner licence?

    • 15Correct answer
    • 14
    • 16
    • 17

    Yukon sets 15 as the minimum age for a Class 7 learner licence. You must be at least 16 before you can move up to the Class 5 Novice licence.

  2. During which hours may a Yukon learner drive?

    • Between 5 a.m. and midnightCorrect answer
    • Any hour, provided the co-driver is present
    • In daylight only
    • Between 6 a.m. and 10 p.m.

    The learner stage carries a curfew: driving is allowed between 5 a.m. and midnight. A co-driver beside you does not lift it.

  3. How many demerit points bring a suspension for a driver in the graduated licensing programme in Yukon?

    • SevenCorrect answer
    • Five
    • Ten
    • Fifteen

    Graduated licensing drivers are suspended at seven demerit points. Every other licence holder is suspended at fifteen, so a new driver has less than half the margin.

  4. Your vehicle has no automatic daytime running lights. What does Yukon law require of you in daylight?

    • You must drive with your low beam headlights onCorrect answer
    • Nothing, headlights are only for darkness
    • You must use your high beams
    • You must switch on your hazard lights

    On Yukon roadways a vehicle without automatic daytime running lights must be driven with the low beams on during the day. It is a legal requirement, and it makes you far easier to see against snow, dust and a low northern sun.

  5. What does Yukon recommend doing to your vehicle before the winter driving season?

    • Install winter tires and carry an emergency winter driving kitCorrect answer
    • Fit the widest tires you can find
    • Let air out of the tires for extra grip
    • Take the spare tire out to save weight

    Winter tires stay soft and keep gripping at temperatures where all-season rubber goes hard. Yukon pairs that advice with carrying the emergency kit, because the two problems arrive together.

  6. Who must be free of alcohol and drugs when a Yukon learner drives?

    • The learner and the co-driverCorrect answer
    • Only the learner
    • Only the co-driver
    • Neither, below 0.05

    Zero tolerance applies to both the new driver and the co-driver. A co-driver who has been drinking is not a co-driver.

Common questions

How many questions are on the real Yukon knowledge test?

Yukon does not publish a number. The regulations leave the basic test to the registrar and set no count for it. A commercial site states two parts of twenty questions each, which the regulation does not support, and our mock test uses a count of our own and says so.

What is the pass mark?

Eighty percent. That one is official: the Motor Vehicles Regulations set it, and the same line says it applies to every class of licence.

Are signs and rules two separate tests?

No. The regulations describe one combined written and sign recognition test. We keep signs and rules as two study pages because it is a better way to learn, not because Yukon runs two exams.

Where do I write the test, and can I write it online?

In person at a Motor Vehicles office. Yukon's online service covers booking a road test, and no official page offers the written test online.

Can I take the test in French?

Yukon does not publish an answer either way. The handbook and the licensing pages are bilingual, and the Guide de la route du Yukon is an official edition, but no official page says which languages the examination itself is given in. Ask the Motor Vehicles office before you go.

Who can sit beside me while I practise?

A co-driver who has held at least a Class 5 licence for two years, who must be with you every time you drive and who must have no alcohol in their blood. Your own blood alcohol must be zero as well.
