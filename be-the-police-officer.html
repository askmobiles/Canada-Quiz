👮 Be the Police Officer

You are a community police officer in a Canadian town. Twelve calls come in. Safety, the public's trust, the clock and the rules you must follow all pull against each other.

📖 How to play

Each call comes from someone in the community. Pick one of three answers. Every answer moves four things: safety, the community's trust, your time on shift, and the rules — doing it lawfully and properly. If any one reaches zero, your shift ends early. At the end you will see which parts of the job you leaned on.

👮

    How a police officer is expected to act

    • Keep the oath. Every police officer in Canada swears an oath or makes an affirmation before starting work, promising to do the job honestly and according to law.
    • Respect the Charter. Police must respect everyone's rights, including the right to know why you are arrested and to speak to a lawyer without delay.
    • Follow a code of conduct. Every police service has rules of conduct, and the public can complain about an officer through an official process.
    • Treat everyone equally. Human rights laws forbid police, like any public service, from discriminating against anyone.

    How to treat a police officer

    • Slow down and move over. Every province has a law that drivers must slow down and, when safe, move over for stopped emergency vehicles with flashing lights.
    • Pull over for lights and sirens. Provincial traffic laws require drivers to move to the right and stop when an emergency vehicle approaches with its lights and siren on.
    • Never make a false report. Knowingly making a false report to police is public mischief, a crime under the Criminal Code.
    • Don't obstruct or assault an officer. Both are crimes under the Criminal Code. If you disagree with how an officer acted, stay calm and make a complaint afterward.
    About this game

    Be the Police Officer puts you on patrol in a Canadian community for one shift. Twelve calls come in, you pick one of three answers, and four things move: safety, the community's trust, your time on shift and the rules you must follow.

    Every call is an everyday one: a school zone, a lost child at a fair, a noisy party, a mental-health call with a crisis worker, a first-time shoplifter, a roadside breath check, a phone scam. Nothing in it is violent, and each call teaches something real about the law and community safety in Canada.

    At the end the game shows which part of the work you leaned on and names jobs in Canada that use it. It is a game, not a career test.

    Tips for playing

    • Under the Charter, anyone arrested in Canada must be told promptly why, and told without delay of their right to a lawyer.
    • Since December 18, 2018, police can demand a roadside breath sample from any driver they have lawfully stopped, even without suspecting alcohol.
    • For youth aged 12 to 17, the Youth Criminal Justice Act tells police to consider a warning, a caution or a referral before laying a charge.

    Common questions

    Is this game violent?

    No. No one is hurt and no weapon is used. The calls are about traffic, lost children, noise, scams, young people, mental-health support and paperwork — the everyday work of community police.

    Can my shift end early?

    Yes. If safety, trust, time or the rules reaches zero, your shift ends early — just as real police work depends on all four.

    Does this tell me what job I should do?

    No, and it says so on the result screen. It shows which kind of decision you enjoyed making today, and which jobs involve a lot of that.

    Is it good for a classroom?

    Yes. There is no signup and nothing is collected, it works on a phone or a projector, and every call teaches something real about rights, safety and the law in Canada.
