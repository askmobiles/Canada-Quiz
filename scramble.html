🔀 Canada Word Scramble

Unscramble the Canadian word. Use the hint if you get stuck!

📖 How to play

The letters of a Canadian word are mixed up. Read the hint, then type the correct word and tap Check. Get it right to score a point and move on to the next scrambled word!

-----

Solved: 0

About this game

Every round shows one Canadian word with its letters jumbled, plus a hint underneath. Read the clue, work out what fits, type your answer and press Check. Get it right and a fresh scrambled word appears on its own.

The words are all things a person in Canada meets sooner or later: a coin nickname, a city, an animal, a dish. Newcomers practising English spelling and children learning to read both get something out of the same short game.

How it works

  • Twelve Canadian words take turns, from MAPLE and HOCKEY to POUTINE, LOONIE, IGLOO and CARIBOU.
  • A hint line under the scrambled letters describes each word, such as fries with cheese curds and gravy.
  • Type into the answer box and press the Check button, or simply hit Enter on a keyboard.
  • A wrong guess only says try again, so you can keep working on the same word as long as you want.
  • The solved counter climbs by one for each correct word, and a skip button moves you on when you are stuck.

Tips for playing

  • The one-dollar coin is called the loonie because of the loon on its back, and the two-dollar coin later became the toonie.
  • Poutine is fries topped with cheese curds and gravy, a dish that grew out of rural Quebec and spread right across the country.
  • The beaver appears on the Canadian five-cent coin and became an official symbol of the country, partly because of the old fur trade.
  • Caribou and moose both wear antlers, but caribou travel far north in large herds while moose usually keep to themselves in the forest.
  • When the letters look hopeless, say the hint out loud and count how many letters the answer needs. The shape of the word often appears.

Common questions

What should I do if I cannot work out a word?

Press the button for a new word and a different scramble appears right away. Nothing is marked against you, and the word you skipped can come back later for another try.

Does my spelling have to be exact?

The whole word must match, but capital letters make no difference at all. Type everything in lower case if that is easier, because the game reads it exactly the same way.

Is this useful for someone learning English?

Many players use it that way. Each word is short, common in Canada, and paired with a plain-language clue, so you meet the spelling and the meaning at the same moment.

Can two people play this together?

Easily. Pass one phone back and forth, or let one person read the hint aloud while the other types. The solved counter simply keeps a running total for the pair.
