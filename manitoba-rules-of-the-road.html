Manitoba Rules of the Road

The rules half of the Class 5L test, chapter by chapter. Open a question to see the answer and a short explanation, then practise the whole chapter. No timer.

The numbers worth memorising

Keep at least two seconds of space behind the car ahead on a dry road, and more when it is wet or icy. Leave one metre when you pass a cyclist. Slow down and move over for a stopped emergency vehicle. New drivers in the learner and novice stages must have zero alcohol in their blood. Speed limits are 50 km/h in a city or town and 80 km/h outside one unless a sign says otherwise, and school and playground zones drop to 30 km/h during their posted hours.

All 30 rules of the road questions, with every answer explained

This is the whole question bank from the test above, written out so you can read straight through it. The right answer is marked, and every question has a short explanation of why it is the right one.

  1. What is the minimum age for a Manitoba Class 5 Learner licence?

    • 17
    • 15
    • 16, or 15 and a half if enrolled in a high school driver education courseCorrect answer
    • 18

    Sixteen normally, or fifteen and a half while taking a high school driver education course.

  2. How long must a Manitoba learner stay in the learner stage?

    • Three months
    • Twelve months
    • There is no minimum
    • At least nine monthsCorrect answer

    A minimum of nine months, not counting any interruption to the licence, before moving to the intermediate stage.

  3. How much driving experience must a Manitoba learner's supervising driver have?

    • A full licence for at least 36 monthsCorrect answer
    • One year
    • Six months
    • No requirement

    At least thirty-six months fully licensed. They must also be under .05 blood alcohol and must not fail a drug screening test.

  4. Where must a Manitoba learner's supervising driver sit?

    • In the back seat
    • In the front passenger seatCorrect answer
    • Anywhere in the vehicle
    • Directly behind the driver

    Front passenger seat only, so they can see what you see and reach the controls if needed.

  5. Where may passengers sit in a Manitoba learner's vehicle?

    • Only in the front
    • No passengers at all
    • In the rear, up to the number of working seatbeltsCorrect answer
    • Anywhere, with no limit

    The front passenger seat belongs to the supervising driver. Other passengers ride in the rear, one per working seatbelt.

  6. How much alcohol or drugs may a Manitoba learner have in their system?

    • Under .05
    • Under .08
    • No rule applies to learners
    • None at allCorrect answer

    Zero for the whole graduated licensing programme. The supervising driver has a separate limit of under .05.

  7. Which of these may a Manitoba learner NOT do?

    • Tow another vehicleCorrect answer
    • Drive on a city street
    • Carry a rear-seat passenger
    • Drive in daylight

    Learners may not tow, may not drive a Class 3 farm truck, and may not operate off-road vehicles on a highway.

  8. How long do you have to complete the Manitoba knowledge test?

    • No time limit
    • 30 minutesCorrect answer
    • 15 minutes
    • 60 minutes

    Thirty minutes. The test is done on a computer, it is not open book, and phones and other devices are not allowed.

  9. Which three areas does the Manitoba knowledge test cover?

    • Geography, weather and maps
    • Only road signs
    • Traffic laws, safe driving practices and road sign recognitionCorrect answer
    • Mechanics, first aid and insurance

    Study all three in the Driver's Handbook. Sign recognition is the part most people underestimate.

  10. What must you carry whenever you drive in Manitoba?

    • Only your licence
    • Only insurance
    • Nothing
    • Your licence, registration and proof of insuranceCorrect answer

    All three. An officer may ask for each one at any stop, and driving without them can bring a fine.

  11. What is the default speed limit in a Manitoba city or town with no posted sign?

    • 50 km/hCorrect answer
    • 30 km/h
    • 60 km/h
    • 80 km/h

    Fifty inside a built-up area. On a provincial highway with no sign the default rises to 90 km/h.

  12. What is the two-second rule?

    • How long a turn should take
    • A minimum following distance measured in time, not metresCorrect answer
    • A rule about signalling
    • How long to wait at a stop sign

    Time works at any speed, unlike a fixed distance. Double it to four seconds or more in rain, snow or fog.

  13. How far ahead should you signal a turn in town?

    • After starting the turn
    • Signalling is optional
    • At least 30 metresCorrect answer
    • Right as you turn

    Signal early enough for drivers behind to react, then cancel it after the turn so nobody is misled.

  14. Which lane should you normally use on a Manitoba divided highway?

    • The left lane
    • The middle
    • Any lane
    • The right lane, keeping left for passingCorrect answer

    Keep right except to pass. Sitting in the left lane forces others to pass on your right, where their blind spot is worst.

  15. A school bus on an undivided Manitoba road stops with red lights flashing. Who must stop?

    • Traffic in both directionsCorrect answer
    • Only traffic behind the bus
    • Only traffic coming toward it
    • Nobody

    Both directions stop unless a median divides the road. Children may cross in front of the bus without looking.

  16. What must you do when passing a stopped emergency vehicle with flashing lights?

    • Stop completely
    • Slow down, and move over a lane where the road allowsCorrect answer
    • Keep your speed
    • Sound your horn

    Manitoba's slow-down-and-move-over rule protects roadside workers. Give them a full lane whenever a second lane exists.

  17. How should you pass a cyclist on a Manitoba road?

    • Sound your horn to move them over
    • Drive alongside them
    • Leave plenty of room, changing lanes if the lane is narrowCorrect answer
    • Pass close and quickly

    Treat a bike as a vehicle entitled to the lane. Wait for a clear stretch rather than squeezing past.

  18. Manitoba highways get ground blizzards. What is the safest response when visibility drops to nothing?

    • Stop in your lane
    • Speed up to get through
    • Follow the car ahead closely
    • Slow down and, if you must stop, get well off the road with lights onCorrect answer

    Stopping in a travel lane invites a rear-end collision from someone who cannot see either. Get right off, keep lights on and stay belted.

  19. Where does ice form first on a Manitoba road in autumn?

    • On bridges and overpassesCorrect answer
    • In the middle of a sunny street
    • Only in parking lots
    • Only on gravel

    Bridges lose heat from above and below, so they freeze while the rest of the road is only wet.

  20. Why do winter tires help even on a dry, very cold Manitoba day?

    • They make the car quieter
    • Their rubber stays soft below about 7 °C so they keep gripCorrect answer
    • They are wider
    • They only help in deep snow

    Grip comes from rubber that can flex. Below roughly 7 °C an all-season tire stiffens and its braking distance grows.

  21. Your car starts to skid on a Manitoba winter road. Where should you look?

    • Straight down
    • At the vehicle you might hit
    • Where you want the car to goCorrect answer
    • At the ditch

    Your hands follow your eyes. Ease off the gas, look at your escape path and steer gently toward it.

  22. Is holding a phone allowed while driving in Manitoba?

    • Yes, for calls only
    • Yes, at red lights
    • Yes, if you are stopped in traffic
    • No — hand-held use is prohibited, hands-free is permittedCorrect answer

    Hand-held use is prohibited whenever you are in control of the vehicle, including stopped at a light.

  23. Why can prescription medicine be a problem for driving?

    • Some medicines cause drowsiness or slow reactions, which is still impaired drivingCorrect answer
    • It cannot, if a doctor prescribed it
    • Only illegal drugs count
    • It only matters over 65

    Read the label and ask your pharmacist. Allergy pills, cold medicine and some painkillers can make you drowsy enough to be charged.

  24. A friend who has been drinking wants to drive home. What should you do?

    • Give them coffee
    • Take the keys and arrange another rideCorrect answer
    • Let them drive slowly
    • Follow behind them

    Only time clears alcohol. Arranging a ride is a small inconvenience next to a collision or a criminal record.

  25. Who is responsible for seatbelts for passengers under 18 in Manitoba?

    • The vehicle owner
    • Nobody
    • The driverCorrect answer
    • Each passenger

    The driver answers for younger passengers, including the correct child seat or booster for their age and size.

  26. Why should you carry a winter emergency kit in Manitoba?

    • It improves fuel economy
    • Insurance requires it
    • It reduces tire wear
    • Help can be far away and the cold is dangerousCorrect answer

    On a rural highway you may wait a long time. A blanket, warm clothes, a shovel, food, water and a charged phone are the basics.

  27. What does it mean if the ABS light stays on after you start the engine?

    • The anti-lock system has a fault and needs serviceCorrect answer
    • The parking brake is on
    • You are low on fuel
    • The headlights are on

    Your brakes still work, but the anti-lock function may not. Leave extra following distance until it is repaired.

  28. You are first at a collision scene in Manitoba. What comes first?

    • Move the vehicles
    • Make the scene safe and call 911Correct answer
    • Move the injured
    • Take photographs

    Protect the scene and call for help. Do not move an injured person unless there is immediate danger such as fire.

  29. How should you use anti-lock brakes in an emergency stop?

    • Press lightly
    • Use the parking brake
    • Press hard and hold, steering around the hazardCorrect answer
    • Pump the pedal

    ABS pumps for you. The vibration under your foot is normal — keep firm pressure and steer, because ABS lets you brake and steer at once.

  30. Your vehicle breaks down on a Manitoba highway in winter. What is usually safest?

    • Walk to the nearest town
    • Stand beside the road
    • Leave the car in a driving lane
    • Stay in the vehicle with hazards on unless help is very closeCorrect answer

    Your car is shelter and it is visible. Walking in a Manitoba winter is how people get into serious trouble.

Worth remembering

  • A Manitoba learner may not tow another vehicle, may not drive a Class 3 farm truck, and may not operate an off-road vehicle on a highway.
  • Zero alcohol and zero drugs apply to the learner for the whole graduated licensing programme, while the supervising driver must stay under .05.
  • Ground blizzards can drop visibility to nothing in seconds on open prairie. If you must stop, get right off the road and keep your lights on.

Common questions

How long is the learner stage in Manitoba?

A minimum of nine months, not counting any interruption to the licence.

Where do passengers sit?

The supervising driver takes the front passenger seat. Everyone else rides in the rear, one person for each working seatbelt.
