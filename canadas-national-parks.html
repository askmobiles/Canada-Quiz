🏔️ Canada's National Parks and Natural Wonders

A guide to Canada's national parks — Banff, Jasper, Gros Morne and more — plus the natural wonders that make the country famous.

Canada is the second-largest country in the world by land area, and much of it is wild. To protect the best of it, the country has built up a national park system that stretches from the Atlantic to the Pacific and all the way to the Arctic Ocean.

How many national parks does Canada have?

Parks Canada looks after 37 national parks and 11 national park reserves, along with national historic sites and marine conservation areas. A national park reserve is land set aside as a park while Indigenous land claims are still being settled, so the rights of Indigenous peoples are protected in the meantime.

The first one was Banff National Park in Alberta, created in 1885, which makes it one of the oldest national parks anywhere in the world. It started after workers building the railway discovered hot springs in the mountains.

Famous parks to know

  • Banff (Alberta) — turquoise lakes like Lake Louise and Moraine Lake, surrounded by the Rocky Mountains.
  • Jasper (Alberta) — huge, quieter and wilder than Banff, with the Columbia Icefield and some of the darkest night skies in the country.
  • Pacific Rim (British Columbia) — rainforest, long sandy beaches and dramatic winter storms on Vancouver Island.
  • Gros Morne (Newfoundland and Labrador) — fjords and exposed rock from deep inside the Earth, which is why it is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
  • Cape Breton Highlands (Nova Scotia) — the Cabot Trail winds along cliffs above the ocean.
  • Prince Edward Island National Park — red sand cliffs, warm beaches and the landscape behind Anne of Green Gables.
  • Wood Buffalo (Alberta and Northwest Territories) — Canada's largest national park, home to wild bison and the last natural nesting ground of the whooping crane.
  • Nahanni National Park Reserve (Northwest Territories) — Virginia Falls is roughly twice the height of Niagara Falls.

Natural wonders outside the parks

Some of Canada's best-known natural sights are not national parks at all. Niagara Falls in Ontario is one of the most visited waterfalls on Earth. The Bay of Fundy, between New Brunswick and Nova Scotia, has the highest tides in the world — the water can rise and fall by more than 15 metres in a single tide cycle. The northern lights (aurora borealis) are visible on clear winter nights across the North, and Yellowknife is one of the best places on the planet to see them.

Canada also has more lakes than any other country, and it shares four of the five Great Lakes with the United States: Superior, Huron, Erie and Ontario. Lake Michigan is the only one entirely inside the United States.

Visiting with a family

  • Parks Canada offers a yearly Discovery Pass, which is usually cheaper than paying at the gate several times.
  • Children and youth 17 and under enter national parks for free.
  • Many parks have short, easy, stroller-friendly trails as well as long hikes.
  • Book campsites early — popular mountain parks fill up months ahead for summer weekends.
  • Always check bear safety rules before hiking, and never feed wildlife.
