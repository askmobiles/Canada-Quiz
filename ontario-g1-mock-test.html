Ontario G1 Mock Test

Twenty road sign questions, twenty rules of the road questions, a 30-minute timer, and a pass mark of 16 out of 20 in each half. That is our own practice standard: Ontario publishes an 80 percent pass mark, says the real test is split into sections and says it is not timed, but it does not publish how many questions it asks. Questions are picked at random, so every run is a new test.

📖 How to play

Tap an answer to lock it in. You will see straight away whether it was right, with a short explanation, then move on to the next question. At the end you get your score for each half, a PASS or TRY AGAIN, and a list of every question you missed.

Keep studying

Missed a lot of sign questions? Go through the road signs study page, group by group. Struggling with the rules? The rules of the road page takes you chapter by chapter with no timer.

About this quiz

This page copies the shape of the Ontario G1 knowledge test so that nothing surprises you on the day. You answer twenty road sign questions and twenty rules of the road questions, with a thirty-minute timer running across the whole thing.

The pass mark is the strict one: sixteen correct out of twenty in each half, counted separately. A strong score on signs cannot rescue a weak score on rules, which is exactly how the real knowledge test treats you.

This is unofficial practice, written in our own words from the free MTO Driver's Handbook. We are not connected to the Ministry of Transportation, ServiceOntario or DriveTest. Use it to test yourself, then go back to the handbook for whatever you keep missing.

What's inside

  • The test runs twenty sign questions and twenty rules questions, the same split the real knowledge test uses.
  • A thirty-minute timer counts down, and when it reaches zero the test stops and shows your result.
  • Each answer is marked straight away, with a short explanation of why that particular choice is correct.
  • The result screen scores both halves separately and shows PASS or TRY AGAIN against the sixteen-out-of-twenty mark.
  • Every question you missed is listed at the end, with your answer, the right answer and the reason behind it.
  • Questions are drawn at random from sixty sign questions and sixty rules questions, so no two runs are identical.

Good to know

  • Sign questions reward shape and colour recognition, so learn the families of signs before you try to memorise individual wordings.
  • Missing five questions in one half already fails that half, so treat every single question as if the whole result depends on it.
  • Timing rarely defeats anyone here. Thirty minutes for forty questions is about forty-five seconds each, which is generous once you have studied.
  • When two answers both look reasonable, the more cautious one usually wins, because the handbook is built around driver safety.
  • Take the mock test more than once. Random selection means a second run will show you questions the first run left out entirely.

Common questions

Is this the official Ontario G1 knowledge test?

No. This is unofficial practice written from the free MTO Driver's Handbook. We have no connection to the Ministry of Transportation, ServiceOntario or DriveTest, and passing here does not give you a licence.

Do I need an account before starting the mock test?

No account, no email and no download. The test runs in your browser the moment the page opens, and you can close the tab and come back whenever you have half an hour free.

What happens when the thirty-minute timer runs out?

The test stops where you are and takes you straight to the result screen. Questions you never reached count as wrong, so keep moving instead of sitting on one hard question.

Can I review the questions I answered wrongly?

Yes. The result screen lists every missed question with the answer you chose, the correct answer and a short explanation, so you know exactly what to go back and study.

How many times should I pass this before booking the real test?

There is no fixed number. A good sign is passing both halves comfortably several runs in a row, rather than scraping sixteen once on a lucky selection of questions.
