The Cup With Everybody's Name On It

It started life as a silver bowl for punch, and it has been dropped in a canal, left on a snowbank and thrown in a swimming pool.

A governor general buys a bowl

In 1892 the man in charge of Canada for the Queen was called Lord Stanley. He had watched a hockey game in Montreal, and his whole family had fallen in love with the sport.

He decided Canada needed a prize for its best hockey team. So he bought a silver bowl, the kind you would put fruit punch in at a party. It cost about fifty dollars.

The first team won it in 1893. Nobody guessed that more than a hundred and thirty years later it would still be the prize every hockey player in the world dreams of.

It is the oldest trophy that professional athletes in North America play for. Older than every other cup and medal in every other sport here.

Everybody's name is on it

Most trophies just say who won. The Stanley Cup does something different: the name of every single player on the winning team is carved right into the silver.

There are now about 2,600 people carved into it. Coaches and trainers and the people who look after the equipment are on there too.

The Cup would grow taller every year if nothing were done, so when a silver band fills up it is taken off and kept safe in the Hockey Hall of Fame in Toronto, and a fresh empty band is added at the bottom.

If you spell a name wrong it stays wrong. There are spelling mistakes on the Cup that have been there for decades, and nobody rubs them out.

One whole day each

Here is the best part. Every person on the winning team gets the real Stanley Cup for one entire day, to do almost anything they like with.

Players take it home to the town they grew up in. It has been to tiny villages, to farms, to hospitals, and to schools. A man called the Keeper of the Cup travels with it and almost never lets go of it.

This tradition is newer than you might think. Teams have always celebrated with it, but giving every single person their own day started in 1995.

Things that have happened to it

Because real people carry it around, the Cup has had a rough life.

In 1905 some players celebrating in Ottawa drop-kicked it onto the frozen Rideau Canal. They went back for it the next day and there it was, sitting on the ice.

In 1924 a car taking it to a party got a flat tire. Everybody got out to fix the wheel, then drove away and left the Cup sitting on a snowbank.

It has also been dropped into swimming pools more than once, which taught everyone something useful: the Stanley Cup does not float.

Words to know

Trophy
A prize you win and hand back the next year, rather than keep.
Engraved
Letters cut into metal with a sharp tool, so they never wash off.
Governor General
The person who represents the King or Queen here in Canada.
Did you know? If your team wins, your name is carved into that silver bowl and it stays there for the rest of your life, and for everybody else's too.
