What the real BC knowledge test looks like

The Class 7L knowledge test has 50 multiple-choice questions, and you need 40 correct answers to pass. Unlike some provinces, the score is counted across the whole test rather than half by half, so a strong result on signs does help make up for a weaker result on rules.

You take the test at ICBC, and you also need a vision screening and your identification. Our mock test gives you 50 questions with a 45-minute timer so the pace feels familiar before you sit the real one.

How to pass on your first try

Read the official guide once from start to finish, then use practice questions to find your weak spots. Most people lose marks on the signs, not the rules, because there are a lot of signs and many look similar. Study the signs in small groups rather than all at once, and pay attention to shape and colour: a red circle with a line through it always means you must not do something, a yellow diamond always warns you about what is coming, and an orange sign always means road work.

Take the mock test more than once. The questions are picked at random each time, so you will see a different test on every run. When you can pass three times in a row, you are ready to book the real one.

Road Signs

6 of 80

  1. There is no painted line and no marked crosswalk at this sign. Where must you bring your vehicle to a full stop?

    • Right at the edge of the cross street, before entering itCorrect answer
    • In the middle of the intersection where you can see best
    • About one car length past the corner of the cross street
    • Anywhere within ten metres of the corner is acceptable

    Without a stop line or crosswalk you stop where the cross street begins, then creep forward only if you need a better view.

  2. On which kind of street would you most expect to find this sign?

    • A quiet residential street with a weak old bridge on itCorrect answer
    • A highway ramp leading down to a busy ferry terminal
    • A four-lane road through an industrial park at the port
    • A rural highway climbing steadily toward a mountain pass

    Communities post this where heavy vehicles would damage the road, shake nearby houses or squeeze past parked cars.

  3. This sign appears on a shaded mountain highway in November. What should you expect?

    • Ice on the surface long after the sun has dried the restCorrect answer
    • Loose gravel thrown up by the vehicle ahead of you
    • Deep puddles standing in the wheel tracks
    • Wet leaves piled along the edge of the pavement

    Shaded curves, bridges and mountain passes freeze first and thaw last, so grip can vanish without any warning.

  4. What is the main thing this sign asks you to do before the crossing?

    • Slow down enough that you could stop if a train comesCorrect answer
    • Change into a lower gear and keep your speed steady
    • Sound your horn twice as you approach the tracks
    • Switch off your radio and open your driver's window

    This is an advance warning, giving you room to slow, look both ways and listen before you reach the rails.

  5. You have just passed this sign. A person controlling traffic waves you through, but the signal beyond them shows red. What do you do?

    • Follow the person's direction, since they control this zoneCorrect answer
    • Stop and wait for the light to turn green first
    • Sound your horn to ask them to check the light
    • Reverse out of the work area and take another road

    In a work zone the person directing traffic overrides signs and signals, because they can see what the equipment is doing.

  6. What does this yellow sign warn you about?

    • A school bus stop somewhere aheadCorrect answer
    • A school that has closed
    • A no-parking area
    • A bus terminal

    It is a warning, not an order. The order to stop comes from the bus itself when its red lights flash.

Rules of the Road

6 of 100

  1. A learner in British Columbia wants to answer a call using a hands-free speaker. Is that allowed?

    • No, learner and novice drivers may not use any electronic deviceCorrect answer
    • Yes, because hands-free is allowed for every driver
    • Yes, but only on roads posted at 50 km/h or less
    • Yes, if the call lasts less than one minute

    In British Columbia learners and novice drivers may not use any electronic device at all, not even a hands-free one, while fully licensed drivers may use hands-free.

  2. You are about to pass a person riding a bicycle. How much space should you leave?

    • At least one metre between your vehicle and the bicycleCorrect answer
    • Just enough to slip by without touching the bicycle
    • Half a metre is plenty at low speed
    • Whatever space is left over in your lane

    Leave at least one metre when you pass a cyclist, and more at higher speeds. Never try to share your lane with a bicycle.

  3. What is the penalty in British Columbia for using a hand-held phone while driving?

    • A fine of several hundred dollars plus four penalty pointsCorrect answer
    • A warning letter for the first offence
    • One penalty point and no fine at all
    • An automatic one-year licence suspension

    In British Columbia using a hand-held device while driving brings a heavy fine and four penalty points, and repeat offences add an extra insurance premium.

  4. How should your side mirrors be set?

    • So that blind spots are as small as possible, with just a sliver of your own vehicle in viewCorrect answer
    • So that you can see the whole side of your own vehicle
    • So that they point down at the road beside the wheels
    • So that both of them show the same view as the rear-view mirror

    Side mirrors turned outward shrink your blind spots, but they never remove them, so a shoulder check is still needed before you move over.

  5. From October to April, what do BC winter tire signs on mountain routes require?

    • Winter tires or chains on the designated highwaysCorrect answer
    • All-season tires only
    • Four-wheel drive
    • Nothing, they are advice only

    On designated BC highways it is a legal requirement in that window, not a suggestion, and you can be fined or turned back without them.

  6. How can you tell whether a tire has enough tread left?

    • Check the wear bars moulded into the groovesCorrect answer
    • Count the years since purchase
    • Look at the colour
    • Feel the temperature

    Every tire has raised wear bars between the grooves. When the tread wears down level with them, the tire is finished and must be replaced.

Common questions

How many questions are on the real BC Class 7L knowledge test?

There are fifty multiple-choice questions and you need forty correct to pass, with forty-five minutes on the clock. Our mock test copies all three of those numbers so the pace feels familiar on the day.

Do I have to pass the signs and the rules separately?

No. British Columbia counts the whole test together, so forty correct out of fifty is a pass no matter which section those answers came from. That is different from Ontario, where each half is judged on its own.

Is this website connected to ICBC?

Not at all. We are an independent free practice site with no link to ICBC or the Government of British Columbia. The official free study guide is called Learn to Drive Smart, and we recommend reading it right through.
