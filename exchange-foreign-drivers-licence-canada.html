Trading a Foreign Licence for a Canadian One

Some countries trade straight across with no test at all. Many do not. And the clock starts the day you arrive — in Ontario it is only sixty days.

The first thing to know is the deadline

Every province gives a new resident a grace period to drive on the licence they arrived with. They are not the same length, and Ontario's is the shortest of the lot. If you have just landed, this is the number to write down before anything else.

ProvinceHow long you may drive on your own licence
Ontario60 days
British Columbia90 days
Saskatchewan90 days
Manitoba3 months
Nova Scotia180 days
Quebec6 months after settling

Ontario: who can trade straight across

Ontario has exchange agreements with every other Canadian province and territory, the Canadian Armed Forces, the United States, and a specific list of countries: Australia, Austria, Belgium, Croatia, Denmark, France, Germany, Great Britain, Hungary, the Isle of Man, Japan, Kosovo, New Zealand, Northern Ireland, the Republic of Ireland, South Korea, Switzerland, Taiwan and Ukraine.

Read that list carefully, because Ontario's is noticeably shorter than the western provinces'. The Netherlands, Italy, Spain, Portugal, Sweden, Norway and Poland are not on it, even though they are on Alberta's, Saskatchewan's and Manitoba's. Arriving in Ontario from most of Europe does not mean a straight exchange.

Some countries need an extra document, usually an abstract or letter from the licensing authority, consulate or embassy dated within the last six months, on letterhead, showing when you were first licensed. Japan, South Korea, Taiwan, Kosovo, Ukraine, Hungary, Croatia and Denmark all have a requirement of that kind.

Full G or G2 is decided by your years, not your country

This is the single most misunderstood part of the whole process. The country decides whether you may exchange at all. How much you have driven decides what you get.

Two years or more of experience in the last three years gets you a full Class G licence with no road test. Less than that gets you a G2, and you become eligible for the full licence once you have two years of experience in total. Either way you take a vision test, and there is no knowledge test and no road test where the exchange is granted.

Your existing licence is surrendered when you exchange. Japan is the one exception on the list — Japanese licences are not surrendered.

If your country is not on the list

You do not exchange. You enter Ontario's graduated licensing system like a new driver — but not from zero, because experience abroad can be credited.

The maximum credit for a car licence is twelve months. With twelve months credited you may take the G2 road test straight away, and then there is a mandatory twelve-month wait before the full G road test, which cannot be shortened for drivers from countries without an agreement. You still take the vision test, the knowledge test and both road tests.

You can simply declare your experience, in which case the credit is limited to what your licence itself shows. The stronger route is a letter or driver's abstract from the licensing authority, consulate or embassy, issued within the last six months, on official letterhead, giving your name or licence number, the date you were first licensed, your date of birth, the licence classes, the status and the expiry date. If it is not in English or French it needs a recognised translation.

Worth knowing if you ride: the twelve-month cap applies to the car licence. For a motorcycle licence an acceptable letter can credit more than twelve months.

The international driving permit, and the trap in it

An international driving permit is not a licence. It is a translation of the licence you already hold, and it is only valid when carried together with that licence. It gives you no right to drive that your own licence does not already give you.

Here is the part that catches people out. You must get it in the country that issued your licence, before you travel. Ontario says it plainly: you need it with you when you arrive, and you cannot apply for one once you are here. In Canada the permits issued are for Canadian licences, so once you have landed it is too late to get one for a foreign licence.

The other provinces, briefly

Alberta, Saskatchewan and Manitoba have much longer country lists than Ontario, covering most of western and central Europe. Nova Scotia's list is in the middle and includes Italy, the Netherlands, Portugal and Sweden, which Ontario's does not.

For British Columbia and Quebec this page deliberately gives no country list. British Columbia keeps its list inside an interactive panel we could not read reliably, and Quebec does not publish one consolidated list at all — its agreements exist one country at a time in regulation. Rather than guess, go to the provincial page and check your own country.

One province is unusually clear about experience. British Columbia's public insurer states that it will credit up to fifteen years of driving experience on proof of the date you were first licensed, using a current licence, a driving record or a letter of experience, translated by an approved translator.

Does driving abroad lower your insurance?

In Ontario, most insurers will look at a letter of experience from your previous insurer or licensing authority abroad. But that is company practice, not a rule. Ontario's insurance regulator lists how long you have held a licence as a rating factor and says nothing at all about experience gained in another country, and neither does its guidance to insurers.

So ask your broker before you assume it will count, and get the letter before you leave your old country. It is far easier to obtain while you are still there.

Where these facts come from

The Ontario detail on this page comes from ontario.ca and from DriveTest, which delivers licensing for the Ministry of Transportation, and the other provinces from their own government or public insurer pages.

Country lists change, and they have changed recently. Ontario added Kosovo and Ukraine; Saskatchewan added two dozen European countries in 2023 and Manitoba a similar number. Treat any list, including this one, as a starting point and confirm your own country on the provincial page before you make plans.

If your country is not on the list you can still claim up to 12 months of driving experience with a letter from the licensing authority. This page also explains why an international driving permit must be obtained before you leave home.

Common questions

How long can I drive in Ontario on my foreign licence?

A new resident has 60 days. That is the shortest grace period of the provinces on this page — British Columbia and Saskatchewan give 90 days, Nova Scotia 180 and Quebec six months.

Will I get a full G licence or a G2?

Two years or more of experience in the last three years gets a full G with no road test. Less than that gets a G2. The country decides whether you may exchange at all; your experience decides which licence.

What if my country has no agreement with Ontario?

You enter graduated licensing, but you can be credited up to 12 months of experience with a letter or abstract from the licensing authority issued in the last six months.

Can I get an international driving permit after I arrive in Canada?

No. It must be obtained in the country that issued your licence, before you travel. It is a translation of your licence, not a licence in itself.

Does my driving experience abroad lower my insurance?

Most Ontario insurers will consider a letter of experience, but that is company practice, not a rule set by the regulator. Ask your broker, and get the letter before you leave your old country.
