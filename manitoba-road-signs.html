Read the shape and the colour first

Canadian road signs tell you a lot before you even read the words. A red circle with a slash means you must not do the thing shown, while a green circle means you may. A yellow diamond warns you about something ahead, and an orange diamond means road work. Green and blue signs give you directions and services. Signs are the same right across Canada, so what you learn here also travels with you to another province. Once the shapes and colours are second nature, the sign questions become much easier.

  1. Where exactly must you stop at this sign?

    • At the stop line, or before the crosswalk if there is no lineCorrect answer
    • Anywhere in the intersection
    • Level with the sign post
    • Past the crosswalk

    Stop at the line first. If your view is blocked from there, creep forward slowly and stop a second time once you can see.

  2. What is the difference between this sign and a stop sign?

    • You must stop only if there is no safe gapCorrect answer
    • It means the same thing
    • You never have to slow down
    • It applies only to trucks

    A yield lets you keep rolling when the way is clear. A stop sign requires a full stop every time, clear road or not.

  3. What should you do when you see this sign?

    • Begin adjusting your speed now so you are at the new limit when you reach itCorrect answer
    • Ignore it until the next sign
    • Speed up
    • Brake hard immediately

    It warns that the limit changes just ahead. Adjusting early is smoother and safer than braking at the sign itself.

  4. Why is this sign placed well before the stop sign itself?

    • Because the stop sign is hidden by a hill or treesCorrect answer
    • To mark a parking area
    • To show a detour
    • To allow faster driving

    Start braking here so you arrive at the stop line already slow. It appears where the stop sign cannot be seen from far away.

  5. You are facing this sign at the end of a ramp. What has happened?

    • You are about to drive against traffic and must not enterCorrect answer
    • The ramp is closed for repairs
    • Trucks are banned
    • Parking is banned

    This is the sign drivers see when about to enter an off-ramp the wrong way. Stop, and find the correct entrance.

  6. What is banned where this sign stands?

    • Turning rightCorrect answer
    • Passing
    • Turning left
    • Parking

    Carry on to the next intersection instead. The ban usually exists because of a one-way street or a blind corner.

  7. When may you use a lane marked with this diamond?

    • When you carry at least the number of people shownCorrect answer
    • Only on weekends
    • Only if you drive a taxi
    • Any time you are late

    High occupancy vehicle lanes reward carpooling. Driving alone in one is a ticket, even when the lane is empty.

  8. How far back must you stop behind a Manitoba school bus with red lights flashing?

    • At least 5 metresCorrect answer
    • At least 1 metre
    • At least 20 metres
    • Any distance

    Leave at least five metres so children have room to walk around the bus. Wait until the lights stop and the bus moves off.

  9. What is the reduced speed limit in a Manitoba school zone during posted hours?

    • 30 km/hCorrect answer
    • 50 km/h
    • 40 km/h
    • 20 km/h

    Thirty in a marked school zone during the hours on the sign. Fines in these zones are increased.

  10. Why does this sign matter even when no children are visible?

    • A child can appear from behind a hedge or parked car at any momentCorrect answer
    • It only applies on weekends
    • It only applies in summer
    • It marks a parking lot

    Playground signs mark places where a child can run into the road with no warning, chasing a ball. Stay ready either way.

  11. How close to the nearest rail may you stop at a railway crossing?

    • No closer than 5 metresCorrect answer
    • 10 centimetres
    • There is no rule
    • Right at the rail

    Five metres minimum. Never start across until you can see the far side is clear, and remember one train can hide another.

  12. What does this X-shaped sign mark?

    • A railway crossingCorrect answer
    • A hospital
    • A crossroads
    • A construction zone

    The crossbuck marks the crossing itself. Slow, look and listen — many Manitoba crossings have no gates or lights.

  13. What is the main risk on the rural road this sign marks?

    • Wide, slow machines that may turn into a field with little warningCorrect answer
    • Machines that travel very fast
    • Machines that are banned from the road
    • Machines that always have right of way

    Tractors and combines are wide and slow and block your view ahead. Hold back until you can see far, and watch for a left turn.

  14. A vehicle ahead displays this orange triangle. What does it mean?

    • It travels at 40 km/h or lessCorrect answer
    • It is a student driver
    • It is broken down
    • It carries dangerous goods

    The slow-moving vehicle sign marks tractors, buggies and similar. Slow early — you close on them much faster than you expect.

  15. You hit the brakes for the animal on this sign and it runs off. What should you do next?

    • Stay alert — deer travel in groupsCorrect answer
    • Turn off your lights
    • Sound your horn continuously
    • Speed up to make up time

    Where there is one deer there are usually more. Keep your speed down for the next stretch and scan the ditches.

  16. What changes after this sign?

    • Traffic will come toward you in the next laneCorrect answer
    • The road becomes one way
    • A passing lane opens
    • The limit doubles

    The road stops being divided. Keep right and do not pass without a clear view of the whole road ahead.

  17. How should you drive on the section this sign warns about?

    • Smoothly — no sudden braking, steering or accelerationCorrect answer
    • Brake hard before every curve
    • Accelerate through it
    • Change lanes often

    Low grip punishes sudden inputs. Gentle steering and braking keep the tires working instead of sliding.

  18. Why slow down for the hazard on this sign?

    • A bump unloads the tires for a moment, reducing grip and steeringCorrect answer
    • It is only a suggestion
    • It damages your paint
    • It uses fuel

    Crossing a bump at speed lifts weight off the tires exactly when you may need them. Slow before it, not on it.

  19. What does this sign tell you about the road you are on?

    • It ends ahead and you must turn left or rightCorrect answer
    • It becomes one way
    • A signal is coming
    • It narrows

    Slow early. Arriving at a T intersection too fast is a common single-vehicle crash on rural roads.

  20. A row of these markers lines a bend. What do they tell you?

    • Which way the road curves, and how sharplyCorrect answer
    • Where to park
    • The distance to town
    • A cycling route

    Chevrons follow the outside of the curve. Closely spaced chevrons mean a sharper bend than widely spaced ones.

  21. When should you move over after seeing this sign?

    • Early, while a safe gap still existsCorrect answer
    • At the final cone
    • After the lane ends
    • Only if waved in

    Merging early and smoothly keeps traffic flowing. Racing to the front and squeezing in is what creates the jam.

  22. How do detour signs work?

    • They form a chain — follow each one to the nextCorrect answer
    • They are advisory only
    • They mark the end of a road
    • They show where to park

    Follow the marked route rather than inventing a shortcut, which often ends at another closed road.

  23. How do you treat a flashing red light in Manitoba?

    • Exactly like a stop signCorrect answer
    • Drive through without slowing
    • Wait for green
    • Exactly like a yield sign

    Full stop, then go when the way is clear. It will not turn green, so waiting for green would leave you there all day.

  24. A green arrow points left while the round light beside it is red. What may you do?

    • Turn left — oncoming traffic is being heldCorrect answer
    • Go straight
    • Turn left only after yielding
    • Nothing

    The advance green arrow protects your turn. The red still blocks going straight until it changes.

  25. What do broken white lines between lanes mean?

    • Traffic beside you goes the same way and you may change lanesCorrect answer
    • Traffic comes toward you
    • You may not change lanes
    • The road is one way

    White separates lanes going the same direction. Solid white asks you to stay put; broken white allows a lane change when safe.

  26. What does a broken yellow line beside your lane allow?

    • Passing when the road ahead is clearCorrect answer
    • Nothing
    • Parking
    • Driving in either direction

    Yellow separates opposite directions. Broken means you may pass when you can see far enough to finish safely.

  27. Which route does this maple-leaf marker identify?

    • The Trans-Canada HighwayCorrect answer
    • A toll road
    • A city ring road
    • A provincial park road

    The Trans-Canada crosses Manitoba from east to west and passes through Winnipeg on its way across the country.

  28. Who may park in a space marked with this sign?

    • Only a vehicle displaying a valid accessible parking permitCorrect answer
    • Anyone for a short stop
    • Anyone after 6 p.m.
    • Delivery vehicles

    Only a permit holder, at any hour. Parking there without one carries a heavy fine in every Manitoba municipality.

  29. Who most needs to read this sign?

    • Anyone driving a tall vehicle or carrying a roof loadCorrect answer
    • Motorcyclists only
    • Cyclists only
    • Nobody

    The number is the height of the opening ahead. If your vehicle or load is taller, you must take another route.

  30. What is ahead when you see this sign?

    • A bridge narrower than the road you are onCorrect answer
    • A truck-only bridge
    • A closed bridge
    • A toll bridge

    Slow down, stay well to your side of the centre line, and do not try to pass on the bridge.

About this page

Every road sign we use for the Manitoba practice test is on this page, drawn by us and sorted into groups. Study a group, then press the button underneath to practise only the questions that use those signs. Sign recognition is one of the three areas the real test covers, and it is the one people most often lose marks on.

What you'll find here

  • Signs are grouped so lookalikes sit together and the differences become obvious.
  • Under every group is a practice button that runs only the questions using those signs.
  • Every drawing scales cleanly, so a sign reads just as well on a phone as on a laptop.

Worth remembering

  • Shape and colour carry meaning before you read a word: a red octagon means stop, a yellow diamond warns, and a red circle with a slash forbids.
  • Manitoba's rural highways carry many crossbuck railway signs with no gates or lights, so recognising them matters more here than in a city.
  • The orange slow-moving vehicle triangle is common on prairie roads and marks anything travelling at 40 km/h or less.
