Read the shape and the colour first

Canadian road signs tell you a lot before you even read the words. A red circle with a slash means you must not do the thing shown, while a green circle means you may. A yellow diamond warns you about something ahead, and an orange diamond means road work. Green and blue signs give you directions and services. Signs are the same right across Canada, so what you learn here also travels with you to another province. Once the shapes and colours are second nature, the sign questions become much easier.

About this page

Every road sign we use for the Manitoba practice test is on this page, drawn by us and sorted into groups. Study a group, then press the button underneath to practise only the questions that use those signs. Sign recognition is one of the three areas the real test covers, and it is the one people most often lose marks on.

What you'll find here

  • Signs are grouped so lookalikes sit together and the differences become obvious.
  • Under every group is a practice button that runs only the questions using those signs.
  • Every drawing scales cleanly, so a sign reads just as well on a phone as on a laptop.

Worth remembering

  • Shape and colour carry meaning before you read a word: a red octagon means stop, a yellow diamond warns, and a red circle with a slash forbids.
  • Manitoba's rural highways carry many crossbuck railway signs with no gates or lights, so recognising them matters more here than in a city.
  • The orange slow-moving vehicle triangle is common on prairie roads and marks anything travelling at 40 km/h or less.
