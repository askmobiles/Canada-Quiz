Read the shape and the colour first

Canadian road signs tell you a lot before you even read the words. A red circle with a slash means you must not do the thing shown, while a green circle means you may. A yellow diamond warns you about something ahead, and an orange diamond means road work. Green and blue signs give you directions and services. Signs are the same right across Canada, so what you learn here also travels with you to another province. Once the shapes and colours are second nature, the sign questions become much easier.

  1. There is no painted line and no marked crosswalk at this sign. Where must you bring your vehicle to a full stop?

    • Right at the edge of the cross street, before entering itCorrect answer
    • In the middle of the intersection where you can see best
    • About one car length past the corner of the cross street
    • Anywhere within ten metres of the corner is acceptable

    Without a stop line or crosswalk you stop where the cross street begins, then creep forward only if you need a better view.

  2. You meet this sign on a short ramp joining a busy highway. What does it ask of you?

    • Adjust your speed and enter only when there is a safe gapCorrect answer
    • Come to a full stop at the end of the ramp every time
    • Speed up so highway traffic has to make room for you
    • Sound your horn and move over into the nearest lane

    Yielding means the other traffic keeps its right of way, so you time your entry to fit a real gap instead of forcing one.

  3. Rain is falling hard on the road where this sign is posted. How should you treat the number?

    • As a suggestion that only applies to large or heavy vehicles
    • As the lowest speed you are allowed to travel on this road

    A posted limit is a maximum for good conditions; rain, snow or fog mean the safe speed is lower than the number.

    A begins sign marks the exact spot the new limit takes effect, so plan your slowing down before you reach it.

About this quiz

The sign questions are the part of the Class 7L test you can study with your eyes. This page draws every sign as a clean graphic and sorts them into six groups, with the name written underneath so the picture and the meaning settle in together. Nothing on this page is timed.

Pick a group from the row of buttons, look through the gallery, then press the practice button below it to answer questions on that group alone. Each answer is marked straight away with a short explanation. Working through one group at a time is far kinder to your memory than swallowing every sign at once.

Canadian signs are broadly the same from one province to another, so what you learn here still serves you if you move. This is unofficial practice and we are not affiliated with ICBC or the Government of British Columbia. The free official guide is Learn to Drive Smart.

What's inside

  • The signs are split into six groups: regulatory, warning, temporary, information, traffic lights and road markings.
  • Eighty signs are drawn on the page, each one shown with its proper name below the picture.
  • A row of buttons across the top switches between the groups, and the page opens on the regulatory group.
  • A short note above each gallery explains what that whole family of signs is there to do.
  • A practice button under each gallery quizzes you on the signs in that group only.
  • There is no timer anywhere on this page, so you can sit with one sign for as long as you want.

Good to know

  • A red circle with a line through it always forbids something, while a green circle tells you the action is allowed.
  • A yellow diamond warns you about what lies ahead, and an orange diamond means people are working on the road.
  • Green and blue signs are the helpful ones, pointing you toward directions, services and useful places.
  • Learn the families first and you can often work out a sign you have never seen before from its shape and colour alone.
  • Signs about school zones, playgrounds and crossings come up often, because getting them wrong puts people on foot at risk.

Common questions

Do I have to memorise all eighty signs?

You do not have to recite them, but you should recognise them. Learn the six families and what each shape and colour means first, and the individual signs fall into place much more easily.

Is this study page timed like the mock test?

No. There is no clock at all here, so you can stay with one group of signs as long as you want and practise it as many times as you like. The forty-five-minute timer only appears on the mock test.

Are British Columbia road signs different from those in other provinces?

The great majority are shared right across Canada, because shapes and colours follow one national pattern. A few local signs vary, but learning these families prepares you almost anywhere in the country.

How do I practise only the signs in one group?

Choose the group at the top of the page, then press the practice button under its gallery. The questions that follow come only from that group, and each one ends with a short explanation.

Where do the sign questions come from?

We wrote all of them ourselves, using the free Learn to Drive Smart guide as our reference. There are sixty sign questions in our British Columbia bank, and this page draws its practice from them.
