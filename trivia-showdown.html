🏆 Trivia Showdown

Two teams. Take turns. Who knows the most?

📖 How to play

Split into two teams — Team Red and Team Blue. Teams take turns: read the question out loud, the team talks it over and picks an answer. A correct answer scores a point, then it's the other team's turn. Play through all the questions (or as many as you like) and the team with the most points wins! Pass one phone or tablet around, or play on a big screen.

About this game

Trivia Showdown turns one phone or one screen into a family game night. You split into Team Red and Team Blue, take turns answering questions, and watch the two scores climb side by side. It works with two people or with two big noisy teams.

The questions are mixed on purpose. Some are about Canada, some about animals, space or simple arithmetic, so a seven-year-old and a grandparent both get chances to shine. Nobody is stuck on one subject, and every round feels winnable for both sides.

Nothing is saved and nobody signs up. Open the page, pick a colour, and play. When the last question is answered the page announces the winning team, or calls a tie, and the Restart button shuffles everything for a fresh round.

How it works

  • Two teams play: Team Red on the left and Team Blue on the right, each with its own big running score.
  • The team whose turn it is gets a green border, so nobody argues about who should be answering.
  • Every question gives four answer choices, and the correct one turns green the moment somebody picks.
  • A right answer adds one point, a wrong answer scores nothing, and the turn passes to the other side either way.
  • The question bank holds thirty questions covering Canadian facts, animals, planets, colours, seasons and easy arithmetic.
  • A counter shows which question you are on, and the final screen declares the winning team or a tie.

Tips for playing

  • Read the question out loud before anyone touches the screen, so both teams hear it and the quieter players get a chance.
  • Let the team talk it over for ten seconds. Group discussion is where this game gets funny and where children learn the most.
  • The questions are shuffled every round, so a rematch never follows the same order and nobody can memorise the answers.
  • If your teams are uneven, put the youngest players together and let the adults suggest an answer only when they are asked.
  • Because the turn changes after every question, both teams get exactly the same number of chances: the rest is luck of the draw.

Common questions

Do we need two phones to play?

No. One device is enough. Pass it between the teams, or put a laptop on the table so everyone can see. Only one team answers at a time, so a single screen works well.

How many players can be on a team?

As many as you like. The page only tracks two team scores, not individual people, so you can play one against one or five against five without changing anything.

How long does a full game take?

Playing all thirty questions usually takes fifteen to twenty minutes. You can also stop early: whoever is ahead on the scoreboard at that moment is the winner.

Are the questions too hard for young children?

Most are aimed at primary-school level and offer four choices. Younger players often do very well, especially when an older teammate reads the words out for them.

Does the page keep our scores after we close it?

No, scores reset when the page reloads and nothing is stored about you. Write the results down yourself if you want to run a family championship over several evenings.
