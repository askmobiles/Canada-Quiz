🍁 Canadian Symbols Explained: The Flag, the Beaver, the Maple Leaf and More

Discover the meaning behind Canada's national symbols — the maple leaf, the flag, the beaver, the national anthem, and more.

Every country has special symbols that stand for its people and history. Canada has several well-known symbols that you will see on flags, coins, and buildings. Here is what they mean.

The maple leaf

The maple leaf is Canada's most famous symbol. Maple trees grow across much of the country, and their leaves turn beautiful shades of red and orange in the fall. The maple leaf stands for nature, unity, and Canadian identity.

The Canadian flag

Canada's flag is red and white with a single red maple leaf in the middle. It became the official flag in 1965. Red and white are Canada's official colours.

The beaver

The beaver is an official symbol of Canada. It was important in the early fur trade that helped build the country, and it stands for hard work and building — beavers are famous for building dams.

The national anthem

Canada's national anthem is O Canada. It is sung at schools, sports games, and special events across the country, in both English and French.

Other symbols

  • The Coat of Arms, with the motto “From Sea to Sea”
  • The loonie and the toonie — nicknames for the one- and two-dollar coins
  • The Canadian horse and the common loon
  • The colours red and white

Canada's head of state is the King, currently King Charles III, who is represented in Canada by the Governor General. Learning these symbols is a common part of the citizenship test. Practise them with our free quizzes.
