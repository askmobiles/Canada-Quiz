About this game

Word Illusions is a quick brain teaser built on one small trick. A colour word appears on the screen, but the letters are printed in a different colour, and your job is to tap the colour of the letters rather than the word you read. This game was called Color Match here once, and it took a new name when a colour-mixing game arrived and claimed the old one.

A time bar runs down above the word and it is deliberately short. Every correct tap hands a little time back and every wrong tap takes a slice away, so a long run is a chain of fast, confident choices rather than careful ones. When the bar empties the round is over and your score appears.

Anyone who can read colour names can play, from a child in the early school years to a grandparent. Reading happens by itself while looking takes effort, which is exactly why the game bites harder the faster you go. It is a fair little test of focus and reaction speed, and a round is short enough to fit into a spare few minutes.

How it works

  • Each round puts four buttons on the screen, drawn from six colours: red, blue, green, orange, purple and yellow.
  • The word you read and the colour of the letters almost never agree, and that mismatch is the whole puzzle.
  • A time bar counts down at the top of the game and the round ends the moment it empties.
  • A correct tap puts a small amount of time back on the bar, while a wrong tap removes a larger amount.
  • Your score climbs with every correct answer and stays visible while you play.
  • Your best score is remembered on the device you played on, so there is always something to beat.

Tips for playing

  • The puzzle is a version of the Stroop effect, in which naming the colour of a mismatched word reliably takes longer than reading it.
  • Letting your eyes go slightly soft, so that you see letter shapes instead of reading them, takes much of the pressure out of the word.
  • Hurrying is rewarded here, since only a correct tap returns time to the bar while a pause costs you with nothing gained.
  • Holding a phone in both hands puts every button within a thumb's reach and shaves a fraction off each answer.
  • Focus and rest matter, because the same player scores lower when tired than after a good night's sleep.

Common questions

Do I tap the word or the colour?

Always the colour the letters are printed in, never the word they spell. That sounds easy until the word red arrives in blue letters and your eyes insist on reading it first.

Why is this so hard when the rule is so simple?

Reading is automatic for anyone who reads well, so the meaning of the word arrives before you have decided to look at the colour. Beating the word takes deliberate effort, and effort takes time the bar will not give you.

Where is my best score stored?

It sits in your own browser on the device you played on and never leaves it. Clearing your browsing data or moving to another phone starts the record again from zero.

Is there anything to install?

Nothing at all. The game runs inside this page, so a phone, tablet or laptop with a browser is enough, and there is no account and no email address to hand over.

Is the game suitable for children?

Any child who can read the six colour names can join in, though the short timer feels rushed at first. Sitting beside a younger child and calling the colour out loud together makes it far friendlier.
