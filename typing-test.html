How fast can you type? Find your words per minute!

📖 How to play

Start typing the sentence shown in the box. The timer starts on your first letter. Correct letters turn green, mistakes turn red. When you finish the sentence you'll see your speed in words per minute (WPM) and your accuracy. Press New Text to try a different one. Best on a computer keyboard!

About this game

The Typing Speed Test shows you how fast and how accurately you type. A short sentence appears, you copy it, and the page counts your words per minute and the share of letters you got right. It takes under a minute.

It suits students learning the keyboard, adults returning to office work, and anyone simply curious about their speed. The sentences are ordinary and friendly, and several of them are about Canada, so you are reading real words rather than random letters.

Nothing is timed until you press your first key, so there is no pressure while you settle your hands. Finish the sentence and you get a short, kind comment on your result, plus a button to load a different text.

How it works

  • Three live boxes at the top show your speed in words per minute, your accuracy percentage, and the seconds elapsed.
  • The sentence sits above the typing area, with the letter you are currently on gently highlighted in blue.
  • Correct letters turn green as you type and any wrong letter turns red, so mistakes are visible straight away.
  • The clock starts on your very first keystroke and stops by itself the moment you reach the end of the sentence.
  • Pressing New Text loads another sentence from the collection, several of which mention beavers, hockey and the northern lights.
  • The closing message changes with your speed, from a gentle nudge to keep practising up to a rocket for the fastest typists.

Tips for playing

  • Accuracy matters more than raw speed. Fixing errors costs more time than typing carefully, so slow down slightly and watch the red disappear.
  • Rest your fingers on the home row: left hand on A S D F, right hand on J K L and the semicolon, then reach out from there.
  • Try not to look down at the keys. Keeping your eyes on the sentence feels harder for about a week, then it becomes much faster.
  • Short daily practice beats one long session. Five minutes a day builds the finger memory that makes typing feel automatic.
  • A physical keyboard gives a truer result than a phone screen, because thumb typing and touch typing use completely different movements.

Common questions

Can I take the test on a phone?

You can, and the page works fine, but the score reflects thumb typing rather than keyboard skill. A laptop or desktop keyboard gives a far more useful words-per-minute figure.

What counts as a good typing speed?

Comfortable everyday typing sits around forty words per minute, and experienced office typists often pass seventy. Children learning the keyboard are doing well anywhere above twenty.

How is my accuracy worked out?

The page compares each letter you typed with the letter that belongs in that position, then shows the percentage that matched. Deleting a mistake and retyping it improves the figure.

Is my result stored anywhere?

No. The numbers live only on your screen while the page is open and vanish when you reload. There is no account and no sign-in of any kind.

Can a whole class use this together?

Yes. Each learner opens the page on their own computer, types the same short sentence and reads out their speed. Nothing has to be installed or set up beforehand.
