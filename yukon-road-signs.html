Yukon Road Signs

Yukon runs one combined written and sign recognition test, so sign recognition is part of what you sit rather than a separate exam. Signs here are grouped by type. Look at the pictures first, then practise the group with real test questions. No timer.

Read the shape and the colour first

Canadian road signs tell you a great deal before you read a single word. A red circle with a slash means you must not do the thing shown, and a green circle means you may. A yellow diamond warns about something ahead, an orange diamond means road work, and green and blue signs give directions and services.

Two of them do more work in this territory than they do further south. The handbook shows a regulatory sign meaning that winter tires or chains must be used where it is displayed, which turns a piece of good advice into a legal requirement on that stretch of road. A posted speed limit is the other one, because Yukon states plainly that speed limits are set for optimal road conditions, and on gravel or frost-heaved pavement the safe speed is well below the number.

The school bus signals belong in the same list. Learn what the upper red lights mean before you meet a bus on a highway rather than after, because the rule that follows them changes depending on whether the road is divided by a median.

When the signs feel automatic, move on to the rules half, then put the two together in the timed mock test.

Where to get the official information

Everything on this page comes from what Yukon publishes for free. The territory writes its own book, the Yukon Driver's Basic Handbook, and it is free to download from yukon.ca, so any site telling you that Yukon borrows another jurisdiction's handbook is simply wrong. There is an official French edition as well, the Guide de la route du Yukon. Whether the test itself can be sat in French is not published anywhere, even though the handbook and the licensing pages are bilingual, so we do not claim that it can. Read the handbook alongside this practice, not instead of it.

This is an unofficial free practice site. It is not affiliated with Yukon Motor Vehicles, with Highways and Public Works or with the Government of Yukon, and it cannot issue a licence. Always check the official pages for the current rules and fees before you book.

All 30 road sign questions, with every answer explained

This is the whole question bank from the test above, written out so you can read straight through it. The right answer is marked, and every question has a short explanation of why it is the right one.

Show all 30 questions

  1. You reach this sign at a Yukon intersection. What does it require?

    • A complete stop, then go on only when the way is clearCorrect answer
    • A rolling stop if nothing is coming
    • Stopping only when a pedestrian is waiting
    • Flashing your headlights and carrying on

    A stop means the wheels stop turning. Stop at the stop line, or before the crosswalk if none is painted, and move off only when you can do it without forcing anyone else to slow down.

  2. What does this red and white triangle require of you?

    • Give way to the traffic and pedestrians already using the roadCorrect answer
    • Stop and stay stopped until someone waves you on
    • Speed up so you fit into the gap
    • Turn right without checking

    Yield means give way. Slow right down, be ready to stop, and join only when you will not make another driver or a pedestrian change what they are doing.

  3. This sign faces you at the mouth of a street. What does it mean?

    • You must not enter, because traffic here comes towards youCorrect answer
    • Local traffic only
    • Entry allowed outside busy hours
    • Heavy vehicles must find another route

    The street runs the other way. Driving past this sign puts you head-on into traffic that has no reason to expect you.

  4. A sign like this is posted beside a Yukon road. What does the number mean?

    • The highest speed allowed, and only when conditions are goodCorrect answer
    • A speed suggested only for good weather
    • The slowest speed you may legally travel
    • An average speed to hold over the whole trip

    A posted limit is a ceiling, never a target. Yukon states that speed limits are set for optimal road conditions, so on gravel, frost heaves, ice or poor visibility the safe speed is lower than the number on the sign.

  5. What does this regulatory sign prohibit?

    • Passing another vehicle along this stretchCorrect answer
    • Stopping on the shoulder
    • Parking overnight
    • Towing a trailer

    Passing is banned here, normally because you cannot see far enough ahead to pull back in safely. On a northern highway that view can also be blocked by dust or blowing snow.

  6. Where this sign is posted, what are you still allowed to do?

    • Stop briefly while somebody gets in or outCorrect answer
    • Leave the vehicle for a few minutes
    • Park if you switch on your hazard lights
    • Nothing at all is permitted here

    No parking still lets you pause while a passenger gets in or out. It is the sign that bans stopping altogether that takes even that away.

  7. This sign goes further than a no-parking sign. In what way?

    • It bans stopping for any reason, even to let someone outCorrect answer
    • It applies only during the winter months
    • It applies only to trucks
    • It bans parking but still allows loading

    This is the strictest of the stopping signs. You may not halt here at all, so plan to drop a passenger somewhere else.

  8. A traffic island lies ahead with this sign on it. What must you do?

    • Pass to the right of the islandCorrect answer
    • Pass on whichever side is clear
    • Stop before the island and wait
    • Reverse and find another route

    Keep right of the island. The sign is placed where the road splits around something, and your half of the road is the right-hand half.

  9. Which manoeuvre does this sign forbid?

    • Turning around to head back the way you cameCorrect answer
    • Turning left at the next street
    • Reversing into a driveway
    • Passing a slower vehicle

    No U-turn. Turning right around is banned here because oncoming drivers cannot see what you are doing early enough to react.

  10. This yellow diamond stands beside a Yukon highway. What is it telling you?

    • Deer may be on or near the road aheadCorrect answer
    • Hunting is permitted beside the road
    • A wildlife viewing area is ahead
    • Livestock may be loose on the road

    The handbook uses this sign to mean watch for deer ahead. Ease off, keep scanning both shoulders and be ready for an animal to step out where you cannot see far.

  11. What hazard does this warning sign describe?

    • Rock may fall onto the road, and rock may already be lying on itCorrect answer
    • A quarry entrance is ahead
    • Loose gravel has just been spread on the surface
    • A rock crusher is working nearby

    The handbook wording is to beware of falling rock and to watch for rock already on the road ahead. Keep your eyes well down the road rather than on the cliff face.

  12. You meet this sign on a northern highway. What is ahead?

    • The paved surface ends and the road turns to gravelCorrect answer
    • A bridge deck
    • A closed section of road
    • A weigh station

    Gravel gives less grip than pavement, and Yukon warns that stopping distances get longer on it. Slow down before the surface changes and leave far more room to the vehicle ahead.

  13. What is this warning sign telling you about the road surface?

    • The surface is uneven, so slow down before you reach itCorrect answer
    • A speed hump has been built to slow traffic
    • A cattle guard crosses the road
    • Road work is under way

    Yukon roads built over permafrost can settle into waves and dips that the territory describes as feeling like a roller coaster. Take your speed off before the rough part, not in the middle of it.

  14. What is this yellow diamond warning you to expect?

    • The road can turn slippery, so brake and steer gentlyCorrect answer
    • A car wash entrance
    • A river crossing
    • A section closed each winter

    Grip can disappear without warning on a wet or icy surface. Smooth inputs keep the tires working; sudden braking or steering is what breaks them loose.

  15. You meet this sign at the top of a long grade. What is the safe way down?

    • Pick a lower gear before the descent and hold your speed downCorrect answer
    • Coast down in neutral to save fuel
    • Speed up so you do not lose momentum
    • Run your hazard lights the whole way down

    A lower gear lets the engine do part of the braking. Riding the brakes the whole way down heats them until they stop working well, which matters most with a loaded vehicle.

  16. What does this yellow sign say about the road in front of you?

    • There is a series of bends, so lose speed before the first oneCorrect answer
    • The road ends shortly
    • There is one sharp bend and then it straightens
    • The road narrows to a single lane

    Braking is for the straight before the bend, not for the bend itself. Set your speed early, then steer smoothly through each curve.

  17. This round yellow sign appears beside the road. What is coming up?

    • A railway crossingCorrect answer
    • A roundabout
    • A rest area
    • A tunnel entrance

    A railway crossing is coming. Slow down, look and listen both ways, and never start across unless you can clear the far side without stopping.

  18. This sign marks the start of what?

    • A school zone, where you must drop to 30 km/hCorrect answer
    • A bus terminal
    • A playground with no road beside it
    • A crossing used only in summer

    Yukon asks drivers to reduce speed to 30 kilometres an hour in school zones. Children step out from between parked cars, and 30 is the speed that leaves you time to stop.

  19. What does this diamond warn you about?

    • A bridge narrower than the road you are onCorrect answer
    • A bridge closed to all traffic
    • A ferry landing
    • A culvert with a weight restriction

    The bridge is tighter than the road leading to it. Check for oncoming traffic, hold your lane position and never try to pass on the structure.

  20. An orange diamond like this stands beside the highway. What does the orange colour mean?

    • The conditions are temporary, such as road work aheadCorrect answer
    • The warning is permanent
    • The sign applies only after dark
    • The sign marks a scenic route

    Orange means temporary. Work zones on northern highways often involve loose gravel and equipment crossing the road, so the posted work-zone speed applies whether or not you can see a crew.

  21. What does this orange sign tell you to expect?

    • A traffic control person directing traffic aheadCorrect answer
    • A police checkpoint
    • A weigh scale
    • A closed rest area

    Someone will be controlling traffic through the work zone. Slow right down, be ready to stop, and do exactly what they signal, even when the road ahead looks empty.

  22. What is this orange sign asking you to do?

    • Follow the marked route around a closed piece of roadCorrect answer
    • Stop and turn back the way you came
    • Use the shoulder to get past the work
    • Report to the work crew before continuing

    A detour sends you along a signed alternative. Keep following the detour markers rather than guessing a shortcut, because the side roads may be gravel or dead ends.

  23. What service does this blue sign point you to?

    • A hospitalCorrect answer
    • A pharmacy
    • A first-aid post at a rest stop
    • A veterinary clinic

    Blue signs point to services rather than telling you a rule. Knowing where the hospital sign is on your route matters more in a territory where the next community can be far away.

  24. What is this blue information sign telling you?

    • Fuel is available at the turn or exit aheadCorrect answer
    • A vehicle inspection station is ahead
    • A truck route begins here
    • Parking here is free

    On long Yukon highways the gap between fuel stops can be very large, so fill up when you get the chance. The territory also suggests carrying extra fuel when you travel in remote areas.

  25. What is this blue sign showing you?

    • Food services are available aheadCorrect answer
    • A picnic shelter with no services
    • A grocery store only
    • A weigh station

    The symbol marks a place to eat. On a long northern drive, planning stops around fuel and food is part of preparing the trip, not a luxury.

  26. This sign is painted on a parking space outside a store. What does it reserve the space for?

    • Only a vehicle displaying a valid accessible parking permitCorrect answer
    • Anyone stopping for less than five minutes
    • Delivery vehicles
    • Anyone, once the business has closed

    The space is reserved for a permit holder. Taking it, even briefly, can leave someone with no way to reach the door.

  27. The light turns amber as you come up to the intersection. What does that require?

    • Stop if you can do it safely, and carry on only if stopping would be dangerousCorrect answer
    • Always stop, whatever your speed
    • Always continue through
    • Sound your horn and keep going

    Amber means the red is coming. The decision is made by where you already are: close enough that hard braking would be dangerous, keep going; far enough back, stop.

  28. A signal at an intersection is flashing red. What does it require of you?

    • Come to a full stop, then go when the way is clearCorrect answer
    • Treat it exactly like a green light
    • Slow down but keep rolling through
    • Stop and wait there until it turns green

    A flashing red works like a stop sign. Stop completely, look both ways, and go only when the intersection is clear. It will not change colour.

  29. You are behind a slow truck and a double solid yellow line separates you from the oncoming lane. What does that line mean?

    • Traffic in each direction must stay on its own side and must not cross to passCorrect answer
    • You may cross it because the truck is holding up traffic
    • You may cross it in daylight only
    • It marks the outer edge of the travelled road

    Double solid yellow lines separate opposing traffic where passing is not safe for either side. Wait for a passing lane or a broken line rather than gambling on the view ahead.

  30. The centre line beside your lane is a broken yellow line. What does that allow?

    • Passing when the way ahead is clear and it is safe to do itCorrect answer
    • Parking along the centre of the road
    • Driving in either lane whenever you like
    • Passing, but only for large vehicles

    A broken yellow line permits passing; it does not promise that passing is a good idea. You still need clear sight distance, a gap long enough to get back in, and a surface with enough grip.

About this page

Yukon's regulations describe what you sit as one combined written and sign recognition test, so sign recognition is part of the single exam rather than a test of its own. This page holds the sign half of our question bank.

Every sign on this page is drawn in code rather than copied from a government publication, so nothing here is anyone else's artwork. Each sign carries its own name as a caption and in its alt text, so the page works with a screen reader too.

What you'll find here

  • Thirty sign questions covering regulatory, warning, temporary, information, traffic light and pavement marking signs.
  • Practice for each group of signs, so you can work through one family at a time.
  • The sign requiring winter tires or chains, which turns advice into law on the stretch where it stands.
  • The whole sign bank printed below the practice, with the answer marked and explained.

Worth remembering

  • A red circle with a slash means you must not do the thing shown, and a green circle means you may.
  • A yellow diamond warns about something ahead, while an orange diamond means road work.
  • The handbook shows a regulatory sign meaning that winter tires or chains must be used where it is displayed.
  • Yukon states that speed limits are set for optimal road conditions, which is why a posted number is a ceiling and never a target.
  • Signs are the same across Canada, so studying them here also prepares you for a test in another province.

Common questions

Are these the real Yukon signs?

They are our own drawings of the standard Canadian signs used in the territory. They are not copied from any official publication.

Does the real test include signs?

Yes. The regulations call it a written and sign recognition test, so sign content is part of the exam. How it is weighted is not published.

Is sign recognition scored separately?

Not as far as Yukon publishes. It describes a single combined test at 80 percent, so we keep signs and rules as separate study pages without claiming they are separate exams.

How many signs should I learn?

All of the ones on this page. They are the families the handbook covers, and once the shapes and colours are automatic the wording matters much less.
