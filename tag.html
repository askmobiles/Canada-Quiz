About this game

Tag is a chase game for two people sharing one screen. One player is IT, marked by an arrow above their head, and has to touch the other player to pass it on before the clock reaches zero.

Everything happens on a stage full of platforms you can jump between. Whoever is IT when the sixty seconds run out is the loser, so the last few seconds are usually very loud. Power-ups keep dropping in and can turn a chase around in a moment.

Because two players need room for their thumbs, the game asks you to turn a phone sideways. On a computer the two players share one keyboard instead, one on the left side and one on the right.

How it works

  • Three worlds to choose from: Maple Park with wide easy platforms, Rocky Mountains full of ledges, and a slippery Frozen Lake.
  • Each player picks a colour from the swatches, and the two players are not allowed to take the same one.
  • On-screen buttons sit in both bottom corners: jump, drop down through a platform, move left and move right.
  • On a computer, Player 1 uses the A, D, W and S keys while Player 2 uses the arrow keys.
  • Eight power-ups appear during a round, including speed, super jump, shield, freeze, swapping places and shrinking.
  • The clock at the top counts down from sixty and turns red when the time is nearly gone.

Tips for playing

  • When you are IT, do not chase in a straight line. Cut across the platforms and meet the other player where they are about to land.
  • Dropping through a platform is often faster than running to its edge, and it is the easiest way to shake off someone right behind you.
  • On the Frozen Lake you keep sliding after you let go, so begin slowing down long before you reach a gap.
  • Passing IT back and forth in the final seconds is risky. Hand it over early, then keep as much distance as you can.
  • Maple Park is the fairest world for a first game, because its wide platforms give a new player somewhere safe to stand.

Common questions

Can one person play this alone?

Not really. Tag needs two players, because both sets of controls are worked at the same time. One person could try to hold both sides, but the round is built for two.

How long does one round last?

One minute. The clock at the top starts at sixty and counts down, and the round ends the moment it hits zero, with the player who is IT taking the loss.

Why does the game ask me to rotate my phone?

Two players need buttons on both sides of the screen. Held upright, the stage is too narrow for four thumbs, so a sideways phone gives everybody enough room.

Do I lose points when I am tagged?

There are no points in this game at all. Only one thing matters: who is IT at the final second. Being tagged early is fine, as long as you pass it on in time.
