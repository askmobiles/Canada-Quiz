🏙️ Canada's Biggest Cities: A Quick Guide

Toronto, Montreal, Vancouver, Calgary, Ottawa and more — a simple guide to Canada's largest cities and what makes each one different.

Canada is enormous, but most Canadians live in a surprisingly narrow band close to the southern border, and most of those live in cities. As of April 1, 2026, Canada's population was 41,417,056 — and a large share of that lives in just a handful of urban areas.

One important thing to know first

Canadians talk about cities in two different ways, and it is easy to get confused. The city is the area inside the official city limits. The metropolitan area includes the surrounding suburbs and towns whose residents work and shop there. Toronto the city has around three million people; the wider Toronto area has well over six million. Both numbers are correct — they just measure different things.

The biggest urban areas

  • Toronto, Ontario — the largest city in the country and its financial centre. It is also one of the most multicultural cities in the world; roughly half its residents were born outside Canada. Home of the CN Tower.
  • Montreal, Quebec — the largest city in Quebec and the heart of French-speaking North America. Known for its festivals, food, universities and its old port.
  • Vancouver, British Columbia — on the Pacific coast, wedged between mountains and ocean. Mild, rainy winters and Canada's busiest port.
  • Calgary, Alberta — the centre of Canada's energy industry, close to the Rocky Mountains, and home of the Calgary Stampede.
  • Edmonton, Alberta — Alberta's capital, a northern city with a huge river valley park system.
  • Ottawa, Ontario — the national capital, home of Parliament Hill. In winter, part of the Rideau Canal becomes a skating rink.
  • Winnipeg, Manitoba — near the longitudinal centre of the country, at the meeting of the Red and Assiniboine rivers.
  • Quebec City, Quebec — the provincial capital, with a walled old town that is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
  • Hamilton, Kitchener–Waterloo and Halifax — Hamilton and the Waterloo tech region sit in southern Ontario, while Halifax is the largest city in Atlantic Canada.

Exact rankings shift a little between census updates and between city versus metropolitan measures, so treat any list as a good general picture rather than a fixed order.

Do not confuse the capital

A very common test question: Ottawa is the capital of Canada, not Toronto, Montreal or Vancouver. Queen Victoria chose it in 1857, partly because it sat between English-speaking and French-speaking regions and was further from the American border. Toronto is the capital of Ontario, and Quebec City is the capital of Quebec.

Where people actually live

Roughly nine out of ten Canadians live within about 200 kilometres of the border with the United States. The band between Windsor and Quebec City alone holds a very large share of the country's population. Head north and the map opens up: the three territories together cover about a third of Canada's land area but hold well under one percent of its people.

Quick facts for the test

  • Capital of Canada: Ottawa, Ontario.
  • Largest city: Toronto, Ontario.
  • Largest city in Quebec: Montreal. Capital of Quebec: Quebec City.
  • Largest city in Atlantic Canada: Halifax, Nova Scotia.
  • Most Canadians live close to the southern border.
