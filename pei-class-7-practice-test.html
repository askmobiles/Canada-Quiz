What Prince Edward Island publishes, and what it does not

The Province is generous about how you become a driver here and silent about the written test itself. It says the test exists, that it is based on the driving rules and safety information in the Driver's Handbook, and that you sit it at an Access PEI office along with a vision test. It does not publish how many questions the test has, what mark you need to pass, or how long you get. That is the honest starting point for this page.

Three commercial practice sites state the same question count and the same pass mark, word for word, and not one of them cites a government source for either figure. We are not going to repeat a number that decides whether somebody books a test. The fifteen signs, the fifteen rules, the twelve right needed in each half and the thirty minutes on our mock test are a clearly stated practice standard of our own, and they are not the Province's.

What the Province does publish about the exams is worth knowing, because almost nobody quotes it. If you fail the instruction permit test you wait at least seven days before you rewrite it, and there is no fee to rewrite. If you fail a road test you wait at least fourteen days for the next one. The instruction permit and the permit test each carry a published fee of twenty dollars, and fees change from time to time, so check the current page before you go.

Study the rules

The three stages, one at a time

You start at 16, and if you are under 18 a parent or guardian has to consent. Pass a vision test and the written driving examination and you hold an Instruction Driver's Permit, a Class 7 licence, which is Stage 1 of the graduated programme and is marked by a yellow L decal. The permit itself is valid for two years.

Stage 1 lasts at least 365 days, reduced to 275 days if you complete a certified driver-education program. Stage 2 and Stage 3 last 365 days each, so the whole programme takes a minimum of three years, or two years and 275 days with certified driver education. The Stage 3 restrictions lift on their own when the stage is complete.

One requirement is easy to miss and stops people booking. Before your first road test for a Class 5 licence you must complete the Novice Driver Course, unless you completed certified private driver training instead. Without one of the two you will not be permitted to book that road test at all.

The rules that catch learners out

Demerit points are stricter here for a learner than almost anywhere in the country, and this is the single most important number on the page. Any violation carrying demerit points at all suspends a Stage 1 permit for at least thirty days. There is no cushion of two or three points to lose first. A Stage 2 or Stage 3 driver is suspended at six points in twelve months or nine points in twenty-four, and a fully licensed driver at twelve.

The supervisor rule is the one most sites get wrong, because Prince Edward Island publishes two different alcohol limits for the two people in the front seat. The learner must be at zero blood alcohol. The accompanying driver must be under 0.05, which is lower than the Criminal Code limit but is not zero. That driver must have held a licence for at least four years, must hold the right class for the vehicle, and must sit beside the learner in the front passenger seat.

Two more Stage 1 rules are worth learning before you drive. Nobody other than the learner and the accompanying driver may occupy the front seat, and any other passengers must be immediate family of that accompanying driver. If you are under 21 you may not drive between one and five in the morning, and that curfew follows you into Stage 2.

Where to get the official information

Everything on this page comes from what Prince Edward Island publishes for free. The Driver's Handbook is the book the written test is based on, it is free to download from the Province's own site, and it is published in French, Arabic and Chinese as well as English. Whether the test itself is offered in those languages is not published anywhere, so we do not claim that it is. Read the handbook alongside this practice, not instead of it.

This is an unofficial free practice site. It is not affiliated with Access PEI, the Highway Safety Division or the Government of Prince Edward Island, and it cannot issue a licence. Always check the official pages for the current rules and fees before you book.

Road Signs

30 questions

  1. You are the first vehicle at this sign and the road ahead is empty. What must you do?

    • Come to a complete stop before the line, then go when the way is clearCorrect answer
    • Slow to a crawl and keep going
    • Stop only if a pedestrian is waiting
    • Sound the horn and carry on

    A stop sign means a full stop every time, empty road or not. Stop before the stop line, or before the crosswalk if there is no line, and move off only once you can see that the way is clear.

  2. A yield sign faces you as you join a busier road. What does it require?

    • Give way to traffic already on that road and enter only in a safe gapCorrect answer
    • Stop completely every time before entering
    • Accelerate so that others have to let you in
    • Enter only after sounding your horn

    Yield means give way. Slow down, be ready to stop, and pull out only when nobody already on the road has to brake or swerve because of you.

  3. This sign is posted at the mouth of a street. What is it telling you?

    • You must not drive into that street at allCorrect answer
    • Only residents may drive there
    • You may enter but not park
    • Trucks must use another route

    Do not enter. Traffic is coming towards you, so turning in would put you head-on against a one-way flow.

  4. You want to turn into a street marked with this sign. What does it allow?

    • Travel in the direction of the arrow onlyCorrect answer
    • Driving either way as long as you keep right
    • Driving there during daylight only
    • Turning in from the left lane only

    One way. Every lane runs the same direction, so your turn has to leave you travelling the way the arrow points.

  5. What are you being told on the stretch of road where this sign stands?

    • You may not overtake another vehicle hereCorrect answer
    • The lane ahead is closed
    • Only trucks may overtake
    • Passing is allowed if you signal first

    Overtaking is banned along this stretch, usually because you cannot see far enough ahead to pull back in before an oncoming vehicle arrives.

  6. Where this sign is posted, what may you not do?

    • Leave your vehicle standing at the roadsideCorrect answer
    • Drive past without stopping
    • Turn right
    • Use the lane at all

    Parking is banned here. A brief stop while somebody gets in or out is still allowed; a no stopping sign would forbid even that.

  7. You have missed your street and you see this sign. What does it prevent?

    • Turning around to travel back the way you cameCorrect answer
    • Turning left at the next street
    • Reversing into a driveway
    • Stopping at the kerb

    Turning around is banned here. Carry on to an intersection where the manoeuvre is permitted rather than swinging across in front of oncoming traffic.

  8. This sign appears on the back of a school bus. What does it require of drivers in Prince Edward Island?

    • Vehicles travelling in both directions must stop when the red lights flashCorrect answer
    • Vehicles behind the bus stop, oncoming vehicles may pass slowly
    • Only vehicles in the same lane must stop
    • Drivers must slow down but need not stop

    In Prince Edward Island traffic stops in both directions as soon as a school bus has its red lights flashing, and stays stopped until those lights go out.

  9. You pass this sign on a street in town. What does the number mean?

    • The maximum speed allowed, not a target to reachCorrect answer
    • The speed most drivers choose here
    • A suggested speed for fine weather
    • The slowest speed you are allowed to drive

    It is a maximum. In a residential area of the Island the default limit is 50 km/h, and rain, fog or children near the street often mean a safe speed is well below the posted number.

  10. What should you expect after this yellow sign on a Prince Edward Island country road?

    • Farm equipment using the roadCorrect answer
    • A farm market with parking
    • Livestock crossing at fixed hours
    • A weigh station for trucks

    Farm equipment is wide and slow. The Island's rural roads carry a great deal of it, especially at harvest, so ease off early and overtake only where you can see far ahead.

  11. You catch up to a vehicle displaying this orange triangle. What does it tell you?

    • The vehicle travels much more slowly than normal trafficCorrect answer
    • The vehicle has broken down
    • The vehicle is carrying dangerous goods
    • The vehicle is a school bus

    The orange triangle marks a slow-moving vehicle such as a tractor. On the Island a slow vehicle that is blocking traffic must pull over and let it by, so wait for that rather than crowding it.

  12. What lies ahead when you see this yellow sign?

    • A place where a school bus stops to pick up or set down childrenCorrect answer
    • A crossing guard on duty
    • A bus station
    • A parking area reserved for buses

    A school bus stop is coming up. Expect a bus at a standstill and children near the roadway, often just over a rise or around a bend.

  13. This sign is posted along a stretch of road. What is the danger?

    • Animals may step onto the road, most often at dawn and duskCorrect answer
    • The entrance to a wildlife park
    • Hunting is permitted nearby
    • A farm gate ahead

    Slow down and scan both shoulders. If one animal crosses, others often follow, so do not accelerate away the moment the first one is past.

  14. What does this round yellow sign marked with a cross announce?

    • A railway crossing aheadCorrect answer
    • A crossroads ahead
    • A route to the hospital
    • A dead end

    A level crossing is coming up. Slow down, look and listen, and never start across unless you can clear the tracks completely on the far side.

  15. What should you do when this sign appears?

    • Slow down before the curve, then steer smoothly through itCorrect answer
    • Brake hard in the middle of the bend
    • Move over into the left lane
    • Sound your horn all the way round

    Braking belongs before the curve. Braking once you are in it unsettles the vehicle at the moment the tyres are already working hardest.

  16. Why does this sign matter most in wet or freezing weather?

    • The surface can lose grip, so braking and steering take longerCorrect answer
    • It marks the entrance to a car wash
    • It shows where gravel has been spread
    • It announces a steep descent

    Grip disappears long before you feel it going. Cut your speed, avoid sudden movements of the wheel, and stretch out the gap to the vehicle in front.

  17. What is about to change where this sign is posted?

    • The paved surface ends and a gravel road beginsCorrect answer
    • The street becomes one way
    • The shoulder is closed
    • A toll section starts

    Gravel offers far less grip than pavement. Slow down before the change, hold the wheel firmly, and leave extra room because of dust and flying stones.

  18. This yellow sign appears on a country road. What is it warning you about?

    • The road you are on ends and you will have to turn left or rightCorrect answer
    • The pavement narrows ahead
    • A side road joins from the right
    • A divided highway begins

    Your road ends in a T. Slow down early enough to choose your direction and to give way to traffic already on the road that crosses in front of you.

  19. You see this sign where the intersection is not yet visible. What is coming?

    • A set of traffic lightsCorrect answer
    • A flashing beacon above a school
    • A railway signal
    • A pedestrian crossover

    Traffic lights are ahead, often hidden by a rise or a bend. Ease off the accelerator and be ready to stop in case the light changes as you arrive.

  20. What does an orange diamond sign mean?

    • Road work is going on aheadCorrect answer
    • A detour has just ended
    • A rest area is coming up
    • The road is closed to all traffic

    Orange marks a temporary condition: workers on foot, machinery entering the road, lanes shifted from where they normally run. Slow down and expect the road not to be where you assume.

  21. A person in a safety vest is holding a sign at road works. What is your obligation?

    • Obey their directions exactly as you would a traffic signCorrect answer
    • Pass slowly on the left
    • Carry on if the way looks clear
    • Stop only if they blow a whistle

    A traffic control person directs traffic through the work zone with the same authority as a sign, and their instruction overrides what the road ahead looks like to you.

  22. What does this orange sign with an arrow ask you to do?

    • Follow the marked route around a closed section of roadCorrect answer
    • Take the next exit for services
    • Return to the main road immediately
    • Wait for a pilot vehicle

    It is a detour. Follow the signs the whole way through, because they bring you back to your original road, while an improvised short cut leaves the marked route.

  23. What does this blue sign direct you to?

    • A hospitalCorrect answer
    • A rest area
    • A police station
    • A tourist information booth

    Blue signs point to services for road users. Knowing where the nearest hospital is matters most in the country, where the next town can be a long way off.

  24. What does this green and white shield with a maple leaf mark?

    • The Trans-Canada HighwayCorrect answer
    • The entrance to a provincial park
    • A scenic drive maintained by a town
    • A ferry terminal

    It is the Trans-Canada Highway marker. It confirms you are still on the national route, which is useful where several roads meet and the signs come up quickly.

  25. What is the purpose of this sign on a main highway?

    • It shows where to leave the highwayCorrect answer
    • It gives the speed limit for the ramp
    • It marks a rest area
    • It marks the end of a passing lane

    It announces the exit. Move into the right lane well before it, then do your slowing down on the ramp rather than in the through lane.

  26. A space in a shopping-centre lot carries this sign. Who is it for?

    • Only a vehicle displaying a valid accessible parking permitCorrect answer
    • Any driver, for a short stop
    • Delivery vehicles
    • Visitors to the building only

    These spaces are kept for permit holders. Taking one for a few minutes can leave somebody without the only spot where they can get out of their vehicle.

  27. The light turns amber as you approach the intersection. What is the rule?

    • Stop if you can do so safely, and carry on only if stopping would be dangerousCorrect answer
    • Speed up to clear the intersection
    • Stop no matter where you are
    • Treat it as a yield sign

    Amber warns that red is next. The question is not whether you can get through, but whether you can stop without the vehicle behind running into you.

  28. What must you do at a flashing red light?

    • Stop completely, then go when the way is clearCorrect answer
    • Slow down and go through with care
    • Wait for it to turn green
    • Treat it as a broken signal and ignore it

    A flashing red light works exactly like a stop sign: a complete stop, then go only when the way is clear. It is not going to turn green.

  29. What do two solid yellow lines down the middle of the road mean?

    • Neither direction may cross them to passCorrect answer
    • Passing is allowed in both directions
    • Passing is allowed for the uphill lane only
    • The road is one way

    Two solid lines forbid passing in both directions, because sight distance is too short for a driver to pull back in before meeting oncoming traffic.

  30. A broken yellow line runs along your side of the centre of a two-way road. What does it mean?

    • You may cross it to pass when the road ahead is clearCorrect answer
    • You must stay in your lane at all times
    • The road is about to become one way
    • Only buses may cross it

    A broken line on your side permits passing. It is still up to you to judge that the sight distance is long enough before you pull out.

Rules of the Road

30 questions

  1. How long must a Stage 1 driver in Prince Edward Island hold the instruction permit before moving on?

    • At least 365 days, or 275 days with a certified driver-education courseCorrect answer
    • At least 90 days
    • At least six months for everybody
    • There is no minimum period

    Stage 1 lasts a full year, and a certified driver-education course brings it down to 275 days. The time is there to build real experience, and nothing else shortens it.

  2. What is the shortest possible time from a new instruction permit to the end of the graduated program?

    • Two years and 275 days, with certified driver educationCorrect answer
    • One year
    • Six months
    • Five years

    Each of the three stages runs a year, so the usual route takes three years. Certified driver education shortens Stage 1 only, which is where the 275 days comes from.

  3. What must a Stage 1 driver display on the vehicle?

    • A yellow decal bearing the letter LCorrect answer
    • A green triangle in the rear window
    • No marking at all
    • A sign reading LEARNER ON BOARD

    Stage 1 is marked by the yellow decal with a black L, so that other drivers and police can see at a glance that a learner is at the wheel.

  4. What must most new drivers finish before they are allowed to book a first Class 5 road test?

    • The Novice Driver Course, unless they completed certified private driver trainingCorrect answer
    • A first-aid certificate
    • A written test at a private school
    • Nothing extra is required

    Prince Edward Island will not let you book that first road test until the Novice Driver Course, or a certified private driver-training program, has been completed.

  5. What must you pass at an Access PEI office to be issued an instruction permit?

    • A vision test and a written test on the rules of the roadCorrect answer
    • A road test only
    • A written test only
    • A medical examination arranged by your own doctor

    Both are required. Students in a certified driver-education course write the test in class, but they still have to attend an Access PEI office for the vision test.

  6. You did not pass the written exam for your instruction permit. When may you try again, and what does it cost?

    • After at least seven days, with no extra feeCorrect answer
    • The next day, paying the full fee again
    • After thirty days, at a reduced fee
    • Only after paying for a course

    The province publishes a wait of at least seven days before rewriting, and charges nothing for the rewrite. Use the week to go back through the handbook instead of guessing twice.

  7. You failed a road test. How soon may the next one be scheduled?

    • Not for at least fourteen daysCorrect answer
    • The same week, at the examiner's discretion
    • After seven days
    • Only after another full year of driving

    Fourteen days is the published minimum between road tests. It is the time set aside to work on whatever caused the test to be failed.

  8. How long is a Prince Edward Island instruction permit valid once it has been issued?

    • Two yearsCorrect answer
    • Six months
    • One year
    • Until your eighteenth birthday

    The permit itself is valid for two years. That expiry date is separate from the minimum time you have to spend in Stage 1 before moving on.

  9. Who is allowed to supervise a Stage 1 driver in Prince Edward Island?

    • A fully licensed driver who has held a licence for at least four years and sits in the front passenger seatCorrect answer
    • Any licensed driver over eighteen, sitting anywhere in the vehicle
    • A Stage 3 driver seated in the back
    • A parent, whether or not they hold a licence

    Four years of licence holding is the published minimum, and the accompanying driver has to be beside you, where they can see the road and reach the wheel.

  10. What are the alcohol limits for a Stage 1 driver and for the accompanying driver?

    • The learner must be at zero and the accompanying driver under 0.05Correct answer
    • Both may be up to 0.08
    • Both must be at zero
    • Neither has a limit while the vehicle is stopped

    The province publishes two different limits. A driver at any stage of the graduated program must be at zero for alcohol and drugs, while the accompanying driver has to stay under 0.05.

  11. Who may sit in the front seat while a Stage 1 driver is at the wheel?

    • Nobody except the learner and the accompanying driverCorrect answer
    • Anyone, as long as everybody wears a seat belt
    • Two passengers as well as the learner
    • Only members of the learner's own family

    The front seat is kept for those two people. That keeps the accompanying driver within reach of the wheel and removes a distraction from right beside the learner.

  12. Apart from the accompanying driver, who may ride in the vehicle with a Stage 1 driver?

    • Immediate family members of the accompanying driverCorrect answer
    • Any number of friends, provided all are belted
    • One passenger of any kind
    • Nobody at all

    Other passengers have to be immediate family of the accompanying driver. The rule is aimed at the carload of friends, which is one of the riskiest situations for a beginner.

  13. A Stage 1 driver under 21 wants to drive home at 2 a.m. with a proper accompanying driver. Is that allowed?

    • No, drivers under 21 may not drive between 1 a.m. and 5 a.m.Correct answer
    • Yes, the night restriction applies only at Stage 2
    • Yes, because somebody is supervising
    • Only at weekends

    The night restriction runs from 1 a.m. to 5 a.m. for drivers under 21, and at Stage 1 having an accompanying driver in the car does not lift it.

  14. What happens to a Stage 1 permit when the driver is convicted of an offence that carries demerit points?

    • The permit is suspended for at least 30 days, however few the pointsCorrect answer
    • Nothing until six points have built up
    • A warning letter is sent for a first offence
    • The points are recorded but never lead to a suspension

    At Stage 1 there is no allowance at all. Any conviction carrying demerit points suspends the instruction permit for a minimum of 30 days, which is stricter than the thresholds that apply later on.

  15. How many demerit points bring a suspension for a Stage 2 or Stage 3 driver?

    • Six within 12 months, or nine within 24 monthsCorrect answer
    • Twelve, over any period
    • Three within six months
    • There is no threshold at these stages

    A newly licensed driver is suspended at six points within 12 months or nine within 24. A repeat offence also lengthens the time spent in the graduated program.

  16. How many demerit points bring a suspension for the holder of a full licence?

    • TwelveCorrect answer
    • A single point is enough
    • Six
    • Nine

    Twelve points is the threshold for a full licence. Points come off the record two years after the date of the conviction, not two years after the offence was noticed.

  17. A school bus ahead of you switches on its flashing red lights. Who has to stop?

    • Vehicles travelling in both directionsCorrect answer
    • Only the vehicles behind the bus
    • Only vehicles in the same lane as the bus
    • Nobody, if a painted centre line separates the directions

    On the Island traffic stops in both directions for a bus loading or unloading with its red lights flashing, and stays stopped until those lights stop flashing.

  18. A school bus has stopped with its red lights flashing. Where should your vehicle come to rest?

    • Not less than six metres from the front or the rear of the busCorrect answer
    • Directly behind it, as close as you can get
    • Two car lengths, measured from your mirror
    • Anywhere in the lane will do

    Six metres, about twenty feet, gives children room to walk around the bus and gives you the distance you need to see them before you move again.

  19. A school bus ahead of you has its amber lights flashing. What does that mean?

    • The bus is about to stop, so get ready to stop as wellCorrect answer
    • The bus is pulling away and you may follow it
    • The bus has broken down
    • The bus is empty and may be passed

    Amber is the warning that the bus is slowing to load or unload. Red follows, and by the time it comes on you should already be at a standstill.

  20. What does a driver face for going past a school bus while its red lights are flashing?

    • Twelve demerit points, a three-month suspension and a fine of up to $5,000Correct answer
    • A written warning for a first offence
    • Three demerit points
    • A parking ticket

    Prince Edward Island treats this as one of the most serious offences on the road. The twelve points on their own are enough to suspend a full licence.

  21. You are stopped at a red light and want to turn right. What does Prince Edward Island allow?

    • Turn after a full stop and after yielding, unless a sign forbids itCorrect answer
    • Turn without stopping if the way looks clear
    • Never turn right while the light is red
    • Turn only once a green arrow appears

    A right turn on a red light is permitted unless a sign says otherwise. The full stop comes first, then you give way to pedestrians and to traffic already coming.

  22. Two vehicles reach an intersection with no signs and no signals at the same moment. Who goes first?

    • The vehicle on the right, because the driver on the left yieldsCorrect answer
    • The larger vehicle
    • The vehicle going straight ahead, in every case
    • Whichever driver signals first

    At an uncontrolled intersection the right of way belongs to the vehicle on the right. Make eye contact all the same, because the rule protects nobody if the other driver does not know it.

  23. When does Prince Edward Island require your headlights to be on?

    • From sunset to sunrise, and whenever you cannot see more than 500 feetCorrect answer
    • Only between midnight and dawn
    • Only during the winter months
    • Only when it is raining

    The rule combines the hour and the visibility. As soon as fog, rain or blowing snow cuts your view to 500 feet or less, the headlights go on whatever the time of day.

  24. Unless a sign says otherwise, what are the speed limits in a Prince Edward Island residential area and business district?

    • 50 km/h in a residential area and 60 km/h in a business districtCorrect answer
    • 40 km/h and 50 km/h
    • 30 km/h and 40 km/h
    • 50 km/h in both

    The 60 km/h default in business districts catches visitors out, because they expect the same number as on a residential street. Both figures apply only where no sign says otherwise.

  25. Is it legal to use a working radar detector in a vehicle in Prince Edward Island?

    • No, radar detectors are illegal hereCorrect answer
    • Yes, as long as it is not fixed to the windscreen
    • Yes, for drivers over 25 with a full licence
    • Yes, outside built-up areas

    Radar detectors are against the law in the province. Drivers arriving from a place where they are tolerated need to leave the device behind.

  26. May passengers ride in the open box of a pickup truck in Prince Edward Island?

    • No, riding in the open box is prohibitedCorrect answer
    • Yes, if they sit down on the floor of the box
    • Yes, on country roads only
    • Yes, provided the truck stays below 50 km/h

    It is prohibited. Nothing holds a person in an open box, so a hard stop or a sharp curve is enough to throw them out of the vehicle.

  27. There is no speed sign in sight on an open Prince Edward Island highway. What limit applies?

    • 80 km/h, and no limit posted anywhere in the province is higher than 90Correct answer
    • 100 km/h, as on most Canadian highways
    • 90 km/h everywhere outside a town
    • Whatever suits the conditions, with no fixed number

    The open-highway default is 80 km/h and the highest limit posted anywhere in the province is 90. Drivers used to 100 or 110 km/h elsewhere in Canada are the ones who get caught.

  28. It is harvest time and you catch up to a wide, slow tractor on a country road. What is the sensible thing to do?

    • Hold well back, stay patient, and pass only where you can see far enough aheadCorrect answer
    • Sound your horn until it moves over
    • Overtake at once, since it is slow anyway
    • Follow within a metre so you can see past it

    Farm machinery is a normal part of traffic on the Island and it is wider than it looks. Sitting close behind hides the road from you and hides you from the machine's driver.

  29. You are driving something slow and a line of traffic has built up behind you. What does Prince Edward Island require?

    • Pull over where it is safe and let the traffic go byCorrect answer
    • Keep your speed and let them wait
    • Drive along the shoulder and carry on
    • Speed up to the limit even if the load makes that unsafe

    A slow vehicle that is impeding or blocking traffic has to pull over and let it pass. Drivers trapped behind take chances, so letting them by is the safer choice for everyone.

  30. How much room must you leave when you overtake a cyclist?

    • At least one metre, or as close to that as you can manageCorrect answer
    • Half a metre is enough on a quiet road
    • Enough that your mirror clears them
    • Nothing is set out, you only have to slow down

    The law on the Island sets one metre as the passing distance, or as near to it as possible. Where the road is too narrow to give that, stay behind until it opens up.

About this quiz

This page is free practice for the Prince Edward Island written driving examination, the test you pass before you may drive with somebody beside you. The Province calls the licence an Instruction Driver's Permit, a Class 7 licence, and it is Stage 1 of the graduated licensing programme, marked by a yellow L decal.

Prince Edward Island publishes no question count, no pass mark and no time limit for that test, anywhere. Three commercial practice sites state the same figures as each other, and none of them cites a government source. We are not repeating a number that decides whether somebody books a test, so the numbers on our mock test are a clearly stated practice standard of our own.

Every question was written for this site from the free official Driver's Handbook, the licensing and Rules of the Road pages on the Province's own website, and the graduated licensing and demerit point regulations. Nothing was copied from a handbook, from a real test, or from another practice site.

  • Any violation carrying demerit points at all suspends a Stage 1 permit for at least thirty days, with no cushion of points to lose first.
  • The learner must be at zero blood alcohol while the accompanying driver must be under 0.05, which is two different limits in the same front seat.
  • A Stage 1 driver under 21 may not drive between one and five in the morning.
  • The open highway limit is 80 km/h unless a sign says otherwise, and 90 is the highest limit posted anywhere in the province.
  • If you fail the instruction permit test the Province publishes a wait of at least seven days before you rewrite it, and no fee to rewrite.

Common questions

How many questions are on the real Prince Edward Island written test?

The Province does not publish a number anywhere. Several practice sites state one anyway, and none of them cites a government source. Our mock test uses a count of our own and says so.

What is the pass mark?

That is not published either, and neither is a time limit. The pass mark on our mock test is our own practice standard, chosen to be demanding rather than to imitate a figure nobody has sourced.

Where do I write the test?

At an Access PEI office, by contacting a location to book a written knowledge exam appointment. Students in a certified driver-education program write it in class and still attend Access PEI for the vision test. The Province publishes no online version of the knowledge test.

What happens if I fail?

You wait at least seven days before rewriting the written exam, and there is no additional fee to rewrite it. After a failed road test the published wait is at least fourteen days.

Who can sit beside me while I practise?

A fully licensed driver who has held a licence for at least four years, holds the right class for the vehicle, sits in the front passenger seat and is under 0.05 blood alcohol. Your own blood alcohol must be zero.
