What Prince Edward Island publishes, and what it does not

The Province is generous about how you become a driver here and silent about the written test itself. It says the test exists, that it is based on the driving rules and safety information in the Driver's Handbook, and that you sit it at an Access PEI office along with a vision test. It does not publish how many questions the test has, what mark you need to pass, or how long you get. That is the honest starting point for this page.

Three commercial practice sites state the same question count and the same pass mark, word for word, and not one of them cites a government source for either figure. We are not going to repeat a number that decides whether somebody books a test. The fifteen signs, the fifteen rules, the twelve right needed in each half and the thirty minutes on our mock test are a clearly stated practice standard of our own, and they are not the Province's.

What the Province does publish about the exams is worth knowing, because almost nobody quotes it. If you fail the instruction permit test you wait at least seven days before you rewrite it, and there is no fee to rewrite. If you fail a road test you wait at least fourteen days for the next one. The instruction permit and the permit test each carry a published fee of twenty dollars, and fees change from time to time, so check the current page before you go.

Study the rules

The three stages, one at a time

You start at 16, and if you are under 18 a parent or guardian has to consent. Pass a vision test and the written driving examination and you hold an Instruction Driver's Permit, a Class 7 licence, which is Stage 1 of the graduated programme and is marked by a yellow L decal. The permit itself is valid for two years.

Stage 1 lasts at least 365 days, reduced to 275 days if you complete a certified driver-education program. Stage 2 and Stage 3 last 365 days each, so the whole programme takes a minimum of three years, or two years and 275 days with certified driver education. The Stage 3 restrictions lift on their own when the stage is complete.

One requirement is easy to miss and stops people booking. Before your first road test for a Class 5 licence you must complete the Novice Driver Course, unless you completed certified private driver training instead. Without one of the two you will not be permitted to book that road test at all.

The rules that catch learners out

Demerit points are stricter here for a learner than almost anywhere in the country, and this is the single most important number on the page. Any violation carrying demerit points at all suspends a Stage 1 permit for at least thirty days. There is no cushion of two or three points to lose first. A Stage 2 or Stage 3 driver is suspended at six points in twelve months or nine points in twenty-four, and a fully licensed driver at twelve.

The supervisor rule is the one most sites get wrong, because Prince Edward Island publishes two different alcohol limits for the two people in the front seat. The learner must be at zero blood alcohol. The accompanying driver must be under 0.05, which is lower than the Criminal Code limit but is not zero. That driver must have held a licence for at least four years, must hold the right class for the vehicle, and must sit beside the learner in the front passenger seat.

Two more Stage 1 rules are worth learning before you drive. Nobody other than the learner and the accompanying driver may occupy the front seat, and any other passengers must be immediate family of that accompanying driver. If you are under 21 you may not drive between one and five in the morning, and that curfew follows you into Stage 2.

Where to get the official information

Everything on this page comes from what Prince Edward Island publishes for free. The Driver's Handbook is the book the written test is based on, it is free to download from the Province's own site, and it is published in French, Arabic and Chinese as well as English. Whether the test itself is offered in those languages is not published anywhere, so we do not claim that it is. Read the handbook alongside this practice, not instead of it.

This is an unofficial free practice site. It is not affiliated with Access PEI, the Highway Safety Division or the Government of Prince Edward Island, and it cannot issue a licence. Always check the official pages for the current rules and fees before you book.

Road Signs

6 of 30

  1. You are the first vehicle at this sign and the road ahead is empty. What must you do?

    • Come to a complete stop before the line, then go when the way is clearCorrect answer
    • Slow to a crawl and keep going
    • Stop only if a pedestrian is waiting
    • Sound the horn and carry on

    A stop sign means a full stop every time, empty road or not. Stop before the stop line, or before the crosswalk if there is no line, and move off only once you can see that the way is clear.

  2. Where this sign is posted, what may you not do?

    • Leave your vehicle standing at the roadsideCorrect answer
    • Drive past without stopping
    • Turn right
    • Use the lane at all

    Parking is banned here. A brief stop while somebody gets in or out is still allowed; a no stopping sign would forbid even that.

  3. You catch up to a vehicle displaying this orange triangle. What does it tell you?

    • The vehicle travels much more slowly than normal trafficCorrect answer
    • The vehicle has broken down
    • The vehicle is carrying dangerous goods
    • The vehicle is a school bus

    The orange triangle marks a slow-moving vehicle such as a tractor. On the Island a slow vehicle that is blocking traffic must pull over and let it by, so wait for that rather than crowding it.

  4. Why does this sign matter most in wet or freezing weather?

    • The surface can lose grip, so braking and steering take longerCorrect answer
    • It marks the entrance to a car wash
    • It shows where gravel has been spread
    • It announces a steep descent

    Grip disappears long before you feel it going. Cut your speed, avoid sudden movements of the wheel, and stretch out the gap to the vehicle in front.

  5. A person in a safety vest is holding a sign at road works. What is your obligation?

    • Obey their directions exactly as you would a traffic signCorrect answer
    • Pass slowly on the left
    • Carry on if the way looks clear
    • Stop only if they blow a whistle

    A traffic control person directs traffic through the work zone with the same authority as a sign, and their instruction overrides what the road ahead looks like to you.

  6. A space in a shopping-centre lot carries this sign. Who is it for?

    • Only a vehicle displaying a valid accessible parking permitCorrect answer
    • Any driver, for a short stop
    • Delivery vehicles
    • Visitors to the building only

    These spaces are kept for permit holders. Taking one for a few minutes can leave somebody without the only spot where they can get out of their vehicle.

Rules of the Road

6 of 40

  1. How long must a Stage 1 driver in Prince Edward Island hold the instruction permit before moving on?

    • At least 365 days, or 275 days with a certified driver-education courseCorrect answer
    • At least 90 days
    • At least six months for everybody
    • There is no minimum period

    Stage 1 lasts a full year, and a certified driver-education course brings it down to 275 days. The time is there to build real experience, and nothing else shortens it.

  2. You failed a road test. How soon may the next one be scheduled?

    • Not for at least fourteen daysCorrect answer
    • The same week, at the examiner's discretion
    • After seven days
    • Only after another full year of driving

    Fourteen days is the published minimum between road tests. It is the time set aside to work on whatever caused the test to be failed.

  3. What happens to a Stage 1 permit when the driver is convicted of an offence that carries demerit points?

    • The permit is suspended for at least 30 days, however few the pointsCorrect answer
    • Nothing until six points have built up
    • A warning letter is sent for a first offence
    • The points are recorded but never lead to a suspension

    At Stage 1 there is no allowance at all. Any conviction carrying demerit points suspends the instruction permit for a minimum of 30 days, which is stricter than the thresholds that apply later on.

  4. You are stopped at a red light and want to turn right. What does Prince Edward Island allow?

    • Turn after a full stop and after yielding, unless a sign forbids itCorrect answer
    • Turn without stopping if the way looks clear
    • Never turn right while the light is red
    • Turn only once a green arrow appears

    A right turn on a red light is permitted unless a sign says otherwise. The full stop comes first, then you give way to pedestrians and to traffic already coming.

  5. There is no speed sign in sight on an open Prince Edward Island highway. What limit applies?

    • 80 km/h, and no limit posted anywhere in the province is higher than 90Correct answer
    • 100 km/h, as on most Canadian highways
    • 90 km/h everywhere outside a town
    • Whatever suits the conditions, with no fixed number

    The open-highway default is 80 km/h and the highest limit posted anywhere in the province is 90. Drivers used to 100 or 110 km/h elsewhere in Canada are the ones who get caught.

  6. Until what age must a Prince Edward Island driver keep a zero blood alcohol level?

    • 22Correct answer
    • 18
    • 19
    • 25

    Zero tolerance carries on past the graduated programme: every driver under 22 must have no alcohol at all, even with a full licence.

Common questions

How many questions are on the real Prince Edward Island written test?

The Province does not publish a number anywhere. Several practice sites state one anyway, and none of them cites a government source. Our mock test uses a count of our own and says so.

What is the pass mark?

That is not published either, and neither is a time limit. The pass mark on our mock test is our own practice standard, chosen to be demanding rather than to imitate a figure nobody has sourced.

Where do I write the test?

At an Access PEI office, by contacting a location to book a written knowledge exam appointment. Students in a certified driver-education program write it in class and still attend Access PEI for the vision test. The Province publishes no online version of the knowledge test.

What happens if I fail?

You wait at least seven days before rewriting the written exam, and there is no additional fee to rewrite it. After a failed road test the published wait is at least fourteen days.

Who can sit beside me while I practise?

A fully licensed driver who has held a licence for at least four years, holds the right class for the vehicle, sits in the front passenger seat and is under 0.05 blood alcohol. Your own blood alcohol must be zero.
