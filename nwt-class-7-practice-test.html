What the real Northwest Territories knowledge test looks like

The Northwest Territories publishes its whole test format, which hardly anywhere in the country does. There are two separate tests. One is twenty multiple-choice questions on the rules of the road, and the other is a separate test of twenty questions on road signage. You may not miss more than four questions in each test, so you need sixteen of twenty on each of them, and not sixteen of forty added together.

The government also publishes a time, and here we have to be careful with it. Its wording is that the test lasts 30 minutes, in the singular, and it does not say whether that is thirty minutes for each test or thirty minutes for both of them together. We put thirty minutes on the clock in our mock test and we tell you plainly that the split is not published anywhere.

Because each test is scored on its own, a total can hide a failed half. One commercial practice site tells its readers that they need twenty-five of the questions correct, which is not how the real thing is scored at all. Twenty-five out of forty could be thirteen on one test and twelve on the other, and that fails both of them. Everything on our mock test is the government's own format rather than a standard of ours.

One examination fee covers both parts, because the fee schedule lists the written examination for Class 6 or 7 with the Class 7 signs test included. We are not going to quote the amount. The government's own booking page currently shows two different fee tables under a single heading, with no dates and no labels to say which one is in force, so the honest thing to tell you is that there is one fee and that you should read it off the official page on the day.

Study the rules

The stages, one at a time

You may apply at fifteen. Stage 1 is the Class 7 learner's licence, and it has to be held for at least twelve months before you can move up. Stage 2 is the probationary Class 5P, which has to be held for twelve suspension-free months. The territory says plainly that it takes a minimum of two years to complete both stages.

Graduating out of the programme is not only a matter of time served. The Basic Driver's Manual ties it to having fewer than six demerit points as well, and that number is worth remembering, because six points is also what suspends a new driver's licence outright.

There is no shortcut here. The Northwest Territories publishes no driver-education credit that shortens a stage or the twelve-month holding period, so a page that promises to cut your wait with driver training is describing somewhere else.

The rules that catch learners out

The supervisor rule has four parts and most people know only one of them. The person beside you must be at least nineteen, must hold a full Class 5 licence, and must have at least twenty-four months of driving experience. Time held on a graduated licence does not count towards those twenty-four months.

Both of you have to be at zero blood alcohol. That is the part that surprises people, because the territory publishes the zero limit for the driver and for the supervisor alike, so an adult who would be perfectly legal driving alone can be over the limit while sitting beside a learner.

As a learner you may carry no passenger other than the supervisor, and you may not drive between eleven at night and six in the morning. What the territory does not publish is just as worth knowing. There is no L plate requirement, no towing restriction and no highway restriction for Class 7 holders anywhere in the official material, and we are not inventing any.

Where to get the official information

Everything on this page comes from what the Northwest Territories publishes for free. The Basic Driver's Manual is the book the real test is written from, it is free to download from the territory's own site, and there is an official French edition, the Manuel du conducteur. The Northwest Territories has eleven official languages and publishes its driver manuals in English and in French, but which languages the examination itself is administered in is not published anywhere, so we do not claim it is offered in one language or another. Read the manual alongside this practice, not instead of it.

This is an unofficial free practice site. It is not affiliated with Driver and Vehicle Services, the Department of Infrastructure or the Government of the Northwest Territories, and it cannot issue a licence. Always check the official pages for the current rules and fees before you book.

Road Signage Test

30 questions

  1. You reach this sign where a gravel road meets a Northwest Territories highway. What does it require?

    • A full stop, then go on only when the way is clearCorrect answer
    • A slow roll if you can see both ways
    • A stop only when another vehicle is in sight
    • A tap on the horn before you pull out

    A stop sign means the wheels stop turning. Stop at the line, or before the crosswalk if none is painted, and pull out only when you will not force another driver to slow.

  2. This red and white triangle faces you where two roads join. What does it ask?

    • Give way to traffic and pedestrians already on the roadCorrect answer
    • Stop and wait until somebody waves you through
    • Sound your horn and merge at once
    • Come to a full stop every time, even on an empty road

    Yield means give way. Slow right down, be ready to stop, and join only when nobody already using the road has to brake or steer around you.

  3. This sign is mounted at the mouth of a street in a Northwest Territories community. What is it telling you?

    • You must not drive in, because traffic here comes towards youCorrect answer
    • Only people who live on the street may drive in
    • Entry is allowed once the shops have closed
    • Heavy vehicles must use another street

    The street runs the other way. Going past this sign puts you head-on into drivers who have no reason to expect you.

  4. You are turning onto a street marked with this sign. What must be true once your turn is finished?

    • You must be travelling the way the arrow pointsCorrect answer
    • You may pick either direction once you are on it
    • You must park before the next corner
    • You may drive against the arrow if you keep your speed down

    Every lane on a one-way street runs the same way. Turning the wrong way here is one of the quickest ways for a new driver to end up facing traffic.

  5. This sign stands beside a straight, open stretch of highway. Why does it still apply?

    • Passing is banned here whatever the road looks likeCorrect answer
    • It applies only while a vehicle is coming the other way
    • It applies only to trucks and buses
    • It is advice that may be ignored in daylight

    A no passing sign is an order, not a suggestion. It is usually posted because of a hidden dip, a side road or a crest you cannot see past.

  6. A friend asks you to pull over for a second where this sign is posted. What do you tell them?

    • No, this sign bans stopping even for a momentCorrect answer
    • It is fine as long as the engine keeps running
    • It is fine if nobody gets out of the vehicle
    • It is fine outside business hours

    No stopping is the strictest of the curbside signs. You may not stop here at all, not to wait and not to drop somebody off.

  7. The road divides around a raised island and this sign is fixed to it. What does it order?

    • Pass on the right-hand side of the islandCorrect answer
    • Stop before the island and let others go first
    • Choose whichever side carries less traffic
    • Reverse and find another route

    Keep right of the island. Taking the wrong side puts you into traffic coming from the far side of the obstacle, which is the last thing those drivers expect.

  8. A bus ahead shows this sign and flashing red lights on a two-lane Northwest Territories road. Who must stop?

    • Drivers coming from both directionsCorrect answer
    • Only the drivers behind the bus
    • Only drivers who can actually see children
    • Nobody, as long as the bus is on the shoulder

    In the Northwest Territories traffic stops in both directions while the lights are flashing. Children cross without warning, and the driver cannot hold them back once they have stepped off.

  9. You pass this sign on your way out of a community. What has just changed?

    • A new posted limit applies from this sign onwardsCorrect answer
    • Nothing until the next intersection
    • The number is only a suggestion on this stretch
    • The old limit stays until a second sign cancels it

    A new limit takes effect at the sign itself, not somewhere further along. Have your speed right before you reach it rather than after.

  10. This yellow diamond appears on a northern highway. What is the real danger it points to?

    • The animal is tall, so a collision can bring it through the windshieldCorrect answer
    • The animal may scratch the paintwork
    • The animal always runs away from headlights
    • The animal is only about in summer

    A moose carries its weight high on long legs. In a collision the body is often swept onto the windshield rather than the bumper, which is why cutting speed matters more than steering.

  11. You brake for the animal shown on this sign and it reaches the far shoulder. Why should you stay slow?

    • Because more of the herd is probably still to crossCorrect answer
    • Because the road surface will have been damaged
    • Because your brakes need time to cool
    • Because your headlights need time to recover

    Most animals travel in herds, so where there is one there are often more. It is the second and third animal that catch drivers who speed up too soon.

  12. You are travelling at highway speed when this sign appears. What is about to change under your tires?

    • The pavement ends and a gravel surface beginsCorrect answer
    • A bridge deck begins
    • A painted crosswalk begins
    • A tunnel begins

    Loose gravel gives far less grip than pavement and tugs at the steering. Ease your speed off well before the change rather than at it.

  13. Freezing drizzle is falling where this sign is posted. What does that mean for your braking?

    • You need to brake far earlier and far more gentlyCorrect answer
    • Braking is unaffected once the wipers are running
    • You may brake later than usual because the surface is smooth
    • Braking hard is the safest way to test the surface

    A slippery surface stretches your stopping distance and punishes sudden movements. Brake early, steer smoothly, and leave much more room than you would on a dry road.

  14. This sign is posted on a stretch where the ground has heaved. What is the safe way through?

    • Slow down before it and hold the wheel firmlyCorrect answer
    • Speed up so the wheels skip over it
    • Brake hard as the wheels hit it
    • Move onto the shoulder and pass it there

    Slow down before the rough section, not on it. Braking while the wheels are already bouncing takes weight off the tires just when you need grip.

  15. Why is holding the brakes on all the way down the hill this sign warns about a poor idea?

    • The brakes heat up and lose their biteCorrect answer
    • It wears the tires out
    • It uses more fuel
    • It is only a problem for trucks

    Brakes held on for a long descent get hot and fade. Choose a lower gear at the top so the engine holds the vehicle back and the brakes stay fresh for when you really need them.

  16. This sign appears on a gravel highway. Why is the bend it shows more serious there than on pavement?

    • Loose gravel gives less grip, so the vehicle slides wide more easilyCorrect answer
    • Gravel roads are always narrower
    • The sign means something different on gravel
    • Speed limits do not apply on gravel

    Grip on loose stone is a fraction of what pavement offers. Get your speed down before the bend so you can hold one steady line all the way through it.

  17. Your headlights pick up a row of these on a dark curve. How should you use them?

    • Follow the direction they point and set your speed for the bendCorrect answer
    • Aim for the middle of the row
    • Treat them as the start of a passing lane
    • Ignore them, they mark private driveways

    A chevron shows which way the road turns and how sharply. On an unlit northern highway they are often the first thing your headlights give you.

  18. It is a Sunday morning and this sign is posted. How should you drive?

    • With the same care as on a weekday, because the zone never liftsCorrect answer
    • As though the zone were lifted for the weekend
    • Faster, because the school is empty
    • Slowly only if a crossing guard is on duty

    In the Northwest Territories a school zone applies all day, every day of the year. Children use school grounds outside class hours, and the sign is not switched off at the weekend.

  19. Why do drivers get into trouble at the structure this sign warns about?

    • Two vehicles arrive together and neither one slows downCorrect answer
    • The surface there is always icy
    • It is normally closed for the winter
    • Trucks are not allowed onto it

    The bridge is narrower than the road leading to it. One of you has to give way, so decide early, slow down, and never try to pass on the bridge itself.

  20. The orange sign at the start of a work area is up but no machines are moving. What still applies?

    • The work zone rules and the limit posted for the zoneCorrect answer
    • Nothing, until you can actually see workers
    • Only the rule about headlights
    • Only the rules that apply to trucks

    A work zone stays a work zone while the signs are up. The surface is often loose or uneven, lanes shift without warning, and a worker can step out from behind a parked machine.

  21. This sign is posted ahead of a grader working on a gravel highway. What should you be ready to do?

    • Stop for the person directing traffic, however clear the road looksCorrect answer
    • Pass the grader on the shoulder
    • Sound your horn and hold your speed
    • Turn around and take another route

    A traffic control person's instructions carry the same weight as a sign or a signal. Stop when they tell you to, and move off only when they wave you on.

  22. A highway is closed for repairs and this sign is out. What are you being asked to do?

    • Follow the marked route around the closure until it rejoins the highwayCorrect answer
    • Wait at the barricade until the road reopens
    • Find your own way around using a map
    • Drive through slowly if your vehicle sits high enough

    A detour is a signed alternative route. Keep following the markers rather than guessing at side roads, which in the North may not be maintained and may not connect at all.

  23. Blue signs like this one belong to which family?

    • Information signs that point to servicesCorrect answer
    • Warning signs about the road surface
    • Regulatory signs that carry a penalty
    • Temporary signs used in work zones

    Blue is the colour the sign system uses for services: hospitals, fuel, food and accessible parking. It tells you what is available, not what you must do.

  24. You are between communities with a quarter tank left when this sign appears. Why does it matter more here than in a city?

    • The next chance to fill up may be a very long way offCorrect answer
    • Fuel is cheaper at stations that are signed
    • The sign guarantees the station is open all night
    • Filling up before a long trip is required by law

    Distances between services on northern highways are long, and a station shown on a sign may be the last one for hours. Keeping the tank well up is also what the territory advises for winter travel.

  25. You are following a route marked with this shield. What does the marker help you do?

    • Confirm you are still on the national route you meant to takeCorrect answer
    • Judge the speed limit for the next section
    • Find the nearest fuel station
    • Know when the road turns to gravel

    Route markers confirm which road you are on. They are placed after junctions so a driver can check the turn was the right one without having to stop.

  26. A space marked with this sign is the only one left and you are in a hurry. What is the correct decision?

    • Park somewhere else, because the space is reserved for permit holdersCorrect answer
    • Use it briefly as long as you stay with the vehicle
    • Use it, because the lot is full
    • Use it with your hazard lights on

    The space exists so that somebody who needs the extra room and the short distance can get out of their vehicle. Being in a hurry is not a permit.

  27. This light comes on while you are still well back from the intersection. What is expected of you?

    • Stop before the line, because the red is about to appearCorrect answer
    • Speed up to clear the intersection
    • Cover the brake but carry on at the same speed
    • Stop only if a vehicle is waiting to cross

    Amber means the red is coming. If you can stop safely before the line, that is what the light is asking for; carry on only when stopping would mean braking so hard that the driver behind could not.

  28. You reach an intersection where this light is showing on every approach. How do you treat it?

    • As a stop sign: stop, then go when it is your turnCorrect answer
    • As a green light taken with extra caution
    • As a yield sign that needs no stop
    • As a signal that is out of order and can be ignored

    A flashing red is a stop sign in light form. When every approach flashes red the intersection works as an all-way stop, and the first driver to stop is the first to go.

  29. What do a pair of solid yellow lines down the centre tell both directions of traffic?

    • That neither direction may cross them to passCorrect answer
    • That the road ahead becomes one way
    • That the lanes may be shared when traffic is light
    • That passing is allowed for whichever direction has the shorter line

    Two solid yellow lines mean the view ahead is too short for either direction to pass safely. Yellow always separates traffic that is moving in opposite directions.

  30. Why is the wide white line across your lane painted back from the cross street?

    • To keep stopped vehicles clear of crossing traffic and of people on footCorrect answer
    • To give cyclists somewhere to wait
    • To mark the edge of a parking area
    • To show where a bus has to stop

    The stop line holds you back far enough that turning vehicles and people crossing have room. Stop with your bumper behind it, then edge forward only if you still cannot see.

Rules of the Road Test

40 questions

  1. What is the earliest a person may apply for a Class 7 learner's licence in the Northwest Territories, and how long must it then be held?

    • At 15, and held for at least one yearCorrect answer
    • At 14, and held for six months
    • At 16, and held for three months
    • At 18, and held for two years

    Stage 1 opens at 15, and the Class 7 licence has to be held for at least twelve months before you can move up. The territory publishes no way of shortening that year.

  2. You have passed the road test and now hold a Class 5P licence. What does the second stage ask of you?

    • Twelve months free of any suspension before you can upgradeCorrect answer
    • Six months without carrying a passenger
    • Twelve months of driving in daylight only
    • Three years and a second knowledge examination

    Stage 2 is the probationary Class 5P licence. You have to complete twelve suspension-free months in it, so a suspension during that time sets the clock back.

  3. Counting both graduated stages, what is the shortest time the Northwest Territories programme can take?

    • Two yearsCorrect answer
    • Six months
    • One year
    • Four years

    Twelve months as a learner and twelve suspension-free months on a probationary licence add up to a minimum of two years before a full licence is possible.

  4. Who may act as your supervisor while you drive on a Class 7 licence?

    • A driver at least 19 years old with a full Class 5 licence and 24 months of experienceCorrect answer
    • Any licensed driver aged 18 or over
    • Anyone who has held any licence for six months
    • A driver who is still in the graduated programme

    The supervisor needs 24 months of experience on a full Class 5 licence and must be at least 19. Time spent holding a graduated licence does not count towards that experience.

  5. How much alcohol may be in the blood of a Class 7 driver and of the supervisor riding with them?

    • Both of them must be at zeroCorrect answer
    • The learner must be at zero; the supervisor may be under 0.08
    • Both of them may be under 0.05
    • The supervisor must be at zero; the learner may be under 0.05

    The Northwest Territories puts the zero limit on both people. A supervisor who has been drinking cannot legally supervise, even if the learner has had nothing at all.

  6. Your cousin asks for a ride while you are practising on your Class 7 licence, with your supervisor already in the vehicle. What does the law allow?

    • No passenger other than the supervisor may be in the vehicleCorrect answer
    • One extra passenger is allowed
    • Extra passengers are allowed during daylight
    • Up to three passengers are allowed if everyone is belted

    A Class 7 driver carries the supervisor and nobody else. It is a strict one-person rule, and the territory publishes no exception for family.

  7. Between which hours may a Class 7 driver in the Northwest Territories not drive at all?

    • Between 11 p.m. and 6 a.m.Correct answer
    • Between midnight and 5 a.m.
    • Between 10 p.m. and 7 a.m.
    • There is no restriction on the hours

    The learner stage bans driving between 11 p.m. and 6 a.m., supervisor or no supervisor. Across much of the territory those hours are dark all winter and bright much of the summer, but the rule goes by the clock.

  8. How does the Northwest Territories organise the knowledge examination for a Class 7 licence?

    • Two separate tests of 20 questions: one on the rules of the road and one on road signageCorrect answer
    • One test of 40 questions covering everything
    • One test of 20 questions drawn from both subjects
    • A written test followed by an oral interview

    The territory publishes the format plainly: twenty multiple-choice questions on the rules of the road, and a separate test of twenty questions on road signage.

  9. You get seventeen right on the signage test and fourteen right on the rules test. What is the outcome?

    • A fail on the rules test, because you may miss no more than four questions in each testCorrect answer
    • A pass, because thirty-one out of forty is well over three quarters
    • A pass, because only the combined total counts
    • A fail on both, because the two results are averaged together

    Each test stands on its own. You may not miss more than four questions in either one, so sixteen out of twenty is the mark to beat on each, and a strong score on one half cannot rescue the other.

  10. Besides the two knowledge tests, what does a fifteen-year-old applicant have to pass before a Class 7 licence is issued?

    • A vision screeningCorrect answer
    • A medical examination by a nurse practitioner
    • A hearing test
    • A first-aid course

    A vision screening is required before the licence is issued. A medical report is a separate matter: a Class 5, 6 or 7 driver does not need one until age 75.

  11. At how many demerit points is a novice driver suspended, and at how many is a full licence holder suspended?

    • Six for a novice, fifteen for a full licence holderCorrect answer
    • Nine for a novice, twelve for a full licence holder
    • Fifteen for both of them
    • Twelve for a novice, twenty for a full licence holder

    A learner or probationary driver is suspended once six points have piled up, while a driver with a full licence is not suspended until fifteen. New drivers are given far less room for error.

  12. A novice driver in the Northwest Territories reaches twelve demerit points, and later fifteen. How long are those two suspensions?

    • Three months at twelve points, and six months at fifteenCorrect answer
    • One month, then two months
    • Two weeks, then one month
    • Three months, then three months again

    The scale climbs steeply for a novice: one month at six points, three months at twelve and six months at fifteen. The points themselves come off the record twenty-four months after they were added.

  13. Which drivers in the Northwest Territories are banned from using a hand-held electronic device at the wheel?

    • Every driver, and it has been the law here since January 2012Correct answer
    • Only learner and probationary drivers
    • Only drivers under 21
    • Only drivers of commercial vehicles

    The ban covers every driver and every hand-held device, from a phone to a portable navigation unit. A conviction carries three demerit points, and the fine doubles in a school or construction zone.

  14. A school bus stops on a Northwest Territories road with its lights flashing. What does the law require of the traffic around it?

    • Traffic stops in both directionsCorrect answer
    • Only the vehicles behind the bus stop
    • Traffic slows to walking pace and eases past
    • Traffic stops only when children can actually be seen

    Passing a stopped school bus with its lights flashing is not allowed, and the duty to stop applies to both directions of travel. Wait until the lights stop flashing before you move off.

  15. What is unusual about the way the Northwest Territories runs its school zones?

    • They are in effect 24 hours a day, 365 days a year, and speeding fines in them are doubledCorrect answer
    • They apply only between 8 a.m. and 5 p.m. on school days
    • They apply only while a crossing guard is on duty
    • They are lifted for the summer holidays

    Most places limit a school zone to posted hours or to school days. Here the zone never switches off, and a speeding fine inside it is double what the same offence would cost elsewhere.

  16. When does the Northwest Territories expect your headlights to be on?

    • At all times, because it is the law hereCorrect answer
    • Only between dusk and dawn
    • Only when the wipers are running
    • Only outside the communities

    The territory tells drivers to keep their headlights on at all times and says plainly that it is the law. Long twilight, blowing snow and dust on gravel all make a lit vehicle far easier to pick out.

  17. On which Northwest Territories highways do most bison incidents happen?

    • Highways 3 and 5, with bison also found on Highway 7Correct answer
    • Highways 1 and 8
    • Highways 6 and 10
    • Highway 4 only

    The territory reports that most bison incidents happen along Highways 3 and 5, and notes that bison are also found on and along Highway 7. Knowing which road you are on tells you how much to expect.

  18. Along Highway 3, which stretch carries the greatest risk of a bison collision?

    • From kilometre 21 near Fort Providence to kilometre 70Correct answer
    • The first five kilometres out of Yellowknife
    • Only the sections that are lit
    • The stretch between kilometre 100 and kilometre 150

    The territory names the stretch from kilometre 21 near Fort Providence to kilometre 70 as the highest risk on Highway 3. Treat those markers as a reason to drop your speed and lengthen your view.

  19. When is the risk of hitting a bison at its highest?

    • From dusk until dawn, and especially from August through NovemberCorrect answer
    • Around midday, all year round
    • Early morning in spring only
    • At any hour during January and February

    The dangerous window runs from dusk until dawn, and the dangerous months run from August through November. Slowing down after dusk and again at dawn is the single most useful habit on those highways.

  20. Bison are standing on the road ahead of you at dusk. What does the territory advise?

    • Slow down, do not swerve suddenly, and stop and wait for them to clear the roadCorrect answer
    • Steer around them without slowing
    • Sound your horn and drive between them
    • Switch your headlights off so they do not panic

    A sudden swerve is how drivers lose control and end up in the ditch or in the oncoming lane. Use your high beams whenever you can, flash your hazard lights to warn the drivers behind you, and remember that most animals travel in herds.

  21. Why do winter roads and ice roads carry posted speed limits?

    • Because speed creates waves under the ice that can crack it or make it failCorrect answer
    • To keep fuel use down over long distances
    • Because those roads are patrolled less often
    • Because headlights work less well over ice

    A vehicle moving too fast pushes a wave through the water under the ice. Speed limits keep the weight spread out and the surface stable, and the posted weight limits do the same job, which is also why heavy vehicles are asked to keep extra space between each other.

  22. A winter road you had planned to use has been closed. What is the only acceptable response?

    • Do not drive on it, because a road is closed for the safety of everybody travellingCorrect answer
    • Drive on it slowly if your vehicle is a light one
    • Drive on it if somebody else has already left tracks
    • Drive on it during daylight only

    A road is closed because the surface can no longer carry traffic safely. Tracks left by somebody else tell you nothing about what the ice will hold now.

  23. The territory's winter advice includes keeping at least half a tank of fuel. What reason is given for it?

    • It stops the fuel lines from freezingCorrect answer
    • A full tank makes the vehicle heavier and steadier on ice
    • Fuel is rationed along the winter roads
    • It keeps the engine from overheating

    Fuel lines freeze when the tank runs low, and a stranded vehicle in the North can be a long way from help. The same advice covers a shovel, sand, booster cables, a flashlight, warm clothing, food and water, and a first-aid kit.

  24. How much space does the territory ask you to keep behind the vehicle ahead on a winter highway?

    • At least four secondsCorrect answer
    • One car length for every ten kilometres an hour
    • Two seconds, the same as in summer
    • Whatever feels comfortable at the speed you are driving

    Four seconds gives you room to see and to stop on a surface that may be packed snow or glare ice. Counting seconds works at any speed, which is why it beats guessing at car lengths.

  25. You are catching up to another vehicle on a dry gravel highway. What does the territory advise?

    • Slow down as you pass to cut the dust and flying stones, and keep the windows closed with the vent fan runningCorrect answer
    • Pass quickly, then close your windows afterwards
    • Stay behind until the road is paved again
    • Drive along the shoulder to stay out of the dust

    Stones thrown up at speed break windshields and headlights, and a dust cloud hides whatever is inside it. Closed windows and the vent fan keep the inside of the vehicle liveable on a long gravel run.

  26. What should you know about the ferry crossings on Northwest Territories highways?

    • There are four of them, they cost nothing, and vehicles board first come, first servedCorrect answer
    • They have to be reserved a day ahead
    • They charge according to the length of the vehicle
    • They run right through the year

    Four crossings operate, at no cost to the driver, and there is no booking: vehicles load in the order they arrive. Space on the last scheduled trip of the day is not guaranteed.

  27. You reach a ferry crossing during the first week of the season. What should you be ready for?

    • Ice and changing water levels stopping the service with little or no warningCorrect answer
    • A higher fare early in the season
    • A guaranteed place on every trip
    • A crossing reserved for local residents

    At the start and the end of the season the river decides. Service can stop with little or no notice, so build extra time into any trip that depends on a crossing.

  28. Your vehicle breaks down on a remote winter highway. Why does the territory tell you to stay with it?

    • Because it gives you shelter and makes you far easier for rescuers to seeCorrect answer
    • Because leaving it is against the law
    • Because the heater works better when the vehicle is parked
    • Because the vehicle will be towed automatically

    A vehicle is a windbreak, a landmark and a place to keep warm. Walking away from it in cold and blowing snow is how a breakdown turns into an emergency.

About this quiz

This page is free practice for the Northwest Territories knowledge examination, the written test you pass before you may drive with a supervisor. The territory calls the licence a Class 7 learner's licence, and it is Stage 1 of the graduated driver licensing programme.

The Northwest Territories is one of very few places in the country that publishes its whole test format, so almost nothing on this page is a standard of ours. There are two separate tests, twenty questions each, one on the rules of the road and one on road signage, and you may not miss more than four questions in each of them. That is sixteen of twenty on each test rather than a combined score.

Every question was written for this site from the free official Basic Driver's Manual, the licensing and examination pages published by Driver and Vehicle Services, the territory's own driving safety, bison, winter road, school zone and ferry pages, and the Driver's Licence Demerit Point Regulations. Nothing was copied from a manual, from a real test, or from another practice site.

What's inside

  • Sixty questions in total: thirty on road signs and thirty on the rules of the road, each with a short explanation.
  • A quick ten-question practice round at the top of the page, with signs and rules mixed together and no timer.
  • A timed mock test in the territory's own published format, with each test scored separately.
  • A road signs page where every sign is drawn in code and grouped by type, with practice for each group.
  • A rules of the road page organised by chapter, covering graduated licensing, your supervisor, demerit points, bison, ice roads and the ferries.
  • The whole question bank printed on the page, so you can read straight through it without playing.

Good to know

  • The knowledge examination is two separate tests of twenty questions, and you may not miss more than four questions in each of them.
  • A novice driver is suspended at six or more demerit points, while a full licence holder is not suspended until fifteen.
  • The learner and the supervisor must both be at zero blood alcohol, which is a rule most people only half know.
  • Headlights on at all times is the law in the Northwest Territories, not merely good practice.
  • A vision screening is required before the licence is issued, but a Class 5, 6 or 7 driver needs no medical report until age 75.

Common questions

How many questions are on the real Northwest Territories knowledge test?

Twenty on the rules of the road and twenty more on road signage, as two separate tests. The territory publishes that itself, which is unusual and very helpful.

What is the pass mark?

You may not miss more than four questions in each test, so sixteen of twenty on each. A commercial site claims you need twenty-five of the questions correct, but twenty-five out of forty can still fail both tests.

How long do I get?

The published wording is that the test lasts 30 minutes, in the singular. The territory does not say whether that is per test or for both together, so we do not claim either.

How long must I hold the Class 7 licence?

At least twelve months. Then the probationary Class 5P is held for twelve suspension-free months, and the territory publishes a minimum of two years to complete both stages.

Who can sit beside me while I practise?

Someone at least nineteen years old who holds a full Class 5 licence and has at least twenty-four months of experience, and time held on a graduated licence does not count. You must both be at zero blood alcohol.
