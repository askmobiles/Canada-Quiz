About this game

Inukshuk Builder is a stone stacking game set under a northern night sky. A stone slides left and right above your tower, and you drop it at the right moment. Whatever lines up stays in place; whatever hangs over the edge breaks off and tumbles away.

That single rule creates the whole challenge. Sloppy drops make each stone narrower than the one below it, so a careless start leaves you with a sliver to aim at ten stones later. Precision early is what buys you height at the top.

The page also explains what a real inukshuk is: a stone landmark built by the Inuit in northern Canada, used to point the way, mark good hunting or fishing spots, and show that someone had passed by. Playing it teaches that quietly.

How it works

  • Three controls do the same job: tap the picture, click with a mouse, or press the Space bar to drop.
  • The overlapping part of the stone stays on the tower and the overhang chips off and falls to the ground.
  • The sliding stone speeds up with every level you add, so late drops need much sharper timing.
  • Miss the tower completely and the stone falls away, the run ends, and your final height is shown.
  • Two counters sit above the picture, one for your current height and one for your best height so far.
  • The view scrolls upward as the tower grows, keeping the newest stones in the middle of the screen.

Tips for playing

  • Watch the edge of the moving stone rather than its middle. Your eye judges when two edges line up far better than it judges centres.
  • Drop a fraction early, not late. The stone is still travelling when you tap, so the perfect-looking moment has usually already passed.
  • A near-perfect drop keeps the tower wide, and a wide tower is forgiving. Width, not luck, is what carries a run past twenty stones.
  • The stone starts from the left on some levels and from the right on others, so do not settle into one fixed rhythm.
  • The Space bar gives steadier timing than a finger on glass, so try a keyboard when you are chasing a personal record.
  • Your best height stays on screen while the page is open, so several short runs in a row make a proper contest.

Common questions

Does this game work on a phone as well as a computer?

Yes, on both. On a phone or tablet you tap the picture, and on a computer you click it or press the Space bar. The picture resizes itself to fit the screen you are using.

Is my best height saved for the next time I visit?

It is kept only while this page stays open. Closing the tab clears it, which suits a quick family contest where everybody takes a turn one after another in the same sitting.

How young can a player be for this game?

Small children manage it easily because there is only one action to learn. There is no reading, no time limit and no way to lose points, so a five-year-old can play beside an adult.

Why do my stones keep getting smaller?

Every part that hangs past the stone below is cut away, and the shortened stone becomes the target for the next drop. Tidy drops early in a run are what keep the tower wide.

Is this a real Canadian tradition or just a game idea?

Inukshuks are real stone landmarks built by the Inuit in the North. The game borrows the idea of careful stacking, but a genuine inukshuk is a marker for travellers, not a score to beat.
