🐍 Maple Snake

📖 How to play

Steer the snake with the arrow keys, the on-screen buttons, or by swiping on your phone. Eat the maple leaves to grow and score points. Don't hit the walls or your own tail — and watch out, it speeds up as you go!

About this game

Maple Snake is the old arcade game given a Canadian coat of paint. You steer a red snake around a square board, eating maple leaves. Each leaf makes the snake one segment longer, and the longer it gets the harder it is to stay out of your own way.

Rounds are short, so the game fits a coffee break or the last minutes before dinner. Children pick it up in seconds, and adults who played something like it years ago usually want one more try after the first crash.

How it works

  • The board is a twenty by twenty grid, and the walls end your run the moment the head touches one.
  • Steer with the arrow keys, the W A S D keys, the four on-screen buttons, or a swipe on a touchscreen.
  • Every maple leaf you eat adds one point to the score and one more segment to the tail behind you.
  • The snake speeds up a little every five points, so late in a run the board starts to feel much smaller.
  • Score and best sit above the board, and a new game button restarts everything the moment a run ends.

Tips for playing

  • You cannot turn straight back on yourself, because the game ignores a reverse command. That quietly saves beginners from an instant crash.
  • Hugging the outside edge keeps the middle of the board clear and gives you somewhere to escape once the tail gets long.
  • Grabbing a leaf tucked into a corner is where most runs end, so approach corners along the wall rather than head-on.
  • Because the speed rises with your score, planning two moves ahead matters far more here than having fast fingers.
  • The best score shown belongs to your current visit only, so reloading the page starts that challenge again from zero.

Common questions

Does the game get harder, or does it stay the same speed?

It gets harder on its own. Every five leaves the snake moves a bit quicker, down to a set limit, so a long run always ends up feeling frantic near the finish.

Can I play this without a keyboard at all?

Yes. Four arrow buttons sit right under the board, and a swipe across the board itself turns the snake in that direction. A phone or tablet needs nothing else.

Why did my snake die when the way looked clear?

Almost always the head met its own tail. The tail follows one step behind the head, so a tight turn late in a run can close the gap before your eyes notice it.

Is this suitable for a young child?

There is nothing frightening in it, just a coloured square eating leaves. Very young players may find the later speed too quick, but a new game is only one tap away.
