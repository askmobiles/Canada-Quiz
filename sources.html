📚 Sources and Credits

We are a small independent site. Almost everything we publish rests on work done by governments, museums, archives and encyclopedias. This page names them and links to them, because they earned the credit and because you deserve to know where a fact came from before you trust it.

The short version. We are not affiliated with the Government of Canada, with any province, or with any of the organisations listed below. We link to them because we use their published information. Nothing on this site is official. For anything that affects your citizenship application or your driving licence, the government source is the one that counts, and we link to it every time.

Citizenship practice questions

Our 259 citizenship questions are written from the official study guide, Discover Canada: The Rights and Responsibilities of Citizenship, published by Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada. We write our own questions and our own explanations rather than reproducing the guide, but the facts come from it.

Citizenship sources

Driving test practice

We cover Ontario, British Columbia, Alberta, Quebec and Manitoba. For each province the rules of the road, the sign meanings and the structure of the real test come from that province's own driver handbook and licensing authority. Where a province does not publish the exact number of questions on its knowledge test, we say so on the page rather than inventing a number.

Provincial driving sources

  • Ontario — Ministry of Transportation, Official MTO Driver's Handbook
  • Ontario — DriveTest, G1 knowledge test
  • British Columbia — ICBC, Learn to Drive Smart
  • Alberta — Government of Alberta driver's guides
  • Quebec — Société de l'assurance automobile du Québec
  • Manitoba — Manitoba Public Insurance, Driver's Handbook

How we check things

  • Two sources for anything surprising. If a fact would make a reader raise an eyebrow, we do not publish it on one source alone.
  • Dates get the primary source. For anything constitutional we go to the text of the Act, not to a summary of it.
  • We show our disagreements. When Statistics Canada and a provincial government give different figures, we print both and explain the difference.
  • Every long article shows when it was last checked. A page about a changing situation is dated so you can judge for yourself whether it is still current.
  • We correct mistakes. If you find one, write to us and we will fix it and say that we did.

Technical credits
