🙈 Hold to Head

Categories

Hold the phone on your forehead — your team gives clues, you guess!

📖 How to play

One player holds the phone flat against their forehead so they can't see the screen (everyone else can!). Your team shouts clues — but never says the word. When you guess it right, tap the top green half of the screen. To skip a hard one, tap the bottom orange half. You have 60 seconds to get as many as you can. Then pass the phone to the next player!

Pick a category, hold the phone to your forehead, and go!

About this game

Hold to Head turns one phone into a whole party. One player holds the screen flat against their forehead and cannot see the word on it. Everyone else can see it, and they shout clues until the holder guesses. Each round lasts sixty seconds, then the phone changes hands.

It fits a living room, a break between classes, a long wait at a restaurant or a birthday party. Nothing is downloaded and no account is created — you open the page, choose a category, and a big three, two, one countdown starts the round.

Because the clue-givers do most of the talking, quiet players and young children can join without pressure. Older kids and adults can stretch themselves with the starred advanced categories, so one game keeps a mixed-age group happy around the same table.

How it works

  • Five everyday categories cover Animals, Food and Drink, Actions, Around Canada, and Around the House.
  • Three starred advanced categories add world geography, jobs and people, and science and space for stronger players.
  • Every round runs for sixty seconds, and the countdown gives the holder time to lift the phone up.
  • Tap the green top half of the screen the moment your team guesses the word correctly.
  • Tap the orange bottom half to skip a word nobody can explain, and no points are lost.
  • When the timer ends, the page shows how many words you got and asks you to pass the phone.

Tips for playing

  • Good clue-givers start with the most obvious thing about a word — its shape, its sound, or where you usually see it — and save the small details for later.
  • Skipping fast is often smarter than fighting one hard word, because a skip costs a second while a stubborn word can eat ten.
  • Hands and faces still work as clues, since the holder can see everyone in the room even though the screen is hidden from them.
  • The Around Canada list is full of things newcomers meet early: toques, canoes, loonies, poutine, Mounties and Niagara Falls all show up.
  • Words are shuffled at the start of every round, so the same category feels fresh the second and third time you choose it.
  • Hold the phone steady with both hands; a wobbling screen creates accidental taps that skip a word before anyone has said a thing.

Common questions

Do I need to install an app to play Hold to Head?

No. The game runs inside this web page on the phone you already carry. Open it, pick a category and start. Nothing is installed on your device and no email address or account is asked for.

How many people does a round need?

Two is enough, one holder and one clue-giver, but four or more voices make it much funnier. There is no upper limit as long as everybody can crowd close enough to read the screen.

Can younger children take part in this one?

Yes. Animals, Food and Drink and Around the House use words a five-year-old already knows. Let a young child give clues for the first round so they hear how the game sounds before they guess.

How does the game count my points?

Each word your team guesses adds one point when you tap the green half. Skips cost nothing at all. After sixty seconds the page prints your total for that category on the screen.

Is a computer as good as a phone for this game?

A phone is far better, because the whole idea is holding the screen on your forehead. On a computer you would have to turn the screen away from yourself and let a friend read the word aloud.
