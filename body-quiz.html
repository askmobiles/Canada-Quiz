About this quiz

The Human Body and Health Quiz turns a biology lesson into ten quick questions. Choose a part of the body, whether bones, blood, the brain or the food you eat, and find out how much you already know about the machine you live inside.

Children studying health at school, parents looking for screen time that teaches something, and adults who last saw a diagram of the heart many years ago all get value from it. The language stays gentle and clear throughout.

Answers appear the instant you tap, along with a reason written in everyday words. Even a round full of guesses leaves you knowing a handful of things about your own body that you did not know when you started.

What's inside

  • Six colour-coded topic cards appear first, and each card states how many questions that part of the body holds.
  • Bones and Muscles asks about the skeleton, cartilage and the parts of you that bend without ever breaking.
  • Heart and Blood covers plasma, circulation and the work blood does as it travels around inside you.
  • Brain and Senses looks at how you see, hear, smell and keep your balance without thinking about it.
  • Food and Nutrition, Germs and Staying Well, and Sleep, Exercise and Growing finish the set with everyday health habits.
  • Every round runs to ten questions, with a score counter and a bar showing how far through the set you are.

Good to know

  • Cartilage is the firm but bendy material that shapes your ears and the tip of your nose, which is why they flex without snapping.
  • Plasma is the pale liquid part of blood. It is mostly water, and it carries blood cells and nutrients wherever they are needed.
  • Antibodies work rather like labels: they stick to a germ so that white blood cells can find it and deal with it.
  • Coughing into your elbow works because a cough flings tiny droplets, and a sleeve catches them instead of your hands or the air.
  • Smell receptors sit high inside the nose and catch tiny particles drifting in the air, which is how a scent reaches you.

No. It is a general knowledge quiz about how the body works, made for learning and for fun. Anything concerning your own health belongs with a doctor or a nurse, never with a quiz page.

It sits comfortably with school-age children and upwards. Younger players often do well on the food and sleep topics, while bones, blood and germs suit anyone who has covered a little biology.

Yes, and it is worth doing. The quiz keeps track of what has already been shown on your device and reaches for unseen questions first, so a second visit usually brings different material.

You get a score out of ten with a short encouraging message, then buttons to pick another body topic or move to the wider quiz list. Nothing is stored in an account anywhere.

There is no clock at all. You can sit with a question as long as you like, talk it over with someone beside you, and only tap once everyone is ready to commit.
