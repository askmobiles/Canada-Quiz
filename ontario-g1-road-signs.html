Ontario Road Signs

Every sign you need for the G1 test, grouped by type. Look at the pictures first, then practise the group with real test questions. No timer.

Read the shape and the colour first

Canadian road signs tell you a lot before you even read the words. A red circle with a slash means you must not do the thing shown, while a green circle means you may. A yellow diamond warns you about something ahead, and an orange diamond means road work. Green and blue signs give you directions and services. Once the shapes and colours are second nature, the sign half of the test becomes much easier.

About this quiz

The sign half of the G1 test is the half you can study with your eyes. This page shows the signs as drawings, sorted into groups, with the name of each one written underneath so the picture and the meaning settle in together.

Nothing here is timed and nothing is scored. Open a group, look through the pictures until they feel familiar, then press the practice button underneath that group to answer only the questions that use those particular signs, and no others.

This is unofficial practice with no link to the Ministry of Transportation, ServiceOntario or DriveTest. The free MTO Driver's Handbook remains the book to read; this page is simply where you drill the pictures until they stick.

What's inside

  • Regulatory signs come first: stop, yield, speed limits, turn bans, parking rules, school bus and pedestrian crossover signs.
  • Warning signs are the yellow diamonds for curves, hills, railway crossings, school zones, deer, moose and merging traffic.
  • Construction signs form the orange group: work zones, flaggers, detours, closed lanes and survey crews at the roadside.
  • Information signs are the green and blue ones for hospitals, fuel, food, exits and highway route markers.
  • Traffic lights get their own group, covering red, amber, green, advance green arrows and flashing lights.
  • Road markings finish the set with solid and broken yellow lines, lane lines, stop lines and crosswalks.

Good to know

  • A red circle with a slash through it tells you not to do the thing shown, while a green circle means the same action is allowed.
  • Yellow diamonds warn about what lies ahead, and orange diamonds carry the same kind of warning but only while road work lasts.
  • The X-shaped crossbuck marks the railway crossing itself, while the round yellow sign further back warns you that one is coming.
  • A checkerboard of black and yellow squares means the road turns sharply just ahead, so slow down well before the corner.
  • Green and blue backgrounds are the helpful ones. They guide you to directions, distances and services rather than ordering you around.

Common questions

How many road signs does this study page show?

The gallery holds eighty signs spread across the six groups. Each group tells you how many it contains before you scroll, so you can study one manageable set at a time.

Are these sign pictures the same as the real ones?

They are our own drawings of the standard Canadian shapes and colours, made so the page loads quickly. The shape, the colour and the symbol match what you will meet on the road.

Can I practise just one group of signs?

Yes. Under every group sits a practice button that runs only the questions using those signs, so you can drill warning signs without ever touching the information signs.

Is there a time limit while I am studying the signs?

No clock runs on this page at all. Take as long as you need on each picture, and save the timed experience for the full mock test when you feel prepared.

Does this page replace the official driver's handbook?

No. This is unofficial practice with no government affiliation. Read the free MTO Driver's Handbook for the complete rules, and use this page to make the pictures stay in your memory.
