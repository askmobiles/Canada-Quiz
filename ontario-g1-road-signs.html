Read the shape and the colour first

Canadian road signs tell you a lot before you even read the words. A red circle with a slash means you must not do the thing shown, while a green circle means you may. A yellow diamond warns you about something ahead, and an orange diamond means road work. Green and blue signs give you directions and services. Once the shapes and colours are second nature, the sign half of the test becomes much easier.

  1. What must you do at this sign, even when the road looks empty?

    • Come to a full stop every time, then go when it is safeCorrect answer
    • Slow down and roll through if nothing is coming
    • Stop only during busy hours
    • Stop only if a pedestrian is waiting

    A full stop is needed every time. Stop at the line, look both ways, and move ahead only when the road is clear.

  2. What does this sign ask you to do?

    • Let the traffic already on the road go first, and stop if you need toCorrect answer
    • Stop completely every time before you go
    • Speed up so you can join traffic quickly
    • Take your turn after the car on your left

    You give the right of way here. Slow down, look, and enter only when there is a safe gap — stop if there is no gap.

  3. What does this sign mean?

    • 50 km/h is the fastest you may legally drive hereCorrect answer
    • You must drive at least 50 km/h
    • The road ahead is 50 km long
    • 50 km/h is only a suggestion in good weather

    It is a legal maximum, not a target. In rain, snow or heavy traffic you must drive slower than the posted number.

    • Trucks may use this road only when they are empty

    A red ring with a red slash means the thing shown in the circle is banned. Truck drivers must take another route, while cars and other small vehicles may still use this road.

  5. What does this sign tell you?

    This sign warns you that the limit is about to change. Start adjusting your speed now so you are at the new limit when you get there.

    A left turn on red is allowed only from a one-way street onto another one-way street, and only after a full stop when nothing is coming.

    • A car wash is ahead

  27. You see this sign on a highway. What does it tell you?

    • This is the place where you can leave the highwayCorrect answer

    • You must leave the highway here

    Green signs give directions and guidance. Move into the correct lane early instead of cutting across at the last second.

  28. What does this sign tell you?

    Route markers confirm you are still on the right road. Look for them after every large intersection or interchange.

  29. What does this sign mean?

    • You are on the highway that crosses the whole countryCorrect answer
    • You are crossing into another province
    • A national park entrance is ahead
    • A border crossing to the United States is ahead

    This route links Canada from coast to coast, so the same marker appears in every province along the way.

  30. Who may park in a space marked with this sign?

    • Anyone, if they stay less than ten minutes
    • Anyone, if no other space is free
    • Only ambulances and other emergency vehicles

    These spaces are wider and close to the door because some drivers and passengers need them. Using one without a permit can get you a ticket, even for a short stop.

  31. What does this sign mean?

    • The road is closed to the right
    • You must turn right here
    • The right lane ends ahead

    Slow down before you get to the curve. Braking hard once you are inside a sharp curve can make the car skid.

  32. You see this sign on a northern highway after dark. What is the safest thing to do?

    • Slow down and scan both shoulders, using your high beams when no other vehicle is nearCorrect answer
    • Speed up so you spend less time on that stretch of road
    • Turn your headlights off so you can see the road better
    • Drive close behind the vehicle ahead so you can use its lights

    A moose is tall, dark and very hard to see at night, and headlights reflect little from it. Because the body sits so high, a crash can push the animal onto the windshield. Slow down and be ready to brake early.

    • Loose gravel has been spread on the road

    • A railway crossing is ahead

  35. What does this sign mean?

    • There is a steep hill going down aheadCorrect answer
    • Trucks are not allowed on this road
    • A truck weigh station is ahead
    • A parking area for trucks is ahead

    Shift into a lower gear before you start down. Holding the brakes all the way down can make them overheat and fade.

    • A weigh station for farm trucks is ahead
    • The road is reserved for farm vehicles only
    • A livestock market is ahead

    Farm equipment often travels far below the speed limit, so you close the gap much faster than you expect. It can also be wide and may swing across the lane when turning into a field. Slow down early and pass only where it is legal and you can see far ahead.

    • The street ahead is closed to cars
    • You may park here while children play

    A ball rolling onto the road is often followed by a child. Keep your foot near the brake, hold your speed down and look between parked cars. This warning applies every day, not only during school hours.

  42. What does this sign mean?

    • A playground is ahead
    • Children are not allowed on this road
    • A daycare is ahead

    Children may be beside the road or on it. Drive slowly, obey the posted school speed limit, and watch for crossing guards.

  43. What does this sign mean?

    • There is a deer farm ahead
    • Hunting is allowed beside this road
    • A wildlife park is ahead

    Animals can step onto the road with no warning. Slow down, especially at dawn and dusk, and watch both shoulders.

  44. What does this sign mean?

    • A bridge is ahead
    • Two roads join together ahead
    • A rest stop is ahead

    • A tunnel is closed ahead
    • A weight limit starts ahead
    • A narrow bridge is ahead

  46. What does this sign mean?

    • A railway crosses the road aheadCorrect answer
    • A road crosses a river ahead
    • A rest area is ahead
    • A truck route starts ahead

    This round yellow sign warns you well before the tracks. Slow down, look and listen both ways, and be ready to stop.

  47. What must you do when you reach this sign?

    This sign marks the tracks themselves. You do not have to stop every time, but you must be sure no train is coming before you cross.

  48. What does this sign mean?

    • A circular parking area is ahead
    • You must turn right ahead
    • Traffic joins your road from the left

    Slow down as you get close and enter only when there is a safe gap. Drivers already in the circle go first.

  49. What must you do at this light?

    • Come to a full stop before the stop line and waitCorrect answer
    • Slow down and go through if the way is clear
    • Stop only if another vehicle is coming
    • Speed up to clear the intersection

    Stop completely before the line. Wait for green before going straight. A right turn on red is allowed only after that full stop, and only if no sign forbids it.

  50. What must you do at this light?

    • Treat it the same as a green light

    Amber means the light is about to turn red. Braking hard when you are already at the line can cause the driver behind you to hit you.

  51. What must you do at this light?

    • Go, because you always have the right of way over everyone
    • Slow down and get ready to stop

    A green light lets you go, but it does not let you block the intersection. If traffic on the far side is not moving, wait where you are.

  52. What must you do at this light?

    • The light is broken, so treat it as a stop sign
    • You may turn only after yielding to oncoming traffic

    A green arrow gives you a protected turn. Still check the crosswalk for people before you move.

    • It is a warning that the light is about to turn red, so slow down
    • The light is out of order, so treat it as a stop sign
    • Only buses and taxis may go

    In Ontario a flashing green means you have an advance turn. Drivers coming the other way have a red light, so you may turn left in front of them.

  60. What does this sign mean?

    • School buses are not allowed on this road
    • A school parking lot is ahead

    The sign warns you that a school bus stop is close by. When a school bus is stopped with its upper red lights flashing on a road with no median, traffic in both directions must stop and wait until the lights go off and the bus moves again. Children may cross in front of the bus.

  61. You reach this sign and a person is walking across. When may you drive on?

    • Once they are completely across and back on the sidewalkCorrect answer
    • As soon as they are out of your lane
    • Right away, if you steer around them
    • As soon as they pass the middle of the road

    At a pedestrian crossover you must wait until the person is all the way across and safely on the sidewalk. Not yielding here costs up to $1,000 and four demerit points.

  62. How much of the road must you leave clear at a pedestrian crossover?

    • The whole width of the roadCorrect answer
    • Only your own lane
    • Half the road
    • Just the crosswalk paint

    You must yield the entire width of the road, not only your lane. A driver in another lane may not see the person, so nobody moves until the crossing is finished.

  63. This sign swings out from a school bus with red lights flashing. What must you do?

    • Stop, whether you are behind the bus or coming toward itCorrect answer
    • Sound your horn and keep going
    • Pass slowly on the left
    • Stop only if you are behind the bus

    Traffic in both directions must stop for a school bus with its red lights flashing. The only exception is a road divided by a median, where only traffic behind the bus stops.

  64. How far back from a stopped school bus with flashing red lights must you stay?

    • At least 5 metres
    • At least 50 metres
    • Any distance, as long as you stop

    Stop at least 20 metres behind the bus so children have room to walk around it. Wait until the lights stop flashing and the bus moves.

    This is one of the most serious offences on the road: $2,000 to $4,000 and six demerit points. For a novice driver, six points alone triggers a second warning letter.

    • The speed limit is removed
    • Only local traffic may drive there

    • Only on 400-series highways
    • Only in the far north

    They are set up by the local municipality wherever people on foot need extra protection — school areas, playgrounds and busy neighbourhood streets are the most common.

    Yellow diamonds warn of something ahead. Here it means people cross the road further on, so cover the brake and scan both shoulders.

    Ontario law asks for at least one metre between your vehicle and the bike where practical. If the lane is too narrow for that, change lanes to pass or wait.

  71. What does this sign tell you to expect?

    • A stop sign just ahead
    • A railway crossing ahead

    It appears where the lights are hard to see in time — after a hill or a curve. Ease off the gas so you can stop comfortably if the light is red.

    • A quarry entrance ahead
    • A gravel road begins

    Rock can break loose from the cut beside the road, especially in spring thaw or after heavy rain. Scan the pavement ahead and do not stop under the slope.

  83. What happens just past this sign?

    • The paved surface ends and gravel beginsCorrect answer
    • A toll booth begins
    • A new passing lane begins
    • The road is closed

    Pavement gives way to gravel. Slow down before the change — gravel gives far less grip, and braking hard on it can start a skid.

  84. What kind of sign is this blue one with a knife and fork?

    • An information sign pointing to food servicesCorrect answer
    • A regulatory sign you must obey
    • A construction sign
    • A warning sign

    Blue signs give you information about services near the road — food, fuel, lodging and accessible parking. They do not order you to do anything.

  85. Beyond slowing down, what is the most useful thing to do in a school zone?

    • Watch between parked cars, where a child can step out without lookingCorrect answer
    • Drive in the middle of the road
    • Turn on your high beams
    • Sound your horn to warn children

    Children step out from between parked cars without checking. Cover the brake, keep your eyes moving and be ready to stop for someone you have not seen yet.

  86. Why does this sign matter even when you cannot see any children?

    • A ball or a child can come out from behind a hedge or parked car at any momentCorrect answer
    • It does not, if the playground looks empty
    • It only applies in summer
    • It only applies on weekends

    Playground signs mark places where a child can appear with no warning, chasing a ball into the road. Slow down and stay ready whether or not you see anyone.

  87. What does this yellow sign tell you?

    • A school bus stop is somewhere aheadCorrect answer
    • School buses are banned here
    • This is a bus terminal
    • A school bus is stopped right now

    It warns that buses stop for children further along the road. It is a heads-up, not an order to stop — the order comes from the bus's own flashing red lights.

  88. What is the main risk this sign warns you about?

    • Farm vehicles are wide and slow, and may turn into a field with little warningCorrect answer
    • Farm vehicles are not allowed on the road
    • Farm vehicles always have the right of way
    • Farm vehicles are very fast

    Tractors and combines are wide, slow and hard to see around. Hold back until you can see far ahead, and watch for a left turn into a field entrance.

  89. Who most needs to pay attention to this sign?

    • Anyone driving a tall vehicle such as a van with a roof box or a moving truckCorrect answer
    • Only motorcycle riders
    • Only cyclists
    • Nobody, it is only decoration

    The number is the height of the opening ahead. If your vehicle, roof box or load is taller than the number, you must find another route.

  90. Why is hitting the animal on this sign especially dangerous?

    • Its body sits high, so it can come through the windshield in a crashCorrect answer
    • It is small and hard to see
    • It always runs toward headlights
    • It travels only in daylight

    A moose is tall and heavy, and its body rides above the bumper. Slow down at dawn and dusk in these areas and scan the ditches, not just the road.

  91. In Ontario, what does this flashing green light give you?

    • The right to go straight, turn right, or turn left before oncoming trafficCorrect answer
    • A signal to stop and wait
    • A warning that the light is about to turn red
    • Permission to turn right only

    A flashing green in Ontario means oncoming traffic is being held by a red light. You may go straight through, turn right, or turn left first.

  92. How do you treat this flashing red light?

    • Exactly like a stop signCorrect answer
    • Exactly like a yield sign
    • Drive through without slowing
    • Wait for it to turn green

    Come to a full stop, then go when the way is clear. It will not turn green, so waiting for green would leave you there all day.

  93. The light turns amber as you approach and you are already very close to the line. What is correct?

    • Continue through if stopping safely is no longer possibleCorrect answer
    • Speed up to beat the red
    • Stop in the middle of the intersection
    • Brake hard no matter what is behind you

    Amber means stop if you can do so safely. If you are so close that hard braking would cause a crash from behind, carry on through and clear the intersection.

  94. At a red light you want to turn right. What must happen first?

    • A full stop, then yield to people walking and to traffic, unless a sign forbids the turnCorrect answer
    • Nothing, a right turn on red is automatic
    • Only a slow roll is needed
    • You must wait for a green arrow

    A right turn on red is allowed in Ontario after a complete stop, but only when no sign prohibits it and only after you yield to pedestrians and cross traffic.

  95. A green arrow points left while the round light beside it is still red. What may you do?

    • Turn left; oncoming traffic is being held for youCorrect answer
    • Turn left only after yielding to oncoming cars
    • Nothing, red always wins
    • Go straight through

    The advance green arrow protects your turn — oncoming traffic has a red light. You may turn in the arrow's direction, but the red still blocks going straight.

  96. Your light is green but cars are backed up on the far side of the intersection. What should you do?

    • Wait behind the line until there is room to clear the intersectionCorrect answer
    • Sound your horn and enter
    • Reverse out of the lane
    • Enter anyway, green means go

    Never enter an intersection you cannot clear. Blocking it stops cross traffic when their light turns green, and in Ontario that is an offence.

  97. A double solid yellow line runs down the middle of the road. What does it stop you doing?

    • Passing another vehicle by crossing the lineCorrect answer
    • Stopping at the curb
    • Using your signal
    • Turning left into a driveway

    You may not cross a double solid yellow line to pass. You may still cross it to turn left into a driveway or side road when it is safe.

  98. What does a broken yellow line beside your lane allow?

    • Passing, when the way ahead is clear and safeCorrect answer
    • Nothing, it is decoration
    • Parking overnight
    • Driving in either direction in that lane

    Yellow separates traffic going opposite ways. Broken means passing is permitted from your side when you can see far enough ahead to complete it safely.

  99. What do broken white lines tell you?

    • Traffic beside you is going the same way and you may change lanesCorrect answer
    • The road is one way only
    • Traffic beside you is going the other way
    • You must not change lanes

    White lines separate lanes moving in the same direction. Broken white means you may move across when it is safe; solid white means stay in your lane.

  100. There is a stop line painted before the crosswalk. Where do your wheels stop?

    • Behind the stop lineCorrect answer
    • In the middle of the intersection
    • Past the crosswalk, so you can see
    • On the crosswalk

    Stop behind the line first. If your view is blocked from there, creep forward slowly and stop a second time once you can see.

  101. A person has stepped onto these painted lines. What must you do?

    • Yield and wait for them to finish crossingCorrect answer
    • Drive around behind them
    • Flash your lights and continue
    • Reverse

    Painted crosswalk lines mark where people cross. Once someone has stepped on, you wait — turning across in front of them is both dangerous and an offence.

  102. You reach this X-shaped railway sign and a train is approaching. Where must you stop?

    • No closer than 5 metres from the nearest railCorrect answer
    • As close to the rails as possible
    • Right on the tracks so you can see
    • 30 metres back

    Stop no closer than 5 metres from the nearest rail. Never start across until you can see that the whole crossing is clear on the far side.

  103. The gates at a crossing lift, but a second train is coming the other way. What do you do?

    • Wait until you can see clearly that both tracks are clearCorrect answer
    • Sound your horn and cross
    • Go, the gates are the only rule
    • Drive between the tracks and wait

    On a multi-track crossing, one train can hide another. Look both ways along every track before you move, even after the gates rise.

  104. You are about to enter the roundabout this sign announces. Who has the right of way?

    • Traffic already going around inside the circleCorrect answer
    • The largest vehicle
    • Traffic entering from the right
    • Whoever arrives first

    Yield to traffic already in the roundabout, look left for a gap, and go counter-clockwise. Signal right just before the exit you want.

  105. You are on the main road and this sign warns of traffic merging. What is the courteous and safe move?

    • Adjust your speed or move over so the merging driver has a gapCorrect answer
    • Drive alongside them in their blind spot
    • Stop on the main road
    • Speed up so nobody can get in

    Merging works when both drivers help. Where safe, move over a lane or ease off slightly so the joining vehicle has room to blend in at speed.

  106. You have been on a divided road and now you see this sign. What changes?

    • Traffic will now come toward you in the next laneCorrect answer
    • The speed limit doubles
    • The road becomes one way
    • A passing lane opens

    The road stops being divided. Oncoming traffic is now right beside you, so passing needs a clear view of the whole road ahead.

  107. Why is passing banned where this sign stands, even if the road looks empty?

    • You cannot see far enough ahead to pass safely, because of a hill or curveCorrect answer
    • Trucks use the road
    • The pavement is new
    • There is a school nearby

    No-passing zones sit where sight lines are short. A car can appear from over the crest or around the bend in the seconds you would spend beside another vehicle.

  108. What is the difference between this sign and a no-parking sign?

    • No stopping means you may not even pause; no parking still lets you stop briefly to load or drop someone offCorrect answer
    • No stopping applies only at night
    • No stopping applies only to trucks
    • There is none

    No stopping is the stricter of the two — you may not halt at all except to obey a police officer or a traffic control. No parking still allows a brief stop with the driver present.

  109. When may you use the lane marked by this diamond sign?

    • When your vehicle carries at least the number of people shown on the signCorrect answer
    • Only on weekends
    • Only if you are a taxi
    • Any time you are in a hurry

    High occupancy vehicle lanes reward carpooling. The sign states the minimum number of people, and driving alone in one is a ticket with three demerit points.

  110. An island splits the road and this sign faces you. What must you do?

    • Pass to the right of the islandCorrect answer
    • Pass on either side
    • Pass to the left of the island
    • Stop before the island

    The arrow tells you which side to take. Going the wrong way around the island puts you head-on into traffic coming the other way.

  111. Besides where this sign stands, where else is a U-turn never allowed?

    • On a curve or hill where drivers cannot see you from far enough awayCorrect answer
    • At a green light
    • On a quiet residential street
    • In an empty parking lot

    U-turns are banned wherever you cannot be seen coming — on curves, near the crest of a hill, and on a road divided by a median.

  112. You turn onto a street and see this sign pointing away from you. What has happened?

    • You are going the wrong way and must pull over safely and turn aroundCorrect answer
    • The street is closed for repairs
    • Parking is banned
    • The street is for buses

    The arrow shows the only legal direction of travel. If it points against you, stop as soon as it is safe, then back out or turn around before you meet traffic.

  113. A traffic control person in a work zone signals you to stop while your light is green. Who do you obey?

    • The traffic control personCorrect answer
    • Whichever comes first
    • Neither, use your judgement
    • The green light

    A person directing traffic overrides signs and signals. Ignoring their direction in a construction zone is an offence and puts workers in danger.

  114. What happens to speeding fines in a marked construction zone when workers are present?

    • They are doubledCorrect answer
    • They stay the same
    • They are cut in half
    • They are cancelled

    Fines double in construction zones when workers are on site. Slow to the posted work-zone limit as soon as you see the orange signs.

  115. This sign shows the right lane closing ahead. When should you move over?

    • As soon as you safely can, before the cones startCorrect answer
    • After the lane has ended
    • At the very last cone
    • Only if someone lets you

    Move over early and smoothly while there is still room. Racing to the front and squeezing in at the last cone is what turns a slow zone into a stopped one.

  116. What should you do when the road you planned to take is marked with this sign?

    • Follow the marked route, watching for the next detour signCorrect answer
    • Reverse down the road
    • Park and wait
    • Drive around the barricade

    Detour signs come in a chain. Follow each one to the next rather than trying a shortcut of your own, which often ends at a closed road.

  117. What does this orange sign tell you to watch for?

    • A crew working on or beside the road, sometimes standing in a laneCorrect answer
    • A weather station
    • A rest area
    • A police radar unit

    Orange means temporary conditions. A survey crew may be standing near the driving lane with equipment, so slow down and give them a wide berth.

  118. What kind of information does this green and white marker give you?

    • Which provincial highway you are on or heading towardCorrect answer
    • The speed limit
    • The distance to the border
    • The weight limit

    Route markers name the highway. Ontario's 400-series highways are the fast, divided freeways — and a G1 driver may not drive on most of them.

  119. A space carries this sign. Who is allowed to leave a car there?

    • Only a vehicle displaying a valid accessible parking permitCorrect answer
    • Delivery vehicles
    • Anyone, for a short stop
    • Anyone after 6 p.m.

    Only a vehicle with a valid permit may use the space, at any hour. Parking there without one carries a heavy fine in every Ontario municipality.

  120. Why is this sign placed well before the stop sign itself?

    • To give you time to slow down where the stop sign is hidden by a hill or treesCorrect answer
    • To mark a parking area
    • To show a detour
    • To let you speed up first

    It is used where the stop sign cannot be seen from far away. Start braking at this sign so you arrive at the stop line already slow.

About this quiz

The sign half of the G1 test is the half you can study with your eyes. This page shows the signs as drawings, sorted into groups, with the name of each one written underneath so the picture and the meaning settle in together.

Nothing here is timed and nothing is scored. Open a group, look through the pictures until they feel familiar, then press the practice button underneath that group to answer only the questions that use those particular signs, and no others.

This is unofficial practice with no link to the Ministry of Transportation, ServiceOntario or DriveTest. The free MTO Driver's Handbook remains the book to read; this page is simply where you drill the pictures until they stick.

What's inside

  • Regulatory signs come first: stop, yield, speed limits, turn bans, parking rules, school bus and pedestrian crossover signs.
  • Warning signs are the yellow diamonds for curves, hills, railway crossings, school zones, deer, moose and merging traffic.
  • Construction signs form the orange group: work zones, flaggers, detours, closed lanes and survey crews at the roadside.
  • Information signs are the green and blue ones for hospitals, fuel, food, exits and highway route markers.
  • Traffic lights get their own group, covering red, amber, green, advance green arrows and flashing lights.
  • Road markings finish the set with solid and broken yellow lines, lane lines, stop lines and crosswalks.

Good to know

  • A red circle with a slash through it tells you not to do the thing shown, while a green circle means the same action is allowed.
  • Yellow diamonds warn about what lies ahead, and orange diamonds carry the same kind of warning but only while road work lasts.
  • The X-shaped crossbuck marks the railway crossing itself, while the round yellow sign further back warns you that one is coming.
  • A checkerboard of black and yellow squares means the road turns sharply just ahead, so slow down well before the corner.
  • Green and blue backgrounds are the helpful ones. They guide you to directions, distances and services rather than ordering you around.

Common questions

How many road signs does this study page show?

The gallery holds eighty signs spread across the six groups. Each group tells you how many it contains before you scroll, so you can study one manageable set at a time.

Are these sign pictures the same as the real ones?

They are our own drawings of the standard Canadian shapes and colours, made so the page loads quickly. The shape, the colour and the symbol match what you will meet on the road.

Can I practise just one group of signs?

Yes. Under every group sits a practice button that runs only the questions using those signs, so you can drill warning signs without ever touching the information signs.

Is there a time limit while I am studying the signs?

No clock runs on this page at all. Take as long as you need on each picture, and save the timed experience for the full mock test when you feel prepared.

Does this page replace the official driver's handbook?

No. This is unofficial practice with no government affiliation. Read the free MTO Driver's Handbook for the complete rules, and use this page to make the pictures stay in your memory.
