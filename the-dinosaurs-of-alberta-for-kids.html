The Valley in Alberta Where Dinosaurs Keep Turning Up

There is a place in Alberta where you can walk along and see a real dinosaur bone sticking out of the ground.

A strange, beautiful, empty place

In southeastern Alberta, along the Red Deer River, the flat prairie suddenly drops away into a maze of bare hills, gullies and stone towers. People call it the badlands.

Almost nothing grows there, which turns out to be the point. With no grass and no trees, the rock lies open to the rain and the wind.

This is Dinosaur Provincial Park, and it is so important that the whole world agreed to protect it. It became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1979.

Why so many bones are in one place

About 75 million years ago this was not dry at all. Big slow rivers wound across a warm, green, low-lying land near the edge of a shallow sea.

When an animal died near a river, sand and mud covered it quickly. That is the secret of a good fossil: get buried fast, before anything can scatter your bones.

Millions of years of sand and mud turned into layers of rock, one on top of another, with the animals inside. Then the land dried and rose, and rivers and rain began cutting it back open.

So every heavy rain in that park uncovers a little more. Fossils are not really found there so much as revealed.

What has been found

More than forty different kinds of dinosaur have come out of that one park, along with hundreds of skeletons. They are in museums all over the world.

One of them is named after the province. In 1884 a man called Joseph Burr Tyrrell was looking for coal along the Red Deer River and found a dinosaur skull instead. It belonged to a meat-eater now called Albertosaurus.

A large museum in the town of Drumheller is named after him. It opened in 1985 and it is one of the great dinosaur museums anywhere.

Drumheller also has a Tyrannosaurus statue 26 metres tall that you can climb up inside. It is not built to the right size. It is about four and a half times bigger than a real T. rex, which is part of the fun.

If you ever go

You may not dig, and you may not take anything home. A fossil tells scientists far more when they can see exactly which layer it came out of, and a bone in a pocket has lost that forever.

If you spot a bone, the right thing to do is leave it exactly where it is and tell a park worker. Children have found important fossils that way.

Words to know

Fossil
The shape of a plant or animal turned to stone over a very long time.
Badlands
Dry, bare, crumbly hills where the rock is not covered by plants.
Palaeontologist
A scientist who studies fossils and the living things they came from.
Did you know? Nobody planted those dinosaurs there. The bones were always in the rock. The park is simply a place where the rock happens to be open.
