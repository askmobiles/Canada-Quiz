What Newfoundland and Labrador publishes, and what it does not

The province is clear about one number and silent about the rest. A mark of 85 percent is required to pass, and that figure appears on the graduated licensing page, on the licence application page and in the Driver's Handbook itself. It is the standard you practise against here.

What the province does not publish anywhere is how many questions the test has, how long you get, or how long a pass stays valid. Other practice sites state a question count confidently, and one of them even prints a different pass mark. We are not going to invent a number that decides whether somebody books a test, so the question count and the timer on our mock test are a clearly stated practice standard of our own.

The test itself covers the rules of the road, safe driving practices and road sign recognition. You can write it online through the province's own service account, or in person at a Motor Registration Division office, where it is also offered in French and in Ukrainian by appointment. Passing online does not put you on the road: you still have to attend an appointment for the vision test and your documents first.

Study the rules

The three levels, one at a time

You start at 16, and if you are under 19 a parent or guardian has to consent. Pass the written test, the vision test and the document check and you hold a Class 5 Level I licence, which is valid for two years from the day it is issued.

Level I has to be held for twelve months before the road test, or eight months if you finish an approved Driver Education Program. That eight-month reduction is worth knowing, because it is shorter than the usual reduction in other provinces and people assume it is six.

Level II is the first twelve months after you pass the road test. Blood alcohol stays at zero, and between midnight and five in the morning you still need somebody licensed for four years beside you, unless a work exemption applies.

The rules that catch learners out

Demerit points bite far faster for a new driver here than for anybody else. A novice driver is suspended after six points in a single year, while an experienced driver has twelve points over two years. A written warning arrives at three.

At Level I you may carry no passenger at all except the person supervising you, and you may not drive between midnight and five in the morning. The person beside you needs at least four years of driving experience and must be at zero blood alcohol, the same as you.

The Novice Driver sign has a legal specification that surprises people: it goes on the rear of the vehicle, in black lettering on a white background, at least five centimetres high. It is also one of the things you have to bring with you to the road test.

Where to get the official information

Everything on this page comes from what Newfoundland and Labrador publishes for free. The Driver's Handbook is the book the real test is written from, it is free to download from the province's own site, and there is an official French edition as well. Read it alongside this practice, not instead of it.

This is an unofficial free practice site. It is not affiliated with the Motor Registration Division or the Government of Newfoundland and Labrador, and it cannot issue a licence. Always check the official pages for the current rules before you book.

Road Sign Recognition

30 questions

  1. You reach this sign on an empty street early in the morning. What does the law require?

    • A complete stop, even when you can see the way is clearCorrect answer
    • A rolling stop if nothing is coming
    • A stop only when a pedestrian is waiting
    • A tap on the brakes to show you saw it

    The sign means stop every time. Stop at the stop line, or before the crosswalk if there is no line, and move off only when the way is clear.

  2. You are joining a highway on a ramp posted with this sign. What does it require?

    • Give way to traffic already on the highway, stopping if you have toCorrect answer
    • Come to a full stop at the end of the ramp
    • Keep your speed and expect drivers to open a gap
    • Use the shoulder until a gap appears

    Yield means the other traffic has the right of way. Match your speed to the traffic, look for a real gap, and stop if one does not come.

  3. What is this black and white sign telling you?

    • The highest speed allowed here in good conditionsCorrect answer
    • The speed most drivers here choose
    • The lowest speed allowed
    • A speed suggested for trucks only

    A posted limit is a maximum for a clear, dry day. In rain, snow or fog the safe speed is lower, and driving too fast for the conditions is still an offence.

  4. This sign faces you at the entrance to a street. What should you do?

    • Do not enter, and look for another way roundCorrect answer
    • Enter slowly with your headlights on
    • Enter only to make a delivery
    • Enter and keep well to the right

    The street runs the other way. Driving past this sign puts you head-on into traffic that has no reason to expect you.

  5. You are behind a slow truck and you see this sign. What does it tell you?

    • You must stay behind until the restriction endsCorrect answer
    • You may pass if the truck driver waves you by
    • You may pass if you can do it in under ten seconds
    • You may pass by using the shoulder

    The restriction is posted where you cannot see far enough ahead to pull back in safely. Staying behind costs you a minute; being wrong costs far more.

  6. You want to leave your car here for ten minutes while you run into a shop. This sign is posted. May you?

    • No, leaving the car standing is exactly what this sign forbidsCorrect answer
    • Yes, if you stay under fifteen minutes
    • Yes, if you leave the engine running
    • Yes, if your hazard lights are on

    Parking means leaving the vehicle standing, with or without a driver in it. Hazard lights and a running engine change nothing.

  7. Your phone rings and you look for somewhere to pull over. You see this sign. What does it mean for you?

    • You may not stop here at all, so carry on until the restriction endsCorrect answer
    • You may stop as long as you stay in the vehicle
    • You may stop for up to five minutes
    • You may stop if you switch the engine off

    This is the strictest of the three, and it means no stopping for any reason, even briefly. Carry on to a safe spot beyond the sign before you touch the phone.

  8. This sign is mounted on the side of a school bus and swings out when the bus stops. What does it require of you?

    • Stop and wait until the arm folds back and the lights stop flashingCorrect answer
    • Pass slowly on the left
    • Pass on the right if the shoulder is wide
    • Sound your horn and continue

    Wait until the arm folds back and the red lights stop flashing. Children may still be crossing in front of the bus, where you cannot see them.

  9. A concrete island splits the road ahead and this sign stands on it. What does it tell you?

    • Go by on the right-hand side of the islandCorrect answer
    • Go by on whichever side is clear
    • Stop before the island and wait
    • The road ahead is closed

    Keep right of the island. Choosing the wrong side puts you into traffic coming the other way, which is why the sign is planted on the island itself.

  10. You see this yellow sign on a Newfoundland highway. What is it telling you?

    • You are entering a stretch where moose are often on the roadCorrect answer
    • A farm keeps animals beside the road
    • Hunting is allowed beyond this point
    • A wildlife park entrance is ahead

    The province posts these where moose collisions keep happening. Cut your speed, scan both shoulders far ahead, and be ready for an animal that will not move out of your way.

  11. What is coming up when you pass this warning sign?

    • The pavement runs out and the surface becomes gravelCorrect answer
    • A bridge deck
    • A weigh station
    • A tunnel

    Slow down well before the change. Loose gravel gives far less grip than pavement, and where a gravel road in this province carries no speed sign the limit is 60 km/h.

  12. Rain has just started and you pass this sign. What extra care does it call for?

    • Ease off the accelerator and avoid sudden steering or brakingCorrect answer
    • Turn on your hazard lights
    • Move over to the shoulder
    • Change to a lower gear and hold the brake down

    The road loses grip as soon as it is wet, and the first few minutes of rain are the worst as oil lifts off the surface. Smooth inputs and a bigger gap keep you out of a skid.

  13. You meet this sign at the top of a long descent. What is the safest way down?

    • Slow down before the hill and use a lower gear so the engine helps hold you backCorrect answer
    • Coast down in neutral to save fuel
    • Keep steady pressure on the brake all the way down
    • Speed up so you can climb the next hill

    Riding the brakes the whole way heats them until they fade. A lower gear does most of the work and leaves the brakes fresh for the moment you really need them.

  14. What is the road like on the stretch where this sign is posted?

    • There is a series of curves in both directionsCorrect answer
    • There is one sharp curve, then the road is straight
    • The road ends shortly
    • The road is closed in winter

    A winding road means several bends one after another. Set your speed before the first one and stay off the brakes in the middle of each curve.

  15. This sign appears before a bridge. What should you expect?

    • The bridge is narrower than the road, so the lanes tightenCorrect answer
    • The bridge carries one direction only
    • The bridge has a weight limit
    • The bridge deck is gravel

    The lanes squeeze together on the structure. Slow down, place your vehicle in the middle of your lane, and never try to pass on the bridge.

  16. What does this sign mean for the way you drive on the stretch ahead?

    • Children may be near or on the road, so slow down and be ready to stopCorrect answer
    • Children are only present in September
    • You may park on both sides
    • Buses have priority over you

    School zones are where children step out without checking. Where a school zone in this province carries no speed sign of its own the limit is 50 km/h, and posted school zones run lower, from 30 to 50.

  17. A row of these markers is lined up on the outside of a bend. What are they for?

    • They show which way the road turns and how sharplyCorrect answer
    • They mark a pedestrian crossing
    • They mark the edge of a parking area
    • They show where snowploughs turn round

    Chevrons point the way through the curve. If you can see several of them stacked close together, the bend is tighter than it looks from the approach.

  18. You pass this sign on a road cut into a hillside. What are you being warned about?

    • Rock may have fallen onto the road from the slope aboveCorrect answer
    • Loose gravel spread by a truck
    • Blasting is going on nearby
    • The road is unpaved beyond this point

    Rock comes down after rain and after a freeze and thaw. Scan the surface far enough ahead that you can steer around a boulder instead of meeting it.

  19. You reach this white X-shaped sign at a railway line that has no gates and no lights. What is your duty?

    • Slow down, look and listen both ways, and cross only when it is clearly safeCorrect answer
    • Cross without slowing, since there are no gates
    • Stop every time, as you would at a stop sign
    • Sound your horn and cross

    The crossbuck marks the crossing itself and gives you no protection at all. A train needs far more distance to stop than you do, so the looking is yours to do.

  20. Orange signs like this one appear beside the road. What do they mean?

    • Roadwork is going on, so expect workers, equipment and changed conditionsCorrect answer
    • A permanent change to the road layout
    • A route set aside for large trucks
    • A tourist route

    Orange always means temporary. Slow to the posted work-zone speed, watch for people on foot, and expect the lane to shift without much notice.

  21. This orange sign warns that somebody will be directing traffic ahead. How much authority do their signals carry?

    • You must obey them, even where they contradict the usual rulesCorrect answer
    • They are advice you may take or leave
    • They apply to trucks only
    • They apply only when no police officer is present

    A traffic control person guides you through the work zone and their instructions come first. Watch the paddle, and do not move until you are waved on.

  22. What does this orange sign ask you to do?

    • Leave the usual route and follow the marked way around the closureCorrect answer
    • Leave the highway for good at the next exit
    • Stop and wait for a pilot vehicle
    • Turn back the way you came

    A detour routes you around a closed section and back onto your road. Follow the whole run of detour markers instead of guessing your own way through side streets.

  23. What does this route marker identify?

    • The Trans-Canada Highway, the route that crosses the province and the countryCorrect answer
    • A city bypass
    • A scenic coastal loop
    • A road that is closed in winter

    The marker keeps you on the Trans-Canada. Where the paved Trans-Canada in this province carries no speed sign, the limit is 100 km/h.

  24. What does this blue sign point you to?

    • A hospitalCorrect answer
    • A pharmacy
    • A rest area
    • A police station

    Blue signs point to services rather than telling you what to do. Knowing this one matters more on a long highway drive than anywhere else.

  25. You are running low and you see this blue sign. What is it telling you?

    • Fuel is available at the next exit or turningCorrect answer
    • A weigh station is ahead
    • A vehicle inspection station is ahead
    • Parking is available

    Blue service signs are worth watching on long stretches with few communities, because the next chance to fill up can be a very long way further on.

  26. Who may use a parking space marked with this sign?

    • Only a vehicle displaying a valid accessible parking permitCorrect answer
    • Anyone, once the lot is full
    • Anyone stopping for under ten minutes
    • Delivery vehicles

    The space exists because somebody needs the extra room and the short distance. Using it without a permit is an offence, however briefly you are there.

  27. The light turns amber as you approach and you are still some distance back. What is required?

    • Stop, unless you are so close that stopping would be unsafeCorrect answer
    • Speed up to clear the intersection
    • Cover the brake but carry on through
    • Stop only if a vehicle is waiting on the cross street

    Amber means the red is coming, not that you have a few seconds in hand. Stop if you can do it safely, and go through only where braking would put you or the driver behind you at risk.

  28. You reach an intersection where the light is flashing red. What must you do?

    • Stop completely, then go when the way is clearCorrect answer
    • Slow down and go through if nothing is coming
    • Treat it as a green light
    • Wait for the light to become steady

    A flashing red works exactly like a stop sign. Every driver arriving at it must stop, so take your turn rather than assume the right of way is yours.

  29. Two solid yellow lines separate you from oncoming traffic. What are you not allowed to do?

    • Cross them to pass the vehicle aheadCorrect answer
    • Drive at the posted speed limit
    • Change lanes on your own side of the road
    • Follow the road around a curve

    Yellow separates traffic moving in opposite directions, and a solid line on your side means passing is not permitted along that stretch.

  30. A wide white line is painted across your lane at an intersection. What is it for?

    • It shows where the front of your vehicle must stopCorrect answer
    • It marks where pedestrians walk
    • It separates two lanes going the same way
    • It marks a bus stop

    Stop with your bumper behind the line rather than on it. Stopping past it blocks the crosswalk and hides you from traffic on the cross street.

Rules of the Road and Safe Driving

40 questions

  1. How long must a Class 5 Level I licence be held in Newfoundland and Labrador before the road test?

    • Twelve months, or eight months with an approved driver education programCorrect answer
    • Three months, or one month with driver education
    • Six months, or three months with driver education
    • Twenty-four months, with no reduction available

    The province cuts the wait from twelve months to eight for a driver who completes an approved driver education program. That eight-month figure belongs to this province, so do not carry a number over from somewhere else.

  2. Who may ride in the vehicle with a Level I driver?

    • Nobody except the accompanying driverCorrect answer
    • As many people as there are seat belts
    • Family members only
    • One friend, plus the accompanying driver

    At Level I the vehicle holds you and your supervisor and no one else. The limit loosens at Level II, where it becomes the number of working seat belts.

  3. At what hours is a Level I driver forbidden to drive?

    • Between midnight and 5 a.m.Correct answer
    • Between 11 p.m. and 6 a.m.
    • Between 1 a.m. and 4 a.m.
    • There is no restriction on hours

    The curfew runs from midnight to five in the morning, and it does not simply vanish at Level II: at that stage those hours are allowed only with a qualified accompanying driver, or under a work exemption.

  4. What must the driver who accompanies a learner in this province meet?

    • At least four years of driving experience and no alcohol at all in their bloodCorrect answer
    • Two years of driving experience and a blood alcohol level under 0.05
    • Any full licence, with no experience requirement
    • Ten years of driving experience and a clean record

    The province asks for four years behind the wheel and zero blood alcohol from the supervisor. That person is there to do the thinking when something goes wrong, which is impossible after a drink.

  5. How many demerit points may a novice driver collect within one year before the licence is suspended?

    • SixCorrect answer
    • Three
    • Four
    • Twelve

    A novice driver is suspended at six points in a single year, with a written warning at three. The figure of twelve that people quote belongs to full licence holders and is counted over two years.

  6. What mark does Newfoundland and Labrador require on the written knowledge test?

    • 85 per centCorrect answer
    • 70 per cent
    • 75 per cent
    • 80 per cent

    The province states 85 per cent on its licensing pages and in the Driver's Handbook. Some practice sites publish 80 per cent, which is not the province's figure, so study to the higher one.

  7. How must the NOVICE DRIVER sign be displayed on a learner's vehicle?

    • On the rear, in black letters at least 5 cm high on a white backgroundCorrect answer
    • On the windscreen, in any colour
    • On both front doors, at least 10 cm high
    • Anywhere on the vehicle, as long as it can be read

    The sign goes on the back, where the drivers behind you can see it, and its size and colours are set out in law. Bring it to the road test as well, because it is on the list of things you must have with you.

  8. What is the minimum age to enter the graduated licensing program here, and what else does a seventeen-year-old need?

    • Sixteen, and anyone under nineteen needs consent from a parent or guardianCorrect answer
    • Fifteen, with a letter from the school
    • Seventeen, with no further condition
    • Eighteen, and nobody younger may apply

    You may begin at sixteen, and an applicant under nineteen needs a parent or guardian to consent. That consent is about the application itself, not about who sits beside you afterwards.

  9. You pass the knowledge test online through MyGovNL. May you start driving with a supervisor that afternoon?

    • No, you must first attend an appointment for the vision test and to have your documents checkedCorrect answer
    • Yes, the online pass is all that is needed
    • Yes, as long as you print the result
    • No, you must wait thirty days

    The online pass is only the first step. Until you attend the appointment, have your vision tested and your documents verified, you are not allowed to drive.

  10. In which languages may the written test be taken in this province?

    • English online, and English, French or Ukrainian in person by appointmentCorrect answer
    • English only, in every case
    • English and French online
    • English online, and English or Spanish in person

    The online version runs in English. If you would rather sit the test in French or in Ukrainian, book an appointment and take it in person at a Motor Registration Division office.

  11. Where may an off-road vehicle cross a highway in this province?

    • Only where there is at least 150 metres of visibility in both directionsCorrect answer
    • Anywhere, as long as the rider wears a helmet
    • Only at an intersection controlled by traffic lights
    • Anywhere between sunrise and sunset

    The crossing rule is built around what can be seen: 150 metres each way, so a driver has time to react to you and you to them. Cross straight across and do not linger on the road.

  12. An off-road vehicle needs to use the shoulder of a highway to reach a trail. What are the limits?

    • No more than 1 km, at no more than 20 km/hCorrect answer
    • Up to 5 km, at no more than 50 km/h
    • No limit on distance, at no more than 30 km/h
    • The shoulder may never be used for any distance

    The shoulder is a way of reaching the trail, not a route in its own right: one kilometre at a maximum of 20 km/h.

  13. An off-road vehicle is crossing a highway or running along the shoulder. Who has the right of way?

    • Highway traffic, at all timesCorrect answer
    • The off-road vehicle, because it is the more vulnerable
    • Whoever arrives first
    • It depends on the posted speed limit

    The rule gives highway traffic the right of way in every case. An off-road vehicle on or beside a road is a guest there and has to wait.

  14. A fourteen-year-old wants to ride an off-road vehicle. What does the law require?

    • Supervision by a licensed driver who is at least eighteenCorrect answer
    • Nothing, a rider of any age may ride alone away from the highway
    • A learner's permit
    • A helmet and nothing further

    Under sixteen means supervised, and the supervisor must be a licensed driver of eighteen or older. The engine size must also suit the rider's age and weight, as the manufacturer recommends.

  15. In which part of the year do more than 70 per cent of this province's moose collisions happen?

    • Between May and OctoberCorrect answer
    • Between November and February
    • Between January and April
    • They are spread evenly through the year

    More than seven in ten fall in the warmer half of the year, with June to August the worst months. A quiet summer evening on a highway is exactly when a driver relaxes.

  16. At what time of day do most moose collisions occur?

    • Between dusk and dawnCorrect answer
    • Around the middle of the day
    • During the morning rush
    • The time of day makes no difference

    Most happen between dusk and dawn, and the risk of injury then is about twice what it is in daylight. A moose stands tall and dark, and your headlights find almost nothing to catch.

  17. What kind of night carries the greatest risk of a moose collision here?

    • A clear nightCorrect answer
    • A foggy night
    • A rainy night
    • A snowy night

    Most of these collisions happen on clear nights, when a driver feels comfortable and holds the speed up. Good weather is not the same thing as being able to see an animal standing in an unlit lane.

  18. You are driving a dark highway in an area posted with moose warning signs. What does the province advise?

    • Slow down, scan both shoulders far ahead, and use your high beams whenever nobody is comingCorrect answer
    • Follow the vehicle ahead closely so its lights help you
    • Keep your speed up so you spend less time on the road
    • Use your low beams only, so that animals are not startled

    The advice is to buy yourself seeing time: less speed, eyes moving along both shoulders, high beams where they will blind nobody, and a clean windscreen and headlights. Everyone in the vehicle should be belted, because unbelted occupants are far more likely to be seriously hurt.

  19. You plan to drive across the province at 3 a.m. after a snowfall. What should you expect of the roads?

    • Ploughs generally do not run between about 10 p.m. and 4:30 or 5 a.m., so the road may not have been cleared for hoursCorrect answer
    • Ploughs run around the clock, so the road will be clear
    • Ploughs work only at night, so the road will have just been cleared
    • The highway will be closed until morning

    Outside a storm the province does not plough through the small hours except for emergencies. A road that looked fine at ten in the evening can be covered by three in the morning.

  20. On an ordinary winter working day, when do the highway snow-clearing crews start?

    • Between about 4:30 and 6 a.m., five days a week, working through to mid-afternoonCorrect answer
    • At about 8 a.m., like the rest of the working day
    • Only once a storm has finished
    • At noon, so that the salt works in the warmest part of the day

    Crews are out well before the morning traffic and work into the afternoon, and during a storm the ploughing carries on until about 9:30 p.m. or later. Leaving a little later on a bad morning can mean meeting a cleared road instead of an uncleared one.

  21. Does the law in this province require winter tires?

    • No, but the province encourages drivers to fit themCorrect answer
    • Yes, from December to March
    • Yes, on all highways outside the cities
    • No, and the province advises against them

    There is no winter tire requirement here, only encouragement. Four matching winter tires still stop you a great deal sooner on a cold, slippery road than all-season tires do.

  22. In winter the province reminds drivers that snow means slow. What does that ask of you in practice?

    • Cut your speed to suit the surface, whatever the posted limit saysCorrect answer
    • Drive at the posted limit, since it already allows for winter
    • Switch on your hazard lights so that others can see you
    • Stay in the left lane, where the snow is thinner

    A posted limit is set for a clear, dry road. On snow the safe speed is whatever lets you stop inside the distance you can see, and that is often far below the number on the sign.

  23. You are on the paved Trans-Canada Highway in this province and no speed sign is posted. What limit applies?

    • 100 km/hCorrect answer
    • 90 km/h
    • 80 km/h
    • 110 km/h

    Where nothing is posted on the paved Trans-Canada, the limit is 100 km/h. A default is not a target: fog, rain and moose all call for less.

  24. No speed sign is posted on a paved highway that is not the Trans-Canada. What limit applies?

    • 80 km/hCorrect answer
    • 60 km/h
    • 90 km/h
    • 100 km/h

    Other paved highways fall back to 80 km/h. Once the road runs through a community, the default drops again, to 50 km/h.

  25. You turn onto a gravel road with no speed sign anywhere along it. What limit applies?

    • 60 km/hCorrect answer
    • 40 km/h
    • 50 km/h
    • 80 km/h, the same as a paved highway

    Gravel roads in this province fall back to 60 km/h, lower than the paved default for good reason: less grip, longer stopping distances, and stones thrown at whatever is behind you.

  26. You drive through a school zone that carries no speed sign of its own. What limit applies?

    • 50 km/hCorrect answer
    • 30 km/h
    • 20 km/h
    • 40 km/h

    An unmarked school zone falls back to 50 km/h here. Where a school zone is signed, the posted figure sits somewhere between 30 and 50, and the sign always wins.

  27. A school bus ahead of you stops, its red lights flash and the stop arm swings out. What must you do?

    • Stop, and stay stopped until the signals go off and the bus moves awayCorrect answer
    • Pass slowly if no child is visible
    • Pass on the right
    • Sound your horn to warn the children

    You may not pass a school bus that is stopped with its signals showing, and you must obey the signals of the bus and of its driver. The penalties here are heavy, and a child stepping out from in front of the bus is the reason.

  28. When are studded tires permitted in Newfoundland and Labrador?

    • Between 1 November and 30 AprilCorrect answer
    • All year round
    • Between 1 December and 31 March
    • Only on gravel roads

    The studded tire season runs from the first of November to the end of April under the province's traffic law. Outside that window the studs have to come off.

  29. Spring is late and there is still ice on the roads at the end of April. What should a driver running studded tires do?

    • Look for a government advisory, because the province has pushed the removal date back in some yearsCorrect answer
    • Leave them on, since the date is only a guideline
    • Take them off the day the snow stops, whatever the date
    • Have a garage certify the tires for summer use

    The removal date is set in law, but the province has extended it by public advisory in years when winter held on. Look for the announcement rather than deciding for yourself.

  30. What do you have to produce for your road test in this province?

    • Your permit, proof of insurance, a valid registration, the Novice Driver sign, a vehicle in safe working order and your paid receiptCorrect answer
    • A vehicle borrowed from the examination centre
    • A doctor's note about your eyesight
    • A passenger holding a full licence to sit in the back

    The examiner checks the paperwork and the vehicle before the drive begins, and the road test fee is paid through the province's online service in advance of the appointment. A vehicle that fails the check ends the appointment before it starts.

  31. Who must be at zero blood alcohol when a Newfoundland and Labrador learner drives?

    • The learner AND the accompanying driverCorrect answer
    • Only the learner
    • Only the accompanying driver
    • Neither, if it is daytime

    Both the novice driver and the accompanying driver must be at zero. Prince Edward Island allows its supervisor up to 0.05; here it is zero for both.

  32. What must a Newfoundland and Labrador new driver display on the vehicle?

    • A NOVICE DRIVER sign on the rear, letters at least 5 cm highCorrect answer
    • Nothing
    • A yellow decal
    • A flag on the aerial

    The sign goes on the rear of the vehicle, black on white, with letters at least five centimetres high. The size is set in the rules, not left to the driver.

  33. Who may drive between midnight and 5 a.m. at Level II in Newfoundland and Labrador?

    • Only with an accompanying driver, or with a work exemptionCorrect answer
    • Anyone at Level II
    • Only drivers over 21

About this quiz

This page is free practice for the Newfoundland and Labrador Class 5 Level I knowledge test, the written test you sit before you may drive with a supervisor. It covers the three things the province says the test covers: the rules of the road, safe driving practices and road sign recognition.

The province publishes an 85 percent pass mark and nothing else about the format. It does not say how many questions the test has, how long you get, or how long a pass remains valid. We apply the 85 percent exactly and label the rest as our own practice standard, because a wrong number here decides whether somebody books a test before they are ready.

Every question was written for this site from the free official Driver's Handbook and the province's own licensing and safety pages, in English and in French. Nothing was copied from a handbook, from a real test, or from another practice site.

What's inside

  • Sixty questions in total: thirty on road signs and thirty on the rules of the road, each with a short explanation.
  • A quick ten-question practice round at the top of the page, with signs and rules mixed together and no timer.
  • A timed mock test that applies the province's published 85 percent pass mark to a question count of our own.
  • A road signs page where every sign is drawn in code and grouped by type, with practice for each group.
  • A rules of the road page organised by chapter, covering graduated licensing, moose and wildlife, winter conditions and off-road vehicles.
  • The whole question bank printed on the page, so you can read straight through it without playing.

Good to know

  • A mark of 85 percent is required to pass, and that figure is published in three separate places by the province.
  • A novice driver is suspended after six demerit points in one year, while an experienced driver has twelve points over two years.
  • The default speed limit on a gravel road is 60 km/h, and in an unmarked school zone it is 50, whether or not any sign is present.
  • More than seventy percent of moose collisions happen between May and October, and the majority of them between dusk and dawn.
  • The written test is offered in French and in Ukrainian in person at Motor Registration Division offices, by appointment.

Common questions

How many questions are on the real Newfoundland and Labrador knowledge test?

The province does not publish a number. Several practice sites state one anyway, and none of them cites a government source. Our mock test uses a count of our own and says so.

What is the pass mark?

Eighty-five percent. That one is official and appears on the graduated licensing page, the licence application page and in the Driver's Handbook.

Can I write the test online?

Yes, through the province's own service account for a Class 5 permit. You still have to attend an appointment afterwards for the vision test and your documents, and you may not drive until you have.

How long must I hold the Level I licence?

Twelve months before the road test, or eight months if you complete an approved Driver Education Program.

Who can sit beside me while I practise?

A licensed driver with at least four years of driving experience, who must be at zero blood alcohol. At Level I no other passenger may be in the vehicle at all.
