🎆 The History of Canada Day: Why We Celebrate on July 1st

Learn why Canada Day is on July 1st, what happened in 1867, and how Canadians celebrate this national holiday today.

Every year on July 1st, people across Canada celebrate Canada Day with fireworks, parades, barbecues, and lots of red and white. But do you know why we celebrate on this exact day? The story goes back more than 150 years.

What happened on July 1, 1867?

On July 1, 1867, three British colonies joined together to form one new country called the Dominion of Canada. This event is known as Confederation. The three original parts were the Province of Canada (which became Ontario and Quebec), Nova Scotia, and New Brunswick. That is how Canada began with four provinces.

The law that created the country was called the British North America Act. Sir John A. Macdonald became Canada's first prime minister.

From Dominion Day to Canada Day

For many years, the holiday was called Dominion Day. In 1982, the name was officially changed to Canada Day, which is the name we still use today.

How Canada grew over time

Canada did not start with all ten provinces and three territories. It grew over many years as more provinces and territories joined. Today Canada is the second-largest country in the world by land area, stretching from the Atlantic Ocean to the Pacific Ocean and up to the Arctic.

How Canadians celebrate Canada Day

  • Fireworks shows in towns and big cities
  • Parades and outdoor concerts
  • Family barbecues and picnics in the park
  • Wearing red and white and painting maple leaves on faces
  • Citizenship ceremonies where newcomers become Canadians
