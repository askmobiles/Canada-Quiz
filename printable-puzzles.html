🖨 Printable puzzles

Choose a puzzle and how many sheets you need, then print. Every sheet is different, so nobody can read their neighbour's. Seven of the ten come with an answer key, and the key always prints on its own page.

Free to use and to photocopy. No account, no email address, no watermark.

The puzzle opens and your browser's print window appears by itself. If nothing happens, your browser has blocked the print window — press the Print button on the puzzle page instead.

Using these with a class

Ask for as many sheets as you have children rather than printing one and photocopying it. Each sheet is built from scratch, so thirty sheets are thirty different puzzles and nobody can read across the table. The answer keys print after all the puzzles, so you can keep the last pages and hand out the rest.

For a supply teacher's folder, or a wet-weather drawer, print five of each puzzle once and forget about it. Nothing here expires, needs a password or stops working when a subscription lapses, because there is no subscription.

Sudoku is capped at eight sheets at a time. Carving a grid that has exactly one solution is the slowest thing on this site, and thirty at once would leave a classroom laptop sitting still for a minute. Press the button a second time for eight more.

Saving a file instead of printing

In the print window, change the destination from your printer to “Save as PDF”. You get a file you can put in a class drive, attach to an email, or send home. This works in Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari, and on a phone or tablet as well as a computer.

Which games do not print, and why

Most of the games on this site are not on the list above, and that is a decision rather than an oversight. Each one was looked at and left off for a reason worth saying out loud.

  • Word Master and Word Hive — the game is the feedback after each guess. On paper there is nothing to feed back, so the sheet would be dead the moment it was printed.
  • Tic-Tac-Toe, Memory, Connect 4, chess and checkers — board games. A printed empty grid gives a teacher nothing they cannot draw on the board in five seconds.
  • Snake, 2048, Minesweeper, Typing Test and the rest — they need a screen, a clock, or both.

Common questions

May I photocopy these for my class?

Yes, freely, for as many children as you like, and for a tutoring group, a homeschool morning, a library programme or a hospital ward just the same. There is no licence to read and no watermark to work around.

Do students need an account?

No. No child ever signs in to this site, so no information about a child is collected at any point. That is true of the paper sheets and of the games on screen.

Will the same puzzle come back?

Not for the word search, the scramble, the sudoku or the two maths sheets — those are built from nothing every time, so a new sheet is genuinely new. The crossword and Word Groups deal from a fixed bank of puzzles and work through it before repeating.

Are the sheets available in French?

Yes. Switch the site to French and the sheets print in French — the instructions, the labels and the answer key together. The crossword has its own French clues rather than translated ones.

Can I print from a phone?

Yes. The sheet is laid out for a sheet of paper whatever you are holding, so a phone prints the same page a computer does. Saving as PDF works on a phone too, which is often the quickest way to send a sheet to somebody else.
