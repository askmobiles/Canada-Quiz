About this game

Two things that really happened in Canada appear side by side. Tap the one that happened first. The years stay hidden until you choose, then both appear at once, so a wrong answer teaches you as much as a right one.

The game is a crossing. Twelve stepping stones lie between one bank of the river and the other, and every pair you get right moves you one stone forward. A wrong answer costs one of your three lifebuoys but never a stone, so a hard question slows you down instead of sending you backwards. Run out of lifebuoys and you are in the water; reach the far bank and you have crossed.

The events come from the Canada Diary — 339 of them, each written from a named source such as the Canadian Encyclopedia, Parks Canada, Veterans Affairs Canada or the Dictionary of Canadian Biography. Nothing here was invented for the game. After every answer, both events link back to their full entry in the diary.

The first few pairs are centuries apart, so the game explains itself. They close in as you go, and by the tenth pair you are choosing between events a few years apart. There is no clock and no account. Nothing is saved except your best streak and your best crossing, and both stay in your own browser.

How it works

  • Two cards, each naming one real event, with the year hidden until you choose.
  • A streak counter, a best-streak record and a running score of how many you have had right.
  • Both years revealed together the moment you answer, with the earlier one marked.
  • A link under each event to its full entry in the Canada Diary, where the source is named.
  • Pairs that start centuries apart and narrow as you play.

Tips for playing

  • Three hundred and thirty-nine events give more than fifty-six thousand possible pairs, so you will not run out.
  • The oldest event on the cards is from long before Canada existed; the newest is from 2022.
  • Confederation in 1867 is the hinge. Once you know which side of it an event falls on, most pairs become easy.
  • Reading the Canada Diary for a week makes a real difference to your streak, which is rather the point.

Common questions

Do I need an account to play?

No. There is no signup, no email address and nothing to pay. Your best streak is remembered by your own browser and never leaves your device.

Where do the events come from?

From the Canada Diary on this site. Every one was written from a named source, and the link under each card takes you to the full entry so you can check it yourself.

Why are some cards dated only as "thousands of years ago"?

Because that is honest. A few entries describe a time rather than a dated day, and putting a false precise year on them would teach something untrue. Those cards still sort correctly against everything else.

Is this useful for the citizenship test?

Indirectly. The real test asks what happened, not which came first, but knowing roughly when things happened makes the history chapters far easier to hold on to. For the test itself, use the citizenship practice pages.
