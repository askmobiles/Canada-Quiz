♞ Chess

Real chess in your browser — free, no signup, works on any phone.

📖 How to play

Tap one of your pieces and the squares it can move to light up with a dot, then tap the square you want. Play against a friend on the same phone or pick an easy, medium or hard computer opponent. All the real rules are here: castling, en passant, pawn promotion, check, checkmate and stalemate.

About this game

This is real chess in a browser tab, with the complete rule set rather than a simplified version. Castling, en passant, pawn promotion, check, checkmate and stalemate are all handled, so a game ends the way it would on a wooden board.

Play a friend on one phone or face the computer at easy, medium or hard. There is no account, no rating to protect and no clock ticking, which makes it a comfortable place to try an opening you are still learning.

Beginners get the most help from the board itself. Tap a piece and every legal square lights up with a dot, so you learn how the knight and the bishop travel by watching rather than by memorising a diagram.

How it works

  • A drop-down at the top selects two-player mode or a computer opponent at easy, medium or hard.
  • Tapping a piece lights up its legal squares, and tapping one of those lit squares completes the move.
  • Captured pieces are displayed above and below the board so you can judge the material balance at a glance.
  • When a pawn reaches the last rank, four buttons let you promote it to a queen, rook, bishop or knight.
  • Flip board turns the view around, which helps when two people share one phone across a table.
  • Undo move takes back the last move, and a running tally of wins, losses and draws sits above the board.

Tips for playing

  • En passant lasts for one move only: if you do not capture right after the enemy pawn's double step, the chance is gone for good.
  • Castling is not allowed while the king is in check, when it would cross an attacked square, or if either piece has already moved.
  • Promoting a pawn to a knight instead of a queen is occasionally stronger, usually when the new knight arrives with check.
  • Stalemate counts as a draw, so a player who is far ahead should leave the losing king a legal move instead of smothering it by accident.
  • Developing every piece before attacking works better than hunting an early checkmate, which computer opponents defend without much trouble.

Common questions

Can I take a chess move back?

Yes. The undo button steps your last move back, which helps while you are learning and when a finger slips on a small screen. Use it as often as you like.

Which computer level should I start with?

Easy is the right place to begin if you are new or returning after many years. Move to medium once you win comfortably, and save hard for when you want a proper fight.

Does the page remember my results between games?

Small markers above the board count wins, losses and draws while you keep playing. No sign-in is required to keep that tally running as you play more games.

Can two people play face to face on one device?

Yes. Pick two-player mode and use the flip board button so the pieces face whoever is about to move. It works on a phone, a tablet or a laptop.

Is any part of the chess game locked or paid?

No. All three computer levels, the undo button, the flip view and the promotion choices are available immediately, with nothing to unlock and no account to create.
