About this quiz

The AI and Technology Quiz asks how the machines around you actually work: the chip inside a laptop, the address bar of a browser, the code behind a small program. You pick one topic, answer ten questions and see the right answer straight away.

It suits curious kids, teenagers and parents who would rather learn something than scroll. The questions are written in plain language, so no background in computing is needed and nothing is assumed beyond everyday use of a phone.

Each round finishes with a score out of ten and a short message. Because the quiz remembers which questions you have already been shown, coming back later usually hands you a fresh set instead of the same ten again.

What's inside

  • Six topics wait on the start screen, each shown as a card with its own emoji and its own question count.
  • How Computers Work covers processors, memory, storage, binary, pixels and the difference between hardware and software.
  • Artificial Intelligence asks about machine learning, training data, neural networks and famous moments from computing history.
  • The Internet and Coding Basics look at web addresses, email, domains, variables and where programming languages got their names.
  • Robots and Machines plus Staying Safe Online complete the set, mixing engineering with sensible habits for using the internet.
  • A progress bar and a running score sit above every question, and each answer opens a one-line explanation underneath.

Sample questions

  1. What does USB stand for on a computer port?

    Answer: Universal Serial Bus

    USB became a shared standard so one shape of plug could connect many kinds of device.

  2. What is a recommendation system?

    Answer: Software that suggests items you may like

    It compares your choices with other people's choices to guess what you might enjoy.

  3. What is a hyperlink?

    Answer: A clickable connection to another page

    Hyperlinks are what make the Web a web, joining pages to one another with a click.

  4. What does a pulley help you do?

    Answer: Lift a load more easily

    A rope around a grooved wheel changes the direction of your pull, making lifting easier.

  5. Grace Hopper helped create which early programming language?

    Answer: COBOL

    She pushed for languages written in English-like words instead of pure numbers.

Good to know

  • A byte is made of eight bits, which is just enough room to store a single ordinary letter of the alphabet.
  • The phrase artificial intelligence was coined at a 1956 workshop held at Dartmouth, and the name stuck to the whole research field.
  • Computers count in binary because a circuit is either switched on or switched off, so two digits are all the hardware needs.
  • The Python language was named after the comedy show Monty Python, even though a snake ended up becoming its symbol.
  • The letters URL stand for Uniform Resource Locator, the full address that tells a browser exactly where a page lives.
  • Memory called RAM forgets everything the moment power is lost, which is why unsaved work vanishes after an unexpected shutdown.

Common questions

How many questions are there in one round of this quiz?

Ten. Each topic holds more than ten in total, so the ones you see are drawn from a bigger pool and the mix changes when you play the same topic again.

Is this technology quiz suitable for children?

Yes. The wording stays simple and the subjects are everyday ones such as computers, robots and online safety. Families often play together, but a child can also manage a round alone.

Do I find out why an answer was wrong?

You do. The moment you tap a choice, the correct option is marked and a short explanation appears below it, so even a bad guess leaves you with something you did not know before.

Does the quiz work properly on a phone?

Yes. The answer buttons are large and stack down the screen, so a phone or tablet works as well as a laptop. Nothing needs installing and there is no app to download first.

Do I need to sign in for my score to count?

There is no account and no login anywhere. Your score for the round appears on the result screen, and the quiz quietly notes which questions you have seen on that device so later rounds feel new.
