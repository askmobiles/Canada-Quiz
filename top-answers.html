📋 Top Answers

Two teams guess the most popular answers. Game-show fun!

📖 How to play

Pick a category, then read the question out loud. A team shouts an answer. The host taps that card to flip it over and see whether it was on the board and what it is worth. If the team really said it, tap Red or Blue on that card and the points go to them; tap Nobody to leave the points unclaimed. Flip all the rest at the end of the round, press Next question, and the team with the most points after your last board wins.

About this game

Top Answers is a game-show style party game for two teams. A question appears, five hidden answer cards sit on the board, and each one carries points according to how popular the answer is. The board fills the middle of the screen so a whole room can follow along.

One person acts as the host, and the host does two separate things. Tapping a card flips it over so everyone sees the answer and what it is worth. Tapping Red or Blue on that card then hands those points to the team that actually said it. Keeping the two apart means the host can reveal a card safely, argue about it, and only award the points once the room agrees.

It is made for a room full of people rather than one player alone — family evenings, classrooms, birthday parties, or any time you need something loud and quick to explain. One shared screen is enough.

How it works

  • Twelve categories sit above the board — food, Canada, animals, the house, school, sports, travel, jobs, holidays, the body, nature and a harder set — plus a mixed pile that draws on all of them.
  • Seventy-two boards in total, each with five answers, so an evening never runs out and the same board rarely comes back twice.
  • Tapping a card flips it face up and shows the answer with its points. Nothing is scored yet at that moment.
  • Red, Blue and Nobody appear on every flipped card. Red or Blue gives that card’s points to a team, and tapping the same team again takes them straight back, so a mistake costs nothing.
  • Flip the rest turns over every card still face down, which is how you close a round once both teams have run dry.
  • Scores carry from board to board, and Reset scores wipes them when a new match starts.

Tips for playing

  • Flip first, argue second. Because flipping does not score anything, the host can safely reveal a card while the room decides whether the team really said it.
  • The top answer is usually the most obvious one. Teams that overthink and reach for something clever often score the fewest points.
  • Points are far from equal. On most boards the first answer is worth three or four times the last one, so guessing order matters.
  • Set your own time limit, maybe ten seconds per guess. The game has no timer, and a fast pace keeps the room laughing.
  • Choose a category that suits the room. The harder set asks about planets, oceans and inventions, while the food and animal boards work with very young children.
  • Playing host is a real job. Reading the question with a little drama makes an ordinary answer feel like a jackpot.

Common questions

How many people do I need for a game?

Three is enough: two guessers and someone to tap the screen. It gets much better with bigger teams, because the arguments about which answer to say are half the fun.

What is the difference between flipping a card and awarding it?

Flipping only reveals what is written on the card and how much it is worth. Awarding is the separate tap on Red or Blue that moves those points onto a team’s score. A card can stay flipped and unawarded all round.

I gave the points to the wrong team. Can I undo it?

Yes. Tap the other team and the points move across, or tap the same team again, or Nobody, and they come off the board entirely. The score corrects itself instantly.

What happens if a team says something that is not on the board?

Nothing is lost. Press the switch button and let the other side try. Only answers printed on the cards carry points.

Are the questions suitable for children?

Most boards ask for everyday things a young child can name, like a farm animal or a red fruit. The harder category is the one to skip with a very young room.
