🦫 Canada's Animals and Wildlife: A Guide for Families

Meet the amazing animals of Canada — beavers, moose, polar bears, loons, and more. A fun wildlife guide for kids and families.

Canada's forests, mountains, rivers, and Arctic are home to some amazing animals. Here are some of the most famous ones you can find across the country.

The beaver

The beaver is one of Canada's national symbols. These busy animals build dams and lodges out of sticks and mud. You can even find a beaver on the Canadian nickel (the five-cent coin).

The moose

The moose is the largest member of the deer family and can be found in forests across Canada. Males grow huge, flat antlers. Moose are strong swimmers and love wet, wooded areas.

The polar bear

Polar bears live in Canada's cold Arctic north. Canada is home to about half of all the world's polar bears. Under their thick white fur, polar bears actually have black skin to help them stay warm.

The common loon

The loon is a water bird known for its beautiful, haunting call. It appears on the Canadian one-dollar coin — which is why the coin is nicknamed the “loonie.”

More Canadian animals

  • Caribou — pictured on the Canadian quarter (25-cent coin)
  • Canada goose — famous for flying in a V shape
  • Grizzly bears and black bears
  • Beluga whales and orcas off the coasts
  • Snowy owls and bald eagles

In short

This article is a family-friendly tour of the animals that live across Canada's forests, mountains, rivers and Arctic coast. It introduces the beaver, the moose, the polar bear and the common loon, then gathers more creatures worth knowing into a short closing list.

Canadian animals turn up everywhere in daily life here, especially on coins, so knowing them helps newcomers understand small things they see every day. Each section is short and written in simple words, with no scientific vocabulary to learn before you start reading.

Children often find wildlife the easiest way into Canadian geography. Once a child can picture where polar bears live, the map of the North starts to make sense. A button at the end opens our general knowledge quiz so the family can test what it just read.

Key points

  • A section on the beaver explains how these busy animals build dams and lodges out of sticks and mud.
  • The moose section covers its size, its huge flat antlers and its love of wet, wooded ground.
  • Polar bears get their own section, including the surprising colour of the skin hidden under their thick white fur.
  • The common loon is introduced through its haunting call and its place on the Canadian one-dollar coin.
  • A closing list adds caribou, Canada geese, grizzly and black bears, beluga whales, orcas, snowy owls and bald eagles.

Worth remembering too

  • Canada is home to about half of the world's polar bears, which is why the animal appears so often in Canadian photographs and stories.
  • The beaver sits on the Canadian five-cent coin, the nickel, and it is also one of the country's official national symbols.
  • The loon on the one-dollar coin gave that coin its nickname, the loonie, a word you will hear in ordinary Canadian conversation.
  • The moose is the largest member of the deer family, and despite its bulk it swims strongly and stays close to water.
  • The caribou pictured on the twenty-five-cent quarter is one of several Canadian animals used on the money people handle every day.
  • Canada geese are famous for flying in a V shape, a formation families can spot overhead in spring and again in autumn.
