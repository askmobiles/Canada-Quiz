💡 12 Great Canadian Inventions That Changed the World

From insulin to the telephone, basketball to the electric wheelchair — the Canadian inventions and discoveries that changed everyday life.

Canada is a big country with a small population, but it has produced an impressive number of inventions and discoveries. Some saved millions of lives. Others are just part of daily life now, and most people have no idea where they came from.

Inventions that saved lives

  • Insulin (1921–22) — Frederick Banting and Charles Best, working at the University of Toronto with J.J.R. Macleod and James Collip, discovered how to use insulin to treat diabetes. Before this, a diagnosis of type 1 diabetes was a death sentence. Banting and Macleod won the Nobel Prize, and Banting shared his award money with Best.
  • The pacemaker — the first external artificial cardiac pacemaker was developed in the 1950s by John Hopps at the National Research Council of Canada.
  • The electric wheelchair — invented by George Klein and his team to help injured veterans after the Second World War.
  • The G-suit — Wilbur Franks designed the anti-gravity suit that stops pilots from blacking out during sharp turns. It later influenced spacesuit design.

Inventions that connected people

  • The telephone — Alexander Graham Bell did much of his key work at his family home in Brantford, Ontario, and made the first long-distance call between Brantford and Paris, Ontario in 1876. He later lived in Nova Scotia.
  • The walkie-talkie — Donald Hings developed a portable two-way radio in the late 1930s that became vital during the Second World War.
  • IMAX — the giant-screen film format was created by a Canadian team and first shown at Expo 70.
  • The BlackBerry — built in Waterloo, Ontario, it put email in people's pockets and shaped the smartphones we all use now.

Inventions for daily life

  • Basketball — invented in 1891 by James Naismith, a Canadian from Almonte, Ontario, while teaching in the United States. The first hoops were peach baskets.
  • The snowmobile — Joseph-Armand Bombardier of Quebec built practical snow machines that opened up winter travel in the North.
  • The garbage bag — the green polyethylene garbage bag was invented in Ontario in the 1950s by Harry Wasylyk and Larry Hansen.
  • Instant mashed potatoes and the Robertson screw — Edward Asselbergs developed instant mashed potato flakes, and P.L. Robertson invented the square-drive screw that almost every Canadian toolbox still has.

And one for space

The Canadarm, built in Canada, worked on NASA's space shuttles for 30 years, and its successor Canadarm2 helped build the International Space Station and still works there today. It is such a point of pride that a version of it appeared on the Canadian five-dollar bill.

Why this matters

Canada spends a lot on research, universities and hospitals, and it attracts talented people from all over the world. Many of these inventors were newcomers or the children of newcomers. Their work is a good reminder that a country of 41 million people can still change the world.
