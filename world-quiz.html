About this quiz

The World Quiz takes you around the planet ten questions at a time. Choose a topic from the six cards on the start screen, tap the answer you think is right, and find out straight away whether you got it. Every question comes with a short explanation.

It suits mixed groups. A child learning the continents can play beside a parent who wants harder landmark questions. Nothing is timed, so a family can talk each answer over. Newcomers often find it a gentle way to read English while meeting places from back home.

The quiz remembers which questions you have already seen on this device, so a second round brings different ones. At the end you get a score out of ten and a short message about how you did. Then you can go back and pick another subject.

What's inside

  • Flags of the World shows one large flag on the screen and asks which country it belongs to.
  • Capital Cities asks for the capital of countries such as Peru, Poland, Thailand, Cuba and Austria.
  • Famous Landmarks covers Petra, Angkor Wat, Uluru, Stonehenge, Machu Picchu and the buried Terracotta Army.
  • World Geography asks about oceans, continents, the equator, time zones, and the Panama and Suez canals.
  • Countries and Cultures looks at origami, flamenco, the sari, Hangul writing, Finnish saunas and the didgeridoo.
  • Rivers, Mountains and Oceans covers the Andes, the Danube, Lake Baikal, the Dead Sea and Angel Falls.

Good to know

  • Australia's capital is Canberra, not Sydney, and New Zealand's is Wellington, not Auckland. Both questions catch out plenty of confident players in the capitals round.
  • Angel Falls in Venezuela drops 979 metres from the edge of a flat-topped mountain, the longest waterfall drop known anywhere on Earth.
  • Lake Baikal in Siberia is more than 1,600 metres deep, which makes it the deepest lake anywhere in the world.
  • Africa is made up of 54 countries, more than any other continent, which is why African flags turn up so often in quiz rounds.
  • The Dead Sea holds so much salt that swimmers float on the surface without any effort, bobbing along like corks.
  • Hangul, the Korean alphabet, was created in the 1400s so that ordinary people could learn to read and write quickly.

Common questions

Do I need to make an account to play the World Quiz?

No. There is no sign-up, no email address and no password. Open the page, tap one of the topic cards, and the first question appears right away. Your score stays on your own device.

How many questions are there in one round?

Each round gives you ten questions taken from the topic you picked. Answering all ten takes most people three or four minutes, so a game fits neatly into a coffee break.

Will I see the same questions if I play a topic twice?

The quiz keeps track of what it has already shown you in that browser and offers fresh questions first. Once you have worked through everything in a topic, the list simply starts again.

Is this world geography quiz suitable for children?

Yes. The wording is simple and the subjects are school friendly, so it works well for curious kids, for starting a geography lesson, or for a parent and child sharing one phone.

Does the World Quiz work properly on a phone?

Yes. The answer buttons are big enough to tap with a thumb, and the page fits small screens, tablets and laptops without any pinching or zooming.
