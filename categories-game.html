🔤 Categories

Get a letter — race to name something in each category!

📖 How to play

Press Start to get a random letter and a list of categories. Everyone races to think of something in each category that begins with that letter — for example, if the letter is B, a food could be "banana." Say your answers out loud or write them down before the timer runs out. Same answer as someone else? It doesn't count! Pick Advanced for trickier categories.

    About this game

    Categories is a fast party game for a group in one room. The screen gives you a random letter and a list of categories, and everyone races to name something in each one that begins with that letter before the timer runs out.

    It suits a kitchen table, a long drive with a phone propped up, or the last ten minutes of a class. There is nothing to set up, no pieces to lose, and no limit on how many people can join in at once.

    Because the answers come from the players and not from the screen, the game grows with the group. Young children shout the first word they think of, while adults argue happily over whether an unusual answer should count.

    How it works

    • Two levels are offered, Easy and Advanced, and you choose one before pressing the start button.
    • Easy categories include an animal, a food, a colour, a country, a sport, a fruit and something in the kitchen.
    • Advanced categories ask for things like a capital city, a job, a musical instrument, a type of transport or an action word.
    • Six categories are drawn at random for every round, so the same letter never gives you the same list twice.
    • The letter comes from a shortened alphabet that skips Q, U, V, X, Y and Z to keep rounds fair.
    • A sixty-second countdown runs on screen and turns red with a time is up message when it reaches zero.

    Tips for playing

    • If two players give the same answer it does not count, so reaching for the second or third word that comes to mind usually scores better.
    • With a big group, write the answers down instead of shouting them, then compare lists at the end for far fewer arguments.
    • Start with the hardest category on the list, because the easy ones will still be sitting there in the final ten seconds.
    • Switching to Advanced makes a good handicap when adults play against children, since the adult takes on the harder list of categories.
    • Names of people, places and brands cause most disputes, so agree before the first round on whether your group allows them.

    Common questions

    How many people do I need to play Categories?

    Two players are enough, but four or more makes it livelier. Everybody plays at the same time on the same letter, so adding more people never makes a round take any longer.

    Does the game check whether my answers are correct?

    No. The screen supplies only the letter, the six categories and the countdown. The players decide together which answers count, and that discussion is half the fun.

    How long does one round of Categories last?

    One round is sixty seconds plus the time you spend comparing answers afterwards. Most groups fit several rounds into ten minutes and stop whenever someone has to leave.

    Can young children take part?

    Yes, especially on the Easy level, where a colour, a toy or a fruit is within reach of most school-age children. Pair a young child with an adult if reading is still slow.

    Do I have to install anything to play?

    No. The game runs straight in your browser on a phone, a tablet or a computer, with no download to wait for, no account to create and no payment at any point.
