The Fishing Boat That Beat Everybody

Look at a ten cent coin. That ship was real, and it was a fishing boat, not a racing yacht.

A schooner is a sailing ship with tall masts. A hundred years ago, fishing crews in Nova Scotia sailed schooners far out into the Atlantic to catch cod, and it was cold, dangerous work.

The crews were proud of their ships, and they raced them. In 1921 a ship was built in the town of Lunenburg, Nova Scotia, to fish hard and race harder. She was called the Bluenose.

Her captain was Angus Walters. That same autumn the Bluenose won the International Fishermen's Trophy, the big race between Canadian and American fishing schooners.

Then she won it again. And again. For seventeen years no ship beat her in a race series. People called her the Queen of the North Atlantic.

She grew so famous that in 1937 her picture went onto the ten cent coin, where it still is today.

The real Bluenose ended her life carrying cargo in the Caribbean and was wrecked on a reef near Haiti in 1946. Nova Scotia built a copy of her, the Bluenose II, in 1963 at the very same shipyard. You can still go and see it.

Did you know? For sixty-five years the coin did not officially say which ship it was. The Mint only confirmed it was the Bluenose in 2002.
