🤝 What Is Confederation? Canada's Beginning Explained

What is Confederation, and why does it matter? A simple explanation of how Canada became a country in 1867.

If you are studying Canadian history, you will hear the word Confederation a lot. It is one of the most important ideas in the country's story. Here is what it means, in simple words.

Confederation means joining together

Confederation is when separate British colonies joined together to become one new country. That country was Canada, and it was born on July 1, 1867. This is why we celebrate Canada Day on that date.

Which parts joined first?

Canada began with four provinces: Ontario, Quebec, Nova Scotia, and New Brunswick. Before Confederation, these were separate British colonies. Leaders met and agreed to unite so they could be stronger together, defend themselves, and build things like railways more easily.

The Fathers of Confederation

The leaders who worked to bring the colonies together are called the Fathers of Confederation. The most famous of them, Sir John A. Macdonald, became Canada's first prime minister.

How Canada grew after 1867

After Confederation, more provinces and territories joined over time until Canada reached its present size of 10 provinces and 3 territories, stretching from ocean to ocean to ocean.
