🏛️ Be the Mayor

You have one term to run a small Canadian town. Twelve decisions. The snow, the water pipes, the arena roof and the budget all pull against each other.

📖 How to play

Residents bring you a problem. Pick one of three answers. Every answer moves four things: the budget, the population, how happy people are, and the condition of the roads, pipes and buildings. Nothing is free — spending money fixes things but empties the budget, and saving money makes people unhappy. Get to the end of your term without any of the four hitting zero. At the end you will see which parts of the job you leaned on.

🏛️

    About this game

    Be the Mayor puts you in charge of a Canadian town of about five thousand people for one term. Twelve people come to you with a problem, you pick one of three answers, and four things move: the budget, the population, how happy residents are, and the condition of the roads, pipes and buildings.

    Nothing in it is invented. Every decision is something Canadian municipalities are actually responsible for — clearing snow, treating water, collecting garbage, running the fire service, the library and the arena, issuing building permits and setting the property tax rate. The arena roof and the hundred-year-old water main are the two problems small Canadian towns talk about most.

    At the end the game tells you which part of the work you leaned on, and names jobs in Canada that use it. It does not tell you what to be. It is a game, not a career test — that line is on the result screen too, because it matters more than the score.

    Tips for playing

    • Snow clearing is the municipal service Canadians complain about most, and the one they notice first. Most towns clear priority routes before side streets: hospital, schools, bus routes.
    • Canada's freeze-and-thaw cycle is why potholes appear every spring. Water gets into a crack, freezes, expands and breaks the pavement apart — which is why fixing the drainage is often the real repair.
    • Whether the town clears your sidewalk or you must do it yourself is decided municipality by municipality. There is no national rule, and neighbouring towns often differ.

    Common questions

    Does this tell me what job I should do?

    No, and it says so on the result screen. Real career assessments are built on decades of research; a browser game is not one. What it shows is which kind of decision you enjoyed making today, and which jobs involve a lot of that.

    Can I lose?

    Yes. If the budget, the population, happiness or the condition of the town reaches zero, your term ends early and the game tells you which one ran out. Spending on everything empties the budget; saving on everything empties everything else.

    Are the decisions the same every time?

    No. Twelve are drawn from a larger set and shuffled, so two terms are rarely the same, and a choice that worked once may not work when it arrives in a different order.

    Is it good for a classroom?

    It was built with that in mind. There is no signup, no account and nothing is collected, it works on a phone or a projector, and every card teaches a real municipal responsibility — which is also material on the Canadian citizenship test.
