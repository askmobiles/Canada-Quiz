Terry Fox and the Longest Run in Canada

He ran almost a marathon every day, for 143 days in a row.

Terry Fox was a young man from British Columbia who loved sport. When he was eighteen he got very sick, and doctors had to take away part of one leg. He was given an artificial leg to walk on.

In the hospital he met other people who were sick, including children. He decided to do something enormous to help them. He decided to run all the way across Canada and ask people for money for cancer research.

He called it the Marathon of Hope. On the 12th of April 1980 he stood on the beach at St. John's, in Newfoundland, dipped his artificial leg in the Atlantic Ocean, and started running west. He was twenty-one.

He ran about 42 kilometres a day. That is a full marathon, every single day, in rain and wind and heat, on a leg made of metal that hurt him.

At first hardly anybody noticed. Then people began coming out to the road to clap. Whole towns came. He ran for 143 days and covered 5,373 kilometres, more than halfway across the country.

On the 1st of September 1980, near Thunder Bay in Ontario, his illness came back and he had to stop. He died the next summer, at twenty-two. He never finished the run, and that has never mattered.

Every September, schools and towns all over Canada hold a Terry Fox Run. Together they have raised more than 800 million dollars, and the number goes up every year.

Did you know? He did not finish, and he is still one of the most admired people in Canada. Trying was the whole point.
