What the real Saskatchewan knowledge test looks like

SGI splits the knowledge test into two separate parts. The Basic test covers driving situations and the rules of the road. The Sign test covers sign identification. They are marked separately, and that matters: if you fail one part you rewrite only that part, not both. It is the reason this site keeps signs and rules as two study pages rather than one long list.

Four other things SGI states plainly. You may write only once per day, so a failed attempt costs you a day. A passed written result is valid for one year. Phones and electronic devices are not allowed in the test area. And if you cannot work out what a question is asking, you are allowed to ask the examiner what it means — worth knowing, because people sit there guessing instead.

SGI does not publish the number of questions, the pass mark, or a time limit. Other practice sites state those numbers confidently and do not agree with each other. We are not going to invent a number that decides whether somebody books a test. Our mock test runs 30 questions with a 30-minute timer as a clearly stated practice standard of our own — not a copy of the real one, because the real one is not published.

Saskatchewan's graduated licence, stage by stage

You can start at 16, or at 15 if you are taking driver education through a high school. Pass both knowledge tests and you hold a Class 7 learner licence for nine months. Then come Novice 1 for six months and Novice 2 for twelve months — about twenty-seven months from your first day as a learner to a full licence, and the clock stops if your licence is suspended, refused or not renewed.

While you hold a Class 7, a supervising driver must sit in the front passenger seat at all hours. They need a Class 5 or better licence from Canada or the United States, held for at least 365 days within the past three years. You may carry no more people than there are working seatbelts, and between midnight and 5 a.m. your passengers must be immediate family.

Two rules catch new drivers out. Your blood alcohol must be zero — not under the legal limit, zero — right through the learner and both novice stages. And you may not use a cellphone at all, hand-held or hands-free. Hands-free is legal for fully licensed drivers; it is not legal for you. You also cannot supervise another learner, hold a commercial licence or a school bus endorsement, or drive a right-hand drive vehicle.

The Saskatchewan numbers worth memorising

Stop no closer than 5 metres — about fifteen feet — from a school bus showing flashing red lights, from the front or the back. On an undivided highway traffic in both directions stops; on a divided highway only the traffic behind the bus stops. Wait until the lights go off and the stop arm folds in.

Slow to 60 km/h to pass a stopped emergency vehicle or tow truck with its lights flashing. On an undivided highway that applies from both directions; on a divided highway it does not apply to traffic going the other way. If an emergency vehicle is coming toward you with lights and siren, pull as close as possible to the right edge, stop, and stay out of the next intersection until it has passed.

Leave at least two seconds behind the car ahead on a dry road, and far more on snow or ice, where stopping distance can grow many times over. Give a cyclist plenty of room and treat them as traffic. Watch the posted sign for school zone speeds and hours rather than assuming a number, because it is posted on the sign for a reason.

Sign Test

  1. Where must you stop at this sign?

    • At the stop line, or before the crosswalk if there is no lineCorrect answer
    • Level with the sign post
    • Anywhere inside the intersection
    • Only if another vehicle is coming

    Stop at the line first. If a snowbank or a parked truck blocks your view from there, creep forward and stop a second time once you can actually see.

  2. What must you do here?

    • Travel only in the direction of the arrowCorrect answer
    • Choose either direction
    • Stop and wait
    • Use this lane only to turn

    One way means one way. Check the arrow before you turn in, especially downtown where two one-way streets often run side by side.

  3. What does this sign forbid?

    • Turning around to go back the way you cameCorrect answer
    • Turning left
    • Reversing
    • Three-point turns only

    U-turns are banned where oncoming drivers would not see you swing across in time.

  4. What does this sign tell you?

    • A school zone is ahead and a lower posted limit may applyCorrect answer
    • School is closed
    • Children may not cross here
    • Parking for school staff

    Watch for the posted speed and the hours on the sign itself. Children step out from between parked cars without looking.

  5. Why is this animal a particular danger at highway speed?

    • It is tall, so the body comes through the windshieldCorrect answer
    • It always runs away from cars
    • It is only active in summer
    • It is smaller than a deer

    A moose stands so high that the bumper takes out its legs and the body lands on the roof and windshield. Brake hard and aim behind it, never in front.

  6. What does this sign warn about?

    • Traffic is joining your road aheadCorrect answer
    • Your lane ends immediately
    • A passing lane starts
    • A truck stop is ahead

    Merging works when both sides adjust. Make room where you can and match the speed of the traffic you are joining.

  1. How long must you hold a Class 7 learner licence in Saskatchewan before moving on?

    • Nine monthsCorrect answer
    • Three months
    • One year
    • Six weeks

    The learner stage lasts nine months. Time does not count while your licence is suspended, refused or not renewed.

  2. Which is true about cellphones for a new driver in Saskatchewan?

    • You cannot use any cellphone, hand-held or hands-freeCorrect answer
    • Hands-free is allowed
    • You may use one while stopped at a red light
    • You may use one on rural roads

    New drivers get no phone at all. Hands-free is legal for fully licensed drivers, not for you.

  3. How long does a passed written exam result stay valid?

    • One yearCorrect answer
    • Thirty days
    • Three months
    • Forever

    A successful result is good for one year, so do not let too long pass before the next step.

  4. Snow is blowing across the highway and you cannot see far. What is the safest thing to do?

    • Slow down, increase your following distance, and pull well off the road if it gets worseCorrect answer
    • Turn on your high beams and keep your speed
    • Stop in the driving lane
    • Follow the tail lights ahead closely

    Following someone's tail lights in blowing snow is how chain collisions start. If you must stop, get right off the road and put your hazards on.

  5. Who is responsible for making sure everyone in the vehicle is buckled up?

    • The driver is responsible for passengers under 16Correct answer
    • Each passenger only
    • Nobody, it is a personal choice
    • The front seat passenger

    You are responsible for younger passengers, and everyone needs their own working belt.

  6. How many non-family passengers may a Saskatchewan Novice 1 driver carry?

    • OneCorrect answer
    • None
    • Three
    • As many as there are seat belts

    A Novice 1 driver may carry one passenger who is not immediate family. Other passengers must be immediate family, up to the number of seat belts.

Common questions

How many questions are on the real Saskatchewan knowledge test?

SGI does not publish that number, and we are not going to invent one. What SGI does state is that the test comes in two parts, Basic and Sign, that they are marked separately, and that you rewrite only the part you fail.

What is the pass mark?

SGI does not publish a pass mark either. Our mock test uses a practice standard of our own so you have something to aim at, and we label it as ours rather than pretending it is the official one.

Can I take the test more than once in a day?

No. SGI allows one attempt per day. A passed written result stays valid for one year.

Where must my supervising driver sit?

In the front passenger seat, at all hours. They need a Class 5 or better licence from Canada or the United States, held for at least 365 days within the past three years.

Can I use a hands-free phone as a new driver?

No. New drivers may not use a cellphone at all, hand-held or hands-free. Hands-free is legal for fully licensed drivers, not at the learner or novice stages.
