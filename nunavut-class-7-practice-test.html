What Nunavut publishes, and what it does not

Nunavut publishes less about its driving test than any other jurisdiction in Canada, and the honest place to start is with that. What the territory does say is clear enough. The examination has two parts taken at one sitting, sign identification and the rules of the road and safe driving practices, and if you fail one part you rewrite only that part. Where reading the paper is a barrier, the driver examiner may give the test aloud instead.

What the territory does not publish is the number of questions, the pass mark, the time limit or the fee. A commercial practice site states twenty questions, sixteen correct and eighty percent, along with a dollar figure, and cites nothing at all for any of it; another prints the minimum age as sixteen when the territory's own manual says fifteen. We are not going to repeat a number that decides whether somebody books a test. The twenty questions in each part, the fifteen right needed in each part and the forty minutes on our mock test are a clearly stated practice standard of our own.

The fee is the same story. Fees are set by regulation and no amount appears on any Government of Nunavut page, so we do not print one. Ask your local Motor Vehicle service provider or your Government Liaison Officer, or call the territorial enquiry line at 1-888-975-5999, and ask before you travel rather than after.

Study the rules

Two steps, and no graduated programme at all

Nunavut has no graduated licensing programme. There are no stages, no probationary period, no learner curfew, no passenger limit and no requirement to display an L sign, because none of those things appears in the territory's own manual. That is unusual in Canada and it deserves to be said plainly. We are not borrowing a neighbouring territory's stages to fill the gap, and you should be careful with any site that quietly does.

What the manual describes is a two-step licence. The Class 7 licence is the learner licence, the minimum age is fifteen, and it permits the operation of any vehicle in Class 5 or 6 while you are learning to operate it. To obtain it you pass the written knowledge test, meet the basic vision standard and show suitable identification, which means two acceptable pieces of proof of identity along with the application form and the remittance slip.

The person beside you must hold a valid driver's licence and be authorised under the Act, and the examiner may ask to see that licence. Nothing further about the accompanying driver is published anywhere: not a minimum age, not a number of years licensed, not a required licence class, not a seating position, not an alcohol limit of their own. Where the territory is silent, we say that it is silent rather than borrow a rule from somewhere else.

The licence itself, and what happens after you have it

Demerit points are published at two thresholds and no more. Twelve or more points brings a warning letter from the Registrar and an interview. Fifteen or more brings a suspension of the licence for one month. The value of each individual offence lives in the demerit point regulations, which we could not obtain through any channel that permits automated access, so we do not list per-offence points anywhere on this site.

A Nunavut licence is issued for a five-year period. The renewal window opens ninety days before it expires, online renewal is available for a valid licence that is not cancelled or suspended, and a licence that has been expired for more than twelve months cannot be renewed at all, which means being examined again. Online renewal is the only part of this that happens on a screen: there is no online knowledge test.

If you move to Nunavut you have thirty days to apply for a territorial licence, and any change of name or address has to be reported to the nearest Motor Vehicle service provider within fifteen days. A driver's medical examination is required at seventy-five, at eighty, and every two years after eighty. The vision test for a Class 7 licence covers colour perception and field of vision, and the territory publishes no numeric standard for either, only the words basic and higher.

Where to get the official information

Everything on this page comes from what Nunavut publishes for free. The Nunavut Driver's Manual is the book the knowledge test is written from, and it is free to download from the territory's own site. It is published in English only, as are the territory's three other driver manuals, even though the Government of Nunavut website itself is available in Inuktitut, Inuinnaqtun, English and French. That is why the English title appears in the French place on this site: there is no French edition to name. Read the manual alongside this practice, not instead of it.

This is an unofficial free practice site. It is not affiliated with the Motor Vehicles Division, the Department of Economic Development and Transportation or the Government of Nunavut, and it cannot issue a licence. Always check the official pages for the current rules before you book.

Sign Identification

30 questions

  1. You come to this sign where two gravel streets meet in the community. What does it require?

    • A complete stop every time, then go when the way is clearCorrect answer
    • Slow down, look, then keep rolling
    • A stop only when another vehicle is in sight
    • A tap on the horn before crossing

    Stop means the wheels stop turning, whether or not anyone else is around. Stop first, look both ways, and move off only when nothing is coming.

  2. This sign faces you as you join a road that already carries traffic. What is expected?

    • Give way to the vehicles and people already on the roadCorrect answer
    • Come to a full stop and count to three
    • Take the gap and let the others adjust
    • Signal and hold your speed

    Yield means give way. Slow down enough that you could stop, and go only when nobody else has to brake or steer around you.

  3. This sign is posted on a street inside a Nunavut community. What is it telling you?

    • 50 km/h is the most you may drive hereCorrect answer
    • 50 km/h is a suggestion in good weather
    • Traffic ahead is moving at 50 km/h
    • Slow vehicles must keep to 50 km/h

    Inside communities the basic limit in Nunavut is 50 km/h, or whatever is posted. It is a ceiling for ideal conditions, not a target: dust, ice or children playing all call for less.

  4. You pass this sign on your way out of the community. From when does the number on it apply?

    • From the sign itself, onwardsCorrect answer
    • Only after the last house
    • From the next intersection
    • When traffic allows it

    A new limit starts at the sign, not somewhere past it. Outside communities the basic Nunavut limit is 90 km/h unless a sign says otherwise, so read the sign instead of assuming.

  5. This red and white sign faces you at the end of a short street. What does it mean?

    • You may not drive in hereCorrect answer
    • The street is closed for repairs
    • Only residents may drive in
    • Entry is allowed at walking speed

    Do not enter. Something about that street, a one-way flow or a restricted yard, means traffic never comes in from your side.

  6. How may you drive on a street where this sign is posted?

    • Only in the direction the arrow pointsCorrect answer
    • In either direction, keeping right
    • In either direction if you are turning
    • Only when no other vehicle is present

    Every lane on a one-way street runs the same way. Once you have turned onto it you must be travelling the way the arrow points.

  7. You want to leave your vehicle where this sign stands while you go inside a building. May you?

    • No, though you may stop briefly to let someone outCorrect answer
    • Yes, for up to fifteen minutes
    • Yes, if the engine is left running
    • Yes, outside business hours

    No parking bans leaving the vehicle there, but a short stop to pick somebody up or drop them off is still allowed. Read the hours on any tab under the sign.

  8. What are you being told not to do where this sign is posted?

    • Do not drive around the vehicle ahead of youCorrect answer
    • Do not stop on this stretch
    • Do not turn around here
    • Do not go faster than the posted limit

    No passing. On a narrow or blind stretch there is no room to pull out and get back in, and in Nunavut there is no painted line to show you where your side of the road ends.

  9. Someone steps onto the road at this sign as you approach. What must you do?

    • Stop and wait until they have finished crossingCorrect answer
    • Drive slowly around behind them
    • Flash your headlights and carry on
    • Stop only if they raise a hand

    Wait until the person is safely across before you move. Never drive past another vehicle that has stopped here: it may be hiding somebody you cannot see.

  10. Cold rain has been falling on the road where this yellow diamond stands. What is it warning you about?

    • The surface can be slipperyCorrect answer
    • Loose gravel has been spread
    • The road narrows
    • A vehicle wash is ahead

    A slippery surface takes far longer to stop on. Ease off early, steer smoothly, and leave much more space than usual behind the vehicle ahead.

  11. This yellow sign appears on a gravel road. What should you expect just ahead?

    • A rough spot that can throw the vehicle off lineCorrect answer
    • A speed bump you may ignore
    • A change of surface to pavement
    • A stretch where passing is allowed

    Slow down before you reach it. Hitting a bump at speed can bounce the steering out of your hands, and on loose gravel that easily becomes a skid.

  12. You are driving on a paved stretch and this sign appears. What happens next?

    • The pavement gives way to gravelCorrect answer
    • The road ends completely
    • A bridge deck begins
    • A passing lane opens

    Slow down before the change, not after it. Gravel offers much less grip than pavement, so braking and steering both need more room from that point on.

  13. What should you do before starting down the hill this sign warns about?

    • Choose a lower gear and check your speed earlyCorrect answer
    • Shift into neutral to save fuel
    • Speed up so you can coast at the bottom
    • Hold the brake pedal down the whole way

    A lower gear lets the engine hold the vehicle back. Riding the brakes all the way down heats them until they fade, exactly when you need them most.

  14. Where should your braking be done for the curve this sign announces?

    • Before you enter it, while the wheels are still straightCorrect answer
    • Halfway around the curve
    • At the sharpest point
    • After the curve straightens out

    Brake in a straight line, then steer. Braking and turning at the same time on gravel is how vehicles end up sideways.

  15. This yellow sign is posted on a street with no sidewalk. What does it ask of you?

    • Expect people to be walking on or near the roadCorrect answer
    • Expect a bus stop
    • Expect a parking area
    • Expect a delivery entrance

    Where there is no sidewalk, people walk on the road itself. Slow down, cover the brake and be ready for somebody to step out from between parked vehicles.

  16. What does this sign tell you about the area you are entering?

    • Children may run out without lookingCorrect answer
    • Parking is reserved for families
    • Children are not allowed near the road
    • The area is closed in winter

    Nunavut asks drivers to keep to the posted speed near playgrounds and schools, and to a reasonable and prudent speed where nothing is posted. Children judge distance and speed poorly.

  17. You are approaching the zone marked by this sign at the start of the school day. What is expected of you?

    • Drive at the posted speed, or slowly and carefully if none is postedCorrect answer
    • Use your horn to warn children
    • Stop at every corner
    • Keep to the centre of the road

    School zones in Nunavut are driven at the posted speed, and where no speed is posted the standard is a reasonable and prudent one. Watch for children crossing between vehicles.

  18. This sign stands ahead of you on a community road. What is about to happen?

    • The road you are on ends and you must turnCorrect answer
    • The road becomes one way
    • A bridge is coming
    • A parking area opens on the right

    The road ends in a T. Slow down early so you can choose a direction and turn, instead of sliding straight on into whatever lies beyond.

  19. A row of these markers stands where the road narrows beside a building. What are they for?

    • They mark an obstacle you must keep away fromCorrect answer
    • They mark a place to park
    • They mark the boundary of the community
    • They mark a bus route

    A hazard marker shows something solid at the edge of the travelled road. The stripes slope down towards the side you should pass on.

  20. Why are the signs around a work area orange rather than yellow?

    • Orange marks a temporary warning, for work going on nowCorrect answer
    • Orange signs are only advice
    • Orange signs apply only at night
    • Orange means the road is closed

    Orange marks a condition that was not there last week and may be gone next week: crews, equipment and people working close to moving traffic.

  21. This sign warns that a person will be directing traffic ahead. Whose instructions do you follow there?

    • The person directing traffic, ahead of any signCorrect answer
    • Whichever sign was posted before the work started
    • Your own judgement about the safest path
    • The vehicle in front of you

    A traffic control person stands in the middle of a work area that changes by the hour and can see what the signs cannot. Their signals come first.

  22. The road ahead is dug up and this sign points to the side. What should you do?

    • Follow the marked route around the closed sectionCorrect answer
    • Wait beside the work until it reopens
    • Drive through slowly at your own risk
    • Turn back the way you came

    A detour is a temporary route around a closure. Follow the signs the whole way; they normally bring you back to the same road past the work.

  23. A passenger in your vehicle needs medical care. What does this blue sign point to?

    • A hospitalCorrect answer
    • A pharmacy
    • A fire hall
    • A police detachment

    Blue signs point to services rather than telling you how to drive. Following this series of signs is faster and safer than trying to navigate from memory while worried.

  24. You are low on fuel and see this sign. What is it showing?

    • Where fuel can be boughtCorrect answer
    • A vehicle inspection station
    • A truck route
    • A rest area

    Fuel is not sold everywhere, so it is worth filling up before a long trip rather than once the gauge is already low.

  25. You have been driving for hours and see this blue sign. What does it mark?

    • Somewhere to stop and eatCorrect answer
    • A grocery delivery point
    • A place to stay overnight
    • A community hall

    Service signs exist so drivers can plan a break instead of pushing on while tired. Stopping to eat does more for your alertness than opening a window.

  26. Who may leave a vehicle in a space marked with this sign?

    • Only a vehicle displaying a valid accessible parking permitCorrect answer
    • Anyone, for a short stop
    • Delivery vehicles
    • Anyone outside business hours

    The space is wider than the others for a reason: somebody needs that room beside the vehicle to get out. Using it without a permit takes the room away.

  27. What does a steady light of this colour require?

    • Stop before the intersection and wait for the light to changeCorrect answer
    • Slow down and pass when the way is clear
    • Stop only if another vehicle is crossing
    • Carry on if you are already close to it

    A steady red means stop and stay stopped until the light changes. Where no line is painted on the road, stop before the part of the intersection other traffic uses.

  28. This light comes on while you are approaching the intersection. What is the rule?

    • Stop if you can do it safely, because the red is comingCorrect answer
    • Speed up to clear the intersection
    • Treat it the same as a green light
    • Stop only if a vehicle is waiting to cross

    Amber means the light is about to turn red. Stop, unless you are so close that braking would be more dangerous than carrying through.

  29. Nunavut roads carry no painted lines, but your licence lets you drive elsewhere. What do these two solid yellow lines mean?

    • Neither direction may pass hereCorrect answer
    • Parking is allowed on both sides
    • The road is one way
    • Only the left lane may be used

    A double solid line separates traffic going opposite ways and tells both sides that passing is not permitted. Nunavut has no road markings at all, so this is worth learning before you drive in another jurisdiction.

  30. On a road outside the territory you meet a broken white line between two lanes. What does it tell you?

    • Both lanes go the same way and you may change lanes when it is safeCorrect answer
    • The lane beside you is closed
    • Traffic beside you comes the other way
    • You must stay in your lane until the next intersection

    White lines separate traffic travelling the same way, and a broken one may be crossed. No such line exists anywhere in Nunavut, where you judge your position from the edges of the road instead.

Rules of the Road and Safe Driving

30 questions

  1. What is the youngest age at which you may hold a Class 7 learner licence in Nunavut?

    • 15 years oldCorrect answer
    • 16 years old
    • 17 years old
    • 18 years old

    Nunavut sets 15 as the minimum age for the Class 7 learner licence. Some practice sites print 16, which is the minimum age for a full Class 5, not for the learner licence.

  2. What does a Class 7 licence allow you to do?

    • Operate any vehicle in Class 5 or Class 6 while you are learning to operate itCorrect answer
    • Drive alone in anything up to a light truck
    • Drive a bus carrying fewer than 24 passengers
    • Drive during daylight hours only

    The Class 7 is a learner licence: it covers the vehicles of Class 5 and Class 6 while you are learning them, and it is used with an accompanying driver rather than on your own.

  3. How is the Nunavut knowledge test put together?

    • Two parts at one sitting: rules of the road and safe driving, and sign identificationCorrect answer
    • One paper covering everything at once
    • Two papers written on separate days
    • A spoken interview only

    Nunavut's manual describes an examination in two parts taken together: one on the rules of the road and safe driving practices, and one on identifying signs.

  4. You pass the sign identification part but fail the other one. What do you have to rewrite?

    • Only the part you failedCorrect answer
    • Both parts, from the beginning
    • Nothing, a pass on one part carries the other
    • A road test instead

    Nunavut has you rewrite only the part that was failed. The part you passed stands, so your studying can go where you actually came up short.

  5. Which stages does a new Nunavut driver pass through after the learner licence?

    • None, because Nunavut has no graduated licensing programme; a Class 7 holder who passes the road test moves to a full Class 5Correct answer
    • A six-month probationary stage with a night curfew
    • Two novice stages of one year each
    • A three-stage programme that ends at age 18

    Graduated licensing with stages, curfews and passenger limits is common elsewhere in Canada, but Nunavut publishes no such programme. Anything you read about Nunavut learner stages has been borrowed from another jurisdiction.

  6. Who may sit with you while you drive on a Class 7 licence?

    • Someone who holds a valid driver's licence and is authorised under the ActCorrect answer
    • Any adult who has driven before
    • Any passenger aged 18 or over
    • Nobody is needed once you have practised enough

    The person beside you must genuinely be licensed and authorised to accompany you, and the examiner may ask to see that licence. Nunavut publishes no minimum age or years of experience for that person.

  7. Your community has no Motor Vehicles Office. How are driver services delivered there?

    • Through the Government Liaison Officer programme, which has an office in every communityCorrect answer
    • You must travel to Iqaluit for every service
    • Everything is handled by mail
    • Only during one yearly visit by an examiner

    Nunavut has Motor Vehicles Offices in Iqaluit, Rankin Inlet and Gjoa Haven, and Government Liaison Offices in all twenty-five communities support the rest of the territory. Some examining is also done by third-party examiners.

  8. Reading the written test is difficult for you. What does Nunavut offer?

    • The examiner may give you the test out loud insteadCorrect answer
    • A shorter version of the test
    • A pass based on the road test alone
    • A friend may write it for you

    Where somebody cannot read and understand the written test, the driver examiner may give a spoken test. It is the same material, asked aloud.

  9. How long does a Nunavut driver's licence run before it has to be renewed?

    • Five yearsCorrect answer
    • One year
    • Two years
    • Three years

    Licences are issued for five years. Renew before the expiry date: a licence that has been expired for more than twelve months cannot simply be renewed and the examinations must be taken again.

  10. You move to Nunavut holding a licence from another province. How long do you have to apply for a Nunavut licence?

    • Thirty daysCorrect answer
    • Fifteen days
    • Six months
    • One year

    New residents have thirty days from the move to apply. Separately, a change of name or address has to be reported to your Motor Vehicle service provider within fifteen days.

  11. Nothing is posted on the street you are driving along inside a community. What speed applies?

    • 50 km/hCorrect answer
    • 30 km/h
    • 40 km/h
    • 60 km/h

    Inside communities the basic limit is 50 km/h unless a sign says otherwise. It is a ceiling for good conditions, not a speed to hold regardless of dust, ice or people on the road.

  12. What is the basic speed limit outside the communities where nothing is posted?

    • 90 km/hCorrect answer
    • 60 km/h
    • 70 km/h
    • 80 km/h

    Outside communities the basic limit is 90 km/h, day or night, unless a sign says otherwise. Loose gravel and poor visibility very often call for far less than that.

  13. What does Nunavut law require about seat belts and child seats?

    • Every driver and passenger must be belted, and small children must be in a child car seatCorrect answer
    • Only the driver must be belted
    • Belts are required outside the communities only
    • Child seats are recommended but not required

    The requirement covers everybody in the vehicle, and small children need a car seat rather than an adult belt across them. It applies on a two-minute trip across the community just as much as on a long one.

  14. Which animal does the Nunavut driver's manual tell you to watch for around your vehicle?

    • DogsCorrect answer
    • Caribou
    • Foxes
    • Ravens

    The manual names dogs. They are often close to parked vehicles and can move without warning, so look around the vehicle before you start off and stay alert for animals on the road.

  15. No speed is posted at a playground in your community. How fast should you drive past it?

    • At a speed that is reasonable and prudent for what is happening thereCorrect answer
    • At the highest speed the street allows
    • At the same speed as the vehicle ahead of you
    • Speed does not matter where nothing is posted

    Where a school or playground zone has a posted speed you drive at it; where nothing is posted the standard is a reasonable and prudent speed, which means slow enough to stop for a child who runs out.

  16. You only ever drive inside your own community, never on a road out of it. Do you still need a licence?

    • Yes, a valid licence is needed to operate any motor vehicle in NunavutCorrect answer
    • No, a licence is only needed between communities
    • No, as long as the vehicle stays off the main street
    • Only if the vehicle is registered

    Nunavut publishes no exemption for short local trips. To legally operate a motor vehicle anywhere in the territory you must hold a valid driver's licence.

  17. The vehicle ahead of you on a gravel road is throwing up a thick cloud of dust. What does the manual tell you to do?

    • Do not pass it, and stay back where you can seeCorrect answer
    • Pass quickly on the left before the dust thickens
    • Sound your horn and pass on the right
    • Switch on your high beams and pass

    You cannot pass what you cannot see around. Inside a dust cloud you can see neither oncoming traffic, nor the edge of the road, nor anybody walking on it, so drop back and wait for a clear stretch.

  18. Why does Nunavut warn drivers not to travel too fast on loose gravel?

    • The tires lose their grip, so braking and steering both take longerCorrect answer
    • Gravel damages the paint on other vehicles
    • Fuel use rises sharply on gravel
    • Gravel roads carry a lower posted limit than paved ones

    Loose stone rolls under the tires. The vehicle takes longer to stop, drifts wide in a turn, and one sudden movement of the wheel can put it sideways.

  19. How much room should you leave behind a vehicle on a dusty gravel road?

    • Enough to stay out of its dust and still see the road aheadCorrect answer
    • One vehicle length, so you do not lose sight of it
    • Close enough to follow its tracks
    • The same distance as on a paved road

    The extra space does two jobs: it keeps you out of the dust so you can see, and it gives you the longer stopping distance that gravel demands.

  20. Which set of checks does the Nunavut manual ask you to make on the vehicle before winter?

    • Battery, brake adjustment, wipers, heater and defrosterCorrect answer
    • Paint, seats and radio
    • Tire colour, mirrors and mud flaps
    • Horn, plate light and spare key

    Those are the parts that decide whether you can see and whether you can stop in the cold. A weak battery and a defroster that cannot clear the glass are winter problems, not summer ones.

  21. What belongs in the kit the manual says to carry in winter?

    • A scraper brush, booster cables, a shovel, a tow strap, a traction mat, sand and a flashlightCorrect answer
    • A spare windshield, an extinguisher and flares
    • A first aid kit and nothing else
    • A second set of keys and a road map

    Every item on that list is there to get a stuck or dead vehicle moving again, or to let you see and be seen while you work on it. Carrying it costs nothing until the day it matters.

  22. The road has turned slippery and your vehicle has no anti-lock brakes. How does the manual say to brake?

    • Pump the brakes rather than locking the wheelsCorrect answer
    • Press hard and hold the pedal down
    • Use the parking brake instead
    • Steer sharply to scrub off speed

    Locked wheels slide and cannot be steered. Pumping keeps them turning, which is what lets you slow down and still point the vehicle where you want it. Watch for patches of black ice, which look like nothing more than wet road.

  23. You come up behind a vehicle showing flashing orange lights while it clears snow. What should you do?

    • Stay well back, because flashing orange marks slow work and thrown snowCorrect answer
    • Pass it on whichever side is clearest
    • Follow it closely so you stay in the cleared track
    • Sound your horn until it pulls over

    Flashing orange marks a vehicle working on the road, not an emergency. It moves slowly, stops without warning and throws snow and stone out to the side, so give it plenty of room.

  24. A Nunavut driver's record reaches twelve demerit points. What happens?

    • The Registrar sends a warning letter, and an interview followsCorrect answer
    • The licence is cancelled at once
    • A fine is added to the next renewal
    • Nothing until the licence expires

    Twelve points brings a warning letter and an interview. It is a chance to change how you drive before the next threshold is reached.

  25. How long is the suspension once a driver reaches fifteen demerit points?

    • One monthCorrect answer
    • One week
    • Three months
    • One year

    At fifteen points the Registrar suspends the licence for one month. Points are added to the record as convictions come in, so they build up quietly.

  26. At what ages must a Class 5, 6 or 7 driver in Nunavut provide a driver's medical examination?

    • At 75, at 80, and every two years after 80Correct answer
    • At 65, then every five years
    • At 70 only
    • Only when a doctor asks for one

    The schedule is set by age: once at 75, again at 80, and every second year from then on. The higher classes, such as buses and tractor-trailers, require a medical whatever the driver's age.

  27. Besides how clearly you see, what else does the Nunavut vision test look at?

    • Colour perception and field of visionCorrect answer
    • How quickly you read a page
    • Your hearing
    • Your reaction time

    Seeing to the sides matters as much as seeing straight ahead, and colour perception matters for signals and signs. If the standard is not met, a vision specialist gives a further test; glasses and contact lenses are allowed and are noted on the licence.

  28. How many painted centre lines and lane lines will you find on Nunavut roads?

    • None at all, because Nunavut roads carry no road markingsCorrect answer
    • They are painted on the main roads of the larger communities
    • Only on the roads between communities
    • Only in summer, once the snow has gone

    Nunavut's own manual states that there are no road markings in the territory, although that may change one day. Practice questions about solid and broken lines are describing roads somewhere else.

  29. With no centre line to follow, how do you keep to your own side of the road?

    • Judge your position from the edges of the road, and keep right of oncoming traffic by eyeCorrect answer
    • Drive down the middle and move over only when a vehicle appears
    • Follow the tracks left by the vehicle before you
    • Stay to the left, because the road is firmest there

    Without paint, the shoulders and the edges of the travelled surface are your reference. Keep inside them, look far ahead for oncoming vehicles, and move over early rather than at the last moment.

  30. You meet a yellow diamond-shaped sign. What kind of message does it carry?

    • A warning about something ahead on the roadCorrect answer
    • An order you can be fined for breaking
    • Directions towards a service
    • A temporary instruction for a work area

    Shape and colour tell you the kind of message before you are close enough to read it. Yellow diamonds warn, red and white signs order, blue signs point to services, and orange signs mark temporary work.

About this quiz

This page is free practice for the Nunavut Class 7 knowledge test, the written examination you pass before you may drive with somebody beside you. The territory describes it as two parts taken at one sitting, sign identification and the rules of the road and safe driving practices, and if you fail one part you rewrite only that part.

Nunavut publishes less about its driving test than any other jurisdiction in Canada. There is no published question count, no pass mark, no time limit and no fee. Commercial sites state such figures anyway and cite nothing for them, and at least one prints the minimum age as sixteen when the territory's own manual says fifteen. The numbers on our mock test are a clearly stated practice standard of our own.

Good to know

  • The Class 7 licence is the learner licence, the minimum age is fifteen, and it permits the operation of any vehicle in Class 5 or 6 while you are learning to operate it.
  • The examination has two parts taken at one sitting, and failing one part means rewriting only that part.
  • Nunavut has no graduated licensing programme: no stages, no probationary period, no curfew and no passenger limit are published anywhere.
  • There are no road markings anywhere in Nunavut, which means no painted centre line and no lane lines.
  • Three Motor Vehicles Offices serve the whole territory, at Iqaluit, Rankin Inlet and Gjoa Haven, and there are Government Liaison Offices in all twenty-five communities.

Common questions

How many questions are on the real Nunavut knowledge test?

The territory does not publish a number. Commercial practice sites state one anyway, and none of them cites a government source. Our mock test uses a count of our own and says so.

What is the pass mark?

That is not published either, and neither is a time limit. The widely repeated eighty percent has no Government of Nunavut source behind it, so the pass mark on our mock test is our own practice standard.

How old do I have to be?

Fifteen for a Class 7 licence, which is what the territory's own manual says. Some practice sites print sixteen, which is the minimum age for a Class 5 licence, not for the learner licence.

Does Nunavut have graduated licensing?

No. The territory publishes no stages, no stage lengths and no probationary period. What the manual describes is a Class 7 learner licence, then a road test, then a Class 5 licence.

Who can sit beside me while I practise?

Somebody who holds a valid driver's licence and is authorised under the Act, and the examiner may ask to see that licence. Nothing further is published about the accompanying driver, and we are not going to borrow another jurisdiction's rule to fill that in.
