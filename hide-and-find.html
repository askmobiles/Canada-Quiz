🔍 Hide & Find

One player hides five things in a busy scene. The other has to find them.

📖 How to play

This one needs two players and one screen. Player 1 picks a scene, and Player 2 looks away. Player 1 taps five places in the picture to hide five objects, then passes the device over. Player 2 taps the picture to hunt for them, and the clock and the miss counter are running the whole time. When all five are found, swap roles and see who did it faster. Easy makes the objects big, hard makes them tiny and fills the scene with things that look almost the same.

About this game

Hide and Find is a hidden object game for two people sharing one screen, and the twist is that nobody wrote the puzzle in advance. One player hides the five objects wherever they like while the other looks away, so every round is made by a person who knows exactly how to be annoying.

The picture underneath is deliberately crowded. Hundreds of small drawings are scattered across a hockey arena, a campsite by the lake, or a city street in winter, and the five hidden things sit among them at the same size as everything else.

A clock and a miss counter run while the second player hunts, which turns the whole thing into a fair contest: hide, find, swap roles, and compare the two times.

How it works

  • Three scenes to hide in: a hockey arena, a campsite beside a lake, and a city street in winter, each with its own set of things lying around.
  • Five objects a round, and the scene decides which ones. The arena hides a key, a maple leaf, a beaver, a scarf and a present.
  • Easy, medium and hard change two things at once: how big the hidden objects are, and how much clutter is piled around them.
  • Hard also scatters look-alikes through the scene — dead leaves next to the maple leaf, a sock next to the mitten — so a quick glance is no longer enough.
  • A cover screen sits over the picture between the two turns, so the hider can pass the device across without the seeker catching a glimpse.
  • A dice button hides all five for you, which is what you want when only one person is around and you just fancy a hunt.

Tips for playing

  • Hide near an object of the same colour rather than in an empty corner. Empty space is the first place a seeker looks, because it is the easiest to scan.
  • Edges and corners feel clever and almost never are. Most seekers sweep the border first precisely because it feels like a hiding place.
  • As the seeker, hunt one object at a time. Holding all five shapes in your head at once is what makes an easy board feel impossible.
  • Sweep in strips rather than jumping around. Left to right, one band at a time, beats letting your eye wander wherever it likes.
  • A wrong tap costs a miss but never ends the round, so guessing at a suspicious shape is usually worth it.
  • Play both roles before deciding who won. Hiding well is a skill of its own, and the fairest score is the two hunting times side by side.

Common questions

Can one person play this alone?

Yes. Press the dice button and the five objects are hidden for you at random, then hunt for them yourself. It loses the mischief of a human hider, but the clock still runs and the hunt is the same.

How does the second player avoid seeing where things were hidden?

A full screen cover drops over the picture the moment the fifth object is placed, and it only lifts when the second player presses the ready button. Hand the device over while the cover is up.

What exactly changes on hard?

The objects shrink to about half the easy size, the scene holds roughly three times as much clutter, there are no hints, and copies of things that look like the hidden objects are mixed into the scene on purpose.

Is the scene the same every time?

No. The clutter is scattered again from scratch at the start of every round, so the same scene never looks twice the same and nobody can memorise a good spot.

How young can a player be?

On easy the objects are large and there is no reading beyond the five picture names, so a five-year-old manages it. Hard suits older children and adults who like a proper search.
