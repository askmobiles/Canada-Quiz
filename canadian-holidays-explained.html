📅 Canadian Holidays and What They Celebrate

A friendly guide to Canada's main holidays — Canada Day, Victoria Day, Thanksgiving, Remembrance Day, and more — and what each one means.

Canada has several special holidays throughout the year. Each one has its own meaning and traditions. Here is a simple guide to the main ones.

Canada Day — July 1

Canada Day celebrates the birthday of Canada as a country, back in 1867. People enjoy fireworks, parades, and barbecues, and wear red and white.

Victoria Day — May

Victoria Day is celebrated on the Monday before May 25. It honours Queen Victoria and also marks the unofficial start of summer for many Canadians.

Thanksgiving — October

Canadian Thanksgiving is on the second Monday in October. Families gather to share a big meal and give thanks for the harvest and for good things in their lives.

Remembrance Day — November 11

On Remembrance Day, Canadians honour the soldiers who served and died for their country. Many people wear a red poppy and pause for a moment of silence at 11 a.m.

Other important days

  • Labour Day (first Monday in September) — honours workers.
  • Family Day (February, in some provinces) — a day to spend with family.
  • New Year's Day, Good Friday, and Christmas Day are also statutory holidays.

In short

This guide walks through the holidays that shape a Canadian year, one short section at a time. Canada Day, Victoria Day, Thanksgiving and Remembrance Day each get their own explanation, followed by a list of the other days when many workplaces and schools close.

If you are new to the country, the calendar can be puzzling at first. Knowing that Thanksgiving falls in October here, and what the red poppy in November stands for, helps you join in instead of watching from the side.

Families and teachers can also read it just before a holiday arrives. The sections are short enough for the breakfast table and specific enough to answer the question a child asks in the car on the way to school.

Key points

  • Canada Day on July 1 is explained through the country's beginning in 1867, plus fireworks, parades and backyard barbecues.
  • Victoria Day is placed on the Monday before May 25 and described as the unofficial start of summer for many Canadians.
  • Thanksgiving is set on the second Monday in October, with the family meal and the harvest behind it explained simply.
  • Remembrance Day on November 11 covers the red poppy and the moment of silence kept at eleven in the morning.
  • A final list adds Labour Day, Family Day, New Year's Day, Good Friday and Christmas Day.

Worth remembering too

  • Canadian Thanksgiving lands on the second Monday in October, well before the American one, because the harvest here finishes earlier in the year.
  • Victoria Day honours Queen Victoria and always falls on the Monday before May 25, which is why its date shifts a little each year.
  • The red poppy worn in early November is a quiet way of remembering soldiers, and many people stop for a moment of silence on November 11.
  • Family Day exists in some provinces and not in others, so a February day off your friend enjoys may not be a holiday where you live.
  • Labour Day, on the first Monday in September, honours workers and for many families marks the last weekend before school starts again.

Common questions

Does this page list every holiday in Canada?

It covers the main ones and adds a short list of other statutory days. Provinces set some of their own holidays, so check your provincial rules if you need an exact list for work.

Why do the dates of some Canadian holidays change?

Several holidays are set by weekday rather than by number, such as the second Monday in October. The article shows which ones move around and which ones stay on a fixed date.
