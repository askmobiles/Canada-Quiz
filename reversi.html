⚫⚪ Reversi

Trap a line of your opponent's pieces and they all flip to your colour. Also known as Othello.

📖 How to play

Take turns placing a piece on the board. Your move must trap at least one straight line of the other colour between the piece you just played and another piece of your own — across, down or diagonally. Every trapped piece flips to your colour. The small pale dots show you where you are allowed to play. If you have no legal move your turn is skipped automatically. When neither player can move the game ends, and whoever has more pieces on the board wins. A minute to learn and a lifetime to master — the corners are worth far more than they look.

About this game

Reversi, sold for many years as Othello, is a board game with one rule and a great deal of depth. You place a piece so that it traps a straight line of your opponent's pieces between it and another piece of your own, and every trapped piece flips to your colour. That is the whole game.

The pale dots on the board show every square you are allowed to play, so nobody has to hunt for legal moves. If you have no move at all your turn is skipped automatically, which is part of the real rules and catches people out.

You can play against a friend on the same phone or tablet, or against the computer at three strengths. Easy simply grabs the most pieces it can see this turn, which is the classic beginner mistake and makes it beatable. Medium thinks two moves ahead and values corners. Hard searches five moves ahead and will punish you for handing it a corner.

Tips for playing

  • Having the most pieces in the middle of the game means very little. Strong players are often behind on count for most of the game and win at the end.
  • Corners can never be flipped, because there is no square beyond them to trap against. That single fact is most of the strategy.
  • The square diagonally next to a corner is the most dangerous on the board. Playing there usually hands your opponent the corner.

Common questions

Is Reversi the same as Othello?

Almost. Othello is a commercial version of Reversi with a fixed starting position and a standard board. What you play here is the Othello form, which is what almost everyone means by Reversi today.

Why was my turn skipped?

Because you had no legal move. If you cannot trap any line, the rules say you pass and the other player goes again. The game only ends when neither side can move.
