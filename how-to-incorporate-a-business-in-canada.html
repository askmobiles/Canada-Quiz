How to Incorporate a Business in Canada

What it costs, what the steps are, and the two obligations afterwards that catch new owners out — one of which can quietly dissolve your company.

Federal or provincial: the first decision

You can incorporate federally under the Canada Business Corporations Act, through Corporations Canada, or provincially under your province's own act. Federal incorporation gives you the right to use your name across Canada. Provincial incorporation protects the name in that province.

The fees are not the same, and neither is the paperwork.

WhereGovernment feeNote
Federal, online$200Processed in one business day. An express option adds $100 for four hours.
Ontario$300Immediate online. A name search report is required for a word name.
British Columbia$350 plus $30The extra $30 is the name approval, so about $380 in total.

The steps, in order

First, the name. You can have a word name or a numbered one. If you incorporate federally online, the name search and pre-approval are built into the application — Corporations Canada says plainly that you do not need to order a separate report beforehand. Ontario is different and does require a name search report for a word name, dated within the previous ninety days. A numbered company, something like 12345678 Canada Inc., skips all of that.

Second, the articles of incorporation. This is where you set the share structure and any restrictions on transferring shares, the number of directors, any restriction on what the business may do, and the language of the documents.

Third, a registered office address, where corporate records are kept and legal documents can be delivered. Fourth, your directors — a basic federal incorporation allows up to ten, and for each one you say whether they are a resident Canadian.

The director rule that surprises newcomers

For a federal corporation, at least twenty-five percent of directors must be resident Canadians. If you have fewer than four directors, at least one of them must be. In practice that means a one, two or three-director federal corporation needs one resident Canadian director.

A few sectors go further and need a majority: airlines, telecommunications, book retailing and film or video distribution.

Provinces differ, and this is often the deciding factor. Ontario removed its twenty-five percent requirement, and British Columbia's act sets no residency requirement at all. If nobody in your group is a resident Canadian, that difference matters more than the fee.

Directors must be individuals, at least eighteen, not bankrupt and not legally incapable.

The annual return is not a tax return

Corporations Canada has a whole page explaining this, which tells you how often people confuse them. They are two different filings, to two different departments, and doing one does not do the other.

The annual return goes to Corporations Canada. It costs twelve dollars online or forty by mail, and it is due within sixty days of your anniversary date. Every federal business corporation must file it, no matter how small.

Skip it and your corporation can be dissolved. Corporations Canada's own words are that it will assume the company is not operating, and once dissolved you can no longer conduct business. In practice it dissolves after two years of non-filing and sends a final notice giving another hundred and twenty days. Ontario's annual return, for comparison, is free.

Incorporating federally does not finish the job

You still have to register in every province and territory where you actually carry on business. Corporations Canada is explicit: registration is different from incorporation, and while a corporation incorporates only once, it can register to carry on business in any number of jurisdictions.

Ontario, Nova Scotia and Newfoundland and Labrador can be done at the same time as the federal incorporation. The others are separate, and the fees are on top of the two hundred dollars.

Then there is the tax return, and two different dates

A corporation must file a T2 return every year, and the CRA is blunt about the scope: all corporations, including non-profit, tax-exempt and inactive ones, have to file for every tax year even when there is no tax payable. A dormant company with no income still files.

Now the part that costs people money. The return is due six months after your year end. The tax itself is due two months after your year end, or three months for a qualifying small business corporation. Those are different dates, and interest starts running from the payment date even though the return is not late yet.

Filing late costs five percent of the unpaid tax plus one percent for each complete month, up to twelve months. Do it again within three years and it doubles to ten percent plus two percent a month, up to twenty months.

Where these facts come from

The fees, steps, director rules and annual return details come from Corporations Canada, the Canada Business Corporations Act itself, and the Ontario and British Columbia government fee pages. The tax filing rules come from the CRA.

Some figures are missing on purpose. There is no mail-in federal incorporation fee here, because Corporations Canada publishes no such row. There is no Alberta fee and no Alberta director rule, because neither could be confirmed. And there is no price for a name search report, because those are sold by private companies rather than by a government.

About this page

Incorporating federally through Corporations Canada costs $200 online and is processed in one business day. Ontario is $300 and British Columbia about $380. Federal incorporation protects your name across Canada; you must still register in each province where you operate.

Two ongoing obligations catch people out: the annual return to Corporations Canada, which is not a tax return and can lead to dissolution if skipped, and the T2 corporate tax return, which is due six months after year end while the tax itself is due at two or three months.

Common questions

How much does it cost to incorporate in Canada?

Federally, $200 online through Corporations Canada, processed in one business day. Ontario charges $300 and British Columbia about $380 including name approval. Provincial registration fees may be on top.

Do I need a Canadian director to incorporate federally?

Yes. At least 25 percent of directors must be resident Canadians, and with fewer than four directors at least one must be. Ontario and British Columbia have no residency requirement.

Is the annual return the same as a tax return?

No. They are separate filings to separate departments. The federal annual return costs $12 online, is due within 60 days of your anniversary date, and skipping it can get your corporation dissolved.

Does an inactive corporation still have to file taxes?

Yes. The CRA requires a T2 return for every tax year from all corporations, including inactive ones, even when no tax is payable.

Do I need to register in my province after incorporating federally?

Yes. Registration is different from incorporation. You incorporate once but register in every province and territory where you carry on business.
