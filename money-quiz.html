About this quiz

This is a money quiz built for Canadian kids and for the grown-ups helping them learn. It uses real Canadian coins, bills and shopping situations, so the answers connect to money a child actually holds. Six topics wait on the start screen, and each round is ten questions long.

Every question gives instant feedback. Tap an answer and the correct one lights up right away, together with a short explanation of the idea behind it. Nothing is hidden until the end, so a wrong guess becomes a small lesson instead of a lost mark.

Parents and teachers use it as a gentle way to open conversations about saving, borrowing and choosing. The word problems keep the dollar amounts small, so children practise arithmetic and money sense at the same time without feeling like they are doing homework.

What's inside

  • Canadian Money covers coins, bank notes, the three-letter code CAD, and who makes our coins and notes.
  • Saving and Spending mixes simple word problems with ideas like savings goals and an emergency fund.
  • Banks and Interest explains chequing accounts, savings accounts, debit and credit cards, PINs and bank statements.
  • Earning and Jobs looks at wages, salaries, tips, gross pay, take-home pay, résumés and volunteering.
  • Smart Shopping practises unit prices, percentage discounts, coupons, warranties and comparing two sizes of the same item.
  • Needs versus Wants asks children to sort must-haves from nice-to-haves and to think about trade-offs.

Good to know

  • Canada stopped making the one-cent penny in 2012 because each penny cost more to produce than it was actually worth.
  • Cash totals in Canada are rounded to the nearest five cents, while payments made by card or app are still charged to the exact cent.
  • Canadian bank notes are printed on polymer, a thin plastic that lasts far longer than paper money and is much harder to copy.
  • Raised dots in the corner of every Canadian note let people with low vision feel which bill they are holding.
  • The Royal Canadian Mint strikes our coins, while the Bank of Canada is the body that designs and issues our bank notes.
