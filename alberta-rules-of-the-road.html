The numbers worth memorising

Keep at least two seconds of space behind the car ahead on a dry road, and more when it is wet or icy. Leave one metre when you pass a cyclist. Slow to 60 km/h when you pass a stopped emergency vehicle with its lights on, or to the posted limit if it is lower. Learner and novice drivers must have zero alcohol in their blood. Speed limits are 50 km/h in a city or town and 80 km/h outside one unless a sign says otherwise, and playground and school zones drop to 30 km/h during their posted hours.

About this quiz

This page holds the rules half of the Alberta Class 7 knowledge test, arranged by topic instead of thrown at you all at once. Open any question to see the answer with a short explanation, then practise the whole chapter when you are ready. No timer runs and no score is kept.

The chapters follow the ground the real test covers, from the graduated licensing programme through safe driving, sharing the road with others, difficult driving conditions, keeping your licence, driving while impaired, looking after your vehicle, and what to do at a collision.

Every question here is written in our own words as unofficial practice. We are not affiliated with the Government of Alberta or any registry agent, and the free Alberta Basic Licence Driver's Guide remains the source you should read from cover to cover before your test.

Good to know

  • Leave at least two seconds of space behind the vehicle ahead on a dry road, and considerably more when the surface is wet or icy.
  • Give a cyclist a full metre of room when you pass, which often means waiting for a safe gap rather than squeezing by.
  • Slow to sixty kilometres per hour when you pass a stopped emergency vehicle with its lights on, or to the posted limit if that is lower.
  • Unless a sign says otherwise, the limit is fifty kilometres per hour inside a city or town and eighty outside one, while playground and school zones drop to thirty during their posted hours.
  • Take one chapter at a time and practise it straight away, because eight chapters read in a single sitting rarely stay in your head.

Common questions

Which chapters does this Alberta rules page cover?

There are eight in all: graduated licensing, safe and responsible driving, sharing the road, driving conditions, keeping your licence, impaired driving, your vehicle, and emergencies and collisions. Together they cover the rules side of the knowledge test.

Is this study page timed like the real test?

Not at all. There is no clock and no running score, which is the point of a study page. When you want the pressure of thirty minutes, the Alberta mock test is waiting for you.

Can I work on a single chapter without touching the others?

Yes. Every chapter has its own practice button that runs only the questions belonging to it. That is the fastest way to fix a weak spot the mock test has shown you.

Do these rules apply in other provinces too?

Road signs look the same across Canada, but the written rules and the licensing steps belong to each province. Everything on this page is written for Alberta, so check the local guide if you drive elsewhere.

Are these the exact questions used on the Alberta test?

No. They are original questions we wrote to cover the same ground, and this site is unofficial practice with no government affiliation. The real test draws its own thirty questions, so understand the reasoning rather than memorising our wording.
