📖 Story Maker

Give silly words, then read your funny story out loud!

📖 How to play

Don't peek at the story! Just fill in each silly word — a name, an animal, a funny word — without knowing where it goes. Take turns letting different people choose the words. When you press "Make my story," all your words drop into a story and you read the hilarious result out loud together. Pick Advanced for a longer story with trickier word types.

About this game

Story Maker is a word game where nobody knows the ending. The page asks you for a few words — a name, an animal, a colour, something silly — and drops them into a short story. You only see the result once every blank is filled.

The best way to play is out loud with other people. Let one person read the labels while everyone else shouts out words, then read the finished story together. The sillier the words, the funnier the story becomes.

It works well for long car rides, rainy afternoons, classroom warm-ups, and grandparents who want something to do with small children. Nothing is saved, nothing is scored, and you can start a brand new story whenever you like.

How it works

  • Two levels sit at the top of the page: the easy mode asks for simple words and the advanced mode asks for trickier ones.
  • Easy stories want things like a name, an animal, a colour, a food, a place and one silly word.
  • Advanced stories add word types such as an adjective, a job, an animal in the plural and an action verb.
  • Press the make button and every word you typed appears in red inside the finished story.
  • The new story button shuffles in a different story template with a fresh set of blanks to fill.

Tips for playing

  • Word games like this one quietly teach grammar, because you have to know what a verb or an adjective is before you can give one.
  • Funny names and made-up sounds usually work better than sensible words — a wobbling penguin beats an ordinary dog every time.
  • Reading aloud helps children hear how sentences flow, which is why teachers often use silly stories to build confidence in new readers.
  • If a blank is left empty, four small underscores appear instead, so an unfinished story still reads from beginning to end.
  • Try giving each person only one word type to fill, so nobody can guess the shape of the story before the reveal.

Common questions

Do I need to sign up to make a story?

No. The page works the moment it loads. There is no account, no email box and no download — you type your words straight into the boxes and press the button.

How many people can play at once?

As many as you can fit around one screen. One person types while the others call out words. With a bigger group, give each player a turn to choose the next word.

Is it suitable for young children?

Yes. The easy stories use words most five-year-olds already know, like an animal or a colour. An adult can do the typing while the child chooses the words out loud.

Can I get the same story twice?

Possibly. Each level holds a small set of story templates and the page picks one at random, so a template can come back. The words you choose still make it feel completely new.
