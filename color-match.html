🎨 Color Match

Read the colour name, then mix it yourself and see how close you got.

📖 How to play

You are given the name of a colour, but not the colour itself. Mix what you think it looks like using any of the three tools: the red, green and blue sliders, the colour picker, or the colour wheel with its lightness slider. All three tools control the same colour, so moving one updates the others. When you are happy, press Submit. The real colour appears beside yours and you get a score out of 100 plus a star rating. Each round is 8 colours, and the summary at the end shows your average.

About this game

Color Match gives you the name of a colour and asks you to make that colour yourself. The name appears on its own, with no sample to copy, so everything rests on the picture you hold in your head. When you are ready you lock in your answer and the real colour appears beside yours.

Three controls sit under the name and they all steer the same colour. There are red, green and blue sliders with a number beside each one, a colour picker swatch, and a colour wheel you drag on with its own lightness slider. Move any one of them and the other two follow, which quietly teaches how a colour is built on a screen.

Three levels change how hard the names are. Easy uses everyday colours, medium uses less common ones, and hard uses detailed and unusual names with a short friendly sentence explaining what each one is. A round is eight colours from start to finish, which suits a short break as well as a long evening.

How it works

  • You choose easy, medium or hard on the start screen, and each round then runs for eight colours.
  • Red, green and blue sliders each show their own number, so you can see exactly what you are mixing.
  • A colour picker swatch opens the picker built into your device and feeds the result straight back into the game.
  • A colour wheel lets you drag to find the shade, with a separate lightness slider standing beside it.
  • Locking in an answer gives you a score out of one hundred, a star rating, and both hex codes side by side.
  • The end of a round shows your average, and from there you can play again or switch to another level.

Tips for playing

  • The sliders mix light rather than paint, which is why red and green together give yellow instead of brown.
  • Finding the family of the colour on the wheel first, then adjusting the lightness, is usually faster than pushing three sliders at once.
  • Pale names such as cream and ivory sit high on all three sliders, and the whole trick is knowing which one to pull down.
  • Earthy names lean on plenty of red, a middling amount of green and very little blue.
  • Chasing a perfect hundred is not the point, because landing close on an unusual name at the hard level is already a fine result.

Common questions

Do I have to use all three controls?

No. Any one of them can set the whole colour by itself, because they are three windows onto the same value. Use whichever feels natural to you and leave the others alone.

What does the score out of one hundred mean?

It measures how far your colour sits from the real one and turns that distance into a number, where an exact match gives one hundred. The stars beside it tell you the same thing at a glance.

Does the game work on a phone?

Yes. Everything answers to touch as well as to a mouse, including dragging around the colour wheel, so a phone or a tablet works just as well as a laptop.

What is different about the hard level?

The colour names become detailed and unusual, the sort you meet in an artist's paint box rather than in a box of crayons. Each answer also comes with one short friendly sentence explaining what that colour is, so the level teaches while it tests.

Can two people play at once?

There is one set of controls, so taking turns works best. Mixing one colour each and comparing scores at the end of the round is a good way to share it, and so is crowding around one screen and arguing about the sliders.
