The First Peoples of This Land, Then and Now

People lived here, in every part of this land, for thousands of years before anyone sailed across the ocean. Their families are still here.

Indigenous peoples is the word for the peoples who were here first. In Canada there are three groups: First Nations, Inuit and Métis.

First Nations live all across the country. There are 634 First Nations today, and they are not all the same: different lands, different governments, different stories, different languages.

Inuit live in the far north, in Nunavut, northern Quebec, Labrador and the Northwest Territories. One person is called an Inuk. They have lived in the Arctic for thousands of years and know it better than anyone.

The Métis are a distinct people who grew from both First Nations and European families, mostly in the prairies, with their own language, their own music and their own flag.

More than 70 Indigenous languages are spoken in Canada right now. Some are spoken by many thousands of people. Some are spoken by only a few elders, and children are learning them again in school.

Not all of the history is happy. For more than a hundred years, Indigenous children were taken from their families and sent to residential schools, where they were punished for speaking their own language. Many never came home.

Every year on the 30th of September, Canada marks the National Day for Truth and Reconciliation. People wear orange shirts to remember those children and to promise to do better.

More than 1.8 million people in Canada are First Nations, Inuit or Métis. They are your classmates, your teachers, your doctors and your neighbours. This is not only a story about long ago.

Did you know? The words matter. Say Indigenous peoples, First Nations, Inuit and Métis. Better still, use the name of the nation itself, the way you would say Irish or Japanese rather than just European or Asian.
