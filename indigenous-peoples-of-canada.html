🪶 The Indigenous Peoples of Canada: First Nations, Inuit and Métis

An introduction to the Indigenous peoples of Canada — First Nations, Inuit, and Métis — their history, cultures, and importance to the country.

The Indigenous peoples of Canada have lived on this land for thousands of years, long before anyone else arrived. They are a vital part of Canada's past, present, and future. There are three main groups recognized in Canada.

First Nations

First Nations peoples are the largest of the three groups and include many different nations, each with its own language, culture, and traditions. They lived across the whole country, from the forests to the plains to the coasts, long before Europeans came.

Inuit

The Inuit live mainly in the Arctic, in the far north of Canada. They are skilled at surviving in cold lands and have a rich culture built around the land, the sea, and the animals of the north. The territory of Nunavut, created in 1999, has a large Inuit population.

Métis

The Métis are a distinct people who came from both Indigenous and European ancestors. They have their own culture, traditions, and history, especially in the Prairie provinces. The Métis played an important role in the fur trade and in Canada's early history.

Respect and reconciliation

Indigenous peoples have faced many hardships in Canadian history. Today, Canada works toward reconciliation — building respect, understanding, and a fairer future together. Learning about Indigenous history and cultures is an important part of understanding Canada.

In short

This short article introduces the Indigenous peoples of Canada in plain language. It explains who the three recognized groups are, where they live, and why their history matters to anyone trying to understand this country. It is written for newcomers, students and curious families alike.

It is a starting point, not a full history. Each section is a few sentences long, so you can read the whole page in a couple of minutes and still come away with the names, the regions and the ideas you will meet again and again.

Anyone studying for the citizenship test will find this useful, because Indigenous history is part of what Canada asks people to understand. Teachers can also use it as a gentle first reading before a class discussion about Canada's past.

Key points

  • An opening section explains that Indigenous peoples lived on this land for thousands of years before anyone else arrived.
  • The First Nations section describes the largest of the three groups and its many nations, languages and traditions.
  • The Inuit section covers life in the Arctic and mentions Nunavut, the territory created in 1999.
  • The Métis section explains a distinct people with both Indigenous and European ancestors, strongest in the Prairie provinces.
  • A closing section on respect and reconciliation talks about hardship in the past and building a fairer future together.

Worth remembering too

  • Canada recognizes three groups of Indigenous peoples: First Nations, Inuit and Métis. They are distinct from one another, and the three names are not interchangeable.
  • First Nations peoples lived right across the country, from the forests to the plains to both coasts, long before Europeans arrived here.
  • The territory of Nunavut was created in 1999 and has a large Inuit population, whose culture is tied to the land, the sea and northern animals.
  • The Métis played an important part in the fur trade and in Canada's early history, and they carry their own culture and traditions.
  • Reconciliation means building respect and understanding after real hardship, and learning this history is part of understanding Canada itself.
