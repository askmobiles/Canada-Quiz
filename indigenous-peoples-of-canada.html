🪶 The Indigenous Peoples of Canada: First Nations, Inuit and Métis

An introduction to the Indigenous peoples of Canada — First Nations, Inuit, and Métis — their history, cultures, and importance to the country.

The Indigenous peoples of Canada have lived on this land for thousands of years, long before anyone else arrived. They are a vital part of Canada's past, present, and future. There are three main groups recognized in Canada.

First Nations

First Nations peoples are the largest of the three groups and include many different nations, each with its own language, culture, and traditions. They lived across the whole country, from the forests to the plains to the coasts, long before Europeans came.

Inuit

The Inuit live mainly in the Arctic, in the far north of Canada. They are skilled at surviving in cold lands and have a rich culture built around the land, the sea, and the animals of the north. The territory of Nunavut, created in 1999, has a large Inuit population.

Métis

The Métis are a distinct people who came from both Indigenous and European ancestors. They have their own culture, traditions, and history, especially in the Prairie provinces. The Métis played an important role in the fur trade and in Canada's early history.

Respect and reconciliation

Indigenous peoples have faced many hardships in Canadian history. Today, Canada works toward reconciliation — building respect, understanding, and a fairer future together. Learning about Indigenous history and cultures is an important part of understanding Canada.
