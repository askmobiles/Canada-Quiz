What the real Manitoba knowledge test looks like

Manitoba Public Insurance publishes less about its test than most provinces. What MPI does state is concrete: the test is taken on a computer, it is not open book, and there is a 30-minute limit. It covers traffic laws, safe driving practices and recognising road signs. Tests are available in English, French and more than twenty other languages.

MPI does not publish the number of questions or the pass mark. You will see 40 questions and 80 percent quoted confidently on other practice sites; none of them cites MPI, and they contradict each other on whether that 80 percent is per section or across the whole test. We are not going to guess at a number that decides whether someone books a test. Our mock test gives you 30 questions in 30 minutes, matching the one timing MPI does publish.

The rest of the process is well documented. You can start at 16, or at 15 and a half if you are enrolled in MPI's Driver Z programme at a high school. You need identification and must be registered as an MPI customer before booking, and you must meet the vision standards. The knowledge test costs $12. After passing you spend at least nine months at the learner stage and at least fifteen months at the intermediate stage before a full Class 5. Your supervising driver must have been fully licensed for 36 months. If you fail, learners wait seven days before trying again.

How to pass on your first try

Read the official guide once from start to finish, then use practice questions to find your weak spots. Most people lose marks on the signs, not the rules, because there are a lot of signs and many look similar. Study the signs in small groups rather than all at once, and pay attention to shape and colour: a red circle with a line through it always means you must not do something, a yellow diamond always warns you about what is coming, and an orange sign always means road work.

Take the mock test more than once. The questions are picked at random each time, so you will see a different test on every run. When you can pass three times in a row, you are ready to book the real one.

Road Signs

6 of 30

  1. Where exactly must you stop at this sign?

    • At the stop line, or before the crosswalk if there is no lineCorrect answer
    • Anywhere in the intersection
    • Level with the sign post
    • Past the crosswalk

    Stop at the line first. If your view is blocked from there, creep forward slowly and stop a second time once you can see.

  2. What is banned where this sign stands?

    • Turning rightCorrect answer
    • Passing
    • Turning left
    • Parking

    Carry on to the next intersection instead. The ban usually exists because of a one-way street or a blind corner.

  3. How close to the nearest rail may you stop at a railway crossing?

    • No closer than 5 metresCorrect answer
    • 10 centimetres
    • There is no rule
    • Right at the rail

    Five metres minimum. Never start across until you can see the far side is clear, and remember one train can hide another.

  4. What changes after this sign?

    • Traffic will come toward you in the next laneCorrect answer
    • The road becomes one way
    • A passing lane opens
    • The limit doubles

    The road stops being divided. Keep right and do not pass without a clear view of the whole road ahead.

  5. When should you move over after seeing this sign?

    • Early, while a safe gap still existsCorrect answer
    • At the final cone
    • After the lane ends
    • Only if waved in

    Merging early and smoothly keeps traffic flowing. Racing to the front and squeezing in is what creates the jam.

  6. What does a broken yellow line beside your lane allow?

    • Passing when the road ahead is clearCorrect answer
    • Nothing
    • Parking
    • Driving in either direction

    Yellow separates opposite directions. Broken means you may pass when you can see far enough to finish safely.

Traffic Laws and Safe Driving

6 of 40

  1. What is the minimum age for a Manitoba Class 5 Learner licence?

    • 16, or 15 and a half if enrolled in a high school driver education courseCorrect answer
    • 17
    • 15
    • 18

    Sixteen normally, or fifteen and a half while taking a high school driver education course.

  2. Which of these may a Manitoba learner NOT do?

    • Tow another vehicleCorrect answer
    • Drive on a city street
    • Carry a rear-seat passenger
    • Drive in daylight

    Learners may not tow, may not drive a Class 3 farm truck, and may not operate off-road vehicles on a highway.

  3. Which lane should you normally use on a Manitoba divided highway?

    • The right lane, keeping left for passingCorrect answer
    • The left lane
    • The middle
    • Any lane

    Keep right except to pass. Sitting in the left lane forces others to pass on your right, where their blind spot is worst.

  4. Your car starts to skid on a Manitoba winter road. Where should you look?

    • Where you want the car to goCorrect answer
    • Straight down
    • At the vehicle you might hit
    • At the ditch

    Your hands follow your eyes. Ease off the gas, look at your escape path and steer gently toward it.

  5. What does it mean if the ABS light stays on after you start the engine?

    • The anti-lock system has a fault and needs serviceCorrect answer
    • The parking brake is on
    • You are low on fuel
    • The headlights are on

    Your brakes still work, but the anti-lock function may not. Leave extra following distance until it is repaired.

  6. Between midnight and 5 a.m., how many passengers may a Manitoba intermediate driver carry alone?

    • OneCorrect answer
    • None
    • Three
    • As many as there are seat belts

    Between midnight and 5 a.m. a Class 5I driver may carry only one passenger, unless a qualified supervising driver is in the front seat.

Worth remembering

  • Manitoba Public Insurance gives you thirty minutes for the knowledge test, and it covers three areas: traffic laws, safe driving practices and road sign recognition. The test is taken on a computer and it is not open book.
  • The learner stage lasts a minimum of nine months before you can move to the intermediate stage.
  • Your supervising driver must have held a full licence for at least thirty-six months, must be under .05 blood alcohol, and sits in the front passenger seat.
  • Because the supervisor takes the front seat, any other passengers ride in the rear — one for each working seatbelt.
