What the real Manitoba knowledge test looks like

Manitoba Public Insurance publishes less about its test than most provinces. What MPI does state is concrete: the test is taken on a computer, it is not open book, and there is a 30-minute limit. It covers traffic laws, safe driving practices and recognising road signs. Tests are available in English, French and more than twenty other languages.

MPI does not publish the number of questions or the pass mark. You will see 40 questions and 80 percent quoted confidently on other practice sites; none of them cites MPI, and they contradict each other on whether that 80 percent is per section or across the whole test. We are not going to guess at a number that decides whether someone books a test. Our mock test gives you 30 questions in 30 minutes, matching the one timing MPI does publish.

The rest of the process is well documented. You can start at 16, or at 15 and a half if you are enrolled in MPI's Driver Z programme at a high school. You need identification and must be registered as an MPI customer before booking, and you must meet the vision standards. The knowledge test costs $12. After passing you spend at least nine months at the learner stage and at least fifteen months at the intermediate stage before a full Class 5. Your supervising driver must have been fully licensed for 36 months. If you fail, learners wait seven days before trying again.

How to pass on your first try

Read the official guide once from start to finish, then use practice questions to find your weak spots. Most people lose marks on the signs, not the rules, because there are a lot of signs and many look similar. Study the signs in small groups rather than all at once, and pay attention to shape and colour: a red circle with a line through it always means you must not do something, a yellow diamond always warns you about what is coming, and an orange sign always means road work.

Take the mock test more than once. The questions are picked at random each time, so you will see a different test on every run. When you can pass three times in a row, you are ready to book the real one.

Road Signs

30 questions

  1. Where exactly must you stop at this sign?

    • At the stop line, or before the crosswalk if there is no lineCorrect answer
    • Anywhere in the intersection
    • Level with the sign post
    • Past the crosswalk

    Stop at the line first. If your view is blocked from there, creep forward slowly and stop a second time once you can see.

  2. What is the difference between this sign and a stop sign?

    • You must stop only if there is no safe gapCorrect answer
    • It means the same thing
    • You never have to slow down
    • It applies only to trucks

    A yield lets you keep rolling when the way is clear. A stop sign requires a full stop every time, clear road or not.

  3. What should you do when you see this sign?

    • Begin adjusting your speed now so you are at the new limit when you reach itCorrect answer
    • Ignore it until the next sign
    • Speed up
    • Brake hard immediately

    It warns that the limit changes just ahead. Adjusting early is smoother and safer than braking at the sign itself.

  4. Why is this sign placed well before the stop sign itself?

    • Because the stop sign is hidden by a hill or treesCorrect answer
    • To mark a parking area
    • To show a detour
    • To allow faster driving

    Start braking here so you arrive at the stop line already slow. It appears where the stop sign cannot be seen from far away.

  5. You are facing this sign at the end of a ramp. What has happened?

    • You are about to drive against traffic and must not enterCorrect answer
    • The ramp is closed for repairs
    • Trucks are banned
    • Parking is banned

    This is the sign drivers see when about to enter an off-ramp the wrong way. Stop, and find the correct entrance.

  6. What is banned where this sign stands?

    • Turning rightCorrect answer
    • Passing
    • Turning left
    • Parking

    Carry on to the next intersection instead. The ban usually exists because of a one-way street or a blind corner.

  7. When may you use a lane marked with this diamond?

    • When you carry at least the number of people shownCorrect answer
    • Only on weekends
    • Only if you drive a taxi
    • Any time you are late

    High occupancy vehicle lanes reward carpooling. Driving alone in one is a ticket, even when the lane is empty.

  8. How far back must you stop behind a Manitoba school bus with red lights flashing?

    • At least 5 metresCorrect answer
    • At least 1 metre
    • At least 20 metres
    • Any distance

    Leave at least five metres so children have room to walk around the bus. Wait until the lights stop and the bus moves off.

  9. What is the reduced speed limit in a Manitoba school zone during posted hours?

    • 30 km/hCorrect answer
    • 50 km/h
    • 40 km/h
    • 20 km/h

    Thirty in a marked school zone during the hours on the sign. Fines in these zones are increased.

  10. Why does this sign matter even when no children are visible?

    • A child can appear from behind a hedge or parked car at any momentCorrect answer
    • It only applies on weekends
    • It only applies in summer
    • It marks a parking lot

    Playground signs mark places where a child can run into the road with no warning, chasing a ball. Stay ready either way.

  11. How close to the nearest rail may you stop at a railway crossing?

    • No closer than 5 metresCorrect answer
    • 10 centimetres
    • There is no rule
    • Right at the rail

    Five metres minimum. Never start across until you can see the far side is clear, and remember one train can hide another.

  12. What does this X-shaped sign mark?

    • A railway crossingCorrect answer
    • A hospital
    • A crossroads
    • A construction zone

    The crossbuck marks the crossing itself. Slow, look and listen — many Manitoba crossings have no gates or lights.

  13. What is the main risk on the rural road this sign marks?

    • Wide, slow machines that may turn into a field with little warningCorrect answer
    • Machines that travel very fast
    • Machines that are banned from the road
    • Machines that always have right of way

    Tractors and combines are wide and slow and block your view ahead. Hold back until you can see far, and watch for a left turn.

  14. A vehicle ahead displays this orange triangle. What does it mean?

    • It travels at 40 km/h or lessCorrect answer
    • It is a student driver
    • It is broken down
    • It carries dangerous goods

    The slow-moving vehicle sign marks tractors, buggies and similar. Slow early — you close on them much faster than you expect.

  15. You hit the brakes for the animal on this sign and it runs off. What should you do next?

    • Stay alert — deer travel in groupsCorrect answer
    • Turn off your lights
    • Sound your horn continuously
    • Speed up to make up time

    Where there is one deer there are usually more. Keep your speed down for the next stretch and scan the ditches.

  16. What changes after this sign?

    • Traffic will come toward you in the next laneCorrect answer
    • The road becomes one way
    • A passing lane opens
    • The limit doubles

    The road stops being divided. Keep right and do not pass without a clear view of the whole road ahead.

  17. How should you drive on the section this sign warns about?

    • Smoothly — no sudden braking, steering or accelerationCorrect answer
    • Brake hard before every curve
    • Accelerate through it
    • Change lanes often

    Low grip punishes sudden inputs. Gentle steering and braking keep the tires working instead of sliding.

  18. Why slow down for the hazard on this sign?

    • A bump unloads the tires for a moment, reducing grip and steeringCorrect answer
    • It is only a suggestion
    • It damages your paint
    • It uses fuel

    Crossing a bump at speed lifts weight off the tires exactly when you may need them. Slow before it, not on it.

  19. What does this sign tell you about the road you are on?

    • It ends ahead and you must turn left or rightCorrect answer
    • It becomes one way
    • A signal is coming
    • It narrows

    Slow early. Arriving at a T intersection too fast is a common single-vehicle crash on rural roads.

  20. A row of these markers lines a bend. What do they tell you?

    • Which way the road curves, and how sharplyCorrect answer
    • Where to park
    • The distance to town
    • A cycling route

    Chevrons follow the outside of the curve. Closely spaced chevrons mean a sharper bend than widely spaced ones.

  21. When should you move over after seeing this sign?

    • Early, while a safe gap still existsCorrect answer
    • At the final cone
    • After the lane ends
    • Only if waved in

    Merging early and smoothly keeps traffic flowing. Racing to the front and squeezing in is what creates the jam.

  22. How do detour signs work?

    • They form a chain — follow each one to the nextCorrect answer
    • They are advisory only
    • They mark the end of a road
    • They show where to park

    Follow the marked route rather than inventing a shortcut, which often ends at another closed road.

  23. How do you treat a flashing red light in Manitoba?

    • Exactly like a stop signCorrect answer
    • Drive through without slowing
    • Wait for green
    • Exactly like a yield sign

    Full stop, then go when the way is clear. It will not turn green, so waiting for green would leave you there all day.

  24. A green arrow points left while the round light beside it is red. What may you do?

    • Turn left — oncoming traffic is being heldCorrect answer
    • Go straight
    • Turn left only after yielding
    • Nothing

    The advance green arrow protects your turn. The red still blocks going straight until it changes.

  25. What do broken white lines between lanes mean?

    • Traffic beside you goes the same way and you may change lanesCorrect answer
    • Traffic comes toward you
    • You may not change lanes
    • The road is one way

    White separates lanes going the same direction. Solid white asks you to stay put; broken white allows a lane change when safe.

  26. What does a broken yellow line beside your lane allow?

    • Passing when the road ahead is clearCorrect answer
    • Nothing
    • Parking
    • Driving in either direction

    Yellow separates opposite directions. Broken means you may pass when you can see far enough to finish safely.

  27. Which route does this maple-leaf marker identify?

    • The Trans-Canada HighwayCorrect answer
    • A toll road
    • A city ring road
    • A provincial park road

    The Trans-Canada crosses Manitoba from east to west and passes through Winnipeg on its way across the country.

  28. Who may park in a space marked with this sign?

    • Only a vehicle displaying a valid accessible parking permitCorrect answer
    • Anyone for a short stop
    • Anyone after 6 p.m.
    • Delivery vehicles

    Only a permit holder, at any hour. Parking there without one carries a heavy fine in every Manitoba municipality.

  29. Who most needs to read this sign?

    • Anyone driving a tall vehicle or carrying a roof loadCorrect answer
    • Motorcyclists only
    • Cyclists only
    • Nobody

    The number is the height of the opening ahead. If your vehicle or load is taller, you must take another route.

  30. What is ahead when you see this sign?

    • A bridge narrower than the road you are onCorrect answer
    • A truck-only bridge
    • A closed bridge
    • A toll bridge

    Slow down, stay well to your side of the centre line, and do not try to pass on the bridge.

Traffic Laws and Safe Driving

40 questions

  1. What is the minimum age for a Manitoba Class 5 Learner licence?

    • 16, or 15 and a half if enrolled in a high school driver education courseCorrect answer
    • 17
    • 15
    • 18

    Sixteen normally, or fifteen and a half while taking a high school driver education course.

  2. How long must a Manitoba learner stay in the learner stage?

    • At least nine monthsCorrect answer
    • Three months
    • Twelve months
    • There is no minimum

    A minimum of nine months, not counting any interruption to the licence, before moving to the intermediate stage.

  3. How much driving experience must a Manitoba learner's supervising driver have?

    • A full licence for at least 36 monthsCorrect answer
    • One year
    • Six months
    • No requirement

    At least thirty-six months fully licensed. They must also be under .05 blood alcohol and must not fail a drug screening test.

  4. Where must a Manitoba learner's supervising driver sit?

    • In the front passenger seatCorrect answer
    • In the back seat
    • Anywhere in the vehicle
    • Directly behind the driver

    Front passenger seat only, so they can see what you see and reach the controls if needed.

  5. Where may passengers sit in a Manitoba learner's vehicle?

    • In the rear, up to the number of working seatbeltsCorrect answer
    • Only in the front
    • No passengers at all
    • Anywhere, with no limit

    The front passenger seat belongs to the supervising driver. Other passengers ride in the rear, one per working seatbelt.

  6. How much alcohol or drugs may a Manitoba learner have in their system?

    • None at allCorrect answer
    • Under .05
    • Under .08
    • No rule applies to learners

    Zero for the whole graduated licensing programme. The supervising driver has a separate limit of under .05.

  7. Which of these may a Manitoba learner NOT do?

    • Tow another vehicleCorrect answer
    • Drive on a city street
    • Carry a rear-seat passenger
    • Drive in daylight

    Learners may not tow, may not drive a Class 3 farm truck, and may not operate off-road vehicles on a highway.

  8. How long do you have to complete the Manitoba knowledge test?

    • 30 minutesCorrect answer
    • No time limit
    • 15 minutes
    • 60 minutes

    Thirty minutes. The test is done on a computer, it is not open book, and phones and other devices are not allowed.

  9. Which three areas does the Manitoba knowledge test cover?

    • Traffic laws, safe driving practices and road sign recognitionCorrect answer
    • Geography, weather and maps
    • Only road signs
    • Mechanics, first aid and insurance

    Study all three in the Driver's Handbook. Sign recognition is the part most people underestimate.

  10. What must you carry whenever you drive in Manitoba?

    • Your licence, registration and proof of insuranceCorrect answer
    • Only your licence
    • Only insurance
    • Nothing

    All three. An officer may ask for each one at any stop, and driving without them can bring a fine.

  11. What is the default speed limit in a Manitoba city or town with no posted sign?

    • 50 km/hCorrect answer
    • 30 km/h
    • 60 km/h
    • 80 km/h

    Fifty inside a built-up area. On a provincial highway with no sign the default rises to 90 km/h.

  12. What is the two-second rule?

    • A minimum following distance measured in time, not metresCorrect answer
    • How long a turn should take
    • A rule about signalling
    • How long to wait at a stop sign

    Time works at any speed, unlike a fixed distance. Double it to four seconds or more in rain, snow or fog.

  13. How far ahead should you signal a turn in town?

    • At least 30 metresCorrect answer
    • After starting the turn
    • Signalling is optional
    • Right as you turn

    Signal early enough for drivers behind to react, then cancel it after the turn so nobody is misled.

  14. Which lane should you normally use on a Manitoba divided highway?

    • The right lane, keeping left for passingCorrect answer
    • The left lane
    • The middle
    • Any lane

    Keep right except to pass. Sitting in the left lane forces others to pass on your right, where their blind spot is worst.

  15. A school bus on an undivided Manitoba road stops with red lights flashing. Who must stop?

    • Traffic in both directionsCorrect answer
    • Only traffic behind the bus
    • Only traffic coming toward it
    • Nobody

    Both directions stop unless a median divides the road. Children may cross in front of the bus without looking.

  16. What must you do when passing a stopped emergency vehicle with flashing lights?

    • Slow down, and move over a lane where the road allowsCorrect answer
    • Stop completely
    • Keep your speed
    • Sound your horn

    Manitoba's slow-down-and-move-over rule protects roadside workers. Give them a full lane whenever a second lane exists.

  17. How should you pass a cyclist on a Manitoba road?

    • Leave plenty of room, changing lanes if the lane is narrowCorrect answer
    • Sound your horn to move them over
    • Drive alongside them
    • Pass close and quickly

    Treat a bike as a vehicle entitled to the lane. Wait for a clear stretch rather than squeezing past.

  18. Manitoba highways get ground blizzards. What is the safest response when visibility drops to nothing?

    • Slow down and, if you must stop, get well off the road with lights onCorrect answer
    • Stop in your lane
    • Speed up to get through
    • Follow the car ahead closely

    Stopping in a travel lane invites a rear-end collision from someone who cannot see either. Get right off, keep lights on and stay belted.

  19. Where does ice form first on a Manitoba road in autumn?

    • On bridges and overpassesCorrect answer
    • In the middle of a sunny street
    • Only in parking lots
    • Only on gravel

    Bridges lose heat from above and below, so they freeze while the rest of the road is only wet.

  20. Why do winter tires help even on a dry, very cold Manitoba day?

    • Their rubber stays soft below about 7 °C so they keep gripCorrect answer
    • They make the car quieter
    • They are wider
    • They only help in deep snow

    Grip comes from rubber that can flex. Below roughly 7 °C an all-season tire stiffens and its braking distance grows.

  21. Your car starts to skid on a Manitoba winter road. Where should you look?

    • Where you want the car to goCorrect answer
    • Straight down
    • At the vehicle you might hit
    • At the ditch

    Your hands follow your eyes. Ease off the gas, look at your escape path and steer gently toward it.

  22. Is holding a phone allowed while driving in Manitoba?

    • No — hand-held use is prohibited, hands-free is permittedCorrect answer
    • Yes, for calls only
    • Yes, at red lights
    • Yes, if you are stopped in traffic

    Hand-held use is prohibited whenever you are in control of the vehicle, including stopped at a light.

  23. Why can prescription medicine be a problem for driving?

    • Some medicines cause drowsiness or slow reactions, which is still impaired drivingCorrect answer
    • It cannot, if a doctor prescribed it
    • Only illegal drugs count
    • It only matters over 65

    Read the label and ask your pharmacist. Allergy pills, cold medicine and some painkillers can make you drowsy enough to be charged.

  24. A friend who has been drinking wants to drive home. What should you do?

    • Take the keys and arrange another rideCorrect answer
    • Give them coffee
    • Let them drive slowly
    • Follow behind them

    Only time clears alcohol. Arranging a ride is a small inconvenience next to a collision or a criminal record.

  25. Who is responsible for seatbelts for passengers under 18 in Manitoba?

    • The driverCorrect answer
    • The vehicle owner
    • Nobody
    • Each passenger

    The driver answers for younger passengers, including the correct child seat or booster for their age and size.

  26. Why should you carry a winter emergency kit in Manitoba?

    • Help can be far away and the cold is dangerousCorrect answer
    • It improves fuel economy
    • Insurance requires it
    • It reduces tire wear

    On a rural highway you may wait a long time. A blanket, warm clothes, a shovel, food, water and a charged phone are the basics.

  27. What does it mean if the ABS light stays on after you start the engine?

    • The anti-lock system has a fault and needs serviceCorrect answer
    • The parking brake is on
    • You are low on fuel
    • The headlights are on

    Your brakes still work, but the anti-lock function may not. Leave extra following distance until it is repaired.

  28. You are first at a collision scene in Manitoba. What comes first?

    • Make the scene safe and call 911Correct answer
    • Move the vehicles
    • Move the injured
    • Take photographs

    Protect the scene and call for help. Do not move an injured person unless there is immediate danger such as fire.

  29. How should you use anti-lock brakes in an emergency stop?

    • Press hard and hold, steering around the hazardCorrect answer
    • Press lightly
    • Use the parking brake
    • Pump the pedal

    ABS pumps for you. The vibration under your foot is normal — keep firm pressure and steer, because ABS lets you brake and steer at once.

  30. Your vehicle breaks down on a Manitoba highway in winter. What is usually safest?

    • Stay in the vehicle with hazards on unless help is very closeCorrect answer
    • Walk to the nearest town
    • Stand beside the road
    • Leave the car in a driving lane

    Your car is shelter and it is visible. Walking in a Manitoba winter is how people get into serious trouble.

  31. What are the three stages of Manitoba's graduated licensing programme?

    • Class 5L, Class 5I and Class 5FCorrect answer
    • L, N and full
    • G1, G2 and G
    • Learner, probationary and regular

    Manitoba uses Class 5L (learner), Class 5I (intermediate) and Class 5F (full). Ontario uses G1, G2 and G; British Columbia uses L and N.

  32. How long must a Manitoba driver stay at zero alcohol?

    • Through both new stages and the first 36 months of the full licenceCorrect answer
    • Only while a learner
    • Until the intermediate stage ends
    • Until age 21

    Manitoba is unusual: zero alcohol does not stop when you get your full Class 5F licence. It continues for the first 36 months of that stage, roughly five years from the day you start.

  33. How long must a Manitoba learner's supervising driver have held a full licence?

    • 3 yearsCorrect answer
    • 6 months
    • 1 year
    • 10 years

    The supervising driver must have held a full Class 5F for at least three years, must be the only front-seat passenger, and must be under .05.

  34. Between midnight and 5 a.m., how many passengers may a Manitoba intermediate driver carry alone?

    • OneCorrect answer
    • None
    • Three
    • As many as there are seat belts

    Between midnight and 5 a.m. a Class 5I driver may carry only one passenger, unless a qualified supervising driver is in the front seat.

  35. What does Manitoba use instead of demerit points?

    • The Driver Safety Rating scaleCorrect answer
    • A warning letter system
    • A points bank
    • Nothing at all

    Manitoba runs a Driver Safety Rating scale from +20 down to −20. Good driving moves you up and cuts your premium; convictions move you down.

  36. What happens to a Manitoba driver caught using a hand-held phone?

    • A $672 fine and a 3-day licence suspensionCorrect answer
    • A warning
    • A $200 fine only
    • 3 demerit points only

    A first offence is $672 and an immediate three-day suspension, rising to seven days next time, plus a drop of five levels on the Driver Safety Rating.

  37. What is the default speed limit on a Manitoba rural highway with no sign?

    • 90 km/hCorrect answer
    • 80 km/h
    • 100 km/h
    • 110 km/h

    Manitoba's rural default is 90 km/h, and 50 km/h in a city or town. Saskatchewan's rural default next door is 80 km/h.

  38. When are studded tires illegal in Manitoba?

    • Between 30 April and 1 OctoberCorrect answer
    • All year
    • Only in Winnipeg
    • They are never illegal

    Studded tires may not be used between 30 April and 1 October. Winter tires themselves are not required by law in Manitoba.

  39. When may a child stop using a booster seat in Manitoba?

    • At 145 cm, 80 lb or age 9 — whichever comes firstCorrect answer
    • At age 5
    • At 18 kg
    • At age 12

    A child may leave the booster on reaching 145 cm, or 80 lb, or age 9 — whichever happens first.

  40. What is the speed limit in a Manitoba school zone where the regular limit is under 80 km/h?

    • 30 km/hCorrect answer
    • 40 km/h
    • 50 km/h
    • 60 km/h

    Where the regular limit is under 80 km/h a school zone is 30 km/h. Where it is 80 or more the school zone is 50 km/h. The hours are set by each municipality, so read the sign.
