The numbers worth memorising

Keep at least two seconds of space behind the car ahead on a dry road, and more when it is wet or icy. Leave one metre when you pass a cyclist. Slow down and move over for a stopped emergency vehicle. New drivers in the learner and novice stages must have zero alcohol in their blood. Speed limits are 50 km/h in a city or town and 80 km/h outside one unless a sign says otherwise, and school and playground zones drop to 30 km/h during their posted hours.

About this quiz

This page covers the rules half of the British Columbia Class 7L test, arranged by topic instead of arriving in one pile. Eight chapters run from the graduated licensing programme through to what to do after a collision, and you open any of them from the row of buttons at the top. Nothing here is timed.

Inside a chapter, every question sits in a panel you can open. Opening one reveals the correct answer with a short explanation, so you can read your way through a chapter before you test yourself on it. Below the list, an untimed practice run puts the same questions to you one at a time.

The questions are ours, written from the free ICBC guide Learn to Drive Smart. We are not affiliated with ICBC or the Government of British Columbia, so treat this as practice and keep the official guide beside you. It is free to read and it is what the real test is drawn from.

What's inside

  • The first four chapters cover licensing and the graduated programme, safe driving, sharing the road and driving conditions.
  • The last four chapters cover keeping your licence, impaired driving, looking after your vehicle, and emergencies and collisions.
  • Buttons along the top move you between chapters, and the page opens on the licensing chapter.
  • Each question can be opened on the spot to show the correct answer and the reasoning behind it.
  • A practice run under each chapter asks those same questions again, one by one, and marks you as you go.
  • A closing section gathers the following distances, passing gaps and speed limits that are worth learning by heart.

Good to know

  • Two seconds of space behind the vehicle ahead is the minimum on a dry road, and you want more than that when it is wet or icy.
  • Leave a metre of room when you pass someone riding a bicycle.
  • Slow down and move over when an emergency vehicle is stopped at the roadside with its lights flashing.
  • Unless a sign says otherwise, the limit is fifty kilometres an hour inside a city or town and eighty outside one.
  • School zones and playground zones drop to thirty kilometres an hour during the hours posted on their signs.

Common questions

How many rules questions are on this page?

Sixty, spread across the eight chapters, and every one of them was written by us with an explanation attached. They are the same rules questions the mock test draws from.

Can I study one chapter at a time?

Yes, and that is exactly what the page is built for. Choose a chapter at the top, read through its questions, practise it, then come back another day for the next one.

Is there a timer on the rules study page?

No. Study here is deliberately untimed so you can read slowly and think. The forty-five-minute clock belongs to the mock test, where the pressure is part of the point.

Will I be told why an answer is right?

Every question carries a short explanation in plain language, both when you open it to read and when you practise. Understanding the reason serves you far better on test day than memorising a letter.

Is passing here enough to get my learner licence?

No. You still have to sit the real knowledge test at an ICBC driver licensing office, along with a vision screening and your identification. This site is unofficial practice with no connection to ICBC or the Government of British Columbia.
