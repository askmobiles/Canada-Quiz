The numbers worth memorising

Keep at least two seconds of space behind the car ahead on a dry road, and more when it is wet or icy. Leave one metre when you pass a cyclist. Slow down and move over for a stopped emergency vehicle. New drivers in the learner and novice stages must have zero alcohol in their blood. Speed limits are 50 km/h in a city or town and 80 km/h outside one unless a sign says otherwise, and school and playground zones drop to 30 km/h during their posted hours.

    • One second

    • Two seconds
    • Three secondsCorrect answer

    Two seconds is the least you should ever leave, and at highway speed three seconds gives you room to react. Make it four in poor conditions.

  23. The vehicle in the lane beside you has stopped at a marked crosswalk. What should you do?

    A stopped vehicle at a crosswalk usually means a pedestrian is crossing where you cannot see them, so stop too instead of passing.

  24. You are approaching a railway crossing as a train comes into view. Why should you never try to beat it?

    • The crossing gates always close too slowly
    • Trains often reverse without warning
    • A train needs about two kilometres to stopCorrect answer

    A train cannot stop for you, since it may need around two kilometres to come to a halt. Trains always have the right of way.

    In British Columbia the winter tire and chain rules on designated highways begin on October 1 and run into the spring, so get ready early. ❄️

  57. Your vehicles are only lightly damaged, nobody is hurt, and you are blocking a lane on a busy road. What is usually best?

    If a small crash leaves the vehicles driveable, moving them clear of the lanes keeps traffic flowing and helps prevent another collision.

  58. Your vehicle starts to skid. What is the right way to steer?

    Ease off the gas and steer smoothly where you want to go, and be ready to correct again, because a vehicle often swings the other way.

  59. You hit a puddle at speed and your tires lose contact with the road. What should you do?

    • Steer quickly to one side to find some grip
    • Ease off the accelerator and hold the wheel straightCorrect answer
    • Speed up to push through the water

    That is hydroplaning. Braking or turning while you are floating makes it worse, so lift off the gas gently and let the tires find the road again.

  60. You hit a deer on a rural British Columbia highway. What should you do first?

    • Nothing, cyclists must avoid doors
    • Sound your horn first

    A cyclist hit by an opening door can be thrown into moving traffic. Some drivers use the far hand to open, which turns your body and forces you to look.

  76. You are driving a long descent in the BC mountains. How should you control your speed?

    • Use the parking brake
    • Shift to a lower gear and let the engine hold you backCorrect answer
    • Ride the brakes the whole way
    • Coast in neutral

    Constant braking heats the brakes until they fade and stop working. A lower gear does the work for you and keeps the brakes fresh for when you need them.

  77. Coastal BC gets heavy rain. When should you turn your headlights on?

    • Only in fog
    • Only on the highway
    • Whenever you use your wipersCorrect answer
    • Only after dark

    Lights make you visible to others, which matters more than how well you can see. Daytime running lights do not light your tail lights, so use full headlights in rain.

  78. What does a flashing green light at a BC intersection mean?

    • Turn left before oncoming traffic
    • Stop and proceed when clear
    • The light is broken
    • The light is pedestrian-controlled — be ready for it to changeCorrect answer

    This differs from Ontario. In BC a flashing green means the signal is controlled by a pedestrian push button, so it can change quickly when someone presses it.

  79. How long must you normally hold a BC Class 7L licence before the first road test?

    • 12 monthsCorrect answer
    • 3 months
    • 6 months
    • 24 months

    Twelve months in the learner stage. Passing the first road test moves you to Class 7N, where you display a green N.

  80. What does the green N sign on a BC vehicle tell other drivers?

    • The vehicle is new
    • The driver is in the novice stage of graduated licensingCorrect answer
    • The vehicle runs on electricity
    • The driver is a student

    N stands for novice. That driver has passed one road test but still has restrictions, including zero alcohol and a passenger limit.

  81. What is an immediate roadside prohibition in BC?

    • A warning letter
    • A vehicle inspection order
    • A licence suspension issued on the spot for impaired drivingCorrect answer
    • A parking ticket

    BC police can suspend your licence and impound your vehicle at the roadside. It applies at a blood alcohol level well below the criminal limit.

  82. You are tired on a long BC highway drive. What actually helps?

    • Opening the window
    • Turning up the radio
    • Drinking coffee and continuing
    • Stopping to rest or sleepCorrect answer

    Only rest fixes fatigue. Air and music mask the symptoms for a few minutes while your reaction time stays as slow as an impaired driver's.

  83. What must you do before driving away with snow on your BC vehicle?

    • Clear all windows, lights, mirrors and the roofCorrect answer
    • Clear the windshield only
    • Nothing, it blows off
    • Clear the front windows only

    Snow left on the roof slides onto your windshield the first time you brake, or flies into the car behind you at speed.

  84. How can you tell whether a tire has enough tread left?

    • Count the years since purchase
    • Check the wear bars moulded into the groovesCorrect answer
    • Look at the colour
    • Feel the temperature

    Every tire has raised wear bars between the grooves. When the tread wears down level with them, the tire is finished and must be replaced.

  85. You are in a collision in BC and someone is injured. What must you do?

    • Move the cars and continue
    • Call your insurer first
    • Stop, call for help, and give your informationCorrect answer
    • Exchange numbers and leave

    Stopping is a legal duty. Call 911 for any injury, then give your name, address, licence and insurance details to the others involved.

  86. Your vehicle skids on a wet BC road. What should you do with the accelerator?

    • Press it harder
    • Nothing, keep it steady
    • Pump it
    • Ease off it smoothlyCorrect answer

    Easing off returns grip to the tires. Braking or accelerating hard during a skid makes it worse — smooth inputs and eyes on your escape path are what recover the car.

  87. A BC transit bus signals to pull out from a stop. What must you do?

    • Yield to it where the sign requiresCorrect answer
    • Speed up to pass
    • Sound your horn
    • Stop completely

    On roads posted at 60 km/h or less, you must yield to a bus signalling to re-enter traffic. Slow down and let it in.

  88. What is the correct way to enter a BC highway from an on-ramp?

    • Use the shoulder until there is room
    • Match the speed of traffic, then merge into a gapCorrect answer
    • Stop at the end of the ramp
    • Enter slowly and let others adjust

    Use the acceleration lane to reach highway speed, check your blind spot, signal and merge. Stopping on a ramp is dangerous because traffic behind expects you to be moving.

  89. Which lane should you normally use on a BC multi-lane highway?

    • The middle lane
    • Any lane you like
    • The right lane, keeping left for passingCorrect answer
    • The left lane

    Keep right except to pass. Sitting in the left lane forces others to pass on your right, which is where their blind spot is worst.

  90. May a BC learner drive alone to a nearby store?

    • Yes, for short trips
    • Yes, during daylight
    • Yes, if under 5 km

    There is no exception for short or local trips. Driving without your supervisor is driving without a valid licence for that vehicle.

About this quiz

This page covers the rules half of the British Columbia Class 7L test, arranged by topic instead of arriving in one pile. Eight chapters run from the graduated licensing programme through to what to do after a collision, and you open any of them from the row of buttons at the top. Nothing here is timed.

Inside a chapter, every question sits in a panel you can open. Opening one reveals the correct answer with a short explanation, so you can read your way through a chapter before you test yourself on it. Below the list, an untimed practice run puts the same questions to you one at a time.

The questions are ours, written from the free ICBC guide Learn to Drive Smart. We are not affiliated with ICBC or the Government of British Columbia, so treat this as practice and keep the official guide beside you. It is free to read and it is what the real test is drawn from.

What's inside

  • The first four chapters cover licensing and the graduated programme, safe driving, sharing the road and driving conditions.
  • The last four chapters cover keeping your licence, impaired driving, looking after your vehicle, and emergencies and collisions.
  • Buttons along the top move you between chapters, and the page opens on the licensing chapter.
  • Each question can be opened on the spot to show the correct answer and the reasoning behind it.
  • A practice run under each chapter asks those same questions again, one by one, and marks you as you go.
  • A closing section gathers the following distances, passing gaps and speed limits that are worth learning by heart.

Good to know

  • Two seconds of space behind the vehicle ahead is the minimum on a dry road, and you want more than that when it is wet or icy.
  • Leave a metre of room when you pass someone riding a bicycle.
  • Slow down and move over when an emergency vehicle is stopped at the roadside with its lights flashing.
  • Unless a sign says otherwise, the limit is fifty kilometres an hour inside a city or town and eighty outside one.
  • School zones and playground zones drop to thirty kilometres an hour during the hours posted on their signs.

Common questions

How many rules questions are on this page?

Sixty, spread across the eight chapters, and every one of them was written by us with an explanation attached. They are the same rules questions the mock test draws from.

Can I study one chapter at a time?

Yes, and that is exactly what the page is built for. Choose a chapter at the top, read through its questions, practise it, then come back another day for the next one.

Is there a timer on the rules study page?

No. Study here is deliberately untimed so you can read slowly and think. The forty-five-minute clock belongs to the mock test, where the pressure is part of the point.

Will I be told why an answer is right?

Every question carries a short explanation in plain language, both when you open it to read and when you practise. Understanding the reason serves you far better on test day than memorising a letter.

Is passing here enough to get my learner licence?

No. You still have to sit the real knowledge test at an ICBC driver licensing office, along with a vision screening and your identification. This site is unofficial practice with no connection to ICBC or the Government of British Columbia.
