🗣️ Canada's Two Official Languages: English and French

Why does Canada have two official languages? A simple guide to English and French in Canada — where they are spoken and why it matters.

One of the special things about Canada is that it has two official languages: English and French. This means the government provides services in both languages across the country. Here is a simple explanation of why.

Why two languages?

Canada's two official languages come from its history. Both French and British settlers helped build the early country hundreds of years ago. To respect both groups, Canada made both English and French official. This was made law with the Official Languages Act.

Where is French spoken most?

French is the main language in the province of Quebec, where most people speak it every day. There are also large French-speaking communities in New Brunswick — Canada's only officially bilingual province — and in parts of Ontario, Manitoba, and other provinces.

What does bilingual mean for Canadians?

  • Federal government services are offered in both English and French.
  • Product labels in stores show both languages.
  • Children can often learn the second language at school through French immersion or English classes.
  • Both languages appear on Canadian money and official documents.
