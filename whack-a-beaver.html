🦫 Whack-a-Beaver

Tap the beavers to score. Grab the 🍁 for bonus — but don't tap the skunk!

📖 How to play

Beavers pop out of the holes — tap them fast to score points before they duck back down! A golden maple leaf 🍁 is worth 3 bonus points. But watch out: if you tap the skunk 🦨, you lose points. You have 45 seconds — get the highest score you can. It gets faster as time goes on!

The beaver 🦫 is one of Canada's national animals — you'll find it on the Canadian nickel (5-cent coin)!

About this game

Whack-a-Beaver is a quick tapping game with a Canadian twist. Beavers pop up out of nine holes and duck back down again. Your job is to tap them before they vanish, and to keep an eye out for the two surprises hiding in the grid.

A golden maple leaf is worth three points instead of one, so grabbing it changes everything. A skunk costs you two points, which makes the last frantic seconds genuinely risky. A round lasts forty-five seconds and speeds up as it goes.

It makes a good five-minute break for children and adults alike, and a fair way to settle a family argument about who has the quickest fingers. Your best score of the visit stays on screen so you have something to chase.

How it works

  • A grid of nine holes fills the screen, and one critter at a time pops up from a random empty hole.
  • Tapping a beaver scores one point, a golden maple leaf scores three, and hitting the skunk takes two away.
  • Your score can never drop below zero, so a run of skunk mistakes will not ruin the whole round.
  • The timer starts at forty-five seconds, and the critters appear more and more often as it counts down.
  • Three pills at the top track your current score, the seconds remaining, and the best score of this visit.
  • It responds to a finger on a touchscreen or a mouse click on a computer, with no setting to change.

Tips for playing

  • Keep your hand hovering over the middle of the grid. From the centre your finger is never more than one hole away from any pop-up.
  • Watch the whole grid instead of staring at one hole. Side vision spots movement faster than a deliberate scan does.
  • The maple leaf shows up less often than the beaver, so treat it as a welcome bonus rather than something to wait around for.
  • When the pace picks up near the end, slow down a little. One careless skunk tap wipes out two hard-earned beavers.
  • The beaver has long been a Canadian emblem, and you can find one on the five-cent coin that everyone calls the nickel.

Common questions

How long does one round of Whack-a-Beaver last?

Forty-five seconds from the moment you press Start. That is short enough for a real break and short enough that most players immediately want another go.

Is Whack-a-Beaver suitable for young children?

Yes. There is nothing to read beyond the score, no frightening content, and the characters are simple animal emoji. Little ones often play happily on a tablet.

Why did my score go down?

You tapped the skunk. It costs two points, and it looks close enough to a beaver that everybody gets caught by it at least once in a fast round.

Is my best score kept for next time?

It stays on screen while the page remains open, but it clears when you reload or close the tab. Nothing about you is stored anywhere.

Can two people play at the same time?

Not in the same round, but taking turns works well. Play forty-five seconds each and compare the numbers, since the page keeps showing the highest of the session.
