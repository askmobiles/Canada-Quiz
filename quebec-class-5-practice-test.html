What the real SAAQ knowledge test looks like

The SAAQ knowledge test is multiple choice and has three sections: the Highway Safety Code, road signs and pavement markings and traffic signals, and the behaviours and techniques of driving a passenger vehicle. You must score 75 percent in each section — a strong result in one part does not make up for a weak result in another. SAAQ says the test takes between 30 and 60 minutes.

SAAQ does not publish how many questions are on the test. Other practice sites will tell you 40, or 64 — they do not agree with each other and neither cites a source, so we will not repeat a number we cannot stand behind. Our mock test uses 24 questions in two sections with the real 75 percent pass mark applied to each, so the standard you practise against is the standard SAAQ actually publishes.

Two things surprise newcomers to Quebec. First, a driving course at a recognised school is mandatory — 24 hours of theory and 15 hours behind the wheel, spread over at least 168 days, and you pass a test at the school before you even get the learner's licence. Second, you must hold that learner's licence for 10 months before you may sit the SAAQ knowledge test, and 12 months before the road test. If you fail, you wait 28 days — but you only retake the sections you failed.

How to pass on your first try

Read the official guide once from start to finish, then use practice questions to find your weak spots. Most people lose marks on the signs, not the rules, because there are a lot of signs and many look similar. Study the signs in small groups rather than all at once, and pay attention to shape and colour: a red circle with a line through it always means you must not do something, a yellow diamond always warns you about what is coming, and an orange sign always means road work.

Take the mock test more than once. The questions are picked at random each time, so you will see a different test on every run. When you can pass three times in a row, you are ready to book the real one.

Road Signs and Signals

30 questions

  1. In Quebec this sign reads ARRÊT. What must you do at it?

    • Come to a complete stop every time, then go when it is safeCorrect answer
    • Stop only in daylight
    • Yield to the right only
    • Slow and continue if clear

    Quebec uses the French word ARRÊT on its octagonal stop signs. The rule is the same everywhere: a full stop, at the line, every time.

  2. What does this triangular sign require of you?

    • Stop completely every time
    • Let traffic already on the road go firstCorrect answer
    • Speed up to merge
    • Turn right only

    Slow down, look, and enter only when there is a safe gap. Stop if there is no gap — yielding does not mean forcing your way in.

  3. What does this sign tell you?

    • A recommended speed in good weather
    • The minimum speed allowed
    • The maximum speed allowed hereCorrect answer
    • The distance to the next town

    It is a legal maximum, not a target. In rain, snow or heavy traffic you must drive slower than the number shown.

  4. You are facing this sign. What does it mean?

    • Stop and wait for a signal
    • Parking is banned
    • Trucks only
    • Do not enter — traffic comes the other wayCorrect answer

    Entering here would put you head-on into traffic. Find another route rather than reversing into the intersection you just left.

  5. What does this arrow sign tell you about the street?

    • Traffic flows in one direction onlyCorrect answer
    • Parking is on one side only
    • The street is closed
    • Cyclists only

    Follow the arrow. If it points against you, the street is one way and you must not enter.

  6. What is forbidden where this sign stands?

    • Parking
    • Turning leftCorrect answer
    • Going straight
    • Stopping

    A red ring with a slash bans what is drawn inside it. Continue and turn where it is permitted instead.

  7. What manoeuvre does this sign prohibit?

    • Passing
    • Reversing
    • A U-turnCorrect answer
    • A three-point turn in a driveway

    U-turns are also banned wherever you cannot be seen from far enough away — on curves, near the crest of a hill and across a median.

  8. What does this sign allow you to still do?

    • Park overnight
    • Park on weekends
    • Leave the car and go shopping
    • Stop briefly to drop someone offCorrect answer

    No parking still permits a brief stop with the driver present, for example to let a passenger out. No stopping would forbid even that.

  9. How is this sign stricter than a no-parking sign?

    • You may not halt at all, even brieflyCorrect answer
    • It applies only to trucks
    • It bans left turns as well
    • It applies only at night

    No stopping means exactly that. You may only stop to obey a police officer, a traffic signal or to avoid a collision.

  10. Why is passing banned where this sign stands?

    • The road is newly paved
    • You cannot see far enough ahead to pass safelyCorrect answer
    • Trucks use the road
    • There is a school nearby

    No-passing zones sit where a hill or curve shortens your view. A car can appear in the seconds you would spend on the wrong side.

  11. An island splits the road and this sign faces you. Which side do you take?

    • Stop and wait
    • Either side
    • The side the arrow points toCorrect answer
    • Always the left

    Follow the arrow. Going the wrong way around the island puts you into oncoming traffic.

  12. Who may not use the road past this sign?

    • Motorcycles
    • Cyclists
    • Buses
    • TrucksCorrect answer

    Trucks must take another route, while cars and other small vehicles may still use this road.

  13. A person is crossing at this sign. When may you drive on?

    • Once they are all the way across and back on the sidewalkCorrect answer
    • Immediately, if you steer around
    • After counting to three
    • As soon as they leave your lane

    Wait until the person has finished crossing. A driver in another lane may not see them, so nobody moves until the crossing is complete.

  14. A Quebec school bus has its red lights flashing and this arm out. What must you do?

    • Pass slowly on the left
    • Stop, whether behind the bus or coming toward itCorrect answer
    • Stop only if behind it
    • Sound your horn

    Traffic in both directions stops, unless a median separates the road. Failing to stop for a school bus is among the heaviest fines in Quebec.

  15. What should you watch for most in the area this sign marks?

    • Falling rock
    • Trucks reversing
    • Children stepping out from between parked carsCorrect answer
    • Farm machinery

    Children do not check before they step out. Cover the brake, keep your eyes moving and expect someone you have not seen yet.

  16. What is this yellow diamond warning you about?

    • A parking entrance
    • A hospital
    • A bus terminal
    • People crossing the road aheadCorrect answer

    Yellow diamonds warn of what is coming. Ease off the gas and scan both shoulders as you approach.

  17. What does this sign warn you about?

    • The road may be slippery when wetCorrect answer
    • A gravel road begins
    • A bridge is ahead
    • Snow removal in progress

    Slow before the slippery section, not on it. Braking hard on a low-grip surface is what starts a skid.

  18. How should you take the curve this sign announces?

    • Brake in the middle of it
    • Slow before it, then hold a steady speed throughCorrect answer
    • Accelerate into it
    • Change lanes inside it

    Set your speed before you turn the wheel. Braking mid-curve shifts weight off the rear tires just when you need their grip.

  19. You see this sign before a long descent. What is the right technique?

    • Coast in neutral
    • Turn off the engine
    • Shift to a lower gear and let the engine slow youCorrect answer
    • Ride the brakes all the way

    Constant braking heats the brakes until they fade. A lower gear does the work and keeps the brakes ready for when you truly need them.

  20. This sign warns of merging traffic. What is the courteous, safe response?

    • Drive in their blind spot
    • Stop on the main road
    • Speed up so nobody gets in
    • Adjust your speed or move over to leave a gapCorrect answer

    Merging works when both drivers help. Move over a lane where safe, or ease off slightly so the joining vehicle can blend in at speed.

  21. What must you never do at the crossing this sign announces?

    • Start across before you can see the far side is clearCorrect answer
    • Look both ways
    • Open your window to listen
    • Slow down

    Never enter a crossing you cannot clear. One train can also hide another, so check every track before you move.

  22. Who has the right of way in the roundabout this sign announces?

    • Whoever arrives first
    • Traffic already circulating insideCorrect answer
    • The largest vehicle
    • Traffic entering from the right

    Yield to traffic already going around, look left for a gap, travel counter-clockwise and signal right just before your exit.

  23. When is the animal on this sign most likely to be on the road?

    • Only during rain
    • At midday
    • At dawn and duskCorrect answer
    • Only in July

    Deer move most at first and last light, exactly when your eyes are adjusting. Scan the ditches, and expect more than one.

  24. What changes in the orange zone this sign marks?

    • Parking becomes free
    • The road becomes one way
    • Nothing changes
    • Speeding fines can be doubled when workers are presentCorrect answer

    Slow to the posted work-zone limit as soon as you see orange, and watch for people working close to your lane.

  25. A traffic control person signals you to stop while the light is green. Who do you obey?

    • The traffic control personCorrect answer
    • Whichever is closer
    • Neither
    • The green light

    A person directing traffic overrides signs and signals. Ignoring them in a work zone puts workers in danger and is an offence.

  26. The light turns amber as you approach. What is correct?

    • Always accelerate through
    • Stop if you can do so safely; continue only if stopping would be dangerousCorrect answer
    • Always brake hard
    • Sound your horn

    Amber means the red is coming. If you are so close that hard braking would cause a rear-end collision, carry on and clear the intersection.

  27. Where in Quebec is a right turn on a red light forbidden?

    • Only at night
    • Nowhere, it is always allowed
    • On the island of Montreal, and wherever a sign forbids itCorrect answer
    • Only on highways

    Right on red is allowed in most of Quebec after a complete stop, but not on the island of Montreal, and never where a sign prohibits it.

  28. A double solid yellow line runs down the middle of the road. What does it stop you doing?

    • Stopping at the curb
    • Signalling
    • Turning left into a driveway
    • Crossing it to pass another vehicleCorrect answer

    You may not cross it to pass. You may still cross it to turn left into a driveway or side road when it is safe.

  29. Someone has stepped onto these painted lines. What must you do?

    • Yield and waitCorrect answer
    • Drive around behind them
    • Flash your lights
    • Reverse

    Painted lines mark where people cross. Once someone is on them, you wait — turning across in front of them is dangerous and an offence.

  30. What kind of sign is this blue one?

    • A warning of danger
    • An information sign showing a service nearbyCorrect answer
    • An order you must obey
    • A construction sign

    Blue signs point to services — hospital, fuel, food, accessible parking. They inform rather than command.

Highway Safety Code and Driving

30 questions

  1. How much alcohol may a Quebec learner's licence holder have in their blood?

    • Under .08
    • No limit applies
    • ZeroCorrect answer
    • Under .05

    The zero-alcohol rule applies for the whole learning period, and to every driver aged 21 and under regardless of licence.

  2. Who may accompany a Quebec learner driver?

    • A parent only
    • Anyone over 18
    • Anyone with a licence
    • A holder of a valid Quebec Class 5 licence with at least two years of experienceCorrect answer

    The accompanying driver needs a valid Class 5 and at least two years of driving experience, and must be able to help you at any moment.

  3. Between which hours may a Quebec learner not drive?

    • Midnight and 5 a.m.Correct answer
    • 10 p.m. and 6 a.m.
    • 1 a.m. and 4 a.m.
    • There is no restriction

    Learners must be off the road between midnight and 5 a.m., the hours when fatigue, poor visibility and impaired drivers combine.

  4. What is the minimum age to hold a learner's licence in Quebec?

    • 18
    • 16Correct answer
    • 15
    • 17

    Sixteen. If you are under 18 you also need the written consent of a parent or guardian.

  5. How long must you hold a Quebec learner's licence before the road test?

    • 6 months
    • 24 months
    • At least 12 monthsCorrect answer
    • 3 months

    At least twelve months. The learner's licence itself is valid for eighteen months.

  6. What demerit point bracket applies to a Quebec learner driver?

    • Fifteen points
    • Eight points
    • There is no limit
    • Four pointsCorrect answer

    A learner is in the four-point bracket, far tighter than an experienced driver. Points accumulate quickly on a new record.

  7. What must you carry with you whenever you drive in Quebec?

    • Your licence, the registration certificate and proof of insuranceCorrect answer
    • Only your licence
    • Only the registration
    • Nothing, records are electronic

    All three. A police officer may ask for each one, and driving without them can bring a fine even if you own the car.

  8. What is the default speed limit on a Quebec city street with no posted sign?

    • 80 km/h
    • 50 km/hCorrect answer
    • 30 km/h
    • 60 km/h

    Fifty in a built-up area. On a rural road with no sign it is 90 km/h, and most autoroutes are posted at 100.

  9. What is the standard following distance in good conditions?

    • Half a second
    • Ten metres at any speed
    • Two secondsCorrect answer
    • One car length

    Pick a fixed object and count. Reaching it less than two seconds after the car ahead means you are too close — and double that in rain or snow.

  10. What must you check that your mirrors cannot show you before changing lanes?

    • The fuel gauge
    • The radio
    • The odometer
    • The blind spot, by looking over your shoulderCorrect answer

    Mirrors leave a gap beside and behind you large enough to hide a car or a motorcycle. Only a shoulder check reveals it.

  11. You want to turn left at a green light with oncoming traffic. Where do you wait?

    • In the intersection with your wheels straightCorrect answer
    • In the intersection with your wheels turned left
    • Behind the stop line
    • On the shoulder

    Wheels straight. If you are struck from behind with the wheels turned, your car is pushed into oncoming traffic instead of straight ahead.

  12. Two vehicles reach an all-way stop at exactly the same moment, side by side. Who goes first?

    • Whoever moves first
    • The vehicle on the rightCorrect answer
    • The vehicle on the left
    • The larger vehicle

    The driver on the right. Make eye contact and be ready to yield even when the rule is on your side.

  13. How much room must you leave when passing a cyclist on a Quebec road posted over 50 km/h?

    • One metre
    • No set distance
    • At least 1.5 metresCorrect answer
    • Half a metre

    A metre and a half where the limit is over 50 km/h, and one metre where it is 50 or under. If the lane is too narrow, change lanes or wait.

  14. An emergency vehicle approaches with lights and siren. What should you do?

    • Speed up to clear the road
    • Stop in the middle of the lane
    • Follow closely behind it
    • Pull as far right as you safely can and stopCorrect answer

    Signal, move right and stop clear of the intersection. Never follow closely — a second emergency vehicle may be right behind.

  15. Why should you avoid sitting beside a large truck's rear wheels?

    • It is the driver's worst blind spotCorrect answer
    • It uses more fuel
    • It is illegal everywhere
    • The noise is loud

    If you cannot see the driver's face in their mirror, they cannot see you. Pass decisively or hold well back.

  16. What must you do before opening your door on a Montreal street?

    • Sound your horn
    • Check your mirror and look over your shoulder for cyclistsCorrect answer
    • Open slowly, that is enough
    • Nothing, cyclists must avoid doors

    A cyclist struck by an opening door can be thrown into traffic. Checking first takes a second and prevents a serious injury.

  17. When are winter tires mandatory on passenger vehicles registered in Quebec?

    • From 1 November to 30 April
    • Only north of Quebec City
    • From 1 December to 15 MarchCorrect answer
    • All year

    Quebec is the only province with a mandatory winter tire law for passenger vehicles. Driving without them in that window brings a fine.

  18. When is a wet Quebec road usually most slippery?

    • After hours of rain
    • Only when freezing
    • Only on gravel
    • In the first minutes of rainCorrect answer

    Rain lifts oil and rubber off the pavement first, leaving a greasy film. Slow down as soon as the first drops fall.

  19. Where does black ice usually form first on a Quebec road?

    • On bridges, overpasses and shaded spotsCorrect answer
    • In the middle of a sunny street
    • Only in parking lots
    • Only on gravel

    Bridges lose heat from above and below, so they freeze before the road does. Shaded curves are the other classic trap.

  20. Which lights should you use in heavy fog?

    • Hazard lights while moving
    • Low beamsCorrect answer
    • High beams
    • No lights

    High beams reflect off the fog and blind you. Low beams sit under the fog layer and light the road ahead.

  21. Is a hands-free phone allowed while driving in Quebec?

    • Yes, holding is fine at red lights
    • Only for calls under one minute
    • Yes, but holding any device is prohibitedCorrect answer
    • No device of any kind is allowed

    Holding a phone or any portable screen while driving is prohibited, even stopped at a light. Hands-free use is permitted.

  22. Cannabis is legal in Canada. What does that mean for driving in Quebec?

    • You may drive after using it
    • It only matters under 25
    • It is fine if you feel alert
    • Driving while impaired by it remains a criminal offenceCorrect answer

    Legal to buy is not legal to drive on. Cannabis slows reaction time and judgement, and the impaired driving law applies as it does with alcohol.

  23. What actually makes a person who has been drinking safe to drive again?

    • Only timeCorrect answer
    • Strong coffee
    • A cold shower
    • Fresh air

    Nothing speeds up how fast the body clears alcohol. Coffee produces a wide-awake impaired driver, which is no safer.

  24. Who is responsible for making sure every passenger under 16 is buckled up?

    • A parent, even if absent
    • The driverCorrect answer
    • Each passenger
    • The vehicle owner

    The driver answers for every passenger under 16, including the correct child seat or booster for their size.

  25. Why must you clear snow from the roof of your car, not just the windows?

    • It saves fuel
    • It is not necessary
    • It can slide onto your windshield or fly into the car behindCorrect answer
    • It looks better

    A slab of roof snow blinds you the moment you brake, or strikes the driver behind at speed. Clear the lights and plate too.

  26. How often should you check your tire pressure?

    • Once a year
    • Only when one looks flat
    • Never, sensors handle it
    • About monthly, when the tires are coldCorrect answer

    Cold weather lowers pressure, and underinflated tires grip worse and wear faster. Check monthly and before a long trip.

  27. You are first at the scene of a collision in Quebec. What comes first?

    • Make the scene safe and call 911Correct answer
    • Move the injured out of the cars
    • Photograph everything
    • Move the vehicles

    Protect the scene and call for help. Do not move an injured person unless there is an immediate danger such as fire.

  28. A tire blows out at highway speed. What should you do first?

    • Switch off the engine
    • Hold the wheel firmly and ease off the gasCorrect answer
    • Brake hard immediately
    • Steer sharply to the shoulder

    A blowout pulls the car sideways. Grip the wheel, come off the gas gently, and brake only once the car is slow and straight.

  29. Why is fatigue treated as seriously as alcohol behind the wheel?

    • It only matters on long trips
    • Coffee removes the risk
    • It slows reaction and judgement much like impairment, and can cause microsleepsCorrect answer
    • It is not, fatigue is harmless

    A driver awake too long makes the same kind of mistakes as an impaired one. The only real fix is to stop and rest.

  30. Your vehicle breaks down on a Quebec autoroute. Where should you stop?

    • In the right lane with hazards on
    • In the left lane
    • On the median
    • As far onto the right shoulder as possibleCorrect answer

    Get right off the travelled lanes, put on your hazard lights, and if it is safe leave the car on the passenger side, away from traffic.

Common questions

How is the Quebec knowledge test scored?

You need 75% in each of the three sections, and a section you fail can be retaken after at least 28 days. Our mock test mirrors that idea by scoring the signs half and the rules half separately.

Who may sit beside me while I practise?

Someone who holds a valid Quebec Class 5 licence and has at least two years of driving experience. They sit in the front passenger seat, and you may not drive between midnight and 5 a.m.

Are these the real questions from the SAAQ?

No. Every question here is written in our own words from the free information the SAAQ publishes. This is an unofficial practice site with no connection to the SAAQ or the Gouvernement du Québec.
