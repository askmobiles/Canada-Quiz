Newfoundland and Labrador Road Signs

Road sign recognition is one of the three things Newfoundland and Labrador says its knowledge test covers. Signs here are grouped by type. Look at the pictures first, then practise the group with real test questions. No timer.

Advertisement

Read the shape and the colour first

Canadian road signs tell you a great deal before you read a single word. A red circle with a slash means you must not do the thing shown, and a green circle means you may. A yellow diamond warns about something ahead, an orange diamond means road work, and green and blue signs give directions and services.

Two warning signs matter more here than almost anywhere else in the country. The moose sign marks a stretch where collisions are known to happen, and the province publishes the pattern behind it: more than seventy percent of moose collisions happen between May and October, and most of them between dusk and dawn.

When the signs feel automatic, move on to the rules half, then put the two together in the timed mock test.

Where to get the official information

Everything on this page comes from what Newfoundland and Labrador publishes for free. The Driver's Handbook is the book the real test is written from, it is free to download from the province's own site, and there is an official French edition as well. Read it alongside this practice, not instead of it.

This is an unofficial free practice site. It is not affiliated with the Motor Registration Division or the Government of Newfoundland and Labrador, and it cannot issue a licence. Always check the official pages for the current rules before you book.

All 30 road sign questions, with every answer explained

This is the whole question bank from the test above, written out so you can read straight through it. The right answer is marked, and every question has a short explanation of why it is the right one.

Show all 30 questions

  1. You reach this sign on an empty street early in the morning. What does the law require?

    • A complete stop, even when you can see the way is clearCorrect answer
    • A rolling stop if nothing is coming
    • A stop only when a pedestrian is waiting
    • A tap on the brakes to show you saw it

    The sign means stop every time. Stop at the stop line, or before the crosswalk if there is no line, and move off only when the way is clear.

  2. You are joining a highway on a ramp posted with this sign. What does it require?

    • Give way to traffic already on the highway, stopping if you have toCorrect answer
    • Come to a full stop at the end of the ramp
    • Keep your speed and expect drivers to open a gap
    • Use the shoulder until a gap appears

    Yield means the other traffic has the right of way. Match your speed to the traffic, look for a real gap, and stop if one does not come.

  3. What is this black and white sign telling you?

    • The highest speed allowed here in good conditionsCorrect answer
    • The speed most drivers here choose
    • The lowest speed allowed
    • A speed suggested for trucks only

    A posted limit is a maximum for a clear, dry day. In rain, snow or fog the safe speed is lower, and driving too fast for the conditions is still an offence.

  4. This sign faces you at the entrance to a street. What should you do?

    • Do not enter, and look for another way roundCorrect answer
    • Enter slowly with your headlights on
    • Enter only to make a delivery
    • Enter and keep well to the right

    The street runs the other way. Driving past this sign puts you head-on into traffic that has no reason to expect you.

  5. You are behind a slow truck and you see this sign. What does it tell you?

    • You must stay behind until the restriction endsCorrect answer
    • You may pass if the truck driver waves you by
    • You may pass if you can do it in under ten seconds
    • You may pass by using the shoulder

    The restriction is posted where you cannot see far enough ahead to pull back in safely. Staying behind costs you a minute; being wrong costs far more.

  6. You want to leave your car here for ten minutes while you run into a shop. This sign is posted. May you?

    • No, leaving the car standing is exactly what this sign forbidsCorrect answer
    • Yes, if you stay under fifteen minutes
    • Yes, if you leave the engine running
    • Yes, if your hazard lights are on

    Parking means leaving the vehicle standing, with or without a driver in it. Hazard lights and a running engine change nothing.

  7. Your phone rings and you look for somewhere to pull over. You see this sign. What does it mean for you?

    • You may not stop here at all, so carry on until the restriction endsCorrect answer
    • You may stop as long as you stay in the vehicle
    • You may stop for up to five minutes
    • You may stop if you switch the engine off

    This is the strictest of the three, and it means no stopping for any reason, even briefly. Carry on to a safe spot beyond the sign before you touch the phone.

  8. This sign is mounted on the side of a school bus and swings out when the bus stops. What does it require of you?

    • Stop and wait until the arm folds back and the lights stop flashingCorrect answer
    • Pass slowly on the left
    • Pass on the right if the shoulder is wide
    • Sound your horn and continue

    Wait until the arm folds back and the red lights stop flashing. Children may still be crossing in front of the bus, where you cannot see them.

  9. A concrete island splits the road ahead and this sign stands on it. What does it tell you?

    • Go by on the right-hand side of the islandCorrect answer
    • Go by on whichever side is clear
    • Stop before the island and wait
    • The road ahead is closed

    Keep right of the island. Choosing the wrong side puts you into traffic coming the other way, which is why the sign is planted on the island itself.

  10. You see this yellow sign on a Newfoundland highway. What is it telling you?

    • You are entering a stretch where moose are often on the roadCorrect answer
    • A farm keeps animals beside the road
    • Hunting is allowed beyond this point
    • A wildlife park entrance is ahead

    The province posts these where moose collisions keep happening. Cut your speed, scan both shoulders far ahead, and be ready for an animal that will not move out of your way.

  11. What is coming up when you pass this warning sign?

    • The pavement runs out and the surface becomes gravelCorrect answer
    • A bridge deck
    • A weigh station
    • A tunnel

    Slow down well before the change. Loose gravel gives far less grip than pavement, and where a gravel road in this province carries no speed sign the limit is 60 km/h.

  12. Rain has just started and you pass this sign. What extra care does it call for?

    • Ease off the accelerator and avoid sudden steering or brakingCorrect answer
    • Turn on your hazard lights
    • Move over to the shoulder
    • Change to a lower gear and hold the brake down

    The road loses grip as soon as it is wet, and the first few minutes of rain are the worst as oil lifts off the surface. Smooth inputs and a bigger gap keep you out of a skid.

  13. You meet this sign at the top of a long descent. What is the safest way down?

    • Slow down before the hill and use a lower gear so the engine helps hold you backCorrect answer
    • Coast down in neutral to save fuel
    • Keep steady pressure on the brake all the way down
    • Speed up so you can climb the next hill

    Riding the brakes the whole way heats them until they fade. A lower gear does most of the work and leaves the brakes fresh for the moment you really need them.

  14. What is the road like on the stretch where this sign is posted?

    • There is a series of curves in both directionsCorrect answer
    • There is one sharp curve, then the road is straight
    • The road ends shortly
    • The road is closed in winter

    A winding road means several bends one after another. Set your speed before the first one and stay off the brakes in the middle of each curve.

  15. This sign appears before a bridge. What should you expect?

    • The bridge is narrower than the road, so the lanes tightenCorrect answer
    • The bridge carries one direction only
    • The bridge has a weight limit
    • The bridge deck is gravel

    The lanes squeeze together on the structure. Slow down, place your vehicle in the middle of your lane, and never try to pass on the bridge.

  16. What does this sign mean for the way you drive on the stretch ahead?

    • Children may be near or on the road, so slow down and be ready to stopCorrect answer
    • Children are only present in September
    • You may park on both sides
    • Buses have priority over you

    School zones are where children step out without checking. Where a school zone in this province carries no speed sign of its own the limit is 50 km/h, and posted school zones run lower, from 30 to 50.

  17. A row of these markers is lined up on the outside of a bend. What are they for?

    • They show which way the road turns and how sharplyCorrect answer
    • They mark a pedestrian crossing
    • They mark the edge of a parking area
    • They show where snowploughs turn round

    Chevrons point the way through the curve. If you can see several of them stacked close together, the bend is tighter than it looks from the approach.

  18. You pass this sign on a road cut into a hillside. What are you being warned about?

    • Rock may have fallen onto the road from the slope aboveCorrect answer
    • Loose gravel spread by a truck
    • Blasting is going on nearby
    • The road is unpaved beyond this point

    Rock comes down after rain and after a freeze and thaw. Scan the surface far enough ahead that you can steer around a boulder instead of meeting it.

  19. You reach this white X-shaped sign at a railway line that has no gates and no lights. What is your duty?

    • Slow down, look and listen both ways, and cross only when it is clearly safeCorrect answer
    • Cross without slowing, since there are no gates
    • Stop every time, as you would at a stop sign
    • Sound your horn and cross

    The crossbuck marks the crossing itself and gives you no protection at all. A train needs far more distance to stop than you do, so the looking is yours to do.

  20. Orange signs like this one appear beside the road. What do they mean?

    • Roadwork is going on, so expect workers, equipment and changed conditionsCorrect answer
    • A permanent change to the road layout
    • A route set aside for large trucks
    • A tourist route

    Orange always means temporary. Slow to the posted work-zone speed, watch for people on foot, and expect the lane to shift without much notice.

  21. This orange sign warns that somebody will be directing traffic ahead. How much authority do their signals carry?

    • You must obey them, even where they contradict the usual rulesCorrect answer
    • They are advice you may take or leave
    • They apply to trucks only
    • They apply only when no police officer is present

    A traffic control person guides you through the work zone and their instructions come first. Watch the paddle, and do not move until you are waved on.

  22. What does this orange sign ask you to do?

    • Leave the usual route and follow the marked way around the closureCorrect answer
    • Leave the highway for good at the next exit
    • Stop and wait for a pilot vehicle
    • Turn back the way you came

    A detour routes you around a closed section and back onto your road. Follow the whole run of detour markers instead of guessing your own way through side streets.

  23. What does this route marker identify?

    • The Trans-Canada Highway, the route that crosses the province and the countryCorrect answer
    • A city bypass
    • A scenic coastal loop
    • A road that is closed in winter

    The marker keeps you on the Trans-Canada. Where the paved Trans-Canada in this province carries no speed sign, the limit is 100 km/h.

  24. What does this blue sign point you to?

    • A hospitalCorrect answer
    • A pharmacy
    • A rest area
    • A police station

    Blue signs point to services rather than telling you what to do. Knowing this one matters more on a long highway drive than anywhere else.

  25. You are running low and you see this blue sign. What is it telling you?

    • Fuel is available at the next exit or turningCorrect answer
    • A weigh station is ahead
    • A vehicle inspection station is ahead
    • Parking is available

    Blue service signs are worth watching on long stretches with few communities, because the next chance to fill up can be a very long way further on.

  26. Who may use a parking space marked with this sign?

    • Only a vehicle displaying a valid accessible parking permitCorrect answer
    • Anyone, once the lot is full
    • Anyone stopping for under ten minutes
    • Delivery vehicles

    The space exists because somebody needs the extra room and the short distance. Using it without a permit is an offence, however briefly you are there.

  27. The light turns amber as you approach and you are still some distance back. What is required?

    • Stop, unless you are so close that stopping would be unsafeCorrect answer
    • Speed up to clear the intersection
    • Cover the brake but carry on through
    • Stop only if a vehicle is waiting on the cross street

    Amber means the red is coming, not that you have a few seconds in hand. Stop if you can do it safely, and go through only where braking would put you or the driver behind you at risk.

  28. You reach an intersection where the light is flashing red. What must you do?

    • Stop completely, then go when the way is clearCorrect answer
    • Slow down and go through if nothing is coming
    • Treat it as a green light
    • Wait for the light to become steady

    A flashing red works exactly like a stop sign. Every driver arriving at it must stop, so take your turn rather than assume the right of way is yours.

  29. Two solid yellow lines separate you from oncoming traffic. What are you not allowed to do?

    • Cross them to pass the vehicle aheadCorrect answer
    • Drive at the posted speed limit
    • Change lanes on your own side of the road
    • Follow the road around a curve

    Yellow separates traffic moving in opposite directions, and a solid line on your side means passing is not permitted along that stretch.

  30. A wide white line is painted across your lane at an intersection. What is it for?

    • It shows where the front of your vehicle must stopCorrect answer
    • It marks where pedestrians walk
    • It separates two lanes going the same way
    • It marks a bus stop

    Stop with your bumper behind the line rather than on it. Stopping past it blocks the crosswalk and hides you from traffic on the cross street.

About this page

Road sign recognition is one of the three subjects Newfoundland and Labrador says its knowledge test covers, alongside the rules of the road and safe driving practices. This page holds the sign half of our question bank.

Every sign on this page is drawn in code rather than copied from a government publication, so nothing here is anyone else's artwork. Each sign carries its own name as a caption and in its alt text, so the page works with a screen reader too.

What you'll find here

  • Thirty sign questions covering regulatory, warning, temporary, information, traffic light and pavement marking signs.
  • Practice for each group of signs, so you can work through one family at a time.
  • The moose warning sign, which matters more in this province than in most of the country.
  • The whole sign bank printed below the practice, with the answer marked and explained.

Worth remembering

  • A red circle with a slash means you must not do the thing shown, and a green circle means you may.
  • A yellow diamond warns about something ahead, while an orange diamond means road work.
  • The province publishes that most moose collisions happen on clear nights rather than in bad weather.
  • Signs are the same across Canada, so studying them here also prepares you for a test in another province.

Common questions

Are these the real Newfoundland and Labrador signs?

They are our own drawings of the standard Canadian signs used in the province. They are not copied from any official publication.

Does the real test include signs?

Yes. The province states that the test asks about the rules of the road, safe driving practices and road sign recognition.

Is sign recognition scored separately?

The province does not say. It describes one test covering three topics, so we keep signs and rules as separate study pages without claiming they are separate tests.

How many signs should I learn?

All of the ones on this page. They are the families the handbook covers, and once the shapes and colours are automatic the wording matters much less.
