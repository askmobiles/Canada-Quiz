📖 Winter in Canada

Canada has long, cold winters. Winter brings snow, short days, northern lights, and animals with clever ways to survive the cold.

1947

The coldest day

On February 3, 1947, it was about −63 °C at Snag, in Yukon. That is the coldest temperature ever officially recorded in Canada.

December 21

The shortest day

The winter solstice comes around December 21. It is the shortest day of the year, with the least daylight. After it, the days slowly get longer.

Every winter

A month without sunrise

Inuvik, in the Northwest Territories, is north of the Arctic Circle. The sun does not rise there for about 30 days, from early December to early January. The town celebrates its return with a sunrise festival.

Every winter

The northern lights

The northern lights happen when tiny charged particles from the Sun hit gases high in the air. Yellowknife, in the Northwest Territories, is one of the best places in the world to see them.

Every winter

A white winter coat

The snowshoe hare is brown in summer and turns almost pure white in winter. Shorter days tell its body when to change.

Every winter

Sleeping through winter

Black bears spend the winter in dens in a deep winter sleep. They live off the fat stored in their bodies and do not need to eat.

Every winter

Wind chill

Wind makes cold air feel colder. When the wind chill is −28 to −39, uncovered skin can freeze in 10 to 30 minutes. Hats, mittens and scarves keep it covered.

Long ago

The igloo

Inuit built igloos, houses made of blocks of snow. In Inuktitut, the word iglu simply means house.

📖 Winter fun, made in Canada

Snowshoes and toboggans come from Indigenous peoples, the first recorded organized indoor hockey game was played in Montreal, and Canadian cities throw big festivals in the middle of winter.

Long ago

Snowshoes

Indigenous peoples across most of Canada made and used snowshoes to walk on deep snow without sinking. People still use them for fun and for work today.

Long ago

The toboggan

The word toboggan comes from the Mi'kmaq word topagan or the Maliseet word 'tapakon, through Canadian French.

1875

The first indoor hockey game

On March 3, 1875, the first recorded organized indoor hockey game was played at the Victoria Skating Rink in Montreal.

1955

The Quebec Winter Carnival

The modern Quebec Winter Carnival began in 1955. Its mascot is Bonhomme Carnaval.

1979

Winterlude

Winterlude began in Ottawa in 1979 and is held every February.

2007

The Rideau Canal Skateway

In winter, 7.8 km of the Rideau Canal in Ottawa becomes a skating rink, the largest naturally frozen rink in the world. The canal became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2007.

2010

Vancouver 2010

At the 2010 Winter Olympics in Vancouver, Canada won 14 gold medals, the most any country had won at a single Winter Games until then.

December

Winter tires in Quebec

In Quebec, the law says cars must have winter tires from December 1 to March 15, because they grip better on snow and ice.

📖 Holidays in the cold months

Canadians celebrate many holidays in December and January, from Hanukkah and Christmas to Kwanzaa, the New Year and Lunar New Year.

December

Hanukkah

Hanukkah, the Jewish Festival of Lights, lasts eight nights. Each night, one more candle is lit on the menorah.

December 25

Christmas

Christians celebrate the birth of Jesus at Christmas, on December 25. Christmas Day is a statutory holiday across Canada.

December 24

Réveillon and tourtière

In Quebec, many families share a réveillon, a feast late on Christmas Eve after midnight mass. Tourtière, a meat pie, is a famous dish at the table.

December

Mummering

In Newfoundland and Labrador, mummers (also called janneys) put on disguises during the 12 days of Christmas and visit homes, where hosts try to guess who they are.

1642

The Huron Carol

Canada's first Christmas carol, Jesous Ahatonhia, is in the Wendat (Huron) language. It is widely believed to have been written by the missionary Jean de Brébeuf around 1642.

January 7

Christmas in January

Many Orthodox Christians in Canada celebrate Christmas on January 7. Since 2023, many Ukrainian churches in Canada have moved to December 25, so families may celebrate on either day.

December 26

Boxing Day and Kwanzaa

Boxing Day is December 26. It is a statutory holiday in Ontario and for federally regulated workers. Kwanzaa, a celebration of African heritage, runs from December 26 to January 1.

January 1

The first to ring in the New Year

The island of Newfoundland is on Newfoundland Time, half an hour ahead of Atlantic Time, so Newfoundland and Labrador is the first province to ring in the New Year.

January or February

Lunar New Year

Many Canadians of Chinese, Vietnamese, Korean and other Asian backgrounds celebrate Lunar New Year in late January or February, often with special family meals.

1982

Santa's postal code

Since 1982, Canada Post has helped Santa answer children's letters sent to the postal code H0H 0H0. Santa answers in more than 30 languages, including braille.

1917

A tree for Boston

After the Halifax Explosion of December 6, 1917, Boston sent help. Halifax thanked the city with a Christmas tree in 1918, and since 1971 Nova Scotia has sent Boston a tree every year.
