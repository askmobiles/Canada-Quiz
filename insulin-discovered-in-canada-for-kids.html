The Canadian Discovery That Saved Millions of Lives

Four people in Toronto found the thing that turned a deadly illness into an illness you can live with.

A hundred years ago, a child who got diabetes did not live long. There was nothing anyone could do. Doctors could only make the children comfortable.

In 1921, at the University of Toronto, a doctor named Frederick Banting and a student named Charles Best began working on the problem. A professor called John Macleod gave them the laboratory. A chemist named James Collip found a way to make their medicine pure enough to use.

They called the medicine insulin.

On the 23rd of January 1922, they gave it to a boy named Leonard Thompson. He was fourteen years old and very ill. Within days he was better. He was the first person in the world that insulin saved.

Insulin is not a cure. Leonard still had diabetes. But he lived another thirteen years, and today millions of people take insulin every day and live long, ordinary lives.

The discoverers could have become extremely rich. Instead they sold the rights to their university for one dollar each, so that insulin could be made cheaply everywhere. Banting said insulin did not belong to him. It belonged to the world.

Did you know? They sold the most valuable medicine of their time for one dollar each, on purpose.
